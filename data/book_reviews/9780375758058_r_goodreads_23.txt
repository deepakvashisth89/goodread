I'm so surprised that Fanny Burney could write in such a modern way: her style is not at all dated, and the story is funny and compelling.
The Evelina of the title is a young girl, not recognized by her noble father Lord John Belmont, and raised up by a vicar in the country.
She is pure and naive, and at the beginning of the novel she is invited to meet some acquantances in London. She will find herself in a whirlwind of balls, concerts and plays, at disadvantage among vain people, so different from her. Through her letters she will keep her adopted father (and the reader too) updated of what will happen to her in the big city.
The prose is really brilliant, a real treat.
