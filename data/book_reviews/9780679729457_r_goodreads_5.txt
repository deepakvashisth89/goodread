"...if Lyndon Johnson was not a reader of books, he was a reader of men--a reader with a rare ability to see into their souls."-- Robert A Caro, The Path to PowerI'll write more tomorrow, but if the next three (and the final, yet to be written book) are as polished and well-researched as this one, this may end up being the definitive biography of any president. I loved Morris'Theodore Roosevelt Trilogy. It and Ron Chernow's biography ofAlexander Hamiltonwere nearly tied in my affection and esteem as my favorite political biographies. This one just jumped ahead.Caro writes as well about place and people as John McPhee. He seems to possess all the qualities you want in an academic and popular historian. I bought these novels years ago. They sat collecting dust behind me and haunted me, but I was afraid to pick them up. They are just damn intimidating. Not just their thickness, but also the heft. The books are dense and heavy. So, each time I turned to read them, I imagined some immense mountain I would need to climb. But Caro's narrative is so easy, so fascinating, so compelling that the mountain practically pulls you up. After the first chapter I had a hard time putting this beast down.
