Virgil presents a universe in which human beings possess remarkably little free will. The gods are vain, vindictive, manipulative, and deceitful, pulling the strings behind nearly every conflict and tragedy. Much of the suffering in the epic seems less the result of human choice than of divine caprice.
What impresses me most is that this masterpiece was written more than two millennia ago. I was captivated by its narrative, its subtle yet unmistakable political propaganda, its exquisite prose, its wisdom, and the astonishing variety of gruesome ways warriors meet their end on the ancient battlefields.


(My comment: More gods meant more wars. History has shown that humanity has always found ways to wage war in the name of the divine. It is no surprise that the Roman Empire eventually abandoned its pantheon of gods and embraced the monotheism of Christianity)
