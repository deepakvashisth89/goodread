This book is more like poetry in prose form. The images are dream-like and fantastical, while having down-to-earth imagery that makes them come alive. For the Arthurian enthusiast, this book will not satisfy - Arthur gets only passing mention. But as a work of fantasy and emotion, it's quite good. The word 'anachronism' doesn't apply here, as the action takes place whenever it needs to (a 19th-century snowstorm is mentioned, along with Arthurian-age history, so nothing is out of bounds). The stories are more about mood than plot, and in that it succeeds very well.
