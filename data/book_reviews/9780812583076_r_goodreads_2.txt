Father Marc Dupre comes from a background of strife and war. Huron Andiora is living through strife and war. She has had a dream vision of a blonde Black Robe who would bring a terrible disease to her people and kill many. Father Dupre travels from France to New France and Huron country, meeting Andiora and some of her family outside of Quebec. The Hurons have agreed to guide the Catholic priests (Father Dupre travels with Father Luc Penchant and Brother Phillippe Raimont) back to their village and the mission there led by Father Jean de Brebeuf. On the way, autumn weather turns to winter, and when Andiora falls through some ice on a river, Marc dives in to save her. Their relationship begins but is fraught with suspicion, cultural clashes and misunderstandings. Will they overcome it all to be together? Will the Black Robes survive the winter and Iroquois attacks? Oh, by the way, Father Dupre is blonde.This novel is an historical romance that strives to reveal the Huron way of life contrasted with Catholic devotion. It is also quite violent at times, but I believe the violence is justified. The Native Americans fought viciously when they fought, and their warriors had some extremely cruel and violent customs. The writing is adequate to the task, with some lovely turns of phrase at times and images that stick with the reader.I guess I'm not really a romance reader, although I've enjoyed some other romances in the past. I found the characters to be 2-dimensional, struggling over and over with the same issues without finding creative solutions, and reacting rather than acting. Pierre Mongrave was especially irritating to me. I had a hard time believing that this French sea captain would say "Darlin'" as an endearment because to my ear it sounds like Southern American English rather than French. So that is a tricky thing in writing dialogue -- the European characters are French, but their English doesn't support that. There are ways to suggest a foreign accent or language when writing dialogue in English. I also felt like the story rambled and had no real forward momentum. The ensemble cast was also too large, diffusing the power of their characterizations and keeping them more 2-dimensional.I liked the historical aspects of this novel, and enjoyed some of the writing. I'd recommend it to romance readers interested in historical romance, or readers interested in 17th century North America.
