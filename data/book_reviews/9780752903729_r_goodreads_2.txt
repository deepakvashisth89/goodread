There’s a lot to love about this piece of anti-communist propaganda. While I found the net of lies Nicky finds himself in dizzying and pedantic, and a bit overdone, and the ending was more bittersweet than I hoped for, there’s no denying that this is a thrilling novel and period piece which really gives you a glimpse of how people thought at the time, and I’m sure Wheatley drew from actual stories of the USSR to craft this tale. I found myself much more invested in the second half of the novel once the action picks up. I also love that Wheatley inserted himself into the novel when Nicky references some of his other books, a la Stephen King, only Wheatley did it first. Not the best Wheatley book but well worth your time if you like his style. I’ve only read his occult books so I wasn’t sure what to expect, but I’ll definitely be checking out more of his non-horror work.
