The original eco-novel, published in 1941, this tracks a storm from its formation off the coast of Japan as it barrels across the Pacific and rages along the California coast for several days, before eventually moving inland and dying.Stewart toggles back and forth between meteorologists, airport traffic controllers, utility workers, and a host of other people affected (some fatally) by this storm. I also really enjoyed how minor things, like the burrowing of a chipmunk or a stray shot from a kid's BB gun, had "butterfly effect" ramifications in the days of rain and snow that followed. Stewart was a really smart dude, and while his dialogue is stiff as a board, this was a wild ride of a book, a really fun read that propels the action along at a great clip.Fun fact, this book prompted the National Weather Service to start naming storms, which is why we do it today. :)
