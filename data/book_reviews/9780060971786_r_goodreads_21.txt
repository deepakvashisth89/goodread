Arthur Miller told the tale of his generation in DEATH OF A SALESMAN and THE CRUCIBLE, and in the process saved American theater. He also took time, not that he wanted to, to testify as an unfriendly witness before the HUAC and get himself indicted, share Marilyn Monroe with Elia Kazan, finally marrying her himself, writing the script for ON THE WATERFRONT, and fighting with Kazan over his friendly testimony for the HUAC, costing him the job, working to nominate Gene McCarthy in 1968 to stop the Vietnam War, and losing his grip over Broadway after the flop of THE CREATION OF THE WORLD in 1970. It's all here in vivid prose; the triumphs, regrets and literary immortality of an American original. Be sure to read the paperback edition. Miller has attached a new forward lamenting that the hardback did not sell so well, "probably because people expected more details about my marriage to Marilyn Monroe".
