I'm used to experiencing a time warp when I enter the world of a Patricia Highsmith novel. Whoosh, and I'm back in the 50s or 60s, people casually drinking highballs at all times of the day or night, unbothered by modern technology, sexually and socially censored (but cleverly revealed nonetheless).Found in the Street, her twentieth and penultimate novel, published in 1986, felt like a different kind of time warp, and is about as modern as one of Pat's books is going to get. She uses words like "AIDS" and "orgasm" and "masturbate". And her gay characters are openly gay.Because she was able to be less restrained, the book feels modern-ish. Still, all her brilliance is unchanged. This novel is about obsession, the object of obsession being the beautiful 20 year old Elsie, who captures the attention and adoration of pretty much anyone who meets her. Whether it's Ralph Linderman, a fabulous weirdo who has a dog named God, or Jack Sutherland, a sophisticated artist, or his wife, Natalia, or any number of other men and women.For those who think that this is a thriller, you'll have to recalibrate. She's not playing a game with the usual tropes. There's plenty of tension, and she keeps you guessing as to where she's taking you, but the delight for me is that this is far more cerebral, far more of a study of alienation, obsession, and the shadows of self, than it is a thriller.If there is a game here, it's the game of "who's the creep". Everyone has their eyes on Elsie. Is it the atheist God-walking Ralph? Is it respectable, married Jack, who loves from afar? How about Natalia, who isn't about to let a marriage or household keep her from doing exactly as she pleases?What chance does Elsie, this lovely, unspoiled creature have, in a city of creeps?God only knows...The trick was the delicate balance between dreaming and trying, Jack thought, feeling happier by the minute.
