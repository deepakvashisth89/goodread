If you’re a fan of classic Cold War espionage, "Code Name November" by Bill Granger is an absolute gem that deserves its place among the great spy thrillers.What struck me most is how effortlessly Granger builds tension without relying on gimmicks. The story feels grounded, sharp, and intelligently paced, pulling you into a shadowy world where alliances shift constantly and nothing is ever quite what it seems. The setting—hovering around the Irish Sea—adds an extra layer of atmosphere, giving the novel a cold, uneasy edge that perfectly matches its themes.At the heart of it all is Devereaux, the November Man himself. He’s not your typical spy hero—he’s complex, calculating, and often operating in morally gray territory. That’s exactly what makes him so compelling. He’s both hunter and hunted, a man caught between powerful forces like the CIA and the KGB, yet always playing his own game. Watching him navigate this dangerous landscape is incredibly satisfying.The plot is tightly woven, with high-stakes intrigue involving assassination attempts and geopolitical maneuvering, but it never feels overwhelming. Instead, it keeps you engaged from start to finish, rewarding careful attention while still delivering plenty of suspense and action.Overall, "Code Name November" is a smart, gripping introduction to a fascinating character. It’s the kind of thriller that reminds you why the genre became so popular in the first place—cool, clever, and impossible to put down. I’m definitely looking forward to continuing the series.
