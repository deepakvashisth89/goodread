Lush lyrical language. The character development is strong, but not quite satisfying for me, probably because the plot spans so much time & we are brought inside the consciousness of so many characters--there are many vignettes but also many summary ("telling" rather than "showing") sections. There's no central point of view. Interestingly, overall it's a novel about generations of women, yet the most likable, interesting character is a male: LaRue (known to some as Bone), brother, inveterate storyteller, eventually husband/father. Despite my caveats, it's a worthy story of African American lives in a southern state (the novel doesn't tell us the place early on, & by now I've forgotten) & (a little) in Detroit.
