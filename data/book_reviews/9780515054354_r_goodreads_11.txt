Killer Dolphin (Roderick Alleyn, #24) by Ngaio Marsh.The title had me wondering and I'd think it may have thrown quite a few people off just thinking about it's meaning. It's meaning doesn't come completely clear until a bit into the story. Peregrine Jay is a theater director and a playwright. His life is centered on the theater and he is currently focused on the old Dolphin theater. He actually dwells on it's glorious past and his heartfelt wish it to have it restored to its' original glory. Then one evening he decides to take a tour of the Dolphin alone but with the caretaker's (Henry Jobbins) permission, of course.Jobbins warns Peregrine about damage on stage that could be quite dangerous if he fell through that hole. Peregrine remembers that warning a bit too late and falls through into deep water with nothing to hang on to. That is until a hand is extended to him and brings him safely out of that abyss. That's where out story begins.The author tells this story with knowledge since she spent some time working in the theater in New Zealand. There a re some characters I grew to like which made the reading all the more enjoyable. Another 5 stars for a favorite author of mine.
