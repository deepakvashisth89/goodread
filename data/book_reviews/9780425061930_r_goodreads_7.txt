Came for the sentient kelp religion, stayed for the sentient kelp religion.

Despite the underdeveloped, superficial, cardboard cut-out characters, a highlight for me was the way this book shares with Herbert's Dune series the rich and fertile idea that a nondual, oneness experience might form the universal core of religion. Only here, the authors build an entire narrative world around the speculation that that same core might be found at the heart of the religious experience of very different kinds of beings. Intriguingly, the narrative explores the way that this core constitutes a psychological common ground between us and them that makes possible a deep level of communication with both alien and artificial sentience.
