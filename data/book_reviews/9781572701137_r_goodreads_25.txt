"I suppose sooner or later in the life of everyone comes a moment of trial. We all of us have our particular devil who rides us and torments us, and we must give battle in the end."

Rebecca is a stunningly written and deeply eerie gothic mystery that follows the new Mrs de Winter, struggling to escape the shadow of her predecessor, the late Rebecca, while sinking unwittingly into an obsession with her.

This novel is incredibly atmospheric and lyrical, keeping me fascinated and on edge from the first page to the last as the tension increased to impossible levels. I felt for our unnamed protagonist, so young and sheltered and shy, unable to step into the shoes of the dazzling Rebecca, lonely and tentative and afraid as she was. She is out of place in this world, and everyone knows it.

This chilling novel had twists and turns that took the narrative in directions I didn't expect, and the descriptions of Manderley, the flowers, the forest, and the sea transported me to another place and time.

Rebecca is a new absolute favourite.

"The road to Manderley lay ahead. There was no moon. The sky above our heads was inky black. But the sky on the horizon was not dark at all. It was shot with crimson, like a splash of blood. And the ashes blew towards us with the salt wind from the sea."


Trigger/Content Warnings: death, murder, toxic relationship, terminal illness, emotional abuse, suicide, incest, adultery, fatphobia, outdated use of the word "queer" and treatment of a mentally disabled character
