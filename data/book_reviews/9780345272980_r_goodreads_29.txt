He was there at the beginning and was there to see it end. Phillip Caputo was among the first Marines to land at Da Nang in 1965 and then in 1975 he was among those evacuating, then as a reporter, Saigon when it fell. The main story is a memoir about his first days in Vietnam in 1965 when he was a newly minted Lieutenant where he led men in the jungles and rice paddies. He shares his experiences in firefights, ambushes, heat, patrol, mosquitoes, landmines, booby-traps and the constant stress of never knowing where or when the enemy would strike.Lieutenant Caputo goes from a motivated charging Marine Platoon leader to someone who saw no sense in what he was being ordered to do. Patrols and firefights soon become monotonous with their uncertain outcome and often re-fighting the same ground repeated. The fact that the majority of casualties came from an unseen enemy from booby traps and ambushed who would often slip away unseen afterward added to the stress.Part way during his tour he is put in charge of counting casualties for both sides. He had to often record and verify casualties in person. He begins to see that what matters to command is the numbers which places pressure on troops in the field. One commander even posts a reward of beer and time off to enjoy it if they killed the enemy.He is put back in command after demanding a transfer. It is after which he is nearly court-martialed after some of his troops kill a couple of Vietnamese, who were believed to be innocent, in cold blood. After this incident he is transferred out. The story ends with his returning at the end of the war evacuating as a reporter as the North Vietnamese Army is entering Saigon marking the end of the war. It is notable that he was there at the beginning and end of the conflict.The book paints a brutal picture of both the horror and brutality of the American war in Vietnam. As I listened to the book I have to admit a lot of the combat seemed wear me out as it seemed repeat so often, it was then I realized that was, whether intentional or not, part of the point. It showed the tedium and monotony was part of the battle. A major part of the stress was the long periods of quiet tension followed by short intense periods of action and horror.If one wants an on the ground feel and story of what it was like in Vietnam as a Marine you couldn’t go wrong with this one. The details of war are neither glossed over, sugar coated or glorified. I highly recommend this book for those looking for a gritty on the ground look the war in Vietnam on the ground.
