Power struggles always become more interesting when pride starts steering every decision.

The Iron King by Maurice Druon opens the historical fantasy-inspired series The Accursed Kings with the reign of King Philip IV of France. The story centers on political tension, royal scandals, betrayal, and the fallout from the downfall of the Knights Templar. As accusations spread and loyalties shift, nearly every character seems driven by ambition, fear, or revenge.

I liked how sharp and readable the political drama felt despite the large cast and historical setting. I expected something distant and heavy, but the intrigue pulls you in quickly. Alliances change constantly, and there’s this lingering sense that nobody truly trusts anyone. A few chapters genuinely made me pause just to process how calculated certain decisions were.

The writing carries a cold, restrained tone that fits the ruthless world of the French court. Philip IV comes across as intimidating even in quieter moments, while characters like Isabella add tension in more unpredictable ways. That said, the number of names and political connections can feel overwhelming early on, especially if you’re not already comfortable with historical fiction.

The pacing stays steady, focusing more on schemes and consequences than action. Themes of corruption, control, religion, and legacy run throughout the novel without feeling overly forced. Even when the story slows, the tension underneath never fully disappears.

I’m giving this 4 out of five stars.

Readers who enjoy political maneuvering, royal drama, and morally complicated characters will likely find a lot to appreciate here, especially fans of historical fiction with a darker edge.
