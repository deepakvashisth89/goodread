I am slowly working my way through all of Tolkien's work, which is a relatively daunting task (mostly because of the ever-looming HOME), but it is also spattered with little works such as this. I'm not sure if it was ever marketed as a 'children's book', though it certainly feels like one; there are no dark lords, dragons or rings to be found here. Only special cakes and languid trips to the Fay world.Smith of Wootton Majorwas the final thing Tolkien published in his lifetime, as I suppose the obsessions of Middle-earth were finally loosening their grip (if they ever did). This is no Tolkien at the height of his power giving us one final, abstract and dense piece of writing that might hold the key to all others; it reads more like an old man, quiet and tired, writing one last gentle story before slipping away. Christopher Lee's favourite story, so it seemed, was recounting the time he spotted Tolkien once entering a pub he was drinking in. I can't remember everything Lee said about it, but I remember him describing Tolkien as "jovial" looking. Of course, plenty of people have tried to find an allegory hidden within this little tale about a boy who eats a cake with a magic star in it that eventually ends up in his forehead and gives him access to the Fay world. It is no surprise that most allegories lean towards the religious. Perhaps. But more importantly, it's a sweet little tale from one of England's greatest writers.
