His delightful writing style sweeps you along with the actor growing up in WW2 England. 'The child is father to the man' as they say. He introduces us to his family, friends, and adventures, along with the unique dialects of his youth, including the whimsical Cockney argot that befuddles so many Americans: 'trouble and strife' = 'wife', 'dog and bone' = phone. Strange.I had no idea that O'Toole was so obsessed with the life of Adolf Hitler, but it's understandable, I suppose, since Germany was busily bombing England at the time and O'Toole was relocated to the countryside, as were many if not most children during those terrible years. So as a kind of bonus, you get a brief bio of the Fuehrer too.
