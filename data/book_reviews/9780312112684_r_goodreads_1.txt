Quite different from the previous books in this series. The story does not involve the school or students. It revolves around a stolen identity and how that stolen identity puts our heroes in harms way from Mexican drug lords. There are the typical scenes of running from the enemy, the slow response of the police to help Tom and Scott and the graphic episodes of violence caused by the villains. The only part of the book that was truly engaging was when Tom explores an abandoned house looking for Scott. Other than that, I'm afraid the progression of the plot was by the numbers. On the plus side, Tom was not as obnoxious and selfish as he has been in previous books. Even at that, however, there were still plenty of those characteristics in this book. I realize the series must be quite popular because there are so many volumes that have been published. But, Tom is the type person who believes that it is his way or no way. I do not find that a very beneficial way to realize a main character. The character of Tom in this series just comes off as an angry gay man that sees the straight world as out to get all gay people. A hint here on how life works. Nothing exists in terms of all right and all wrong. There are lots of gray areas that make life bearable.
