A superb travelogue that for me surpassed Paul Theroux's best. Japan really is like nowhere else, and Ferguson, who sucked me in right from the off and made for such great company, gives much illuminating insight into Japanese culture and history, as he meets many interesting and friendly people on his fantastic hitchhiking journey. It is just as much a fascinating study of the Japanese people as it is a personal travelogue, and for that I'm going to bump it up from 4 to 5 stars. Certain things didn't come as a surprise; like the fact that Japan has such a low crime rate where even a mugging is considered a major news story. Some things though I wasn't expecting to come across; like bullfighting, medieval castles and sex museums. Overall, a thoroughly enjoyable read that, whilst featuring a great sense of humour - in places it's so funny, it also had a deeply affecting side to it too. Ferguson might not be the perfect role model when it comes to the western hitchhiker, but those that seemed to find his narrative voice to be offensive or spiteful in any way, I'm really not sure what book they were reading. The only bitterness I found here was when I put one too many lime slices in my rum and coke.
