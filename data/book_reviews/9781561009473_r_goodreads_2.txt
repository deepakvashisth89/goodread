This is a book that I bought and gave as a Mother’s Day gift to my mom in 1997 shortly after it was published. After my mom’s death, I kept the book and many times I planned to read all the articles about each set of mothers and daughters, but never seemed to find time. I was always reading novels and don’t like to read more than one book at a time. So, finally, after these many years, I picked it up and began to read.This was a fascinating book to read; not at all boring as I had thought it might be. These articles of mothers and daughters are mostly interviews with both women/girls talking frankly about their relationship. Some genuinely happy and enjoyable, but some distinctly touched with unhappiness, even rancor. The photos of each mother, some with a single daughter or, in one case, as many as 9 daughters, reveal the tone of their relationship. I love the fact that most were everyday people and only a few were famous or well known names like Janet Leigh and her daughter Jamie Leigh Curtis. I was also delighted to find Cokie Roberts, and her mother Lindy Boggs included as I was a fan of both Southern born ladies, especially the well known TV journalist/newswoman Cokie Roberts.Although this looks like just an attractive coffee table book, it is so much more, as you will discover as you read through the book, it makes you question your relationship with your own mother and/or daughter.
