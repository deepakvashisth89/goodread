Honestly, a little gem of a book. If only I have read it after my course at the Atlantic Theatre Course which was founded form the practical aesthetics workshop from David Mamet's and W.H Macy's acting classes. But it has been on my mind since - a beautiful full circle moment.

I appreciate their framework for the job/role of the actor who can only bring himself to the theatre in optimum condition: 'identifying what things he can do to put himself in optimum condition and then doing them consistently so that they become habitual and will give the actor the satisfaction of always knowing what to do - what his job truly is'. Examples include developing measurable skills: a strong, clear resonant voice (working daily on effective vocal exercises); a strong, supple and graceful body (exercise and study of movement); your ability to analyse a script correctly, your ability to concentrate. The actor must also look at himself honestly, which requires bravery and common sense to determine your own shortcomings and which of these are within your control to change. In short, use your will to become to the fullest possible extent the person he would ideally like to be. Next step: list those things that are your responsibilities and separate them from things that are not.

Another major part of the actor's job is to find a way to live truthfully under the imaginary circumstances of the play. Their approach to do so is deciding what is going in the text in simple, actable terms.

The common sense to translate whatever you are given into simple actable terms; the bravery to throw yourself in the action of the play despite fear of failure and self-consciousness; the will to adhere to your ideals.

What’s been such a release is to not put any strain on my feelings since they are not within my control: there’s no common sense to ‘I must feel this certain way’. Instead this is what I’m doing irrespective of how it makes me feel. I think because I received the note to sustain my emotional connection to the text, I put a lot of focus on it. And I’m still only starting learning to let go of it. Last week actually, having done the prep work feelings just rose up that I did not indulge in.

All in all, and I’ve always intuitively followed this path of developing the craft that will work for me. I am still very much in the process of acquiring skills and making them habitual!
