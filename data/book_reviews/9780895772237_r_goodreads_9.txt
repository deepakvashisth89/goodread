It might just be a "me" problem, but I found it highly difficult to keep track of the eccentric characters. Out of nowhere, names like Dick, Barkis, Spenlow, Micawber and Traddles would pop back into the story. Because they frequently vanished and reappeared, it became a bit of a chore to remember who was who.

Evaluating David as a protagonist leaves me deeply conflicted. His evolution from a painfully shy little boy into a confident man who finally knew what he wants even if it takes a few harsh realities to get him there, is methinks, the best character development arc in English Literature's history. However, it is quite impossible to ignore his recklessness. He often idealized the flawed people around him and let raw emotion override common sense at every turn of his life. Furthermore, a bit of elitism and harsh judgment runs beneath his charm. It is precisely this self-righteousness and naivety of him that makes him such a triumph of character development- he is profoundly, realistically flawed. I wanted to shake some sense into him occasionally.

Then there is Agnes, whose absolute goodness feels almost impossible to fathom. Watching David blindly obsess over Dora while Agnes patiently stands by as his quiet confidante is completely selflessness but it is an unattainable standard of behavior. Frankly, I could never be like her. To watch the person you love be completely infatuated with someone else requires a level of emotional saintliness that defies normal human nature. Most of us would be consumed by entirely valid resentment and jealousy.

David’s childhood with the Murdstones are among the most brutal in classic literature. The agonizing trauma of being unloved and completely powerless was perfectly captured here. That sense of raw injustice was really aching and no child should ever endure that. Honestly, after reading a few of his books, it’s hard not to notice how much of his own life is reflected in his characters. Knowing Dickens actually survived a similar childhood makes me feel incredibly sorry for him but it explains why his writing feels so heartbreakingly real.
