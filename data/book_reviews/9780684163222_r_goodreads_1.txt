“I am in a small cabin outside Fairbanks, Alaska, as I write these words. The cold sits down like iron here, and the long hours of winter darkness cause us to leave a light on most of the day. Outside, at thirty below, wood for the stove literally pops apart at the touch of the ax. I can see out across the short timber of the taiga when I am out there in the gray daylight.”

I read Of Wolves and Men the same year I read Lopez's Arctic Dreams; the latter was a gift from my mentor and teacher, a scholar of literacy who was also a lover of the environment. I understood in the process of reading these books that it is indeed a deeply complex thing to understand wolves, or young learners, as if they were One Thing. How we can know a thing—any thing—is complex and difficult work.

Lopez wrote this classic text to share with us his basic view that:

“We do not know very much at all about animals. We cannot understand them except in terms of our own needs and experiences.”

We are ethnocentric, which is to say we view other ethnicities in the world in limiting ways, chiefly in terms of how we see things, and we are of course also human-centric:

“We assume that the animal is entirely comprehensible and, as Henry Beston has said, has taken form on a plane beneath the one we occupy. It seems to me that this is a sure way to miss the animal and to see, instead, only another reflection of our own ideas.”

We do no really know wolves; we have only imagined what they are really like:

“. . . in the wolf we have not so much an animal that we have always known as one that we have consistently imagined.”

There may be better, more recent books on wolves. I have read several things in the past decades to help me understand wolves, from the perspectives of those who want to either eliminate or save them in the American West. I side with the savers, based on what I think I know, though I think I understand the herders' perspective, too. But this is a great book, to help us understand wolves, and ways of knowing, with a lot of beautiful writing.
