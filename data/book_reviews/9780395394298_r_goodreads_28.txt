In my review for the Book of Lost Tales pt II, I mentioned my irritation at the constant reuse of the stories of Beren and Lúthien, Túrin, and Tuor in several books with limited variety.I will redact that statement for this book.Normally, it's taken me 1-2 weeks to get through a non-LotR Tolkien book (the Silmarillion, both Books of Lost Tales, etc.). This one only took me five days. Maybe it was the verse style that made it quicker to read, but it enraptured me into reading at every point I could.I was rather confused at the verse style of the Lay of the Children of Húrin, and how it worked, but I am also definitely not a poet or learned writer. It still was a wildly wonderful spin on the tale- especially given it never really became more than a revised draft. It's not my favorite tale but the new format made it that much more enjoyable.I don't think I can put into words how much I loved the Lay of Leithian, though.If getting through the Children of Húrin section (157 pages) took me three days, the 252 pages of Leithian took me two. That's an incredibly significant amount of pages per day read between the two main parts of the book. That's how much I wanted to devour it.The imagery of the poem is incredible. You can visualize perfectly the setting of Doriath, or of Morgoth's hall. The beauty of the lines comes through in feeling the characters' emotions as well. Gorlim's desperate attempts to see his wife, Beren's rage and helplessness against his father's death and his oath for a Silmaril, Lúthien's determination to help Beren (one of my favorite parts of this tale is her enchantment of her hair and weaving a cloak of shadow, to defy her father), Huan's absolute loyalty to Beren and Lúthien despite being a servant to one of the sons of Fëanor that is restricting their quest. Every bit of it came through crystal clear. It's just a shame that the poem was never finished.I don't read much poetry, but I can guess that this is going to be one of my favorites for a long time.Huan, my canine king, you deserve endless happiness in Valinoron the cover of my copy it was Morgoth battling Fingolfin, but I kept forgetting which "Fin-" it was.
