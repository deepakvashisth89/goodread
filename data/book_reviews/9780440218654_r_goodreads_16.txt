Overall, a quite satisfying and stirring reading experience. I found the following quote which describes the protagonist getting into her mother's bed the morning after her mother has had a male visitor quite exquisite in it's ability to stir the senses: "The bed smelled different than usual it didn't smell of clean sheets and mother and her soap but a bit like the river in a season of drought a bit like the dead fishes washed up on the sand and like water with a lot of salt in it for cooking."Then, the following quote fascinated me greatly: “She would sleep now & in the morning she would kill him.” What kind of a person can fall asleep with the knowledge that they are going to kill someone upon waking? Why not do it first, then, sleep? I might just lay awake pondering this question. Ruth Rendell has this innate ability to truly get under my skin!
