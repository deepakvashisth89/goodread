Having dived back to this early book in the series, it’s pretty clear to me that Crais was still in the process of fleshing out his main characters - Elvis Cole and Joe Pike. Cole is quick with the lip and handy with his fists (as he continues to be through numerous future episodes) and Pike is a silent but threatening presence lurking in his shadows. So in broad terms things remain the same. But only as we learn more and more about these men will they begin to feel more real, more believable. This is the natural order of things, I accept that, but I can’t help feeling that the later books are so much more rewarding.

The story here - Cole being hired to find the long estranged ex-wife of a rich, self obsessed filmmaker - is a bit thin and certainly excessively convoluted. I lost interested towards the end, as my believably radar was tweaked in the wrong direction just too many times. It’s not the best book in the series by a long chalk, but then Crais sets such a high bar with subsequent episodes.
