Lauren Allen seems to be caught in the middle of things--literally! Lauren, 14, is the middle of three daughters, trying to navigate in the everyday world. Her older sister Melissa is 19 and commutes from home to a nearby college. Lauren shares her room with her younger sister Linda, who's ten, a slob and dreams of doing stand-up comedy when she grows up. In the meantime, Linda tends to drive the family crazy with her jokes.Lauren is a ninth grader and in her final semester in junior high school. Besides dealing with her family (and the frequent conflicts with her father) and the never-ending chores at home, her ex-boyfriend Bobby Taylor has dumped her for popular cheerleader Sandy Linwood. (Bobby and Sandy are at the high school.) Lauren has now sworn off dating and men, though her best friend Bonnie Alda wants to fix her up again.On the plus side, graduation is around the corner. Lauren is excited to be one of thirty students picked for a new elective titled "Law for Children and Young People". The class is being taught by Mr. Matthews, who teaches social-studies at the school. At night, he attends law school and is close to graduation and preparing to take the bar exam. Lauren also hopes to become a lawyer and help children down the road.The new semester begins. While Lauren looks forward to the law elective during last period, she dreads her first class. She and Bonnie are in Ms. Solomon's Biology class. Their teacher tends to put Lauren--and sometimes Bonnie--in detention for the most minor infractions. Lauren hates the class as well as Frogface Solomon for "screwing up (her grade-point) average."Lauren makes it to the law class that first day. She enjoys it and is one of Mr. Matthews's top students. In class, she sits next to Zack Davids, an eighth grader who moved with his mother from California to New Jersey at the end of the previous semester. Zack's mother and Bonnie's mother, who are both divorced, grew up together and have rekindled their friendship from their school days. Bonnie, who dates an eleventh grader named Dave, doesn't want much to do with Zack though they're friendly towards one another.Zack has a crush on Lauren, and he asks her to work on their law project. He chooses it on child abuse and is relieved when Lauren decides to work with him. Though she'd dated older guys in the past, Lauren is impressed with how Zack is so mature for his age. She's hesitant at first but decides to eventually go out with him.In the meantime, things tend to get worse for Lauren. Her older sister Melissa moves out of the house to live with her long-time boyfriend Mike over at the college. And word gets around the school about Lauren and Zack going together. She gets called a "cradle robber" due to being a year older than Zack. He ends up getting into a fistfight with the school bully Ed Harmon during their law class. (Both boys wind up suspended and get detention though, due to being provoked and it's his first offense, Zack's punishment is less than Ed's.)Lauren and Bonnie also have a falling out about Zack. Neither girl speaks to one another though during an argument in Biology class, Solomon separates the two to work with different lab partners plus another day in detention. Lauren continues to be ridiculed by stuck-up girls like Mindy Donner, who leads a clique of mean girls at the school. Zack tries to take it all with a grain of salt and, despite the school's "cliques and unfair rules", doesn't seem fazed by a lot.Yet Lauren and Bonnie soon make amends. Lauren soon realizes that not everyone is against her dating Zack. Her sisters, Bonnie and her (now) lab partner Erik Marks all think that she's lucky to have someone like Zack who's "nice and attractive and bright and funny." Lauren also learns to stand up for herself and to protest certain school policies without getting upset or mad. She also writes an unsigned column in the law class's newsletter about unfair detentions, in which Frogface Solomon vows to find the culprit.Lauren also thinks about writing an article about suing her parents for malpractice. But maybe that wouldn't be such a wise thing to do. Lauren's mother says that her father would "countersue" her if it went to court. While some issues are resolved in this book, Paula Danziger has the readers guessing if the others will eventually reach a solution. It would've been nice if Danziger wrote a sequel to this book before she passed away in 2004. Now it's anybody's guess.It's a great book to read and nothing like some of her previous novels (i.e. "The Cat Ate My Gymsuit", "The Pistachio Prescription") which sound like a teen-aged soap opera!!!Rating for "Can You Sue Your Parents For Malpractice?": ****
