The fascinating true story of a woman and her daughter who walked by themselves from Spokane, WA to New York City in 1896 in hopes of winning a cash prize and saving their farm from foreclosure. The author does an excellent job in chronicling their odyssey and describing for modern readers the intense social, ethnic and familial forces that coalesced and lead to the silencing of Helga Estby's story within her family for several generations. As I read about the Estby family's financial struggles and the heart-wrenching details of the horrible economic situation the country was going through I could not help but reflect that there's nothing new under the sun! In that sense it was an inspirational story to me because Helga definitely took matters into her own hands and went against the ethnic, societal and religious norms of Victorian times in order to do so. For this daring, she was severely punished by her family --some of whom went so far as try and erase her story after she died.Book reads like a well-written term paper. I enjoyed it very much.
