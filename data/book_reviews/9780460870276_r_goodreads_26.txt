Many of Chaucer’s tales are hilarious like this. In a prologue to one tale, a woman goes to her fourth husband’s funeral, sees a sexy young man there who is 20 years her junior, checks out his hot body “behind the coffin,” and then marries him within a month. This lady shares her views on marriage and men quite frankly.“A knowing wife if she is worth her salt || Can always prove her husband is at fault.”“What means of paying her can he invent || Unless he uses his silly instrument? || It follows they were fashioned at creation || Both to purge urine and for propagation.”Almost all of Chaucer’s tales are entertaining. In one, the crow tragically loses its song voice and color, forever turning black, to teach the importance of holding one’s tongue. In another, a magician removes rocks from a coast to help a man bed another man’s wife. One tale threatens execution to a man unless he can answer the question of what women most desire. And there’s a tale with some literal ass kissing.“Dark was the night as pitch, as black as coal, || And at the window out she put her hole.”A number of the tales are less entertaining than philosophical, discussing, for instance, the prophetic value of dreams, the danger of flattery, the nature of marriage, or the fickleness of fortune. Several tales pull back the curtain on moral authorities.“‘But let me briefly make my purpose plain; || I preach for nothing but for greed of gain … And thus I preach against the very vice || I make my living out of – avarice.”.I do thinkThe Canterbury Talesare slightly overrated. Overall, they are good, but, despite the genius of Chaucer’s verse, they are not of the same caliber as the stories inThe Decameron. Additionally, many of Chaucer’s tales were clearly taken from (or at least inspired by) others, likeGiovanni Boccaccio.
