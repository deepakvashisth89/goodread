In most Asian families, you grow up with many expectations. You're expected to do good in school, graduate, and have a decent job. This is exactly how it goes for Dan Inagaki. His parents are always comparing him to his older brother, Brad. The straight A, goody-two-shoes, most-like-to-go-to-college, 4.0 GPA, older brother.In Beacon Hill Boys, 16 year-old Dan Inagaki suffers from having to exceed what people want him to be. His parents want him to be like his brother. His friends want him to play around and smoke. Aside from all of this, Dan deals with crushes in high school and wanting to know more about Japanese history.I, personally, did not enjoy the book. But I didn't hate it. It takes while to get to any intense scene and for me, has too many characters to remember. But if you like to read about kids who deal with their race and ethnicity, and having to stand up for what they believe in, I suggest you read Beacon Hill Boys.
