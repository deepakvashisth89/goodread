3.5 stars.

Apparently, Sen did not spend much time talking to "normal" people. Because even though it is argued that the book is written to be accessible to a wider audience, I have read literal economics textbooks that are more comprehensible.
I only managed to understand like two thirds of it. But it is nevertheless a pretty interesting book with important findings and a good addition to "Why Nations Fail" from Robinson and Acemoglu. It's also a dense book; contrary to Robinson and Acemoglu, Sen does not waste much time with examples or clarifications. This, paired with his eloquence, makes "Development as Freedom" challenging to read. So I would recommend this book only under the premise that you have a genuine interest in the topic and some quiet hours to work through this book, because this is not your average free time-easy to digest-reading material.
Below is an actual excerpt from the book, which is the first paragraph of a subsection of chapter 1:

"Individual functionings can lend themselves to easier interpersonal comparison than comparisons of utilities (or happiness, pleasures or desires). Also, many of the relevant functionings - typically the mental characteristics - can be seen distinctly from their mental assessment (not subsumed in "mental adjustment"). The variability in the conversion of means into ends (or into freedom to pursue ends) is already reflected in the extends of those achievements and freedoms that may figure in the list of ends."
