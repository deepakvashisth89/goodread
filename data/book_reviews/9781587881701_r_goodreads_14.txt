I have so much to thank William Gay for - we all do. After reading I Hate to See That Evening Sun Go Down: Collected Stories
it occurred to me that I was onto something special. Very special. Written with languid charm and darkly gothic, Provinces of Night was one of my first ventures into Southern literature. And what an introduction! Set in 1952 and starting rather bleakly with the discovery of a mason jar filled with tiny human bones, the novel then introduces us to the Bloodworth family. 17yr old Fleming is an aspiring writer, his father (Boyd) is angry, jilted and soon to depart to Detroit with murder in mind, while his uncle Brad uses witchcraft to hex people and Uncle Warren is a charming, handsome alcoholic womaniser. I nearly forgot to mention the leader of the gang, Fleming’s grandfather - E.F Bloodworth, a blues musician, con artist and hell raiser. He is hilarious. There are strong women, mad women, bootleggers, drunks, angry hornets, wise fools and the constant threat of backwood violence. Gay captures the time and place with absolute perfection.
Using Tennessee as a background, he paints characters that are so real and vivid you can almost smell them. The dialogue, along with the descriptions of the countryside are what make Gay’s writing so special. He was clearly a huge fan of both William Faulkner and Cormac McCarthy (the title of the book is taken from ). But William Gay was very much his own man. He didn’t get published until late in his life and he died not so long after. And that's a real shame because he had so much to offer. Provinces of Night is the book I always buy for my friends in the UK when they ask me what Southern literature is all about. They are never disappointed. If you’ve not read William Gay, you should, and is the perfect place to start.
