The book starts off so well that I thought it was going to read a lot more like Sebastian Junger's, "A Perfect Storm". However, although it was interesting and informative, it does not contain a great deal of information about the men who served aboard the ship, which might have provided more human interest for the story.The author does a good job of describing the circumstances surrounding the disaster and its aftermath, including a lengthy discussion of the inquiry that followed. Although we will never know exactly what occurred aboard the USS Thresher that day, various scenarios are explored and a probable cause is established. The Thresher's story is also placed in the context of other submarine mishaps that occurred before and after it's sinking.The events are told in a thorough and systematic manner, but not in one that truly engages the reader and creates such an air of suspense that one cannot wait to find out what happens on the next page.
