No. 8 in Sara Paretsky’s V.I. Warshawaski series and it is almost twice the length of the first one. I tend to think a crime novel should be 250 pages maximum – after that I start losing interest. But I’ve been reading the Warshawski books in sections lasting 30 minutes as I pedal on my exercise bike and Warshawski works well on a bike. Tunnel Vision romps through the narrative, although there were times when a character would be mentioned and I would have to flick back through the pages to have my memory jogged. The book had a number of recaps, but these were well integrated into the overall narrative: Warshawski would think through where she was in the investigation or give someone some explanations – they steadied things so we could look around and see where we were. Of course, Tunnel Vision delivers exactly what we expect and want. It depends on our liking Warshawski – if you don’t warm to the character, you won’t like the book. She has the usual sort of adventures…including the ritualistic beating by thugs. She has the same bunch of friends who do what they always do. In the previous book, however, she started a relationship with a cop and during Tunnel Vision there are tensions due to Warshawski’s investigation and the police wanting her to keep out of it…so there is the question of whether their relationship will last the strain. (Although, by now, I don’t know why the cops dismiss Warshawski’s hunches and demand she keeps out of the case – they should know by now that Warshawski is always right.) Bad things are done by bad people with power – although, in this one, some of the bad people are so powerful that they don’t get their just deserts when everything is solved and sorted. Warshawski is almost forty and physically feeling the strain…maybe she will soon retire. There are occasional questions about Warshawski’s methods: she breaks the law, carrying out burglaries to find evidence – this is questioned, is she an irresponsible vigilante? But then the narrative powers on and any qualms are left behind. I enjoy the Warshawski books, but the more I read and the more they dish up exactly the same thing and the more I realise they are designed for comfort, the more I realise I’m very kind in my judgements.
