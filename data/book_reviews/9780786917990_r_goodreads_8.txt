This book is ok, and that's the best I can say about it. It's not bad, but it never excited me that much.To be clear, Elaine Cunningham did a great work in the fist book of the series,Elfshadow, and a miserable one with the second,Elfsong. Probably the absence of Arylin in the second book was one of the reason why it turned out so badly.This book is chronologically set at the same time as the previous book, featuring events experienced by Arilyn. This is made clear toward the end, when Arilyn and Danilo are reunited and it turns out that Danilo had some significant adventure, which clearly corresponds to the events told in the second book.This book recovers significantly in terms of quality, featuring a more compelling story, with several connecting stories by characters that are quite good, and a plot that is rather rich without being repetitive or excessive.On the other hand, what I especially didn't like was the long series of overly-descriptive combat scenes, with each blade stroke and parry laid out in detail. It doesn't go to the point of being as noisome asR.A. Salvatore, but still, at some points it made me shut off my mind against the continuous and redundant stream of fighting moves.Also, as it often happens with books published by TSR, there are many errors in the text, due to a poor editing. Although, I must admit, I've seen worse from the same publisher.
