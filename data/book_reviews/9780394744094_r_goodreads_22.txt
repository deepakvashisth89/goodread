After reading Russo's North Bath trilogy, I decided to binge his work from the beginning.

If you’ve ever dreamed of writing a book but fear you don’t have what it takes and will never be published, take heart and READ THIS BOOK!

Soap operas are called that because early daytime radio and television advertisers’ primary audience were housewives—who else to pitch soaps, detergents, and cleaning products to? And, bless their hearts, the mundanity of daily existence in service of people who couldn’t be bothered deserved a little operatic melodrama.

But this was more like the husbands of said housewives being assigned people-watching court-level stenography at a detergent conference where the keynote speaker sounds like Steven Wright pitching, well, detergent, but is decidedly Steven Wright, and not only is it the only way to keep their jobs, but the payout is the last bottle of scotch on earth and lifetime tickets to every Super Bowl game with first affair dibs on their favorite cheerleader for finding the most interesting character in the crowd to report on accurately in minute detail.

The joke being the conference never ends and the speaker never stops his monotone drone about detergent and no one even a mohawk—god forbid anyone be interesting. They’re all a bunch of identical schmucks (Russo’s seemingly signature species) in identical suits staring glassy-eyed into the nothingness of their lives who never speak and never move a muscle.

Congratulations, you’ve read .

Everyone has to start somewhere, and successfully made it through the entire publishing process.

Now go write and pitch that book!
