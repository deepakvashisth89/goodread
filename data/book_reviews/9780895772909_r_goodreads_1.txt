What a cornucopia of nostalgia! If one remembers that farming was one of the main occupations of the people of this country right up to WWII then one will understand the significance of this book to US history. The cover does suggest an earlier time and some of the contents is. However the color photos of families farming (In 1988) suggest that not much has changed on family farms. Along with the farms, country roads, covered bridges, small towns, county fairs, and roadside stands figure prominently in the first part of the book.As some of these families have been on their farms all their lives, they have rich stories of an earlier time which they shared with the author. Some still practice arts that others today can only learn in a college class, such as pottery making.There is a selection of recipes too that I could only dream about or find at a county fair.The stories included describe country living that remind me of 'The Waltons' TV show.Believe it or not, there is even a selection titled country songs. Two in particular stand out for me. One is 'Amazing Grace' and the other is 'You Are My Sunshine,' which my father-in-law used to sing to my wife when she was a little girl sitting on his knee.As I sat eating tomatoes from my garden while reading about the farm family meals of homegrown and butchered food, I felt a teeny bit of kindred spirit with them, and I think, in that hollow space in my head, that homegrown tastes better!
