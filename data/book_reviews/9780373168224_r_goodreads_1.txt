ok so i have a lot of beef with this book. it’s not at all what the back advertised it as… the plot was way too convoluted & it took so long to get anywhere remotely interesting. i got a lot of jerry maguire vibes from the main dude in this in the worst way (it really doesn’t seem like he’s that into Zoë at all, just that he really loves her daughter and wants to be that father figure for her.) also, the line “my body is set on the full experience of your hunka hunka burnin love” is so f*cking awful it genuinely sours like the whole book for me. there’s a whooole lot of frustrating toxic masculinity & enforcing of gender roles in this to unpack, but that was more or less expected for a book made in 2000. i was hoping to get away from the classic harlequin “romantic cowboy” trope and lean more into the “marriage of convenience” trope i much prefer, but this one but it still came dangerously close to cowboy territory. not what i had hoped for.another small note, Brad is a very unsexy name to me. didn’t love that.pluses: it’s a cutesy little story, easy to digest and quick to read. i like Lark’s character a lot, and i liked the long-time friend/emotional supporter-turned-love interest storyline.
