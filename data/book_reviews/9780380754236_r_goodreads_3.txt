I first read this book somewhere around 9 years old. I loved monsters, sure, but I also enjoyed the message of looking below the surface finding friendship in unlikely places. Unfortunately, I never tracked down any more of the series until I was too old to enjoy them, which was a real shame.Running on nostalgia, I bought a copy to read to my son, and I later read it to my daughter. It held up fairly well, and I further appreciated the themes of embracing differences and overcoming disabilities. It didn't resonate as strongly with my kids as it did with me, but we had a good time.Rather than diving into this series, I've gotten a few of Mel Gilden's books for an adult audience. Hopefully they contain some of that same magic that called to me as a kid.
