I’ve really enjoyed previous Angus Wilson books, Hemlock & After, and the story collection The Wrong Set. But this one was a slog. The setup of a wealthy barrister’s wife and charity busybody feels a perfect occasion for British satire, a la Pym, Brookner, or Wilson etc. But the windup was a bit tedious. The party scene early on felt unnecessary. Maybe the central character wasn’t strongly drawn enough—too prim, too repressed, too clueless—for the satire to work?DNF around 100 pp. Planning to read more Wilson in future though.
