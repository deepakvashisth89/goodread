Cultivating respect and their gardens go hand in hand, literally, as a spiritual family starts a movement of back to nature life in a barren expanse of sand and stone. Working the land, listening to it’s needs via the devas and spirits that also inhabit the area, this small troupe of 6 start a revolution of organic gardening, stoked in spirituality and hard labor. What started as necessity due to loss of employment became a trial and error success story of what can be accomplished when one simply listens and learns. The Findhorn garden is an international gem, heralded by countless professionals in horticultural fields for its unbelievable results from the most basic of care. If I ever get to Scotland, this is on my must see list.Told in chapters by its founding members and their main contribution to its success. Q&A follows it up with tips on soil improvements and such.But really, it’s about how we can all accomplish so much with so little if we only respect the needs and forgo the desires.Black and white images are offered, but not the main draw of this book.
