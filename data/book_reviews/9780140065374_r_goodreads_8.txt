A modern audience may find elements of this book politically incorrect but if you are willing to forget that was originally wrote for 1970s it's very enjoyable and definitely written with the author tongue firmly in his cheek. Set in Australia, a visiting aged Oxford Don; Professor Belville-Smith is on a lecture tour of university's up and down the country. By the time he reaches Drummondale University boredom has long since set. So it comes as a surprise that he is found dead with his throat cut. Needless to say the local police in the form of Inspector Royle, who's only interest is servicing bored house wives. Completely out of his depth is ropes in a local English graduate Bill Bascomb (believed to author Robert Barnard) to help him solve the case. Barnard is given free reign to poke fun in university politics, police and even Australia itself which makes it a great read for this Pom.
