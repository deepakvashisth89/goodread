Once again, Dave Brandstetter investigates a suspicious death for his father's insurance company. If the co-owner of a gay bar died by his own hand or if the beneficiary murdered him, there can be no payout.The police are happy to arrest the young man found holding the gun, but as usual when Brandstetter gets involved, we find that nothing is ever that cut and dried.So far I have enjoyed all the novels in this series, rereleased from the 1970s, when they were a little ahead of their audience. Hansen is great with California description, interior decoration, clothes, curious characters, and a way of life I don't know much about. I love how he paints a scene: "Backgrounding him, the Pacific wrote white scribbles to itself on a blue slate under a wide smile of sky. The surf lipped pale sand beyond a stagger of red dune fences."This particular episode is dizzying because there are so many characters with agendas that might lead to murder or accidental homicide. Hansen kept me (and his detective) guessing right up to a shooting at the raucous Mr Marvelous beefcake contest. The ending was exciting.I was glad that Brandstetter's relationship with Doug is no longer so fraught, and I look forward to seeing if the investigator ever goes independent. His father has warned him that in the case of his death, the company board would surely fire the openly gay son.
