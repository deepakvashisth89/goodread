Patricia Moyes is quickly becoming one of my favorite mystery authors. I especially love the new covers by Felony & Mayhem and that they are reprinting some of these Golden Age gems. I've read several of the novels featuring Chief Inspector Henry Tibbett and his wife Emmy and their (healthy!) relationship is so refreshing. This installment turns out to be equal parts mystery and farce, with an eccentric cast of characters in the Manciple family. Head of the family (but not to be confused with The Head) Major George returns from a fulfilling afternoon on his one-of-a-kind shooting range to discover that his irksome, nouveau riche neighbor Raymond Mason has been shot dead on his driveway. As much as he detested the man, Major George considers it his moral responsibility to get to the bottom of his death. In part to clear his own name, but still.When he reports the incident to the local Chief Constable (Proper Channels!) Sir John Adamson, well, Sir John is about as befuddled as any poor innocent swept into a conversation with a Manciple family member at large. Major George tends to put the cart before the horse and then leave the horse in the dust.Eventually they get a man from Scotland Yard. "Tibbett, Tibbett, hang him from a gibbet" as Aunt Dora intones to set in place her rhyming device to remember names dubs him. (And he has trouble getting it out of his head for the rest of the investigation.) But make no mistake, Aunt Dora is as sharp as a tack despite being 93 years young. She wields her hearing aid with an almost sinister precision, turning up the whistle to inflict maximum damage on those still blessed with acute hearing and in nearby range. Despite her grim mnemonic nickname for Tibbett, he finds himself impressed and even endeared to Aunt Dora. So it's all the more upsetting when she dies a few days after Mason.Mason's death was a blessing in no disguise, as he was almost unequivocally loathed across town. A rich bookie trying to play the role of the country lord, his attempts to ingratiate himself with the local landed gentry had the reverse affect and most recoiled at his presence. At best he was tolerated. So when he tried to make an offer on the Manciple family home, Cregwell Grange, he should not have been surprised to be turned down. Instead, Mason took the rejection as a personal offence and the purchase of Cregwell Grange became an obsession for him. He started a campaign to make Major George's life miserable in as many ways as possible to force him out. When that didn't show any signs of working, he turned to attempts to woo their youngest, Maud. What he didn't know was that she was already engaged, or just about. Maud's a brilliant physicist, about to start her career with Uncle Claude at his research facility. Her fiancé Julian Manning-Richards hopes to get a job as Uncle Claude's right hand man as well. Rounding out the family is Uncle Edwin,a crossword junkie and a recently retired bishop for Bogalaland. (I confess to Googling it. My geography is not what it should be and I thought this might be a 1950s term for a part of the world that has now fallen out of fashion or a country annexed and renamed in one of the many global conflicts. Nope. Completely fictional. Moyes just has a brilliant sense of humor as it's clearly borderline believable!) There's also Major George's wife, Violet, doing the work of five and always half apologizing for it, though otherwise an unimpeachable hostess. Tibbett is completely enamored of them all.Outside the family there's Mason's somewhat estranged son Frank, a hot head, a redhead, a socialist, and quite brilliant as much as he tries to mask it. And Mr. Mumford, who manages the bookie operations day-to-day and reveres his employer Raymond while despising the way son Frank treated Raymond. There's also the town doctor, Mr. Thompson, no nonsense and no patience, though his wife went to school with Emmy and as they reconnect Isobel Thompson provides lots of local gossip to inadvertently help Henry Tibbett along with his investigation. (A cast of characters at the beginning of the book WOULD have been helpful!)The lunches, teas, and various encounters with the Manciple family were the highlights of this book, all culminating in a chase scene in the midst of the annual Fete that's worthy of a movie in and of itself. The only question that remains is: WHY hasn't BBC / Britbox / Acorn turned this into a series yet?!
