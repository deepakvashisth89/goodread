The Outermost House chronicles Henry Beston's year of living in his beach hut on the beach of Cape Cod. Each chapter is about a different natural phenomenon or time of the year at the beach. It was written in 1928 and is considered a classic in the genre of nature writing. Being a great lover of the big open water The Outermost House, of course, got me very curious the moment I first heard about it.I can't say I was disappointed by it but it wasn't quite what I expected. The prose was not as poetic as I hoped it would be. Funnily I didn't feel I was carried there, to the great beach almost at all. At times it felt like a logbook kept out of boredom. But there were chapters that were very good too. I especially liked The Headlong Wave which described the waves of Cape Cod - surely the most lulling part of the ocean. I think this book works best for those who have actually been there - on the Cape Cod beach. One of the reasons why Beston's writing maybe fell short for me was that English isn't my native language and despite having a fairly good command of it one thing that always poses difficulties for me is grasping the spatial descriptions of nature. The terminology is not something I am always fully familiar with and the images fail to form in front of my eyes. Perhaps I would have had a different reading experience if I had read it in my native language.
