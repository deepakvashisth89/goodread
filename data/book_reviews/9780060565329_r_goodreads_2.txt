I found this account interesting and pretty even-handed, but I have trouble with the author's insistence that Diana was "the most intelligent" of the Mitford women. I find that hard to believe, even if one accepts the premise that her loathsome political (and personal) views were more or less completely due to her blind loyalty/"love" for Oswald Mosley (who seems a dreadful hate-mongerer as well as a horrible husband and father). I also cannot find that either Diana or Mosley did one single thing for the public good, ever. Further, had she not happened to have looks that pleased, I doubt she would have ever had much personal appeal, not nearly so much as Nancy or Jessica, who had more in the way of true intelligence, vivacity, and wit--and were certainly far more accomplished, and, unlike Diana, made some positive impact on the world and its people.

Really it becomes more and more clear what a total twit Diana was.
