(If it matters to you, it's true that the ebook version of Book One had many formatting errors, but this book has almost none.)This is one of the most emotionally draining fiction books I've ever read—angst upon dread that occurs over and over. But I kept pushing forward because I had to know how it would end, and it was worth it. It's a gut-wrenching saga, and while I was enthralled with it, I was also needing to finish it because it was so intense.I'm told that this was originally intended to be a four book series. If that's the case then it makes sense that Book One (Kilgannon) was such a cake walk compared to this book. Book One told the tale of a charming courtship then some dramatic moments in Alex and Mary's early marriage. We got to know this couple well and understand their cultures, and it was a great set up to Book Two. The beginning of this book is a brief overview of what happened in Book One so it can be read as a standalone but I wouldn't recommend it. You will know and appreciate these characters so much more if you read Book One first.In this Book Two (The Wild Rose Of Kilgannon) all hell breaks loose. I can't give specifics because I wouldn't want anyone to not experience this with virgin eyes. Suffice it to say there is war, betrayal, political corruption, brutality and revenge, intermixed with love, loyalty, honor, pride and bravery. It's an edge-of-your-seat nail biter from beginning to end with multiple points of dread and adventure.God bless Mary Rose. She's hopelessly devoted to an original adrenaline junky. Their further adventures in hypothetical Books Three and Four would have been spectacular. But I can be satisfied with this ending. It was pretty great actually.These are the first two books ever written by this author and she has only grown in her later books. (On a Highland Shore is one not to be missed.) Her writing style is beautifully descriptive, her research is strong, and her passion for Scotland and its history is evident. Unfortunately Ms. Givens passed away in 2010 so we will need to cherish what we now have of her works.
