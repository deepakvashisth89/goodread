Wasserman's book is really a collection of linked, very short essays on life in her small town on the "wrong side" of Buzzard's Bay. As a local I recognize some of what she's talking about, but since I fit her description of "the people who moved here by mistake" to a certain degree, I don't get quite all of it. There's some wonderful essays and some that land flat, and her parodies of upper middle class intelligentsia and outsiders grow tired at some point, especially as I have to wonder how she manages to so completely escape the stereotypes she throws out herself, being an NPR commentator and a talented writer, aspects that must set her apart at times from the working class people that populate her essays and which she seeks so strongly to identify with. I don't know Wasserman or her story well enough to know, but at times it feels like the lady protest too much. These concerns aside, the book if full of interesting sketches of her and others life in her community, and there are touching moments when she talks of her longing for her deceased husband.
