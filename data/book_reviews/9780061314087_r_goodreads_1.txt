I intend to find more books from the series of which this one is part, owing to the broad perspective that it takes. Though it restricts itself to the European, Mediterranean, and Atlantic theatres, the book gives very full coverage of Hitler's half of the war, in three senses. Chronologically, it goes from September 1939 to May 1945 and beyond. Geographically, it reaches from Finland to Libya and from Great Britain to the Urals (I think Iceland is not mentioned). Thematically, it covers, though with little detail, military events, science and technology, propaganda, economics, culture, and perhaps more. My one regret is that these accounts are mostly separate from each other, though there is an overarching claim that Hitler was too optimistic about winning the war in a few months.If you want to know everything about the War, this is a good place to start from.For background, I grew up in the shadow of the War -- my parents had lived through it and many of the books I read when I was young had German villains -- and have read many books and watched many films and documentaries about it, ranging fromHMS Ulyssesto Winston Churchill's twelve-volume account. This book stands unique for its breadth and the way in which it provides context for the others.
