“Then the stars went out, for the bark of Ra, in fiery splendor, burst out of the East. Sunshine flooded the wide desert and the long, green valley of the Nile. The night was over; a new day had dawned for the land of Egypt.”
― Eloise Jarvis McGraw, Mara, Daughter of the Nile

Five stars, five stars, five stars, five stars.

What a wonderful, colorful, gorgeously written book this was!

People who follow my reviews probably know I am obsessed with Egypt. I am obsessed with ancient Egypt and ancient Greece and Rome.

Egypt and Greece and Rome - metaphors unto themselves.

These places are a historian’s dreams in all their blindingly beautiful glory..

They are bright , colorful fountains, pouring forth, fascinating stories. This book was one of them.

I used to dream about living in Egypt. Sometimes I still do.

I’ve had many fantasies about it. In these fantasies I live in a blue and white open air villa. My family is with me. My friends are with me. My pets are with me.

There is color everywhere just like there is in this book.

This book is about Mara.

She is a sassy, spunky, plucky, lovable young Egyptian girl, who is, unfortunately ,
also a slave.

When she gets sold to a new master, she inadvertently walks right into trouble as she becomes a spy, spying for the Egyptian queen Hatshepsut.

It is a scary and duplicitous lifestyle. But that is the price she must pay to walk around freely.

And then there is Thutmose, brother — and enemy of Hatshepsut.

And then, of course, there is Sheftu. Sheftu! A complex and mercurial man, a battle of wits is soon to happen.


What happens to Mara When she finds herself playing the role of double spy and having to play both sides against each other?

This book is filled with spies and boats, and the sun and the moon, and the stars and friendship, and palaces and duplicity and betrayal and romantic love, and above all with Egypt, a magnificent Seastar, who enters the book as her own character


Also, there was color. This author was an artist before becoming a writer, and you can see it in every page as the book drips in gorgeous glorious colors. Everywhere , colors like flocks of bright, colorful, tropical birds, on every page


I was deeply touched, reading the note at the beginning, the introduction from the author’s children. And they said they hope people enjoy the book. Well, I know I did. I’ve read so many books on ancient Egypt and this is at the top of the list, the crème de la crème. I’ve now got to find other books by this author how is it? I’ve never read this before?

The only negative about the book was that it ended. There are some books that bind themselves inside one, and I know that I will never forget this story.
