I love the first 11 books in Cornwell's Scarpetta series, gave them all four or five stars, so I was semi-primed to enjoy this book (semi because while I have a lot of nostalgia-love for 90s Scarpetta, I was also aware of the low average rating for this book), but after 70 pages I've determined that the average rating (3.31 when I wrote this) gives an accurate representation of the quality of the book. I don't understand why there was so much focus on police officers writing a manual for some new computer software that no one seemed to be able to understand. It was very boring to read about and its inclusion in the plot seemed pointless (or possibly a case of the author padding the word count due to lack of actual plot) and this was my main reason for giving up on the book. My mum bought this a couple of decades ago when it was first published, before I really even knew the Cornwell name. She never got around to reading it, but I had always planned to, the time just never seemed right. Then I was doing a major unhaul so I could fit in some new books I'd 'acquired' and, knowing the pretty low average rating I decided that this was a good candidate for the donate box. Then, a few days later, while I was continuing to unhaul I had second thoughts and I got it back out of the donate box so I could give it a go, just to check that I truly didn't like it (it's really hard for me to get rid of a book I've never read). I've read the first four chapters (70 pages, as I said) and it's definitely a no from me. So, it's good riddance to a bad book and I'm glad I never made the effort to buy the first book in the series (I always try to read a series in order), what an annoying waste of money that would have been.Show more
