"Can you see anything, Howard?"
"Yes," he replied... Wonderful things."

Wonderful plastic things! In this archaeological satire, Macaulay has dug up quite a few pointed barbs on archaeologists, ancient historians, and society at large. It was written back in '79 but still rings true today, an almost classic. I found it in a large illustrated paperback format at the public library. While it is a quick read, the ideas remain firmly entrenched in the fertile soil of the imagination, making the reader wonder what future generations will make of USA.

In the book, a future archaeologist discovers a twentieth century American hotel as if it were a lost ancient culture; since he is exploring from the perspective of the year 4022, almost another two thousand years in the future. It takes a riff on important figures of the past, such as Howard Carter and Harry Burton, who together discovered King Tutankhamun's tomb and photographed the discoveries. There are also some pointed asides about "Heinrich von Hooligan," referring to the historical figure of Heinrich Schliemann, the discoverer of ancient Troy.

The characters in the story think the motel is an ancient complex of sacred tombs. The uncanny readings of the intent of the then unknown artifacts is quite humorous. Brand names are misread as specific historical figures or explorer names. The television is "the Great Altar." The artwork is well-done and augments the storyline. It is a good comedic reminder of the absurdities of imposing interpretations on archaeological sites without ample proof. It reminds us how readily we assign religious interpretations on the unknown past. And it does make me wonder are we elevating all the wrong 'things' in our society.
