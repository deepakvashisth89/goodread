Seems I was missing him more than I realized.Re-reading this one, I was noticing how his plot construction seems seamless and effortless, combining somewhat disparate elements in order to effectively confound (while entertaining) the reader.Agatha Christie does something similar in a few of her novels that I prefer over some of her more 'mainstream' ones. And I've noticed Tim Sullivan do the same in two of his DS George Cross novels. Sometimes the result can feel needlessly complicated... but, then, that's how life in general can also feel.Unlike Christie and Sullivan, Chandler's cynicism lends the added effectual atmosphere of noir, peopled almost entirely with types we're not meant to like. A lot of light and glitter, a lot of scenery, a lot of clothes, a lot of sound, an all-star cast, and a plot with all the originality and drive of a split fingernail.") or a floozy ("From thirty feet away she looked like a lot of class. From ten feet away she looked like something made up to be seen from thirty feet away.")Marlowe gets to be romantic in this one - well, vaguely, still in Marlowe style. It's cute.Chandler made me laugh a lot here ...as I sidestepped the bodies. This is one crackerjack read.
