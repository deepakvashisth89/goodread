Description (from Goodreads):

Written in 1929 at the height of the Harlem Renaissance by one of the movement's most important and prolific authors, Plum Bun is the story of Angela Murray, a young black girl who discovers she can pass for white. After the death of her parents, Angela moves to New York to escape the racism she believes is her only obstacle to opportunity. What she soon discovers is that being a woman has its own burdens that don't fade with the color of one's skin, and that love and marriage might not offer her salvation.


When I read "Harlem Rhapsody" by Victoria Christopher Murray last year, I berated myself that I had never heard of Jessie Redmon Fauset, a doyenne of the Harlem Renaissance, and I pledged that I would read one of her books. Well...let's just say that I am no longer shocked that her name has fallen out of common knowledge. "Plum Bun" was...odd: a rather stolid mixture of philosophy and fiction, and I found Fauset's writing to be awkward sometimes to the point of almost being unreadable. (Sidenote: the Mint Editions copy published in 2024 that I got from our library was filled with some truly egregious typos, like sentences that were completely out of order. I had to download a digital copy to read instead. But even once the major typos were gone, I still found the prose to be clunky and tedious.)

So you may be wondering why I gave the book 4 stars, which is a decent rating for me. In spite of disliking Fauset's writing and finding the plot to be dull and predictable, I still thought "Plum Bun" was an intriguing case study of a time period. Angela's decision to pass and her ongoing debates about its morality were interesting. There were a few moments of real human emotion that moved me. And I ultimately think I will find the characters memorable, even if they were not particularly well-developed. So it worked a weird kind of magic on me, in which I was both annoyed by it and compelled by it at the same time.

But man, that end was cheesy. 😆 And just too neat for my tastes - like with a literal bow on it?
For a novel of that same time period on passing, I would recommend Nella Larsen's "Passing" over "Plum Bun." Interestingly, Fauset and Larsen were friends and encouraged each other's writing.
