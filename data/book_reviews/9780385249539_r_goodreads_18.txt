I read this after seeing the film for the first time recently. I haven't been as affected by a film, perhaps...ever. So I HAD to read the book to get some more background on the characters and story.

I am still processing the book (and the film) and probably will be for quite some time. I did find the film to be darker, more harrowing, and much more believable (though, admittedly, I have not lived through anything similar, nor do I know anyone who has).

It is shocking to know how prevalent stories of this type are in our world, right under our noses...

The thing I am having the hardest time with is the character of Jesse in the book. In the film you come to completely understand and empathize with her plight in the end, as well as her motivations. In the book she just doesn't seem believable. It was quite disappointing, and really tainted the entire read for me. The ending is also extremely problematic, obviously. I understand the point the author is making, but it just feels...wrong...even if there is some grounding in reality.

While it is very well written and a page-turner, I don't think I can recommend this title.

And honestly, there is nothing you can do to prepare yourself to watch the film, either. It will ruin you...but that is kind of the point, given Tim Roth's personal investment in the material.
