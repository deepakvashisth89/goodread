I hate to bust on a book that had such groundbreaking importance in city planning, but that was over 50 years ago and this book hasn’t aged well at all. The author relies way too much on anecdotal stories of her fictional Mr. Rogers Neighborhood, and not nearly enough on facts. Instead of this poorly written personal memoir, I would suggest you read Howard Kunstler’sThe Geography of Nowhere. I did, and it has made all of the difference.Or instead of readingThe Geography of Nowhere, you could just come to Spain and visit my neighborhood and check out the gorgeous city of Valencia. You will soon learn everything you need to know about how to construct a vibrant, modern, and livable city.You begin by making apartment buildings from 5 to 11 floors, depending on the surrounding area. You put this around a block with the back of the units facing into an open area away from the street (this is called apatio de manzanain Spanish). This will give your city the population density required to make the neighborhood lively.Next, require the ground floor of these building be set aside for commercial use. On my block I have a supermarket, a pharmacy, a bakery with a sidewalk terrace, an internet café, a car repair garage, a yoga studio, a tobacconist, a couple of small shoe and clothing stores, a computer shop, a jewelry store, an optometrist, a shoe repair shop, a beauty parlor, and home appliance store. I may have missed one or two places. I can get to all of these without crossing a street. If I cross the street to the next block, I have an equal amount of small businesses to choose from.Next, make sure you have adequate bike infrastructure and mass transit to lure people out of their cars. Throw in some dazzling parks (Turia Park is the best city park I have ever seen, and we also have an amazing beach) and you are well on your way to have a city worth living in.
