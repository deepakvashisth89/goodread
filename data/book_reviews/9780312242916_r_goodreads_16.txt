An intriguing beginning to Pelecanos's DC Quartet. The first quarter or third of the book sets up the nearly inevitable conclusion. I just love the sense ofplaceandtimethe author gives to Washington, DC, and its people. He nearly always avoids any of the national politics in the books I've read. Here he focuses on Pete Karras, a WWII veteran who had a bit of a tough life growing up and then finds himself with his life altered. The characters are fantastic, though it's still a "noir" novel and all that entails. The main flaw is really that the women hardly get any kind of spotlight (though Vera was one of the more intriguing ones).However, I'm really starting to realize that I need to read the original Nick Stefanos books, there are clearly too many easter eggs in here, so I'll read those first before I continue on with the DC Quartet.
