4.5 starsThe Silver Crown by Robert C. O'Brien is one of my favorite childhood books. I remember reading it on more than one occasion growing up, and I think it spurred my early love for novels about secret societies. The story follows a girl named Ellen. When she wakes up on the morning of her 10th birthday, she finds a silver crown by her bed. Assuming it is a gift, she places it on her head and goes for a walk outside since the rest of her family is still sleeping. Ellen returns home from her walk to discover that her house burned down and her entire family was killed in the fire. The Silver Crown follows Ellen as she attempts to find her Aunt Sarah in the wake of this tragedy.I was happy to discover that I still really enjoyed The Silver Crown as an adult. While reading, it is important to keep in mind that this is a children's novel written in 1968. Some parts of the book may not translate as well in the 21st Century because of new technology and new ways of life, and other parts are clearly written for a young audience. For example, while Ellen faces a lot of challenges in the book, things sometimes work out a bit too conveniently for her. This would annoy me in an adult novel, but it makes sense in a children's book. So long as you have this framework in mind, the book becomes more enjoyable for adult readers.In the same way, I had a lot of fun discovering what parts of The Silver Crown I forgot, and even parts I remembered incorrectly! My favorite aspect of this book is the amazing story. The urban fantasy aspect is a lot of fun, and I think it's amazing that a 10-year-old girl is our brave protagonist. The writing flows well, and I don't find the ending predictable at all. The Silver Crown is a great mix of adventure and seriousness.The only part of the book I find I bit weak is the last chapter. This part includes a big information dump all from one character. That being said, my edition of the book also includes an alternate ending that was published in the British edition. I absolutely love this alternate ending, so I decided to take a half-star off my total rating for the original last chapter I did not like.All in all, I'm so glad I reread The Silver Crown. It is still one of my favorite books, and I highly recommend picking it up no matter your age. The sense of adventure is a ton of fun, and there is something for everyone in the story.
