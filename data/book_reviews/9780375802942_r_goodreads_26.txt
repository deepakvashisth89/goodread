“You’re not a real lunch maker Jones,” she said. “They’re just letting you play pretend. Don’t you know that?” All of a sudden, I felt steamy mad inside. ‘Cause I am sick and tired of that dumb girl!”

This book tries to teach the responsibilities of a having a job and taking care of the people you serve. It also demonstrates that disregarding someone’s feelings can cause an outburst. This story is about a first grade girl who is fascinated by her new lunchbox and the fact that she brings home lunch and everyone else buys school lunch. She becomes jealous and decides to try and be the “boss of lunch” by becoming the lunch lady. In the end there were a couple of mistakes, but the experience was exciting. I rated this book a 4 out of 5 stars mainly because of how she thought her lunch beat out everyone else’s and I liked how there were different parts of the story that connected in the end. More importantly, I loved the little kid humor it had, as it made me feel like I was back in elementary school, and I was appealed by how the protagonist doesn’t appear in every scene me of the story but plays the biggest part. On the opposite hand, what I didn’t like was that it didn’t show what Junie B. learned from the mistakes she made. It just ended on a balky no with no reflection.

Overall this is still one of my favorite Barbara Park books asked on the fact that it’s relatable, goofy, and a fun time.
