"And I hope we shall prevail. More than that: I'm certain we shall prevail. Because reason must prevail."D-503 "We"This pioneer dystopian book caught my attention only recently after watching TV Show “Brave New World” and revisiting Huxley’s book right after.Being an older work, released in 1921, Zamyatin’s “We” deserves a very special attention. Of course, Orwell’s 1984 was also inspired by the Soviet book. So, it certainly makes it an essential read for every Sci-fi afficionado.The ideas are rich, brave, out of the ordinary and full of hidden dreed. The very story line is a bit clunky though, but that is the result of a peculiar writing style Zamyatin utilized. Biblical tones are also in abundance. The whole Adam and Eve template is very nuanced, yet apparent.What really fascinates me is the fact that Zamyatin was actually writing about the post October Revolutionary USSR-Russia, even though the story line is set in distant future. It took some courage to even dare to get the book published, and he experienced real terror trying to get it published abroad.The concept of the totalitarian "One State" based on mathematics and logics is in the core of the story. Our protagonist-narrator is keeping a diary and reflects on his day to day routines working as a rocket scientist and generally enjoying the current state of affairs. His name is D-503, as all the citizens have designated coded names only and he is initially engaged in a love triangle with R-13 (a man) and O-90 (a woman). Confusing enough?No! Let's continue then...There enters the mysterious lady I-330 who shatters D's complete outlook on life. She is free in spirit, engages in various "sins" and tempts our reluctant narrator D-503.Of course, much happens as a result of their relationship and D-503 learns about things he never thought were possible. He even learns that there is an underground movement which is trying to change things in "One State" run by the ruler called the Benefactor.I will not spoil any more of the book, so you can enjoy it and learn what happens till the end of this highly original novel...But, is this book an easy, smooth read?It wasn’t for me at all! And I couldn’t figure out why at first.First, I thought it was a translation issue, but very quickly I realized that the translation was impeccable. So, it must be something stylistic that bothered me, the narration itself. We are constantly in D-503's head and he is not really the most entertaining of narrators. For a relatively short book (just a bit more than 200 pages) it took me a whole week to finish.And I really wanted to know how the story of our reluctant protagonist ends, what were the repercussions of his choices. I managed to cross the finish line and was satisfied with the ending which shines with some glimmers of hope (unlike the USSR at the time of publication).Humanity’s triumph and hunger for freedom cannot be subdued and eliminated by reducing everyone to “human tractors”. Benefactor may entrap and enslave the majority, but cannot keep it going forever.It took almost 70 years for the USSR to collapse - "One World" in "We" could only have a shorter life span having in mind all the cracks on the walls that surrounds it.Do read this book!If you don’t enjoy it that much as a work of art you are certain to appreciate its importance, influence and creativity.
