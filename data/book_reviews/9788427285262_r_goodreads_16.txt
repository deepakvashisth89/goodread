You, Hercule Poirot—the world-renowned Belgian detective—have been invited to a dinner at Mr Shaitana’s Park Lane flat, an eccentric millionaire with a deranged fascination with killers on the loose.

At the gathering, you meet Ariadne Oliver, the famous best-selling crime novelist. Colonel Race and Superintendent Battle are also there. Four more guests arrive, and after making their acquaintance over dinner and wine, Shaitana makes a curious remark.







Trying to ease the tension, someone makes a casual comment about how late it is, and you keep talking as usual, wondering why Shaitana might have brought up such an unpleasant subject. He then invites you to play bridge at two tables while he rests in a chair by the fireplace. When you finish playing, you approach him to say your goodbyes, only to find him stabbed in the chest.

Now it’s up to you, Ariadne, Race, and Battle to solve the crime, and to determine whether one of the guests has committed a crime before, as Shaitana suspected. As always, you’ll focus your attention on the character of the killer. It must have been someone bold, astute, willing to take a risk. They had only so much time between dinner and Shaitana’s death, all while playing bridge to make sure nobody noticed. Now time’s running out to catch the killer. Four sleuths, four alleged murderers. Almost like a game.

Only this time, the game is deadly.
