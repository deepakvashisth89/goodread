Wow! This was a very real and incredibly distressing story. I've never been very familiar with the genocide in Guatemala, and I don't think I'm the only American who can say that. That's not surprising since the Genocide in Guatemala was caused by soldiers who were outfitted with weaponry, uniforms and military training by the United States Government. It is and should be considered a great shame of our nation, one of many if I'm going to be very blunt.Gabriela, a happy girl with a wonderful family and friends, has her life ripped apart when the soldiers begin their bloody rampage across the country, decimating villages, raping and torturing citizens, and leaving no one alive in their wake. Gabriela watches everyone she loves systematically slaughtered and somehow survives to make her way alone to safety. You think that things must get better with every page in this book, as they can't seem to get much worse. But they do get worse. If ever I were to say a book was almost TOO MUCH, this one would be it. However, I respect it for its unapologetic honesty and authentic portrayal of this type of tragedy, of which there are too many to count. I don't think I'll be forgetting about this book any time soon.
