This classic work by William Manchester presents a balanced biography of Douglas MacArthur. It covers both his private/family life and his vast military career spanning two world wars and the Korean conflict. Many biographers come under the spell of their subject but not Manchester. He gives us both the positive and negative characteristics of MacArthur. MacArthur certainly was a prima donna, egoist, rude, blunt, insensitive but he also was a military genius, a leader both on the battlefield and off. He knew the Asian people/culture when the US needed someone there after WWII. MacArthur reorganized the Philippines, wrote the Japanese constitution, and more importantly dealt with both peoples in a respectful way. However, he let his ego cause trouble in the political world where he distrusted the media and disrespected President Truman. He was insubordinate toward the President, the US military, and the United Nations. Unlike WWI and WWII, the Korean conflict was a “limited” war where military minded MacArthur didn’t fit in and being stubborn to the end, led to his downfall. (MacArthur sounds a lot like a current president!) A good read.
