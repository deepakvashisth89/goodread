I'm half-done with "The Path to Power," and while I would love to finish this approximately 800-page volume--and read the three other volumes of The Years of Lyndon Johnson series that are already published, and hope Robert Caro will finish the fifth-- Well, you get the idea: There are scores of other books in the world that I want to read, too.I'm not sure I can add anything to what's already been said of Caro's monumental and unique work.(I previously read his book "The Power Broker" as well.)--The depth of research is simply awesome--and as a longtime journalist, I really really appreciate the effort that must have gone into the anecdotes, quotes, numbers, and details. Years of tracking down hard-to-find records and patiently re-interviewing (and coddling) reluctant sources.--The writing is so wonderfully rare for nonfiction: rich, lively, engrossing.And of course, LBJ! I will never think of this complicated man the same way I used to.But OMG is there no editor on this planet who will say to Caro: "Bob, you've got to learn to edit."The books are so long because there is TOO MUCH REPETITION. Four examples when 2 would be sharp enough. Four brilliant quotes all saying the same thing. An opening sentence and closing sentence of many paragraphs that repeat each other -- and multiple paragraphs that make the same point.Sure, I need to learn to skim-- but all the anecdotes are so fascinating and the writing so easy-to-read that I just keep going.So, I put this book aside for a while in order to read a few other novels. I will resume.
