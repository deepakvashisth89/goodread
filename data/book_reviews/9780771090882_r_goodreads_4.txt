Basically, think the Las Vegas Golden Knights first season, if they were Winnipeg high schoolers of yore. Charming, positive depiction of band of misfits from all different ethnicities and classes bonding together to form a hockey team and defy expectations. Simply written, but does successfully get me hella invested in the final will-they-won't-they scene.This book was originally written in the Fifties and it SHOWS. French-Canadian and Eastern European aren't races by modern standards, but they are referred to as such a couple of times. The aesthetics are rather '50s and the way gear and injuries are talked about... it's old. But. This book was also republished in the Eighties and that shows too, in mentions of the Solidarity movement in Poland, Pete reading about his current fave, Trottier of the New York Islanders, and discussion of such Eastern European names as Gretzky and Stastny tearing it up in the NHL.Like, I cannot mentally reconcile these two things, and the text doesn't seem to bother trying. The Eighties references are very shoehorned in, but on some parts they feel rather informative to Bill Spunska's character. And there's a minor character called Ron MacLean and there just wouldn't be in the '80s, not when actual Ron MacLean was on his way to becoming a HNIC icon. (I found this delightful review on the NHL website that identifies what filled these spots before which... does somewhat alleviate my confusion on how this didn't totally change the plot. But doesn't change the time brain confusion.https://www.nhl.com/news/scrubs-on-sk...)(Also, because it is relevant to hockey discourse 2019, there is mention of "ragging" the puck up ice at one point. So I guess that's a real thing.)
