I read this assigned novel at West Point, and the year before that at The Citadel. Both schools wanted to emphasize how terrible war was in the literature assigned to us and in required films, such as military footage of the Battle of Monte Casino or the taking of Japanese occupied islands in the Pacific (especially horrific). I re-read The "Red Badge of Courage" again in less turbulent circumstances in 1972 in Kandyla, Arcadia, Greece, which had been the scene of fierce fighting between Turks & Greeks in their War of Independence, Italians & Greeks in the Second World War, Germans & Greeks in the same war, Greeks and Greeks in their civil war immediately after they had finished with both the Italians and the Germans and in ancient times enemies of Sparta, of which Kandyla was an ally, and just about everyone else. Crane's descriptions in this masterpiece truly shows you the visceral horror of war, this time the American Civil War. The outstanding feature of war is Fear. Fear kills the spirit of Man as surely as does the blade, the bullet or the blast.
