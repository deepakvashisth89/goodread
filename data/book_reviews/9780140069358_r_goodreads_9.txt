A fun, well written murder mystery with excellent attention to detail and surprising emotional depth. It will transport you to 80s Florence by infusing the story in small realistic details: the clothing, the weather, the routines, the traffic patterns, the neighborhoods, the tourist spots and grimy dives, other aspects daily life in the city. The characters largely feel like real individuals, each with their own habits, history and speaking patterns.Pacing was on point — the fractured nature of goings on and realistic timeline of getting information helped build suspense and urgency. It is nice to read a story where the main character is not a high powered genius with near supernatural detecting abilities; this protagonist is a policeman slightly out of his depth, doing his best to puzzle through things and find answers despite his limited power. The culprit was slightly predictable but the details much murkier until the very end.As in the other Marshall Guarnacca book I read, there were some duplicitous female characters who fake their emotions to generate sympathy, gain attention or pressure the police to do something. (These are the only characters who seem straight out of a box to me.) However this is partially deconstructed, especially when it comes to the treatment of older women.
