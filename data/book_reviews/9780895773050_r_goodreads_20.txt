I thought this was a great book. It has a noble and intelligent male title character who falls for an independent schoolteacher that wants very much to be self sufficient without needing or wanting to get married. It's a wonderful love story that also includes humor, suspense, and a view of western life in the 1880's as told by the narrator (also known as the "tenderfoot".)Like the author of "A Town Like Alice", I applaud Owen Wister for creating Molly Wood's character to be similar in spirit, sense of adventure, and intelligence much like Mr. Shute's main character Jean Paget was. I also truly enjoyed the maturation of the "tenderfoot" throughout the book. He goes from a geographically challenged and naive Easterner to a man who develops a better instinct of his surroundings and tracking skills in the west. (This is mostly due to the Virginian's guidance.)This book has been the basis of several western movies - none of which I have seen. Yet. (My personal rule has always been to read the book first.) The most recognizable adaptation is probably the successful television series made in the 1960's. If the reader has seen the first three seasons of the television series, then they will discover the only commonalities to the book are a few names (Trampas, Steve, Judge Henry, Molly, the Virginian) and the town of Medicine Bow in the Wyoming territory. Oh, and that the Virginian is a handsome good guy with black hair in both the book and the series.While I read this book, I kept an open mind and allowed myself to envision the characters as they were described both physically and personally by the narrator. For example, Trampas is not a fair haired man that looks and acts like the late actor Doug McClure did for eight seasons of The Virginian. And while it's easy to see why the late Mr. James Drury was cast as the title character in 1962, it was not his face, voice, and demeanor I saw while reading Mr. Wister's book. The book's true character of the Virginian was a soft spoken man that rarely raised his voice, was quite humble, courageous, loyal, and not vengeful in any way.Overall, a book very worthy of five stars and a place on my list of well loved stories.Quick update.
