This is a sweet story of a young, blue-collar heroine taming a commitment-phobe, white-collar hero. She is a pixie-sized landscape designer who is very good at her job and proud of it, with a wonderful family to back her up, loving, supportive parents and a big brother who has a mental disability due to a bout of childhood meningitis. The hero is a famous, handsome, wealthy architect who hires her to landscape for his newly designed home. He is immediately smitten with her but just for a bit of a good time. This bloke ain't the marrying kind. Heroine has very firm family values and romantic aspirations to marry and found a family like her parents. But her volcanic attraction to the hero puts her on shaky grounds.Hero invites heroine one night to the home of his snooty, snobby, condescending rich friends. The heroine ends up playing a game of pool on the host's brand new, state of the art, very expensive pool table, to the horror of hero, who thinks she is going to make a fool of herself. But the heroine grew up with a pool champion dad and it is the rich, hoity-toity friends who are made the fool. The pool playing scene was one of the best scenes I have ever read in a Harlequin book because it is just so cleverly used as metaphor for heroine’s class and gender struggles against these awful snobs and the hero who underestimates her. The great tell-off she gave the smug, arrogant hero afterwards was EPIC.This was truly one of the best pieces of writing from this husband and wife writing team. There was even a very pleasant cameo from the couple ofA World Apart. I wish the hero managed to tell the OW to fuck off after she called the heroine’s mentally disabled brother a half-wit, alas he tells the heroine that SHE is overreacting and the OW’s passed-on apologies should suffice. No bueno :( Read it for the great heroine and a satisfying grovel from the hero at the end.
