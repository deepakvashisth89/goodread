This book was written in the 70s so there’s still gonna be some bias and some racism in it. And the violence was crazy. But I do recall reading this as a child and finding the adventure chilling but also amazing. Having always wanted to be royalty myself I would’ve loved to of woken up to a crown on my pillow on my birthday. Of course I would’ve preferred not to have been chased by the evil villain with the black crown whose sole purpose was to take over the whole world. But the adventure with Ellen and Otto made the story worth re-reading. And I will say that I prefer the alternate ending at the end of the book.Spoilers below....-I’m surprised that for a re-printed book there was no rewrite of the reference to a Chinaman which is literally three paragraphs and so unnecessary that I found it unsatisfactory that they couldn’t fix this. My edition is from 2001.-The violence in this book starts off right away with Ellen‘s house being set on fire and she thinks her whole family has died in the fire. Then she witnesses the murder of a store manager and finds out later that Officer Drogue, who tried to help her was also murdered by the villain’s minions. And then you have to consider Otto who’s been hiding the “danger” sign on a road to cause truck accidents. Yes he’s a child and yes he’s got a few issues dealing with abandonment but this was a weird plot point. Yes again this book was written in the 70s but it is so violent that at the school Ellen finds the boys are making Molotov cocktails, setting fires to buildings, practicing beating police officers by beating fake dummies, and instigating race riots, looting and violence in the cities. Seriously this does not sound like a children’s book.I still grow up with this book. And so for nostalgia I gave it four stars, but does it hold up to today’s standards? No I don’t think so. It is not a book I would recommend to a child but I would likely enjoy discussing it with other adults who have read it.
