4.5 stars but putting it at 5 because for its nostalgia.So this is one of those books I read once as a kid and spent the next twenty-odd years of my life thinking I loved it. As such I was a little nervous to reread in adulthood lest the story not hold up like it did when I was little. If anything, rather than holding up, it exceeds expectation.On Ellen’s tenth birthday she wakes up to find a silver fabric crown on her pillow. Ellen, assuming it’s a gift, takes the crown and wanders off to play at holding court before her parents wake up. When she comes home, she finds her house is up in flames and her family have all perished.Ellen’s course of action is to go to stay at her aunt’s house many days travel from her own. On the way she meets an amazing cast of distinct characters, some serious hardship to put a ten year old through and unearths a secret that ties to her crown.All in all I remembered this story as being dark, but I was shocked upon reading again quite how dark it got! I mean, brutal things happen to and in front of this kid and the journey she makes is definitely not an easy one. Since it’s a fairly old book now (1968 I believe? Around the 70s anyway) there’s a few throwaway mentions of things that don’t exactly hold up as... politically correct in the modern age, shall we say? Which I’m not saying is acceptable because it’s old, but also that it’s not entirely surprising because it is, in fact, old. You pretty much expect it to happen in any pre-80s and some post 80s books at some point. At least they’re very far and few between but, y’know, fair warning.Lastly, I believe I read the American edition as a child and the British one this time as my edition now includes both endings. The British one is convoluted to the point that suspension of disbelief only runs so far so even though the American one leaves a few plot threads unanswered I would personally suggest sticking to that one.
