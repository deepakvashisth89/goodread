(2026 Reread)

The first time I read this novel, even being a massive Stephen King fan (and apologist), I found it rambling and couldn’t see what everyone loved about it. I don’t really know what was kicking around in my life at that time, because this re-read didn’t feel that way at all. Yes, on occasion it feels like both King and Straub wanted to say the same thing and instead of editing it down they just both said it, so on occasion the narrative feels drawn out. But those instances are brief, and they always have poignant character moments nestled within them, and they are usually buttressed with emotional action scenes on either side. This is fantasy 101 in some ways, a young boy, special though he doesn’t know it, facing against all difficulties as he goes on the journey into adulthood to try and save the world, or, at least, his world. The foes against him are numerous and faceless, from the senseless and uncompromising misery of cancer and illness to individuals so corrupted by greed that they are willing to use any means and sacrifice anything and everyone to accumulate wealth and power. Yes, the authors play into typical fantasy tropes, but they do it so well, with a type of honest dedication to the inner world of the story and the characters that it is easy to fall into the narrative and be carried away by it.

Both authors are great at creating atmosphere and at having moments of horror and shock, and they sprinkle those through this book, mixed with some great action set pieces at well, that make this fantasy dark, exciting, and fulfilling. Look, it isn’t a perfect novel. It is a little shaggy around the edges, a few scenes run a little longer than they need to, and it gleefully leans into fantasy tropes that may seem a little worn-out 40+ years later. Plus, it has a very magical-negro, as well as some questionable representation of people with physical and mental disabilities. But with all of that said this story is a ton of fun. It is an intimate exploration of a young boy learning how to navigate grief and loss, going through the trials and tribulations that will shape him into a man, and it really nails that emotional arc perfectly.
