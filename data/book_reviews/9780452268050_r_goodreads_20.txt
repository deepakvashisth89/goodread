I hate that after *A Country of Old Men,* Joseph Hansen wrote no more stories about his wealthy gay insurance investigator, Dave Brandstetter. I loved the series for Hansen's complicated plots, strong characters, and especially for his evocation of Southern California from post-WW II and into the 1980s. And for his cinematically detailed settings.

In the course of 12 novels, Brandstetter repeatedly unearths murder where insurance claimants see death from natural causes. And, bit by bit, he ages. In this mystery, his age is showing. He gets winded if he chases a suspect. And after a long, stressful day, he needs a rest. Cyril, his much younger true love, begs him to slow down. But Brandstetter insists he's fine.

Brandstetter is supposedly retired, but then an old friend, Madge, begs his help to deal with a small, raggedy boy she found on the beach near her house. The boy seems to have witnessed a murder and been held for a time by the possible murderer.

Each of the plots and subplots in this episode has a connection with aging or dead friends. There's the restaurant Brandstetter bought after the death of his old pal, Max. There are several people he knew in high school creating a tempest in a teapot, a telephone company exec who was always willing to dig out the phone number of a suspect and that friend's younger, reckless love, a victim of a disease all too familiar. Even some of the police officers are nearing retirement. Hansen wants to be sure we know that time is marching on.

Meanwhile, he keeps us happily guessing who's guilty, as one after another, the suspects get cleared and then not cleared.

And he does as good a job as ever describing everything from luminous natural scenery to small everyday details.

Here's a small example of an office description. "Young women moved busily among ferns and ficus trees, working computers, answering telephones, ripping pages off whining printers, turning green for split seconds in the light of copiers." I wish GoodReads would let me bold that last phrase. Who notices such things and writes them down?

Although most of the puzzles in this book eventually get resolved in satisfying ways, what happens with our hero is left ambiguous. So sad that the book had to end!
