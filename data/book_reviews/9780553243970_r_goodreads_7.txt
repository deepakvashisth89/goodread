"Nobody is ever too old to dream. And dreams never grow old."I see by the reviews that a lot of Anne-fans are disappointed in this one. On this, my fourth reading, I must admit that I was, too. I think it is the lack of the "old familiar." Anne is away, serving as principal of a school in Summerside. Gilbert is far, far away, studying medicine. Other than letters, there is no repartee between the two. Avonlea, and her beloved residents, is only visited during the holidays and summer breaks.There are plenty of new characters, however . . . almost too many. For Anne, there are too many Pringles, the "Royal Family" of Summerside. Resenting the fact that a relative was passed over for the job that went to Anne, the Pringles seem to have organized a cabal against her. She is snubbed socially as rumors about her spread, and avirus of disobediencepervades her classroom. Luckily, Anne happens on a secret that causes the entire family to do an about face.Most of this book concerns the irrepressible Anne solving everyone's problems. And though she manages most of them pretty hilariously, it wears a bit thin by the end of the novel. Anne and Gilbert will be reunited in the next book, so hopefully, the old magic will be back again.
