There are things I loved about this book, and things I hated about this book. I hate when heroes and heroines are in relationships with other people when books begin. And how blind the hero was to his fiancee at the beginning of this book did not bode well. It was taken care of fairly quickly, but left a bad taste in my mouth. The heroine for me was kind of superfluous. There was kind of a journey for her, but not really. In spite of that, though, the chemistry between the hero and heroine was instant, passionate, and fiery, which is what we all come to these books for.

The best character was the hero's grandmother. Normally, family interference is annoying in books, but here it was done well, with the best of intentions. All she did was give the hero and heroine access to one another. Beyond that, it was their choice to act on it or not. And since anyone with half a brain could tell the OW was a nasty piece of work (except the hero, apparently), it made it easy to get on the grandmother's side.

I did enjoy reading this book, so I would recommend, but it's not the best Harlequin/Mills & Boon book I've ever read.
