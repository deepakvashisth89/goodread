3.5 stars!You know the drill by now: if it's a YA book, it's probably for my education class for this semester! This one is not an exception.Whale Talk is about a boy named TJ (or The Tao Jones, officially) who is an outcast -- socially, racially, etc. -- that embraces his differences without a second glance. He is a high school senior who defends kids being bullied by assholes and rejects any position on a sports team (even though he is super athletic) because he hates the superficial power roles placed on the athletics teams. He represents justice, kindness, empathy, and care -- in a very angry, loudly emotional, sarcastic manner that immediately makes the reader feel connected to him. I really liked him as a main character, and I found his relationships with other people to be deep and meaningful. His parents were also beautifully written characters.The plot of the book centers around the formation of a swim team at this high school, made up of the "weirdest" outcasts of the school; they are all fighting to get the letter jackets granted to athletes to stick up against the bullies who thought they were undeserving of them. They have a mini found family trope, as they never shy away from what makes them different. Unfortunately, a lot of the plot DID focus on the athletics drama of obtaining these letter jackets, so that's why I was leaning more towards the three star rating; it was a tad boring at times. The other half of the plot focused on racism, abuse, and protecting children... I wish that this side was a tad more prevalent.Let me tell you, the ending of this book caught me off guard and completely broke my heart. No spoilers, but be prepared for the worst guys. The message behind the title is PHENOMENAL. That was the best part!I would definitely recommend for YA readers in high school, to anybody who has ever felt different, and to those who would do anything to protect the vulnerable. <3Favorite Quotes:"'Honey, you're always wanting to make yourself indispensable. Best way to be healthy in any relationship is to take care of yourself and let the other person do the same."'"He is the most patient man I know; it is a patience born of agony.""Georgia's right about bigotry: that absent the element of hate, a person's skin color is only an indication of his or her geographical ancestry.
