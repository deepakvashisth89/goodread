I can now interpret every dream. (Just kidding.)If you want your dreams to remain magical and mysterious, do not read this book. Now, even while I’m dreaming, I catch myself thinking: “This whole thing is just a system of coherent associations that I shouldn’t take literally. These are encoded representations of my desires.” So when some creature is chasing me in a dream, instead of running, I’m like: “You’re just a puzzle made of my memories, ideas, and suppressed wants. Let me decode you first, and then you can kill me.”Jokes aside, time to get serious. When you read a book, you usually assume the author is at least somewhat intelligent, or at worst, just a regular thinker. Well, Freud isn’t just smart—he’s a genius. Of course, I always knew he was considered the father of modern psychology, but after reading this, I’m convinced he’s one of the greatest minds of all time. (I think I’m in love. :D)The reason for this bold claim is also the book’s biggest flaw—most of Freud’s dream interpretations only work if you’re as intelligent as he was. You need to trace a massive chain of logical connections to decode a dream properly. At some point, I even started wondering if he was just making things up—not in the sense that he didn’t dream these things, but that he might be retroactively constructing events that fit his interpretations. But since he applied the same method to his patients, I doubt that’s the case.That said, he does use some convenient arguments to keep his main idea intact—namely, that dreams always express our desires. For instance, he claims that even a fleeting wish, present for just one second, can make its way into our dreams. Even more amusing is his explanation that if a dream seems completely nonsensical, it might be because our desire was for it to have no meaning (wtf).Despite that, the cases he presents are impressively well-analyzed. His breakdown of recurring dream symbols was also particularly well done—I even tested it myself.In conclusion: This book isn’t that practical, because applying its concepts requires an insane level of intelligence. But even so, it’s absolutely fascinating. 🫶
