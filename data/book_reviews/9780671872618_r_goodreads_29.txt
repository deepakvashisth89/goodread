I went into The Lost Mind by Christopher Pike not totally sure what to expect, but wow; that opening hooked me immediately. A girl wakes up in the woods next to a dead body, there’s blood everywhere, a knife in her hand… and she can’t remember anything. Not even her own name. That’s such a strong, creepy setup and it pulled me in right away.

The mystery side of this book really worked for me. Watching Jennifer try to figure out who she is (calling her own house, reading her diary, trying to piece together her life) was honestly more interesting than the murder itself at times. I liked the contrast between the “new” Jennifer and the girl she used to be. It makes you wonder how much of who we are is memory vs. choice.

I also really loved the stuff with her little brother (Gator!). Those scenes added some heart to a story that could’ve felt cold or overly weird. And yes, Pike does go into some spiritual/philosophical territory here. That can be hit or miss for me with his books, but I actually thought it mostly worked this time. It’s strange, a little confusing in places, but interesting enough that I didn’t mind going along with it.

My main issue is the ending. After such a slow build, everything kind of happens very fast at the climax. I would’ve liked the final confrontation and revelations to breathe a little more. A couple side characters also felt kind of just… there.

Still, I was invested the whole time. It’s not super action-heavy (more of a moody mystery with some metaphysical twists) but I had a hard time putting it down. The ending left me with that slightly unsettled feeling Pike is so good at.

Not perfect, but definitely one of the more memorable Pike books I’ve read. If you like ’90s YA thrillers with a weird edge, this one’s worth it.
