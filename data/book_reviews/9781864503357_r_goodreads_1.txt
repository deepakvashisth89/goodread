The Lonely Planet Trans-Siberian guide provides lots of useful information on the Trans-Siberian journey and is a good way of combining most of the information you need into one book. It would be better, if you could carry several books, one for each part of the journey, but that may be adding unnecessary weight to your pack, even if you discard as you go along.The guide provides important information for a backpacker, namely maps and places to stay (with price ranges). Much of the information about the Trans-Siberian journey is available on the internet, but the guide provides handy, easy-to-carry information in case the internet is not accessible while on your trip. For a trip of this magnitude, the guide should be used in tandem with internet research which will be much more up-to-date.The book's information is most pertinent in the larger cities (Moscow, St. Petersburg, Kazan). Unfortunately, the information is out-of-date for smaller cities (Irkutsk), namely because there has been so much growth that some dinky internet cafes no longer exist. Random FYI, hostels in Russia are never clearly marked...so make sure you get a map of the hostel's location before going.I've found that Lonely Planet guides are geared towards backpackers, which is good for this trek, just a tad out-dated.
