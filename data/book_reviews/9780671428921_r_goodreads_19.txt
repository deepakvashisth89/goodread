Mirror of Danger is a gripping middle-grade book about a young, twice-orphaned girl named Lucy. After the death of her parents, Lucy was raised by her aged aunt, who did not do the young girl any favors. Aunt Olive homeschooled Lucy and isolated her from children her own age, giving her some rather twisted views of the world. So, when Aunt Olive dies and Lucy is sent to live with distant relatives, it’s a massive culture shock. She has no idea how to handle people her own age or how modern people think because she’s been raised like a child born a century prior. Add in a good dose of grief and you’ve got a recipe for a very lonely, lost little girl. A primary target for an evil ghost.

What follows is a story about fitting in, different perspectives on situations, and eerie suspense as Lucy falls ever more into the clutches of the past. It’s perfectly suited to its intended age group, but more sensitive kids could be really upset by this one, so parents beware. I also wasn’t a huge fan of Lucy’s new family in this. They did a lot of things wrong and clearly were not fully prepared to deal with a traumatized child. However, the ending is quite lovely, so it is all worth it in the end. Just know that you’ll probably spend a lot of time being upset by the family’s clear lack of perspective. It’s a casebook study in why you can’t just bring a traumatized child into your home and then treat them like a normal kid. You need to really work to make them feel welcome.

Also, fun side note, this is set at Christmas-time, which I did not know going in. It made my choice to read this on Christmas eve far more fitting than I thought it would be!
