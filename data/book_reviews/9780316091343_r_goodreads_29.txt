This is a mix of textbook and history book and reads as such. I read this along side a difficult, detailed assignment for a summer "class". It is fairly comprehensive with both text and pictures. It is meant to be a companion to the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum in Washington, D.C. and it is heavy, both literally heavy (it's a big book) and the material is heavy. But in a world of Holocaust-deniers heavy is important. I learned a lot about a topic that is all over the History channel and historical fiction shelves. If you think you know, well, you may not know.
I do recommend this book as a reference, but I don't recommend reading it as I did -- all at once over in a short time, alongside the questions from the assignment. That was a lot and I absolutely did break down at different times going through the difficult material. I recommend a slow read as Berenbaum includes minute details that can get lost among the pictures and well-known information. It is the minute details that really shocked me - sometimes they felt like throwaway lines in the middle of the horror, but they were so impactful. There are absolutely portions that are useful for a classroom and if you can't make the trip to the USHMM this is a good resource.
