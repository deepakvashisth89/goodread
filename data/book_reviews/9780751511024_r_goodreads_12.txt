Welcome to the Anarchy
A review of the Mysterious Press/Open Road eBook (August 5, 2014) of the MacMillan hardcover original (June 1, 1979).

This continues my current re-read of the early Brother Cadfael books which are available on Kindle Unlimited, so they have the feel of being "free" 😉.

The setting is the Summer of 1138 and the beginnings of the English civil war known as (1138-1153) with cousins and vying for the throne of England. Stephen has been crowned King and his forces lay siege to Shrewsbury Castle which is aligned with Maud. Maud had the right of succession as decreed by her father but as she herself was not in England, Stephen managed to usurp the throne.

Brother Cadfael and the monks of Shrewsbury Abbey try to remain neutral in the fight but with Stephen's taking of the Castle they must acquiesce to circumstances. Stephen takes revenge on the conquered garrison with a mass hanging, but when Brother Cadfael is assigned burial duties he discovers a murdered man among the corpses. Meanwhile, a local lord Hugh Beringar has arrived to declare his loyalty to King Stephen but also begins to act suspiciously around Cadfael, who is protecting some of Maud's allies in secret.

Cadfael has to solve the murder, outwit Hugh Beringar and save an innocent couple from Stephen's wrath. The monk manages a few tricks up his sleeve in order to do it. It all comes down to a trial by combat at the conclusion and in the end a new alliance is formed.




What is interesting about a re-read in 2025 is the easier access via the internet to historical information about the actual events which Ellis Peters fictionalized for her long-running (1977-1994). I plan to continue the books with (Brother Cadfael #3 - 1980).





The monks and abbey settings of Ellis Peters novels allows me to sneak in some music by my favourite Estonian composer (1935-) whose 90th birthday year is being celebrated throughout 2025 with many concerts and festivals dedicated to his music. One of his most popular works is (Latin: Brothers) (1977) which was originally composed for the Estonian Early Music group (Latin: The Music Garden). It has since been adapted into a few dozen different solo and ensemble versions. You can see a performance of the original version by Hortus Musicus on YouTube . Other famous performances are the solo variations version for violin and piano performed by Gidon Kremer and Keith Jarrett and the violin solo with ensemble version performed by Gidon Kremer .
