There is a special story between me and this book. It was given to me as a very special gift many, many years ago. It's a great selection of stories from the horror genre and I appreciated it verily. I loved it so much that I wanted to share it with the world so I lent it to someone... and never got it back. I searched for it for a decade, on bookstores and online, but could never find it again. Every now and then I still looked it up online and finally my stubbornness paid off: All the way across the globe one edition was found in far away England. My quest has come to an end now, I've finally been reunited with my old friend. What a relief, one less unfinished business to turn me into a ghost when I die.

But enough of me, on to the book. This anthology features big names, such as Poe and Lovecraft, and tries to cover a wide range of horror stories, some being your usual undead creature one but it also includes other more recent subjects such as science experiments gone wrong.

The highlights for me were Gautier's , a tale I had read before and enjoyed immensely, and Lovecraft's , which was probably my first contact with Lovecraft's work and left a huge impression on me back in my younger days. Quiroga was a surprising ingredient on the mix, for it's unusual to see writers from Spanish speaking countries taken into account. Poe and Machen were my least favorites since I'm not very kin on their writing style, nor do I like science being mixed with the supernatural, and lastly it didn't feel like their stories packed a punch or had anything unexpected on them.

I would say some of the stories could be too advanced for young readers today because of the fancy style and old words but it isn't an impossible read and I would highly recommend it to people of all ages. I won’t be lending this one to any one, though, that’s for sure.
