I feel guilty criticizing this book and enjoying it so little, when clearly it was a labor of love for Rebecca Brandewyne, who says as much in the afterword. But there it is. After going out on a limb for her gothic-inflected Chandlers series and her excursion into fantasy for the Chronicles of Tintagel, Brandewyne returned to form with this romance, set in 1870s Wichita, Kansas. I say return to form advisedly, because the novel starts with what was becoming a cliché for Brandewyne after first employing it in "Rose of Rapture": a prologue featuring the married protagonists having passionate, overwritten sex, followed by the conventional story. "Heartland" also revisits a bit of "Outlaw Hearts" territory--not just in its Midwestern setting but in the "dangerous" hero and overlooked heroine. This tale of the romance between valiant homesteader Rachel Wilder and former gunslinger Slade Maverick, has little suspense. It's overlaid with orphans a plenty and annoying stereotypical dialect, from both black and white characters. Very, very slight and seemingly indicative of a writer who, after taking some chances with her previous books, was trying to figure out what her readers and publisher wanted.
