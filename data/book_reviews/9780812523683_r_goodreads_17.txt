BZ Gundhalinu is the youngest of three sons and knows he will never inherit his father’s estate, so he leaves and becomes a Police Inspector. When the stargates fold shut he is separated from his love and travels to the harsh planet Four, where he hopes to find the secret of the Fire Lake at World’s End, and perhaps find a way back to his lover, Moon. His greedy and incompetent brothers HK and SB meanwhile have squandered their inheritances and ended up vanishing near Fire Lake in what BZ (travelling as Gedda) finds an odd piece of serendipity. To get to Fire Lake he signs on as a tech with two very dubious companions, one is an obsessed treasure-seeker and the other is a vicious amoral criminal. The arduous trip reminds one of The Treasure Of The Sierra Madre as the lure of riches induces a form of insanity in his companions. But violence is ever near and Gedda arrives at Fire Lake alone. There he is almost consumed by the power of the lake as it twists space and time almost like a star drive, and he finds a sibyl - the strange and possibly insane Song. Joan D. Vinge’s sequel to her award-winning novel The Snow Queen is not quite the tour-de-force that was, but it is exciting and competent, despite some obscure passages of transcendentalism. Well worth a read.
