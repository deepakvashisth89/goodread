GRIFFIN'S FIRST EXAMINATION OF THE 9/11 "REVISIONIST" POSITIONRetired theologian David Ray Griffin nowadays devotes his time to 9/11. This 2004 book was his first on the subject (see its revision, as well as 'The 9/11 Commission Report: Omissions And Distortions,' etc.). This book is still interesting to contrast with his current position.Although he had not looked at any "revisionist" accounts of 9/11 until the spring of 2003, he was intrigued by books such as The War on Freedom: How and Why America was Attacked, September 11, 2001, 9/11 - the big lie and War and Globalisation: The Truth Behind September 11, and determined to "bring together all of the major strands of this evidence." (Pg. xx) He concludes that "the revisionists have made a strong prima facie case for at least some version of the charge of official complicity." (Pg. xxiii).He argues that "whatever did hit the Pentagon simply did not cause nearly enough destruction for the official story to be true." (Pg. 31) And since officials in October of 2000 carried out an emergency drill to prepare for the possibility that a hijacked airliner might be crashed into the Pentagon, "the claim that the possibility of such attacks had not been envisioned is clearly untrue." (Pg. 69)He points out that although in the summer of 2001 Osama bin Laden was America's "most wanted" criminal, "bin Laden spent two weeks in the American hospital in Dubai (of the United Arab Emirates)... being treated by an American surgeon." (Pg. 76) He cites Bush's former Treasury Secretary, Paul O'Neill, as saying that "the Bush administration had from the outset planned to attack Iraq, in large part for oil." (Pg. 94)Most interesting, however, were some intriguing questions he poses to revisionists, such as, "If officials in the Bush administration wanted a new Pearl Harbor... why would they have concocted such a complex scheme... when virtually the same effects could have been achieved with a much simpler hoax, such as an attack by chemical or biological weapons, which could have been carried out by a very small number of perpetrators?... why would they have risked exposure of the fact that the attack on the WTC was an inside job by having the buildings collapsed by explosives?... And why, in any case, would they have demolished WTC-7, thereby undermining the claim that the Twin Towers collapsed because of the impact of the airliners combined with the heat from the jet-fuel-fed fires?" (Pg. 135) He adds, "what plausible account can be given of the role of (Solicitor General) Ted Olsen? Are we to believe that upon learning that his wife had just been killed in an operation overseen by his superiors, he willingly told a lie to help them out?" (Pg. 137)Although Griffin's later books cover much more ground, this first book is still not without interest.
