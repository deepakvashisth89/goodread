some very good essays and some (admittedly) very boring essays. 'eroticism in women,' 'the new woman,' and 'in favour of the sensitive man' were definitely the highlights. although they seem like relatively maintream discourse today, it's certainly noteworthy that nin was writing these in the '70s.i love her love for DH lawrence - i felt the influence even before she referenced him. also loved it when she was lowkey (andrea) dworkin' it out in 'women and men'.I very much appreciated her reflection on being the muse vs being the artist as well, particularly because these essays are from the final years of her life (at 70 something years old) where she is able to reflect on her own submission and subversion to womanhood across a lifetime."And I know you have followed me in the diary when I wanted to be a muse, and I wanted to be the wife of the artist, but I was really trying to avoid the final issue - that I had to do the job myself." banger.nin's prose is always beautiful and she writes with this shocking lightness no matter the gravity of the content. despite writing with curiosity and fascination at the forefront, i found her travel writings, particularly 'women and children in japan' and 'the spirit of bali' to be incredibly orientalising and condescending. but, once again, she was writing these in the '70s.nevertheless, i am, like nin, very much in favour of the sensitive man.
