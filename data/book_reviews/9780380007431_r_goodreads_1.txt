4.5 starsAs part of my reading cycle, I occasionally work in missed children’s classics. “Gentle Ben” most recently got my interest while reading Ron and Clint Howard’s dual memoir, “The Boys”. Although Ron is the better known of the brothers, Clint has starred in a number of shows, including the 60s TV series based on this book.I was also interested to read it because it is set in Alaska, a state I visited two decades ago. While there, we had a chance to visit a salmon stream where many grizzlies were feeding (including a young one who out of curiosity came practically right up to us…both exciting and scary with mama lurking not far away).But mostly, I’m a sucker for stories where humans and animals bond, so I was destined to be drawn in by this tale of 13-year-old Mark who rescues Ben, a young grizzly, from an evil man who killed the young bear’s mother and has been keeping Ben captive in a shed, chained and underfed.Morey movingly captures the yearnings and joys of a boy’s heart, as well as how easily it can be broken. He also beautifully describes the landscapes. In addition to the heartwarming scenes of the burgeoning boy/bear friendship, there’s plenty of action and drama as the lives of Ben, Mark, and Mark’s family/friends are threatened by the elements, other animals, and, worst of all, other humans.A few nits:–The writing style can feel a bit old-fashioned at times.–There is a smattering of 60s-style sexism where men basically say they need to handle things that women couldn’t possibly do.—One rather unbelievable coincidence happens about three-fourths of the way through the book that sets up the events of the final quarter. Mildly eye-roll inducing, but I found that as part of a kids’ book, I could live it.Always touching, often suspenseful. A joy to read.Save the animals!
