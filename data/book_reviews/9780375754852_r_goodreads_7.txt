This is a massive book about the history of rocketry and mankind's initial forays into space - both manned & unmanned - through about the year 2000. So much to digest here even for someone who is a space/science history buff. Particularly interesting is the detailed information about the Soviet/Russian programs that I had not seen before. Other highlights include the contributions of the German/Nazi scientists to both the Soviet and US programs (including the role those scientists played in the Nazi rocketry program and how their participation in the Nazi pogrom was minimized by them and their handlers), the early history of JPL and the intense infighting among the multiple civilian & military agencies in the USA. In spite of the scope of this book, it is a shame (but not surprising) that the role of women in the US programs was almost completely ignored (see: "Right Stuff, Wrong Sex" by Margaret Meitekamp and "Hidden Figures" by Margot Shatterly). Also missing is much discussion about the early days of space medicine including the contributions NASA of Dr Randy Lovelace and the Lovelace Clinic where the Mercury Seven underwent their medical testing (see eg: "Physics and Medicine of the Upper Atmosphere", White and Benson eds concerning the early history of aviation medicine, and "Space Medicine in Project Mercury" NASA SP-4003). With those few quibbles, highly recommended for anyone with an interest in rocketry development and our initial journey into space..
