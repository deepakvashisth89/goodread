Original and interesting philosophy of modern society increasingly centered around 'spectacle' (think advertising, pop culture, film).

Reminded me of this idea of the looking glass self; we build our selves from what we think others perceive us as. And as identity becomes equated to sets of commodities, we aspire to selves that have rather than selves that are. This idea is so scary because it is so pervasive. In the social media era, it seems that we all aspire toward digital versions of ourselves; to be perceived as having rather than being. Marketing culture heavily relies on this desire to build individual identity through buying.

We all aspire to this 'spectacle' precisely because it can never be fulfilled. It necessarily contains its negation in a Hegelian way.
I wish this theory was more fleshed out. It was still a great read but some of the passages were maybe badly translated and borderline impossible to understand, and the good passages left me wanting more. I think it would be hard to understand without a prior grounding in Marx/Hegel. But I would still really recommend it. It's a short but powerful read.
