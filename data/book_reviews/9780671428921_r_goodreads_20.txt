This is the sort of classic children's semi-horror that I loved as a kid, although I didn't come across this particular book during my childhood. Reading it now as an adult I am struck by how beautifully Sykes weaves together the family story and the ghost/time-travel story, so that Lucy's predicament is spooky and scary while still being intensely realistic. I spent a lot of the book being very frustrated by some of the choices Lucy's aunt and uncle were making -- why don't they talk to her about her life with her Aunt Olive?
