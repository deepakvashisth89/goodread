A collection of letters, mostly from Vietnam vets and their wives, answering a question — did hippies spit on you when you returned home, or is that just a myth? The letters are divided into sections. Those with literal spitting stories take up Part 1. Part 2 is devoted to veterans who were never spit on and, for the most part, deride the vets of Part 1 as whiners/hippies. The third part builds upon Part 2, offering stories of happy returns and grateful civilians, while Part 4 (“Some Things Are Worse Than Being Spit On”) focuses on the figurative spit — cold indifference, discrimination in restaurants and on the job, outright insults or physical attacks, etc. In the final and shortest section we find the miscellenae, most of which read more like essays on the war than straightforward answers.It’s an interesting book, for sure. Before I read it, I asked the Vietnam vets in my life what they thought. One, from a big city in California, had a personal story of being spit on. The other, a farmboy from a rural area, said that while most people in his town opposed the war, they opposed it only because so many Americans were dying. For him, the reception was as warm as it gets. Indifferent, but not hostile.The letters are varied and provide an exceptional little time capsule of veterans’ views. There are letters here from staunch hawks and from anti-Vietnam vets, and one letter from a woman who served in the Peace Corps. What most of the letter-writers share, regardless of whether they were spit on, is a desperate need to clarify that they’re not one of “those” vets. Many of them nervously list their credentials, and by that I don’t mean where they served or which medals they earned — they list how many kids they have and whether those kids are college-bound, how long they’ve been happily married, how much they make at a respectable job and how much their houses cost. Even those who claim they were never made to feel inferior still feel the need to stress that they’re good, they’re stable, they’re not whiny or violent or insane.It’s impossible to tell how many of these letter-writers are telling the truth. Many accuse each other of lying. But the need to separate themselves from a stereotype is telling.
