Third Reading: December 2024WithMartin Eden,London creates a virtual life in print, and with such books the ability exists to see different aspects of that life with every reading. I think that my own life at the time of each reading influences what I find strongest in these virtual characters.This time through, I found Martin Eden's dissolutions with his mentors to be significant. As Martin lifts himself up to achieve level upon level of intellectual mastery, the people that he imagines as being superior are found to be rather ordinary.In my own life, I find that I am constantly perceiving people in the collective as being smarter than they repeatedly show themselves to be. Sure, there are the kooks and groups of kooks among us, but when looking at the larger population, I thought that we would be better than we have shown ourselves to be. Martin's disappointments reflect my own.Overall, the book is still amazing. Life is much more than finding disappointment in people and Martin Eden demonstrates this. Martin's experiences with his first love, his pure enjoyment in creating art, and his views on politics all serve to make this novel engaging. I’m looking forward to my next encounter with Martin Eden, the person and the book, and I have no idea of what will strike me hardest in that subsequent experience.And now a few thoughts about the ending...Martin Eden kills himself at the end, which in my first two readings left me wondering why. This time through, and due to our present political environment here in the United States, I believe I reached a certain sense of clarity regarding the reason for Martin's suicide. Martin invested his entire being into achieving intellectual prowess. However, when he achieves this almost miraculous goal, he discovers that he is practically alone in society. He has essentially condemned himself to solitary confinement and feels the hopelessness of a society that would forever exist in such a hopeless state. He saw that there was nothing to live for and found solace in the only state of existence that would end the hopelessness.In the real world, this experience happens more often now than in previous times. Martin would cringe at the platitude "Make America Great Again," as do I. These are words that move the masses and make those that think cringe. It's unfortunate that the masses are indeed the masses and those that think are inconsequential to the present; but it is within our abilities for it to not be so; at least I hope so. Otherwise, Martin's final act may be the only act that will save the thinker's sanity.---Second Reading: December 2015It may have happened like this. Long ago, the creatures that could accept as fact the suggestions of fear were found to outlive and out-reproduce their more scientific brethren. Hence, humans came into being with the ability to believe as fact things unproven or unexplained. We became spiritual beings. We believe in dreams and we imagine what life would be like, if only if.For any given dream, there is a journey. We set out on a quest towards a vision, but along the way, reality takes its toll. By the time we reach our goal the reality of our dream may in fact be very different from our initial vision. This is whatMarti Edenis about: a dream, a journey, and the end of a quest.Martin Edenmakes a statement about London's own dreams and their ultimate value as he achieved his success. According to London in some of his letters that addressedMartin Eden, the only difference in the final fates of London and Eden, was perspective. London was saved by his trust in others. Through this trust he was still able find value in the life he achieved even though it was very different from the life he envisioned.I have often consideredMartin Edento be one of London's best novels. And the reason for its strength lies in how closely the story parallels London's own life. He wrote what he knew best and because of his truly personal connection to the story, his honesty exceeds his desire to simply lead his readers through a story. The book is filled with emotions and passions that cannot be anything but his own. It is filled with life as he experienced it, and as a consequence, he documents the human conditions that surrounded him nearly a century ago.---First Read: January 2005
