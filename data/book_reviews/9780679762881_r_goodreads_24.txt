I've read more business books lately than I've listed on Goodreads, but I only list books on here if I actually finish them. Most business books are 1-2 good ideas packaged up inside hundreds of pages of pointless fluff (I rarely finish books like that). High Output Management is not one of those books. It seems like this book was an actual passion project for Andrew Grove. It is well-structured and full of good (and diverse!) advice for managers at all levels.

Every chapter gives insight and thought on a specific topic. This is not a book that wastes time repeating itself again and again to drive points home (I'm looking at you, Dale Carnegie!). Instead, it sets up each business problem, and then takes you through addressing it step-by-step. Some of these are not actionable for me (I don't interview potential new staff members, for example), but it's all stuff that likely will be useful so I'm glad to have read it. I also think this is the sort of book which would reward re-reading later.
