Grapefruit:Author: Yoko OnoSome books are meant to be read. Others ask to be experienced.Grapefruit by Yoko Ono isn't a conventional book. It's a collection of conceptual art pieces, poetic instructions, and quiet invitations to engage with the world and with your own imagination in a completely different way."Dawn Piece"Take the first word that comes to your mind and repeat it until dawn."Clock Piece"Listen to the clock strokes.Make exact repetitions in your head.After they stop.As I read, I kept asking myself: What genre does this belong to? Is it poetry? Is it art? Or is it something that exists beyond the need for labels?What fascinated me most was Ono's idea of imaginary art. Art that doesn't necessarily exist in a physical form, but comes alive the moment you imagine it. It was a concept I had never truly encountered before, and it challenged me to rethink not only what art can be, but also how we experience time, space, and the universe itself.Grapefruit reminded me that art doesn't always have to be seen or touched. Sometimes, it only needs to be imagined.And perhaps imagination is one of the purest forms of creation we possess.What do you think? Can an artwork exist only in the mind, or does art need a physical form to truly exist?
