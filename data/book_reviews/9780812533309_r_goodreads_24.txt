A brilliant book. From pure a stylistic standpoint have often praised Glen Cook's books as "lean"; no purple prose, not a word wasted. Here the feeling is that the prose is a tiny bit *too* lean. Sometime a description more, a phrase more, wouldn't hurt. This said, "less" is better than "too much", at least for my tastes.
The characters are great. Round and multi-faceted; even the ones you'd expect to be flat and characterless shows enormous depths, hidden motivations and needs. The story is magnificent, starting as a tragedy, and ending as something *slightly* different - but then, it's quite obvious that the story was bridging off into a sequel that has never been delivered. Maybeit's for the best? We can only speculate, and I would say that the chance of a sequel not being planned, and the book ending on this kind of note, could fit with the style and theme of the story.
Truly, a wonderful book, that showcases that even Cook's early writing were truly marvelous.
