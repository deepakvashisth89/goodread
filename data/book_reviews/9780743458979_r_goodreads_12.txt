DINOSAUR TALES, resembles a mash up of short story collection and graphic novel.Lavish illustrations by many of the pre-eminent science fiction artists of the 80's takecenter stage and do their best to disguise the fact that the balance of the book containsonly two poems and four short stories by Ray Bradbury. $6.95 was a good chunk ofchange in 1983 for what was basically reruns packaged as a "collector's edition" forcomic book and sy fy fan boys. It dawned on me when I got the book from the libraryjust now that the book looked familiar. I owned my own copy in the 1980's.I was that fan boy.......The stories include "Besides a Dinosaur, Whatta Ya Wanna Be When you grow Up ?""A Sound of Thunder", "The Fog Horn" and "Tyrranosaurus Rex." The stories areexcellent examples of Bradbury's poetic prose and among his most popular works.Back when, "A Sound of Thunder" was one of my absolute favorites: a time travelstory about dinosaur hunters. Today I was more aware of the glaring holes in thescience than in the stirring fiction. A new favorite was "Tyrannosaurus Rex" : a sadtale of a shy, talented model maker for stop-motion monster films being used andabused by a Hollywood producer.Times and tastes change as we age. Even old fanboys who wouldn't even looktwice at a $6.95 price tag. Especially if the word DINOSAUR appears prominentlyon a bright book cover.
