Fun first contact/thriller by Gear that would have had more umph in 1990 when it was published. Set in the late 80s, humanity becomes aware of the aliens shortly after all the nukes in the world became inoperable due to some statis field. Just after that, a hologram of the alien Wide appears to both the president of the US and the Soviet premier. What do the aliens want? Wide sorted through all the electronic records of Earth and came up with a list of about 400 people he 'requests' accompany him on his ship. The Earth people? Elite soldiers of the US, USSR and Israel, plus about 100 intelligence agents from the KGB, Mossad, MI6, CIA, etc. What's the plan?The alien Wide, and his pilot/navigator Spotted, run the massive ship alone. They, along with others of their race, came to our galaxy billions of years ago. 'Recently', they 'uplifted' another race, the Pashti, who every 760 years or so undergo a breeding cycle that makes them oblivious to reason; basic biology takes over. During this time, they hoard resources for their offspring. Wide thinks that after each cycle, they control more and more of what his race controlled and he wants a change. So, after studying humanity for eons, hatches a plan. He will commandeer a human strike force to destroy the primary space station of the Pashti and make it look like they simply went insane during their cycle. That will set them back some!So much for the plot. The bulk of the book really rests on the human characters and their struggles with their change in roles. Enemies will have to work together and most of the soldiers also suffer some PTSD. The woman in charge of the strike team knows, however, that even if the strike works, humanity will lose. Wide threated to blow up the Earth if the strike team did not do as he wished and people on the ship know that their lives are forfeit in any case. Can the humans, these old enemies, trust one another to do something about it?Meanwhile, as soon as the ship leaves Earth, the USSR sees a great opportunity! With the West's nukes down, Russia can stamp out the rebelling Soviet states and even 'liberate' all of Europe! What could go wrong? We get updates of WWIII as the novel progresses. This did give the novel a dated feel, but if you lived through the 80s and early 90s, you will recognize the players and the context. All in all, fun, but alas, the ending felt rather rushed and Gear dropped in adeus ex machinaas well. I did like Gear's take on politicians and military brass! All slimy and out for their own power. So, 3.5 aliens, but not enough to round up.Show more
