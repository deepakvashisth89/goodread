In the essay at the end of this book, the author talks about why he felt the need to construct what is a loosely connected anthology rather than an actual novel. He'd hoped to close the door on the Cold War as the basis for his novels yet had a number of themes left unexplored. The result is a so-so compilation kept enticing by the cameo appearance of George Smiley between each chapter. The chapters of note are #8 - a nod to Conrad about a former Catholic priest who after a psychotic break offered his services to the Circus and soon is swallowed up in the jungle; #10 - about a cypher clerk suborned by the KGB out of loneliness; and #7 - a brief, surreal episode in Beirut.Most of the book is overwritten and fraught with sermonizing with a style that is decidedly dated. For the author, what seemed to mark the end of the Cold War was a realization that East and West had almost changed places and those in the intelligence services had perpetrated nearly as much evil as they prevented.
