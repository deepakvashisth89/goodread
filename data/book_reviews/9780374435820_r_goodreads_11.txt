Plenty To See Here, FolksThere's more going on here than meets the eye. Well, actually, meeting the eye is sort of the whole point. There's more going on here than is apparent at first glance. The art is witty and loaded with subtle jokes, and the book ultimately makes some gentle points about art, artists, the art world and the fickle nature of public acclaim.For starters we have a gentle and understated drawing style. This looks to me like pencil, and charcoal, and water color. The effect of that is expressive but muted, and it allows for the detail you need for the jokes without any busyness. So right off the bat it's fun and charming to look at, and very welcoming for a little reader.The first level of jokes swirl around Clousseau's paintings coming to life. Here there is much to engage and enchant a youngster as we look for waterfalls pouring out over a frame, and escaped creatures and other sorts of visual jokes. On the second level there is Clousseau himself. He's always slightly hunched over and looking down, with his hands in his pockets, a constant sort of shrug, and no strong facial expression. His general attitude of benign indifference is amusing and an interesting comment on how an artist might see those who would judge him.The book ends with a delightful absurdist joke, which just put a tin cap on the whole experience for me. This is the kind of book that will delight an adult whether reading with or reading to a little one, and I can see it as a family favorite. I certainly understand why it has been reissued.
