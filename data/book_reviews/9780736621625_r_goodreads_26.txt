The Russia House is set during glasnost in the 1980s, as the Soviet Union slowly begins to open up.In every respect this is a typical le Carré novel. The spies are ordinary people, amateurs even, and the so-called masters of espionage are essentially bureaucrats. Conflicting interests without grand or grotesque intentions. It is about continuity and maintaining a balance of power and influence.Le Carré writes in a compact, businesslike and sometimes understated style. Even the ending, which in a spy novel often serves as the apotheosis or catharsis, remains restrained. He is a master at conveying information and atmosphere in as few sentences as possible. In that sense, le Carré always takes his reader seriously.Although it does not belong to his absolute top tier, it is a very strong novel.Show more
