I liked this book, but it has some exasperating flaws. The story concerns Carthew Fairfax, a society man of the 1920s in London who has lost all his money and is getting desperate. As he struggles to find work, he is offered a strange "job" of going out to dinner at fashionable places; at the same time a close friend is in trouble and needs money desperately; also his antagonistic cousin offers him a lot of money if he will admit to a forgery she committed to get her off the hook; finally, an American cousin comes to visit whom he never knew he had, and she becomes determined to get Car back into his rich uncle's good graces. With a net of deceit and betrayal closing around him, Car needs to figure out who is on his side and who is against him.But Car is kind of stupid. Obvious clues pass him right by. He gets out of sticky situations by luck and coincidence (some of it incredibly & delightfully comical), not by his wits. His love interest seems to be an intelligent woman, but she is given nothing useful to do in the story. The American cousin gets to be more active than the love interest. Really, the villains are the best characters. They are interesting and well drawn, and they have brains and use them, mostly, with the exception of a hole in the plan to trap Car into being sent to prison.Read it for the fun chase scenes that wind up the book. Would make a great comedy-drama movie.
