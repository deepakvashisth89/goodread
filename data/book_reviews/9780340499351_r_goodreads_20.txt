A writer worn down by modern life travels to a village in the Outer Hebrides in Scotland at the invitation of an old friend. He meets people and unexpected events happen which change his life. Written in 1971, it demonstrates the difference in the ways books used to be written, which center around honor and fate, contrasted with how I think the same setup would be written about today. Interestingly, the female MC, in the time of "You've Come a Long Way, Baby," is a talented physician, but in the plot's love triangle, is extremely passive. It was good reading, and I miss the unironic heroism that we don't see much of in today's novels. The author also wrote Shoes of the Fisherman and other best-sellers back in the day.
