My fault with it is that it is a product of its context: 1960s, dry British. And it reads like a novelization of a TV show: mostly dialogue with little distinction in the voices to keep track of who’s talking (see also: dry British), very little actual interiority (again, 1960s, but I’d like to think this is more a fault of the book than my 21st century writing education). I think it had potential to actually be psychological, what-is-reality-even literature, but on the whole I found it pedantic (I couldn’t stop groaning through the Shakespeare section), confusing (and not in a good way), and just lacking on a fundamental craft level, even for its time and place.
