a low 4, but still enjoyable. ambitious, surprising, well-crafted and mysterious, it holds up well as long as you don’t pay too much attention to the main character’s absolute lack of self-awareness about his own flaws and personality in general. all of the other characters besides him are throwaways, and he exists in this Gary-Stu state of being where his only actual revealed self is through the oft-florid (albeit objectively good) narration, yet somehow manages to come across as brilliant and envy-inducing to others without a single line of compelling dialogue. the whole book is just HAPPENING to him, and although there are sparks where you witness him becoming an agent— and the world happening to him is enjoyable enough to read about— it never really feels like anything from a Character perspective.

in that lens, the ending is actually near-perfect, a flattening of many points of significance. if you cared more about the characters, it would be disappointing. but our science gets scienced, our mysteries are solved, and you emerge feeling grateful for the world you actually live in— something which not a lot of harder sci-fi tends to offer.
