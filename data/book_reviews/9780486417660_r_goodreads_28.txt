The Royal Book Of Oz - Ruth Plumly Thompson/L. Frank Baum -As a book that was written in an attempt to replicate another’s work, for the most part it worked.To start with it did feel that some of the structure was clumsy and some of the writing was missing bits or badly phrased. And the language was quite racist by today’s standards.But as the story developed I didn’t even recall that I was reading prose by anyone but L. Frank Baum because Ruth Plumly Thompson did a great job of putting across his final idea from the Land Of Oz (I don’t know of course how much of it he had written before his demise and how much she actually contributed).Early on I liked the ensemble element with everyone getting to play their part without just being thrown in as a ‘They were there too’ thing, although it did end up focussing on just a few characters and a lot of them were new ones.Upset by Professor Wogglebug, the Scarecrow went in search of his family tree and found more than he bargained for on Silver Island. Meanwhile Dorothy and The Cowardly Lion rush off to find him and meet with their own adventures along the way where they also befriend Sir Hokus of Pokes.As with so many of the books that followed ‘The Wizard Of Oz’ I was introduced to many yet unexplored parts of the country, which I must admit made it harder once again to believe that Ozma was such a great ruler if she hadn’t even been to or at least made herself aware of these places. The kingdom was basically in constant chaos because she rarely left her city palace and consequently her friends were all put in danger every time they took a turn off the beaten path, but that’s probably an adult way of looking at a children’s story. There would be no fun to be had if she had been a better more aware ruler.The resolutions to many of the problems the characters faced were better thought out than with previous volumes however and not solely reliant on Ozma or Glinda’s spells, the magic picture or the book of everything. They actually used logic and processed thought to get out of the sticky situations and I felt that had a better example for young readers, whom I’m sure would love it without picking it apart as much as I have. I did genuinely enjoy it, but after all these years our stories now have to be more structured and so much more realistic so sometimes I do find it hard to separate that style and need from these older offerings.It was certainly an action packed tale and the new author really got in to the swing of things as it progressed, even if it did end in the same way as most of the others with a party in The Emerald City.8.40/10.
