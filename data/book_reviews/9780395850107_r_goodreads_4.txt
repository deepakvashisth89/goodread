enjoyed the gay gossip of the suffrage section with some persistent qualms about the thoroughness of the reporting, particularly around race relations-- i think when the tone of a book is so clearly "let's admire these women, who are like us and did great things" it is hard to accept it as accurate reporting of complex social history; i would rather perhaps understand and relate to in an honest way than feel obliged to admire, unknowingly. this felt true even for all the times the book was like "her lover," "her partner" about absolutely tons of women, immediately, when these words evidently seemed to mean something somewhat different to them than they do to us, and i remained curious about those differences because i think they tend to be more illuminating and connecting both within our own times! i don't need to feel that someone is the same as me to feel they are my ancestor.i abandoned hope when we moved into labor & rights advocacy and the book seems to credulously accept the intentions and applied practical allyship of early middle- and upper-class white women creating the profession of social work, so long as they were also gay. it could go on to subvert this; the tone indicates not, and i could not persist.
