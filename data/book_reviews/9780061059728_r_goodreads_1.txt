There is a scene where the main character, Scott, doesn’t want to kill an enemy soldier. He merely wounds her and lets her live. A short time later, she winds up making him regret his decision, teaching him that he needs to kill in order to be a good soldier.His next encounter he kills an enemy soldier and vomits, barely able to handle what he’s done.And then suddenly he is a prolific killer more vicious and skilled than anyone else, butchering the enemy with cold efficiency and unparalleled talent.This is a good example of some of the problems with this book. The lack of depth is only highlighted by the attempts to include deeper elements but failing to do so in an effective manner. This isn’t character development, it’s acknowledging that development is important so you throw in a detail or two only to rush past it and call it good.There is a reason this trilogy is what inspired me to start writing my own stories: I knew I could do better.
