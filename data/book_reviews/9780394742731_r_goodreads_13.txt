“If you really want to be sure of getting caught, the thing to do is kill a policeman. This truth applies in most places and especially in Sweden.” p50Mark closed the book he was reading, “The Abominable Man”, and sighed heavily. He scratched his head and tried to reconcile the fact that a book that was written in 1971 could be so completely and chillingly relevant in 2026. He crossed his legs, then uncrossed them, folded his arms and then unfolded them, trying to collect his thoughts about what had impacted him the most.Was it this line?- “The police force took a very dim view of sociologists - particularly in recent years since they’d started focusing more and more on the activities and attitudes of policemen” (p50). Did this somehow correlate with the 2022 finding that the police force in my home state in consisted of a ‘dysfunctional’ workplace culture, including systemic misogyny, sexism, and racism, alongside and entrenched “boys’ club mentality”.Hmmm…Or was it this one? - “He remembered reading in an interview with the National Chief of Police that of the 742 complaints about police misconduct received by the Ombudsman over a period of three years, only one had been delivered to the public prosecutor for legal action” (p93). Perhaps this resonated with the finding in my country that “low substantiation rates [of complaints] and poor communication with complainants, combined with concerns about lack of independence where police are investigating complaints against the police, continue to undermine community confidence in the complaints process”.I wonder…Or this one? - “He fell apart and had to go on sick leave for a couple of months. Lost all interest in everything…. I don’t think they told him the truth right away, but eventually he must have found out what had happened, because in September he sent in his first complaint… But by then the investigation was already closed” (p143). This certainly resonates in terms of the impacts of unresolved complaints and the feelings of helplessness and disempowerment of an individual unable to move on following events where resolutions to injustices are not obtained.How can this be?Or this one? - “If we’d known what society was coming to, we wouldn’t have had any children at all. But they’ve just been leading us on all these years.” “Who?”, said Rönn.“The politicians. The party leaders. The ones we thought were on our side. Just gangsters, all of them “ (p175). Apparently in my country in 2024, only 33 to 46% of adults trusted the federal government to do the right thing, due to lack of transparency, perceived inefficiency and inaction on pressing issues….Do things ever change?Mark sat back and reflected that Maj Sjöwall and Per Wahlöö hit the nail on the head in the Martin Beck series. The political and social commentary woven through each of these police procedural novels is second to none and the very foundation of the Scandi Noir phenomenon.He acknowledged that The Abominable Man is book number seven out of a ten book series and considered it the best he’d read in the series so far. He lamented that there were only three books left to read before the series end.
