I read this book back in the 1970s and again the 1980s when I bought a copy (second hand hardback now lost like so many favourite books) and although I don't think anyone would accept his position on Germany's unique 'path' (a pretty much discredited idea that Germany had taken a 'wrong' path in the 19th century (or earlier there were different schools of thought) making Hitler inevitable and the German experience unique and not something countries like the UK, USA, France, etc. had to worry about - they were unable, because their history went the 'right' way, from being nasty, brutish, evil doers (a point of view that no subject of their empires or systemised racialism would accept but would remain unlistened too for a very long time after the publication of this book) this is still a book worth reading because it remains the only decent history, in English anyway, that looks at the roll that companies like Krupp played in German history, particularly during the Third Reich (comparisons with recent books on the Thyssens are so overwhelmingly to their disadvantage, in a really embarrasing way, that I won't mention author names).

I am sure that the Krupp story could benefit from revision and updating but until someone does this is a fascinating, important and still relevant book.
