I thought I would like this book more than I did. Written by the author of the Cat in the Stacks series, I thought I knew what to expect. I can only guess that since this was written almost 10 years earlier, that the writing improved with age and experience.
First all of the campy names was a real turn off. Second I couldn't determine why it was necessary to write Simon as a vampire, since he is taking some medication that eliminates nearly all of the vampire traits, and it adds very little. If he and the murderer could have had something else as common ground, this designation could have been skipped altogether, it only seems to have been necessary to explain how the killer tried to off him and why she wasn't successful. I can only assume it is because it was written when a lot of vampire tropes started to flood the market. Then, there is the fact that he only recently arrived in England from living in the south in the US, yet he seems to utilize way too many British colloquialisms as they are perceived by Americans. I found it difficult to credit that these would have become part of his everyday speech so quickly.
Also, I was annoyed by the asides where the author is speaking to the reader, while maintaining the character of Simon. Many authors write in the first person, but taking a step back from the storytelling to let the reader in on something was just weird.
I did like the actual murder mystery and sleuthing and solving parts which is what saved this rating from being only 2 stars instead of 3.
