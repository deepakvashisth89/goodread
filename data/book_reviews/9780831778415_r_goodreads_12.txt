4.5⭐️sIf I went into Needful Things, Leland Gaunt would probably sell me endless shelf space on my bookcases because really, what else do I need? I wonder what type of deeds I’d do in exchange for that?This book is really about the corrupting disease of obsessive consumerism, compounded by the evils of human nature in pursuit. Also, it demonstrates the fallacy of being able to obtain materialistic fulfillment because we are so worried about losing said objects, that we fail to ever enjoy them.I see why King says this is a satirical comedy, but I experience it as much more of a horror story whose terror is rooted in how well it captures the ease with which we’ll sell our souls to obtain momentary happiness.The good…The absurdity, the weirdness, the balls-to-the-wall, head-scrambling romp this book is from the very beginning. It’s unrelenting and propulsive, and there aren’t many lulls, which makes an 800 page book feel like a quick read.The less good…I think my only complaint, and this could be a “me thing”, but there was one point towards the end where I was having trouble keeping up with this large cast of characters. Despite this being my second read, keeping track of who did what to whom was like a 12,000 piece puzzle with crossed eye and no corrective lenses.To read, or not to readYou should read it… it slaps
