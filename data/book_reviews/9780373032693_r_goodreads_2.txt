Review can be read at It's About The Book3.5 starsA cute, quick read about a woman trying to fit into a man’s world of sports journalism. I’m not sure if this is a reprint of a series that was originally written in the early 90’s as all three books in this series have a 1990 publishing date as well as 2014, so be check if you have it first before purchasing.The third book in the series about the Hall sisters, this book is about shy, quiet Ivy. A gifted writer, Ivy has decided to become a sports writer with the Austin Globe. Covering men’s sports is difficult enough for a woman, but having to compete with well-known sports figure Rick Scott is embarrassing. He keeps catching Ivy at her most inept–when she’s being ignored by players in the locker room or when she’s blushing the color of a firetruck at seeing men drop their towels. Plus, she’s always felt like she had to compete with her two successful older sisters – and with their looks and success, that’s a hard job to do.Luckily Rick decides to help shy Ivy with the Bambi eyes, so he sends over players, carefully vetted not to drop towels, to give her stories. And he helps her every week to get her story in on time. She wonders why he’s doing it until she realizes that the attraction that she feels for him is mutual — he’s doing it because he likes her. Also, since he’s the only other sports writer on the Austin Globe, helping her helps him, just like he said. But they agree that they will have to keep their personal life separate from their professional life, since they will be competing at work.When Ivy starts covering the local soccer beat and ends up coaching not one girls team but two, Rick again steps in to help her. When his style of coaching differs from hers, she begins to become defensive. Women have to act tough like men, right? They have to play to win, right? She believes that and trains her girls hard; however, Rick seems to have a more fun approach. The man who was once a professional athlete seems to think it’s more important for the girls to have fun? She doesn’t understand. If women have to be tough like men to get ahead why shouldn’t it start when they’re young, in sports?After a while, Ivy decides that she doesn’t want Rick helping her at work–she wants to make it on her own. But she still wants their personal, dating relationship to continue, as does he. But she screws up something Rick asks her to do one night after a game, assuming that he’s trying to help her again. This is incredibly frustrating for Rick, and it creates a barrier between them that wasn’t there before. She misses him and seeks him out when she realizes that he’s avoiding her; and they make up. Rick realizes that not only is she in love with him, but he’s in love with her. He wanted to help Ivy because otherwise, for her to be successful at sports reporting she would have to become aggressive and driven and change her entire personality; and he loves her exactly as she is. And when she won’t let him help, and doesn’t seem to realize that she might take losing personally, it’s only a matter of time before something happens to upset the apple cart.After a series of letdowns, Ivy answers a phone call for Rick that gives her an idea for a lead that turns into a trap. Luckily Rick pulls her butt out of the fire and saves her career. But his story scoops all the papers, and this makes Ivy really unreasonably mad–at him and the profession. She quits the paper and leaves–bitter and disenchanted. Rick had told her that they had to keep their personal and professional lives separate–but she couldn’t. And when he said that they had to compete and not take it personally if the other won–she never realized how much it would hurt. This didn’t seem like her dream job at all.Can these two journalists write themselves a happy ending?While I certainly enjoyed this read, it lacked in heat and length. I would have loved more description of their time together, and I would have liked to have known a little more about the circumstances surrounding Rick’s injury. I think reading the other two books in the series that came before would help. I also think the fact that this book was originally published in 1993 explains the lack of sexy times in it! Would have appreciated a heads up about the two different publishing dates and if this was a reprint, maybe a new edition would have made it more enjoyable and timely. Thanks for the read.
