The highest rated review for this on here describes the first half of the book as dull and the second half as interesting. In terms of pure readability, I think the reverse is true. 1863-1868 is a flash in the pan of possibility. There's a moment for radical change in those years that's exhilarating to watch flourish and awful to watch fail. The second half of the book--1868-1877--just reads as an inevitable unfolding of retrenchment.

The abridged version feels very... abridged. Like it feels like you're getting the conclusions of Foner's work without the analysis that led to it. But really that's my fault for getting the abridged version, isn't it?
