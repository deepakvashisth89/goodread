A fine telling of the life of Louise Brooks. Along with Clara Bow and Lon Chaney, she's one of my favourites in silent cinema. There's something magical about her look and aura, in some ways she seems out of time when you see her in a 1920's movie, you could easily lift her from the celluloid and place her in a 1960's film and she wouldn't seem out of place. Diary Of A Lost Girl and Pandora's Box are amongst the first films I would advise someone unfamiliar with silent cinema to watch (Chaney's The Penalty would be another!).In life, Brooks was a fascinating character. She was a tough outspoken character who lived a turbulent life. You wish she had made more films after seeing the ones available, but Brooks was never one to take an obvious path through life.A very fine book.[There are many great silent films out there that I'm sure would surprise the prejudiced who only think of overacted, jerky, faded films played at the wrong speed and culminate with chases and pies in the face!]
