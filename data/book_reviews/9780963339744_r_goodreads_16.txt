⭐️ ⭐️ ⭐️ ⭐️ 1/2

Some works of art are extraordinary because they are so gruesomely affecting. They tap into something primal and agitate our buried trauma. They disquiet our veiled peace and shake loose the cobwebs of disturbance and fear.

I’m at a loss about how to rate this book. One hand, it’s so horrifying, so repulsive that I want to throw it into a trash can and burn it into nonexistence. But on the other, I want to celebrate its very existence because it’s such a horrific artifact of the nihilistically macabre.

The question of moral obligation sits at the center of this book, and it does an excellent job probing the complicated nature of complacency vs responsibility. David’s internal dilemma brings this struggle to life.

Ruth ranks up there as an all time villain. She is unbelievably terrifying, and yet all too believable. I can envision her perfectly and her moral corruption was absolute, leaving her devoid of any humanity. She was almost supernatural in her vileness.

This book is hard to read, and yet I couldn’t stop reading it. Proceed with caution if you pick up this book. If you’re squeamish, run. If you’re sensitive, hide. If you’re like me and have been systemically desensitized for decades, brace yourself… it’s still rough.
