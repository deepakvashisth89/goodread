Solid introduction to Freud that emphasises the different intellectual phases of his work—for example Wollheim points out that the structural theory of an id, ego and superego did not emerge with the discovery of the unconscious, but developed some time after. The chapter "Dreams, Errors, Symptoms, Jokes" is an especially intelligible and concise overview of some of the most famous Freudian concepts, and could probably be read on its own. The later chapters on sexuality and the neurosis are more difficult, but this is reflective of the ambiguity of the theories themselves. The final section on civilisation and society helps to link psychoanalysis to a social theory more generally, which should be of interest to those looking to come to grips with the critical theory of the Frankfurt school, or, more recently, Slavoj Zizek.
