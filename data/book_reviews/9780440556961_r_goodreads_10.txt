David Quammen is a self-effacing naturalist and travel author which I can't help but compare to Bill Bryson. He has a sharp wit and is good at bringing lofty and complex concepts from the natural sciences to a general audience. His writing is, from a technical standpoint, some of the best I've ever encountered.This book is a collection of columns from a magazine he wrote for over the course of many years. They are roughly grouped together, with the first section zooming in on particular animals (mosquito, bat, and octopus, to name a few), the middle section profiling science and conservationist figures, the fourth section on animal reproduction, and the final section on big-picture topics.The length of the essays are perfect for casual reading: long enough to go relatively deep on a topic, short enough that they don't take long to get through. The result was that it was easy to keep momentum on reading this, even though the stories didn't connect into each other in any particular way.
