Ted Bundy has been a personal obsession since I first read The Stranger Beside Me in 1981. The Deliberate Stranger, The Only Living Witness, The Killer Next Door followed Rule’s book. All were written before his execution in 1989. Bob Keppel was the lead investigator from 1974 in Washington State. He begins with Bundy’s death row confession in graphic detail with the killer carrying Georgann Hawkin’s severed head after cutting it off like a butcher at a deli. Keppel then rehashes the initial investigation from 1974 to Ted’s arrest in Utah in 1975. He was sentenced to death in Florida in 1979. Keppel soon after joined a task force for a few days in 1981 to help with the Atlanta child murders. Wayne Williams was caught and convicted a year later. Keppel was working for the Attorney General’s office for Washington State and began work on the Green River Killer case. The Riverman was quite ambitious, as 47 victims were found. All were hookers, and many of them teenagers. Keppel was an advisor on the case. He received a letter from Bundy in October of 1984 offering his assistance in capturing the Riverman. A second 22 page letter from Bundy advised them to keep watch on recent dump sites Ted expected the killer to return to his victims as he was later known to have done. Bundy was fascinated that the Riverman was an equal opportunity killer, with both black and white victims. I felt the same way as my fellow left-hander. Ted’s funniest moment was when he proposed a slasher film festival with the police photographing the patrons and taking down license plate numbers. Keppel was amused by the suggestion and did not agree that the Riverman would attend the theater. Many pages quoting Bundy during the extensive interviews in 1985 are amazingly insightful and extremely articulate. While on death row, Teddy had read FBI profiles of serial murderers (Bundy included) and had become, as he labeled himself, a P.H.D. in murder. The guy is one entertaining SOB. Unfortunately, Keppel drifts away from Bundy and into long, redundant detail regarding serial murder investigations. The book is from 1995 and the Green River Killer wasn’t caught until 2001. Ann Rule’s “Green River, Running Red” is a comprehensive account of the capture of Gary Ridgway after a 19 year manhunt. Keppel’s strong belief in computers was not the reason Ridgway was caught. A policewoman decoy posing as a prostitute busted him. Finally, Keppel got down to the nitty gritty as he interviewed Bundy just three days before his appointment with sparky, where he would be BBQ. The killer was a natural born storyteller with remarkable recall. The first confession was the abduction and murder of Georgann Hawkins is both disturbing and riveting. Theodore casually recalled knocking the girl out with a crowbar. When she awoke in his VW, he was bewildered by her mentioning a Spanish exam the next day. The crowbar was part of the VW’s toolkit; I guess Hitler’s “people’s car” contained some unusual items. After the Hawkins confession, he was very cryptic regarding specifics of other murders. Keppel’s final question was, “when was your first murder?” and Bundy would not answer. Instead, he asked for another 2 to 3 months to give law enforcement from several states the location of more bodies. His stalling tactic failed, as Theodore Robert Bundy was put to death in the electric chair on January 24, 1989.
