A book that hovers somewhere between 2 and 3 stars, certainly a step down from the previous two entries. The good is pretty clear, as the plot itself is very interesting and the multiple parallel perspectives are done reasonably well in terms of storytelling, but that's about where the positives end.The two main male protagonists, who in their own books were fleshed out and unique, feel incredibly similar here almost to the point of them blending into one. If you removed their previous experiences, they're virtually indistinguishable from one another in terms of personality, reduced to a facsimile of how they were before. It's made even worse when a third perspective is introduced and the character behind it might as well be directly cloned from the other two - the tone and personalities of all three of them is genuinely identical.As if that wasn't enough, the two female supporting protagonists in this, likewise to their male counterparts, seemingly regress in terms of development from the previous books. Aside from the very opening of the book, these two characters get relegated to such a secondary role they may as well not be in the book at all - honestly don't think they say more than a few sentences in the latter third of the book. Pretty disappointing considering how strong their characterisation was in their own books, as they're both identical in terms of how they're written here.Overall, just a little disappointing since the writer's clear usage of an archetypal 'blueprint' to write all of his characters becomes impossible to ignore when so many are involved - as they're essentially reduced to male hero #1, 2 or 3 and female sidekick #1 or 2.
