This is a GREAT biography about Louise Brooks, the only actress, in my opinion, that could have given Garbo any competition had she (Louise) stuck around and continued making films in Hollywood (or making films in general.Louise was a Witicha Kansas girl who went to NYC at the age of 15 to be a "star". She began her career with the Denishawn Dancers and soon earned a place in the front line of the dance troupe. Unfortunately, not only was Louse a "free spirit" but she did not get along well with other women. Consequently, she was fired but soon landed on her feet by being hired by the George White’s Scandals, and then by Ziegfeld Follies. From the Follies, she was given a screen test by Paramount and headed out to CA to star in movies. However, Hollywood moguls did not appreciate Louise's "free spirit" and desire to "do things her way". Though she was courted by THE studio (MGM) in Hollywood, Louise decided to stick with Paramount, for a little while that is.What happens after, you will have to read the books. Needless to say, it is a fascinating biography of a FASCINATING 1920's film star and a FASCINATING individual in and off the screen. The books has been well researched and the author had extensive conversations with Louise herself before she passed away on August 8, 1945On a side note: I live about 40 miles away from Rochester NY where Louise is buried and has visited her grave.
