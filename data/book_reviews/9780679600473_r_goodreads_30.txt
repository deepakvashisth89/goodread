Urbanism has been a passion of mine for a couple years now, and I finally got around to reading the urbanist bible.I half expected the reading to sound like the rest of the urbanist community, out of touch utopian idealists—Instead, I found Jacob’s to be deeply pragmatic and grounded. I can hardly think of other writers who deal in such complex systems. I found myself absorbing her ideas as if they were my own because of the way she made them seem intuitive and almost obvious.She does a great job selling cities too. I love that cities are the most intense places of human expression and relationship. Jacob’s loves this too, and she is able to describe this passion with vivid imagery. This is the driving passion for wanting to understand cities deeply and critique the ways in which they have been mishandled.At times, her prose was dense and confusing for my 21st century smooth brain. In those chapters I did not bother with the details once I got the main idea. She also writes very dry which I found entertaining at times.Overall would recommend for anyone who is interested in systems thinking and social science.Edit: In the months following, I have realized Jane Jacob’s is my intellectual crush. This woman was a genius and is right about most everything.
