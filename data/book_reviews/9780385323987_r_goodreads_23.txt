As a follow-up read to 'LaBrava', I was a little unprepared for 'Get Shorty'. (I hadn't seen the movie version but I'll take care of that anon.)I knew it's one of Leonard's better-known titles, so I suppose I expected a particular charge from the gates. I was wrong. More than wrong, actually. The novel starts out so minimally - with a tiny circle of characters (and it stays that way for awhile) - that I naively thought I sensed lethargy in the storytelling. But, again, I was wrong.Established early on, 'LaBrava' has a kind of complex, whirlwind-weave of relationships and settings that is absent here. 'GS' does, of course, reveal its own complexity - as it goes - but from the beginning it's a slow burn. The pay-off comes by sticking with the tale through a simpler, low-key set-up.Once again, Leonard has no shortage of surprises and his signature wit is in full evidence. I love how he's like a pig in mud when he's roasting tough guys - he doesn't like them any more than he seems to like Hollywood; except for the one tough guy who needs to be the hero, and smarter then the underachievers he's up against.The story has a very entertaining premise: some bad guys get genuinely hooked by the idea of making a real Hollywood movie (but, of course, that's mixed-in with some of the regular intrigue that is a bad guy's bread and butter). One especially hilarious sequence - set-up as suspense- involves two such guys who end up reading a screenplay together to see how it ends. (One of them - surprisingly - actually knows how to read a screenplay; the other one...not so much.)As a novel, I probably enjoyed 'LaBrava' more, but that doesn't mean that 'Get Shorty' shows Leonard as less skillful, not at all. The approach, style and tone are different but it's pure Leonard throughout - and, as such, another very entertaining read.
