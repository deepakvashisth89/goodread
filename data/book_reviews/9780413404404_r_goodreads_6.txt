Even though this book was written in 1985, it feels like it was written for today. Postman talks about how our culture has shifted from being focused on reading and deep thinking to being obsessed with entertainment and quick, flashy media, especially through TV. And now with social media, it’s even more true.

Postman compares George Orwell and Aldous Huxley. Orwell worried about censorship and government control. Huxley feared we’d just stop caring, too distracted and entertained to notice or think deeply.



One of the strongest points he makes is about how news, politics, education, and even religion have turned into forms of entertainment. It's no longer about truth or depth, it’s about looking good and keeping people watching.

The bigger problem isn’t just bad information, it’s that You can fix being uninformed, but not if people think they already know the truth, when they don’t.



Postman definitely idealizes the past a bit, he acts like reading was always this equalizing force, when we know that only the wealthy (or clergymen) were taught. But his main message is powerful: the way we consume media shapes how we think (or don’t). And if we keep letting entertainment replace real discussion, we’re in serious trouble.

It’s a short read and a bit academic, but still eye-opening. I couldn’t help but think of how horrified Postman would be watching us communicate in TikTok videos, or consume "news" in memes and soundbites. We have come to adore the very technologies that destroys our capacity to think...says the woman writing on her iPad and posting this review to a social media site. But hey, at least those of us on this site are still reading!!

Highly recommend if you’re at all interested in media, culture, or why everything feels like a performance now.
