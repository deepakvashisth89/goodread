The daddy of gothic fiction, influential to the horror genre as a whole, introducing the classic themes of love, death, suspense & supernatural beings... I'm afraid does not stand the test of time.

The beginning promises an atmospheric tale of mysterious deaths, supernatural occurrences, characters corrupted by their need for complete power, sorrow, and death. But as we keep on reading, the story loses itself in the romance of it all; suddenly there's a love triangle, a damsel in distress, a prince in disguise, and matters of the heart.

I can appreciate Walpole's genius in creating this new literary genre, but I am very glad to see the gothic/horror genre has evolved into something else.
