"Sexy" is a fast, easy read. I always thought it read like a poem because of its rhythm and because of its ambiguity.If you're looking for a cast of characters to fall in love with or befriend, this isn't that. The book follows an emotional arc at the expense of characters. Almost everyone in this book does something reprehensible (besides a few of the peripheral women), and there are some intense moments too, including violent homophobia, almost none of which is specifically condemned in the narrative. It's up to you to see the through lines and come to your own conclusions."Sexy" is an incredibly powerful story about being your own person and having your own thoughts and opinions. I think it's meant especially for young men caught in a system of toxic expectations, straight or not. This was actually my first Oates book, which I read a decade (or more!) ago, sometime in early high school. I remember checking the book out of the library because I was a young gay kid and there was an attractive guy on the cover. In retrospect, I'm not sure this was the best way to market the book, but I am grateful to have found it.All these years later, upon revisiting the book, I am surprised to see how well the story has held up, and how important it remains to be. As a teenager, I recall being confused and slightly angry about the ambiguity of the narrative, but as an adult I have a more clear theory of what Oates could have intended.I guess all I have to say is that this book has kept me thinking, years after I first read it, and that it changed the way I perceived fiction. It may not do those things for you, but it could.
