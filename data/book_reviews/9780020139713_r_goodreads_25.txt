Cigarettes is a book about love, but not love as it's typically characterized. The crux of Mathews' love lies in domination, in the desire to either dominate or to be dominated. The novel unfolds across several decades and is told through a set of 14 relationships, with each section dedicated to a pair of characters. These characters and their various schemes are all interconnected, and as they slither in and out of New York's art, business, and horseracing scenes, love appears as a tactful maneuver, a flailing escape, a plea, and a death rattle. It's never pure or innocent, but it can certainly be fun.I really enjoyed this but it was less breezy than I expected, and I found myself getting a bit bogged down in the writing from time to time.
