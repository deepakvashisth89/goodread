A very stirring tale of the contrasting lives of two sisters, very different in temperament and whose characters define their very distinct fates. The multifarious vicissitudes of their divergent lives are related with a humour, an irony and a warmth that make the text utterly compelling and very enjoyable.

Beautifully narrated, as good as any Dickens (for my money). Indeed, lacking that rather annoying sentimentality which is so often a blot on the great Dickens novels. The prose is so alive, bubbling with vitality. Hard to understand why Bennett has fallen into relative obscurity, having been such a well-known and important figure in his time.

I also appreciate very much the humility that the writing evinces. The narrative does not put on airs and graces, but rather spills out of an abundance of care and concern and love for the characters. It would seem that Sophia is an especial favourite of the author. She is indeed a great and felicitous product of the pen of this most excellent, much underrated novelist.
