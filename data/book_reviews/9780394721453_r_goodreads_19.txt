This was okay. The ideal way to read this is on one of those weekends in early spring when an ice storm has caused a five-day power outage, and since your beloved had endured enough of your bullshit and left a few weeks back, you are now cold, lonely, surviving on frozen celery and potatoes that were out in the garage, and sitting on the couch with nothing but your sad thoughts to distract you.

I, on the other hand, tried to read it in ten- and twenty-minute bursts between Zoom meetings, hot meals, exercise sessions and potential evacuations due to encroaching wildfires. I ended up having to back up and re-read many lengthy passages just to establish what was going on, because few people in this book are honest, and even fewer feel a commitment to doing the right thing.

What always surprises me in books of this era is how readily women hop into bed with their bosses, their husband's friends, their husband's enemies, the mailman, the bartender, whomever crosses their path that day. Contraceptives were somewhat less reliable in those days, and consequences considerably more severe. But then I have to remind myself that this is just fiction, and Raymond Chandler was probably huddled in the cold in a lonely kitchen, chasing flights of fantasy between bouts of questioning the arc of his miserable life.
