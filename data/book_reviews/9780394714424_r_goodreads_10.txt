I struggled with this book and very nearly gave up. I found it hard to follow and not very reader-friendly. Perhaps, it is the author's polemical intent that makes it such hard work for the reader. I found Part 1 the hardest - views of people she disagrees with are dismissed as misinterpretations of Freud or incorrect with these two claims being treated as identical. They never seems to be much effort to persuade the reader - all I could gather was either: this is not what Freud said and therefore it is wrong, or simply this is wrong. Since I do not think that everything Freud wrote or thought was correct and since I do think that his claims are best understood literally (or even in the way he himself understood them), I found this a bit annoying.The section on Reich was OK as was the section on Laing, but the best bit was the final section of the book. In fact, I think a reasonable approach might be to start with the conclusion (and perhaps only read that part of the book). Mitchell's main claim is that gender is social rather than biological and that the Oedipus complex needs to be understood in cultural/social terms. That seems to me to be an interesting (indeed, valid) point, which it would be interesting to explore in less polemical and less dogmatic terms. But maybe at the time the author's primary aim was to get feminists to think again about Freud and that is why she wrote the hastily-written and polemical book she did.
