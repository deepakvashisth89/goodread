A 1940 thriller from a master storyteller concerning the difficulties of convincing anyone in authority that you have vital information regarding industrial espionage that could tilt the balance of power in a world war that, at the time of writing, was swinging heavy in favour of the Axis powers.A famous middle aged barrister is visited by an exiled Austrian Jewish engineer who is wanted for a murder that he plainly didn’t commit and when he disappears whoever finds him and rescues his invention will have an advantage in air power.Kilmartin, the staid legal man, is forced to become a man of action following various leads to Cornwall, Eastbourne and, more uncomfortably, the cellars of a London merchant bank and their adjacent sewers.There’s rather an odd (and uncomfortable, but of it’s time) juxtaposition of anti-Semitism and fêting of the Jewish inventor’s brilliance and personal courage.Hammond Innes is brilliant at describing action sequences so be prepared for a hectic car chase, pursuit through the City of London’s sewers and a small war taking place on a ship bound for Germany.
