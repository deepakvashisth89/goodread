In my opinion, one of the best Star Wars EU novels. I found the characterization of Leia and Han particularly good, and I enjoyed the struggles Luke faced dealing with his new identity as a Jedi master.She did a good job with the interplay between Han and Leia, considering that at the end of Return of the Jedi, their attraction is obvious, but they haven't spent time together or learned how to resolve disagreements. Well done.No, there's no sex, only thwarted attempts at make-outs.The only real thing I didn't like was Luke's attraction to Gaeriel ... or how ever the hell you spell that. It felt a little Forced (waah waaaah). Gaeriel has that character undergo emotional development, and she becomes a true, three-dimensional person in the novel, which is quite a feat for an EU novel (Kevin J. Anderson, I'm looking at you, bud. Callista was the worst character I've ever witnessed.)I also have a special place in my heart for this book, since it's the first EU book I read in high school.
