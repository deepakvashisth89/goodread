It's a very good post-apocalyptic dystopia. It's also a profoundly bleak post-apocalyptic dystopia. In Holdfast, there are no more animals and what plant life there is is thinning on the ground. Men have built their society around warped memories of masculine virtues and utter hatred of the un-men (anyone who wasn't white) and fems (women). Fems are slave labor and breeding stock, not even considered human, and when we finally get out of male heads and into a chapter from Alldera's point of view, the stark horror of a life where you have to hide even a hint of intelligence lest you be killed as a threat to masculine society is painted clear. When she makes it out of Holdfast and into the wilderness with the Marish and the free fems, that doesn't fix anything. Freedom doesn't take away her trauma, she doesn't have the security and knowledge of a motherline among the Marish, and refuses to take part in the aping of Holdfast society that the free fems have built.
