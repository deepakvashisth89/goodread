This epic story begins, memorably, in the month of April.The earth was pale green, the air itself was all but visible, golden with the rain-washed sunlight, and all the trees and grass and bushes glittered, varnished with the rain-drops.And, just as our young protagonist, Jody Baxter, finds an orphaned fawn and convinces his doting father and stern mother to allow him to raise him as a pet, twin fawns descended upon our neighborhood with their doe mother.I quite literally read from this book from our deck, over the past couple of months, while these two precious (and sometimes destructive) spotted fawns gamboled and butted about in the background, as though a film crew had shown up and was indulging us with animation.We experienced spring and summer, almost simultaneously, with Jody Baxter and his family, and just as we started to feel summer succumb to autumn, we shared the Baxter family's winter and the final spring of this story.And, what a circle it is. It is the proverbial “circle of life,” but it is so much more.It is, quite possibly, the greatest, most effective story about our intergenerational legacy, or, I suppose I could say, the greatest one not written by John Steinbeck.This epic story ends, memorably, in the month of April.A mark was on him. . . so that all his life, when April was a thin green and the flavor of rain was on his tongue, an old wound would throb and a nostalgia would fill him for something he could not quite remember.In short: 5 billion stars.
