The agrarian prophet takes on, not science, but the reductionist religion of science’s loudest voices. Art fares no better; in its aping of the more successful field (of science)—“success” judged by what society presently finds most practical; what brings in the $$$—art hit its own dead end, sputtering into myriad wells in search of the constantly new. Art and science need each other, and though Berry can’t precisely define how—and if he could, I don’t think I’d buy it…because it’d have been reducible then, right?—he’s positive the solution isn’t art as the servant of science. At its root, science means “to know,” and art “to do”; knowing and doing—two sides of the same coin—both in need of each other. In an age of specialization, we’re tempted to pry the two from each other, all to our collapse. Science (knowing) without the right applied art (doing), ends destructively in nukes, disease, and environmental destruction; and art without science—the attempted creation of great art without a foundational knowledge of the past—ends in vapid futility, constantly searching for the new and authentic, but never offering anything of worth.Show more
