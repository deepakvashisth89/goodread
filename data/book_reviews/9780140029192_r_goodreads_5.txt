Although the title was The Wedding, there wasn‘t actually a wedding in the story, and the ending was disappointing.
I understand that The Wedding is a story of the rise of a black middle class, written by a woman who lived it, but I was confused by the way West wrote about race, interracial relationships, complexion, class, power and powerlessness. Much of the time I didn’t know who was black, white or other. Some of the confusion was caused by the constant leaps into the pasts of different characters. I also found the number of characters and the way they were portrayed off-putting.
However, I found Preacher’s story quite interesting, and Clark was quite a complex character, but I didn’t like the character of Lute McNeil, with all the children by white mothers he didn’t even like, or his obsession with Shelby and his determination to seduce her away from her fiancé. He’s a nasty piece of work.
I felt sorry for Lute’s children: Barby, Tina, and Muffin (what kind of name is that for a child?) in their longing for a wholesome, kind and loving black mother. How unsettling it must have been for them to be looked after by strangers, housekeepers and various white women. “Tina wanted a brown mother like next door’s mother. Brown mothers hugged you a lot and made you laugh a lot. White mothers made you feel sorry and sad.” Tina’s death was a shock.
I enjoyed the detailed descriptions of the smells, tastes, and sounds of The Oval, which made me feel I was part of the scene.
