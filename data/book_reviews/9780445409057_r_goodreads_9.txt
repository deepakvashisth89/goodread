If you’re a fan of the Toby peters series by Stuart Kaminsky then you have to read this book. It has all of the usual components: the wisecracking first person narrative from private detective, Toby Peters; movie stars and historical figures from early 20th century Los Angeles; and a bare bones, fast moving mystery with lots of twists and turns that you’re not supposed to be able to solve on your own. I’m not going to give away the plot because frankly this series of books is mostly supported by Kaminsky’s crisp and witty writing style and the little historical interactions you get as the plot unfolds.

I rated this one with only three stars because I wanted a little more involvement and color from the historical figures in the book, especially Basil Rathbone, who probably should have had a much larger role in the book. It was a good Toby Peters book, but not one of the best ones. I don’t believe there was anything developing the Peters character in this book that you would need to read the other books.

I love this character and the way Kaminsky writes. Peters doesn’t take himself too seriously. He’s witty and a little reckless. He interacts with powerful legends like Howard Hughes and General MacArthur, and he treats them just like regular people. In some ways, he reminds me of Bernie Rhodenbarr, another favorite recurring character I enjoy from Lawrence Block. Both Peters and Rhodenbarr speak with a similar sarcastic, world-weary tone that allows them to chuckle even as everything appears to be going wrong. They’re also both more clever and determined than they reveal to others.

If you’re new to the Toby Peters series, you might consider starting with Murder on the Yellow Brick Road, Buried Caesars, or Down for the Count.
