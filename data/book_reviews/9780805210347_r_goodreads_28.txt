I think I was just a bit disappointed because of my own expectations regarding mystical texts. In my mind, they would have an even more mysterious language, similar to reading lore and magical texts. Although there are some cryptic passages, the overall structure of the Zohar (at least as presented in this edition) is not that far from stories we hear during the Seder of Pessach: "Rabbi X said this, Rabbi Y answered that...". The mystical and fantastical happens inside those speeches, but it sounds more familiar than what I expected.That being said, it is indeed a fascinating read and this very brief selection from Gershom Scholem at least presents some of the most important concepts of Jewish mysticism: the sefirot, the three levels of souls, the relationship between God, the Shekhinah and the people of Israel, the destiny of souls after death. The biggest issue is how short it is and it feels just like a "trial version" of the larger text.
