The Old Curiosity Shop is a novel by Charles Dickens, first issued serially in 1840-1841 in the weekly in Dickens' own literary magazine, Master Humphrey's Clock. It is the story of the frail Little Nell Trent about fourteen years old, and her grandfather. They live in a few rooms adjacent to the little storefront full of odd antiques and curiosities. Little Nell runs errands for her grandfather and in her spare time is teaching their servant Kit how to write. Kit is devoted to Little Nell often making sure that she is safe in bed before he leaves for the night.

However, Little Nell's grandfather foolishly gambles away his curiosity shop to his creditor, the dwarf Daniel Quilp, one of Victorian literature's most evil villains. As a result, Little Nell and her grandfather flee London wandering throughout the countryside north of London. It is along the way that many other characters are introduced into the narrative, including a traveling Punch and Judy show. Ultimately, once all of their money is gone, Nell is forced to beg for alms on the street eventually making contact with her previous schoolmaster who arranges lodging for them in a parsonage where he is assigned. There are many plots throughout the tale of as we learn more of the intricacies between different characters and their often conflicting motives. I found myself very engaged throughout the book particularly enjoying all of the scenery portrayed by Dickens in Victorian London and the countryside.
