Keneally tends to be best known forSchindler's Ark, his 1982 Booker-Prize winner and the basis for the filmSchindler's List. AlthoughThe Playmakeris well known to generations of A-Level students, that still seems a pity.Confederatesremains my favourite among his novels for more reasons than I can list, but I am always impressed with how seamlessly he enters the heads of the footsloggers and exits into the wider picture but without diminishing either. That is crucial in a war novel—more so when the focus is on the losing side, with all the myths, half truths and petty bigotries we find absurd but the characters live by. ('To them, crapping where the urge took you was all part of those direct and country ways those Yankees would try to convert you from, if you gave them the chance.')Recommended.
