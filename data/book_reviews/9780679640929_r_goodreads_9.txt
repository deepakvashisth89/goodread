Wow. Kung asks all the sensible questions, which are also, oddly, the ones Catholics are afraid to ask:

Why compulsory celibacy and not voluntary? (MTTW 19:12, 1 TIM 3:2)

Why the need for dubious proofs of popes’ miracles just so that they can be canonized?

Why the alienation of laicized priests?

Why the “almost spooky battle against modern women who seek a contemporary form of life; prohibiting birth control and abortion (even in the case of incest or rape), divorce, the ordination of women, and the modernization of women’s religious orders”?

Why close churches and allow for the collapse of pastoral care instead of simply allowing for married priests, women priests, etc.?

Why defend the idea of papal infallibility when popes have proven themselves and their words so fallible?

Why did the church not take an anti-Nazi stance when it mattered? And why was Pope John Paul “silent about the mistakes of his papal predecessors” when addressing the church’s guilt in its response to the Holocaust?

Good book. Difficult, yes, but important reading, especially for Catholics, seems to me.
