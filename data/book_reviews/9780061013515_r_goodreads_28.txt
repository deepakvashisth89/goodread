I had high expectations for this book, as I’ve seen it along side some of my other favorite survival/adventure/sports narrative nonfiction books, like Into Thin Air or The Indifferent Stars Above. But honestly, this book paled in comparison to those.Because - and I don’t think this is a spoiler to say - The entire ship and crew of the Andrea Gail went down as a result of this unprecedented storm, there are no records whatsoever that the author could use to piece together the narrative of what happened that fateful October night. Whereas other books in the genre try their best to simulate what happened for the reader, Junger truly had nothing to go off of, and all of the descriptions both leading up to and after that night were total guesswork. Sure, some of the descriptions of the physiological process of drowning are morbid and interesting, but what was missing was a sense of connection to the individuals who are actually on the boat.In addition, a huge chunk of this book was dedicated to highly technical descriptions of subjects such as the history of fishing, the process of sword fishing, the mechanics of how a boat works, and the meteorological process of a hurricane forming. I hate to say it, but it’s possible that another author tackling the same subjects would have made it quite fascinating for me to read, but this author wrote of these concepts in very dry and and uninteresting ways.(On an unrelated note, I have to say, the description of the helicopter rescue was the most enthralling and dramatic part of the whole book.)Overall, I was expecting a lot more from this book, hoping it would fall in the ranks of my other favorite survival narrative nonfiction, but it just fell short.
