The second in the Dave Brandstetter “detective” series. Ok so the writing can be uneven and would benefit from harder editing. But, taken on its own terms, this is solid, enjoyable noir, and the elements it brings together make it especially appealing to me: I love the hard-boiled detective framework, the setting in the world of book selling and first-edition books, and above all the fact that it features an openly gay male detective (technically he is a claims investigator for an insurance company) and other gay characters, written in 1973, when that was pretty groundbreaking. Dave Brandstetter isn’t stereotyped or camp; he’s simply a competent, masculine professional who happens to be gay. Nowadays maybe a redundant concept, but arguably a really useful message for a book written in 1973.
I also enjoyed spotting the anachronisms of the early 1970s — the smoking, pay phones, manual credit-card transactions, period decor etc. I always enjoy reading books from other eras partly for this reason: noticing how everyday life, language, and assumptions have changed.
I fully intend to read more of the series. Probably a 3/5 or less on literary merit, but 4/5 for the concept.
