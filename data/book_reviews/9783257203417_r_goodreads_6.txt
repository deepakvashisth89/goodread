There is something about mid-century America that seems perfectly suited to crime. Perhaps it is the cool, matter-of-fact prose in style at the time; perhaps the uneasy coexistence of modern and antediluvian ideas about gender and sexuality; perhaps the sharp way that people dressed, or the state of technology (the phone, but not yet the answering machine); perhaps the impression, liberating and frightening, that it was still possible, then, to skip town in a way that really meant something. To simply be gone.There is something almost perfect aboutThe Cry of the Owl—my first by Patricia Highsmith (and, I take it, a rather odd place to start). Clearly, Highsmith had a sharp eye for human foibles. She manages to be at once sympathetic and merciless, whimsical and cold, implausible and utterly believable.
