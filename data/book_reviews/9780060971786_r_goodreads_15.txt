Only read this for the Marilyn parts. If you’re a Marilyn enthusiast looking for hidden secrets or maybe just details about the person Marilyn, Arthur doesn’t have a lot of offer. He does however give the reader better insight into his thoughts regarding his ex-wife. His sense of self importance is almost comical. He has the emotional intelligence of a fly. He seems to think he is superior to Marilyn. He unfortunately shares a lot of the opinions her detractors did but tries to present it in “helpful” way. As if he’s the hero who will turn her into a “respectable” lady. He is there to elevate her from “little more than a wh*re” into a “real actress”Whatever his intentions were he comes of very condescending and I can see why Marilyn lost faith in him. He glosses over major traumatic experiences and tries to take credit for a lot of her achievements. The Misfits was far from her first dramatic role.In the end you get the feeling he didn’t see her as a talented gifted smart woman striving for better roles… he sees a beautiful joke.
