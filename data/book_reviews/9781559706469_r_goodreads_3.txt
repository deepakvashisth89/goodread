Sir Walter Ralegh gives the inside poop on what the reign of James I was like (not very wonderful, from Ralegh's perspective), his association with Queen Elizabeth (he was a favorite for a while, invited into her bed, but not into her pussy), his final voyage to find gold in Guiana (there was none), his cursed voyage home (made easier now and then by chewing the khoka leaves given him by his faithful Indian companion) and the depth of his betrayals by those he trusted. It is about the rarified life of kings and princes, and how ordinary people stand little chance with them, but they can still come out with a shred of nobility, but not their lives.A quite wonderful book, as good as I remember it from 20 years or so ago, and deeper and more complex than my memory had retained. Wonderfully dark and moody, and accepting of the shit hand the world deals out, for what else can we do but accept it and play it as well as possible?I will remember that old queen, James I, and his fawning, mincing catamites, Ralegh's explanation of what a cocaine high feels like (he likes it!), Queen Elizabeth as an old vamp with cheesy toes, preserving her virginity, but not her virtue.And Ralegh's long but very wonderful final moments on the scaffold.
