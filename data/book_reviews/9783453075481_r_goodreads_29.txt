Mary Higgins Clark has a tendency to weave a very complicated plot surrounding her characters. She was and still is in my opinion the queen of psychological thrillers and her books were frequent visitors to my reading repertoire in the 1990’s.I have been on a personal crusade to revisit the classics, books that set the bench mark for all those other great novels that have followed so I was incredibly pleased when Kray recommend me a classic favourite of hers, all the around the town.I have said it before, and I will say it again – very few books this year have given me that thrill factor and the element of surprise that this book did. Clark has a tendency within her narratives to introduce many plots and then expertly intertwine them together to deliver a masterful ending.This book deals with a lot of psychology, especially that of multiple personality disorders. She expertly injects child abuse, familial responsibilities and bereavement and you feel overwhelmed with the level of emotions you feel. This book was the product of extensive medical research and for the time it was written in, one in which medical advancements were limited, it was pure genius.The story is extremely captivating. One, that pulls on your heart strings with the injustice to the parents and child but also one that keeps you on the edge of your seat. You experience many emotions when reading a Clark book and that to me is what separates the great books from the mediocre ones.Mrs. Clark is one of the few blessed writers who can absolutely trap her readers in the world of suspense and intrigue she spins in her mystery books.This was a riveting story of the after effects of childhood trauma and abuse. I’m bringing the classics back, are you?Zubs
