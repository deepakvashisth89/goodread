A Wolf and Little Daughter is an african american folktale which took place in Little Daughters house, and in the other side of the gate (the forest). In this folktale there is a big problem. Little Daughters father didn’t want Little Daughter to run in the forest, where there were wolves (Pg.157, line 3). Little Daughter, after her father left, saw a yellow flower on the other side of the gate and decided to go and pick it. She kept seeing more and more flowers, and went farther into the forest. As she was getting farther from her house, she heard a noise: "Pit-a-pat,pit-a-pat,pit-a-pat" (Pg 157, line 20). There was a wolf! She was frightened and started to go back to her house, but the wolf was walking right behind her. The wolf kept getting closer and closer to her while she was singing and getting closer to the gate, until she slip in the gate and was once again safe!

This is a folktale because it contains the four elements that are required to be a folktale. This is a story passed from generation to generation. For example in page 157, right below the title, it says that it is an African American foltale retold by Virginia Hamilton. This folktale is based on an African American culture. The whole story is written like an African American person would have told it, for example: "But she hears big and heavy, PIT-A-PAT, PIT-A-PAT, comin behind her." Coming is spelled without the g. In this folktale the words are spelled the way they are pronounced. It also shows their beliefs and traditions,"They sang- tray bla, tray bla, cum qua, kimo" (Pg 157, line 24). This folktale is full of supernatrual features: "Did you move?" The wolf said (Pg 158, line 28). "A wolf and Little Daughter" teaches you a lesson that will help everyone throughout there lives. It teaches you to never take risks and to obey your parents. Overall it is a very good folktale!
