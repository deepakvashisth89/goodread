4.5 Stars for PUBLISH AND PERISH, #1 in Sally Wright’s character-driven Ben Reese Mystery series published 1997. This was my 3rd read of this 6 book series, one I’ve enjoyed over the years with it’s interesting historical and Inspirational perspective. This is a book which causes me to stop and consider ~ while I read. It’s a Mystery, yet more.MY RATING GUIDE:1= dnf/What was that?; 2= Nope, not for me; 3= This was okay/cute; 3.5= I enjoyed it; 4= I LIKED IT A LOT; 5= I Loved it, it was great!! (I seldom give 5 Stars).This is a book series that I will read again and again. The main character is a reserved, thoughtful, intelligent and very capable man whose backstory unveils over the course of the series. I Highly Recommend PUBLISH AND PERISH to readers of Historical Mysteries (particularly WWII period), character driven novels, those who enjoy intelligent mysteries and readers of various genres (as am I). Note that PUBLISH AND PERISH is not an action-packed type adventure (which I also enjoy on occasion). PUBLISH AND PERISH can be read as a standalone title but I believe reading the 6book series in order enriches the overall experience. PUBLISH AND PERISH is book #1.Flashbacks ~ WWIIBen Reese/MMC, 19yrs, enlists and serves as a WWII Army Calvary Scout. His missions in military intelligence and in reconnaissance take him into Normandy, the Battle of the Bulge, among the first to discover the Malme’dy Massacre, always deep into enemy territory. A large caliber weapon finally takes him down during a mission in Trier. After many hours and several modes of transportation, Ben’s unconscious body arrives in Paris for surgery, very nearly dead. No one expects him to survive.Present Day ~ Autumn 1960Ben Reese/MMC, 36, and a healing widower, by night relives frequent nightmares and flashbacks of the war and of his much beloved wife. By day, he is a History professor and archivist at a small, private Ohio college. Ben is tall and rangy built, intelligent, observant, both a solitary and a reserved man with a few close friends.Ben has been in England for 3short weeks on a much anticipated sabbatical research trip when he receives a telegram advising him that his good friend and professor colleague has suddenly died of a heart attack. Ben quickly makes plans to return home while considering the last unfinished words his friend spoke to him. Could those mysterious words and half expressed thoughts relate to his friend’s sudden death? How?What I Liked ~1) PUBLISH AND PERISH is very well written. The characters are well described. I found myself responding to them as if they were real people - whether distancing myself, distrusting, respecting, or feeling sorry for them.2) The story kept my interest, the weaving of Ben’s back story into the present. I kept flipping the pages to see what would happen next.3) Ben, the main character, is a man I would love to know. He seems to be a man of integrity yet without judgement or a sanctimonious attitude. He’s a thinker with a depth within him - ideas, questions, thoughts.4) It is Ben’ perspective in this series that makes it work for me. I need to like and respect the main characters as well as the writing, itself, for me to enjoy the book. In PUBLISH AND PERISH, I find both.5) Wright interweaves questions about integrity, humanity, destiny and purpose in the final pages. Each time I read this series, I am challenged to not only enjoy the mystery but to ponder more.6) My husband had an uncle who served in the same battles and arenas as the Ben Reese character, behind enemy lines. He was a quiet, gentle, reserved, intelligent, loving and humble family man. He never talked about his time in the military. I often think about him as I read this series. And wonder.What I Liked Less ~Nothing.Other Comments -1) WATCHES also provides a sampling of the type of projects and consignments an esteemed archivist who is also an artifact expert, restorer might accept. I found this interesting.2) Another interesting fact is that Ms Wright created the Ben Reese character after a real person who actually served as an Army Scout Ranger during WWII and was present in the battles mentioned in this series. As civilians, the debt we owe these men and women is immeasurable. (Author notes in closing comments).Quotes ~> “The gratifications of food are so immediate; that’s the crux of the problem. Slimness, on the other hand, has to be planned for and worked at like an army maneuver.”READER CAUTIONS - IMO, this series is suitable for readers 17+, those who enjoys history, mysteries, thrillers and thought provoking fiction.VIOLENCE - PUBLISH AND PERISH is a historical mystery/thriller, so people die (off scene); deaths are not described in a graphic or dark manner.PROFANITY - None.SEXUAL SITUATIONS - None.
