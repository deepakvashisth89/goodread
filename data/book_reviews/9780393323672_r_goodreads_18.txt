This is radical even for Highsmith. I don't think I have ever been so terrified reading a book. Ripley is scary, yes but he is also masterful and there's a method to his madness. Not so with David Kelsey, who is not in control of himself and who consequently does the most insane things and makes the most unbelievable blunders. For half of the book my mouth was agape at the self-incriminating things he does and his fearlessness because of his massive entitlement and ego. Being in the mind of an entitled narcissistic sociopath who is not getting what he wants is a truly frightening thing. It's also frightening because we have all known people like this (or know them now) and it increases the fear of these types of people a hundred-fold.
