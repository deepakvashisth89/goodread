Well, I do not often tend to rate books (and in particular novels) with five stars, but honestly, Dorothy Canfield Fisher's 1916 middle grade novel Understood Betsy has truly been a totally wonderful (delightfully ahead of its time) and spectacular reading experience, and indeed the ONLY reason why I am just a wee bit annoyed and frustrated is that I really do wish I had encountered Understood Betsy when I was a young reader, as yes, first reading Understood Betsy in 2023, I certainly do feel like I kind of missed out not knowing abut Understood Betsy when I was the intended age group, when I was from around nine to thirteen years of age.

And I really majorly adore absolutely everything, everything about and regarding Canfield Fisher's featured text, her themes and contents, how Elizabeth Ann's, how Betsy's ridiculously sheltered, fussed over, mollycoddled and dependent life with her Great-Aunt Harriet and Harriet's daughter Frances is shown by Canfield Fisher as totally changing for the better, totally turning everything upside down, inside out and for Betsy to become healthy and independent after she is left in the care of her down-to-earth and no nonsense Putney cousins, with the characters, the descriptions, the structure, and the messages shown and presented in and how the story is on the one hand delightfully episodic but how Dorothy Canfield Fisher also builds each of her chapters on the one before it to show and to demonstrate young Betsy’s systematic and continuous growth from weak, dependent, and overprotected, to strong, confident, and self-reliant, and also not anymore so self absorbed and self-centred but working for, caring for and understanding others (how Betsy helps Molly, little Lias and last but not least her aunt Frances, when she realises that NO ONE, not even Frances wants Betsy to return to the status quo of living with a newly married Frances but to stay with the Putneys in Vermont, leaving a delightful ending for , and also kind of leaving me wishing that there were a sequel to , that there were further stories out there about Betsy, Molly, Lias et al).

Now Dorothy Canfield Fisher's was known as an educational reformer and her friendship with Maria Montessori led to the adoption of the latter's educational philosophies. And indeed, the all encompassing celebration of a Montessori style of education in (both at school and also in the home), this really makes me happily ecstatic. But indeed and no, I personally do not at all (and will never) consider as being in any manner overly and exaggeratedly heavy handed Montessori education style propaganda, but basically and beautifully the promotion of a progressive, children learning at their own paces educational Weltanschauung that is in my opinion as fresh, as current and also as necessary and as important today as it was in 1916, and that for a novel that is over one hundred years old, both and Dorothy Canfield Fisher as an author are wonderful, delightful and avant gardedly forward thinking (and that is a novel, is a book I totally and very highly recommend to and for everyone).

And yes, I am placing on my favourites shelf, and have to say that I have enjoyed Dorothy Canfield Fisher's writing for (and also for her adult novel ) as much as I have enjoyed and continue to enjoy Louisa May Alcott, Lucy Maud Montgomery and Frances Hodgson Burnett, and for me, that is saying a lot.
