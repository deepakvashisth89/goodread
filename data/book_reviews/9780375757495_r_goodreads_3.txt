George Eliot evokes rural England, with its peaceful, good-natured manners, eroded by the Industrial Revolution's furnaces. His prose is catchy, and the narrative mechanics are well-honed. She brings her colorful, straightforward characters to life with benevolence and discreet irony. This classic of Victorian literature, with its emotional charge and literary value, assumes the aspect of a beautiful parable due to its timelessness and universality.
