Pope has written the best poem in the English language "Essay on Man." Sorry Shakespeare, Pope's got you beat. Though Pope wrote no plays, unlike Shakespeare who made his name writing plays. And not all his poems are equally good, but the subject matter of Essay on Man, cosmology and philosophy, makes this a big poem in size and scope. There is a lot of wisdom there. This was the philosopher poet, more any other I have come across. Though I can't say that I am well read in poetry, but you know it when you see it. You don't need to be well read to know when something cannot be outdone.
That being said Pope has his defects. He has not aged well. It can get boring. It's hard to apprehend. The language is too antiquated and doesn't seem to relate to us today. Much of the poetry is stuff you have to force yourself to read; sincerely, it feels like work, not like reading for pleasure. Some poems were unreadable. The Rape of the Lock and the Dunciad didn't appeal to me. So I'm not about to put in all this work to apprehend antiquated language, in difficult poetic form, to get an ounce of pleasure. The cost benefit ratio to Pope's poetry is sometimes too low to be worth it. This is true for a lot of old poetry, and even of Shakespeare's plays. Maybe this is why old work loses popularity.
