No matter what I read from Steinbeck he has a way of imbuing his characters with such humanity and delivers such wisdom in his prose.

Despite this book being, at times, a bit more melodramatic and maybe less 'serious' than some of his heavy-hitters, it still captures a very specific and relatable set of experiences through the eyes of a bunch of unique characters.

This book essentially follows a collection of people who are all on a bus together and frustrated with the delay in travel--some for vacation to Mexico, some to Los Angeles for work, and others leaving their homes behind to seek a new life elsewhere. But before they get there, they're forced into a pitstop at a small garage/cafe in central California as they wait overnight for the bus to be repaired. Between conversations amongst the characters and their inner monologues which Steinbeck renders so effortlessly, moving from person to person in a nearly stream-of-consciousness style, we get to learn about their various walks of life and consider the same scenario from multiple POVs.

While it's not one of Steinbeck's most popular novels these days, it's still a compelling and enjoyable read that has his signature nature writing of the California hill country and memorable characters who all want the same thing: to build a better life that let's them be their true, authentic selves with dignity.
