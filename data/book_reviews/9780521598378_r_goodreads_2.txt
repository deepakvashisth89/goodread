This is sort of a textbook-style book but written for a non-scientist to understand. The author covers several interesting topics related to the search for life elsewhere in the universe. A lot of the first section of the book talks about life as we know it on this planet and what all we know about how our solar system formed, how Earth formed, and how life formed on Earth. The author discusses plate tectonics, fossils, and other clues we have discovered on Earth that tell us what happened in prehistory. Moving on from that he spends a lot of time talking about space explorations and what astronomers have found out by sending satellites and rovers into space. It's pretty amazing that we have actual photos of the surface of Mars and the surface of Titan, Saturn's biggest moon. It's like a sci-fi movie but sci-nonfi!This book was published in 1998 so I did some follow-up reading on the Internet to read updates on the space exploration discoveries. It's kind of neat to read the hope in between the lines in the book talking about the Cassini-Huygens mission and then to read online that so much data was collected and surprising discoveries were made.Reading books about our planet and the universe always brings to mind intriguing questions and there is the constant cycle of: the more you learn, the more questions you have!Show more
