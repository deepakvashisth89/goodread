Greene knew love. Her name was Vivian who gave "the trees shade, the flowers scent and the sun a gold it never had before." On their wedding day he wrote, "all the water in the world will be turned into wine." What he didn't count on after was the hangover.The Heart of the Matter is painful as one. Yet, I felt no regret for reading it again. The first time I did, I was very young and newly married. I thought it was about an affair. Today, I turn the pages and see a marriage, born of love, still full of passion, yet broken beyond repair.What inspiration Greene's own marriage gave to this novel matters less than the steadiness of his hand as he wrote it. I'm reminded with its tropical setting of my own experience trying to write a letter one summer in Japan. The ink spread in the heat on the paper. Words filled like eyes with tears.Only read The Heart of the Matter if you're feeling strong.
