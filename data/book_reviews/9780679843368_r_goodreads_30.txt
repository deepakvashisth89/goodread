“A Wolf and Little Daughter” is a folktale about a little girl that lives in a house with her papa, and around the house there is a dangerous forest, but fortunately there is a gate. One day, Papa had to go away for something. Little daughter was picking some flowers close to the gate and in the other side she saw a special one, so she thought of crossing the gate to pick it, so she did. Then little daughter started to pick the row of yellow flowers that was leading her farther from the gate and closer to danger. Little daughter when being far enough while singing a sweet song, looked up and saw a big wolf. Then the wolf told her not to move, but to sing the sweetest goodest song again and again. Little daughter was scared. She looked to the wolf and he was gone, but when she moved, the wolf came back again and again, and little daughter saw the gate, yet she wasn’t inside. When the wolf was running behind her, she finally closed the gate and stayed safe at home.

“A Wolf and Little Daughter,” is known as a folktale, (“Author Study”) because it has no author, and it is passed from generation to generation. It is also from an African American culture, (“Author Study”) and it has told a lesson: “She is sweetest goodest safe.” Meaning never get close to danger (pg. 156, paragraph 60, line 3). Also supernatural powers are included, because the wolf can talk: “Sing the sweetest, goodest song again…” (Pg. 158, paragraph 50). In this story many lessons are discovered (For example: You should always follow directions pg.157, paragraph 9). I highly recommend this story to someone who loves folktales.
