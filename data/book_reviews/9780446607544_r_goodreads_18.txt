Morrell pulls off a stunning finish—with a WILD twist—for his Mortalis trilogy, which has had its ups-and-downs:The Brotherhood of the Roseis one of my favorite spy novels of ALL TIME (shocks me too tbh), whereasThe Fraternity of the Stonewas so forgettable that I couldn't recall a single plot event beyond the opening (which would've come in handy for TLONAF). But, this book is the redemption arc come full-swing, full-throttle. The historical context that Morrell provides, unflinching and steeped in horror, adds a new dimension of raw reckoning not for the individual (like its two predecessors) but the collective, instead. The twist (the term has never been truer, 180 degrees later) hits like a gut-punch and completely took me by surprise (though in hindsight remembering more from TFOTS could've lent a few clues), while also playing on more meta themes of complicity and cover. Beyond the structure, though, Morrell does what he's best at in this book: a globetrotting, fast-paced chase between an ensemble cast of assassins. I will say that this book does lag in terms of the Drew passages (to be fair, he is an inert character by monastic nature), and the contrast between the PHENOMENAL Saul/Erika sections versus Drew/Arlene feels almost unfair, given that Drew is meant to be a weightier protagonist (and Chris stand-in, even). Icicle's storyline (which was honestly more interesting than Drew's) also felt incomplete, but in another world, as Morrell explains, there would've been more Abelard novels (still have no idea why this trilogy is titled Mortalis when the whole universe rests on, is defined by the Abelard Sanction). On that note, "The Abelard Sanction" short story included at the end is worth a read and just absolutely devastating. This trilogy will always hold such a special place in my heart.
