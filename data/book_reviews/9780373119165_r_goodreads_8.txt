This story had its good points, but some pretty bad ones, too. The whole "Who's my Daddy?" thing was pretty annoying, and the man's sudden metamorphosis from sinner to saint just a tad ridiculous!As for our H and h: both were a bit dense (make that a lot) when it came to marriage. Leo married a fashion model, was proud of that fact, and didn't ask Ginny to stop working, despite his being a wealthy man. Then, he does an about face and resents her work, her time away from home, etc. He knows she was a virgin before him, and that she never took assignments where she'd have to wear too revealing clothes or act provocative, and never flirted with other men, yet he chose to believe all the garbage the tabloids said about her, the old "where there's smoke there's fire" trope, and took her decision to sue for libel as an admission of guilt, rather than standing up for the truth, and her losing the case clinched it for him! He wanted a divorce!On Ginny's part, while I can understand her not liking having to live in Leo's isolated family home (where she stayed when not working, even when he was away on frequent business trips), she could have tried a bit harder to adjust, instead of setting her mind against it so much. Also, she knows it bothered her husband that she took so many assignments where she'd have to be away for a while, yet she kept on taking them, as if his opinion didn't matter. Part of her wanting to be a success so much had to do with a childhood full of insecurities (she was the nerd to end all nerds), and also her belief that she wasn't good enough to be a part of Leo's family, but instead of sharing that with him so he'd understand her better, she kept it to herself.Ironically, she makes a last-ditch effort to save her marriage by being seductive and passionate (something she had trouble with before, afraid of letting herself go), but that only makes Leo suspect that the rumors about her playing around were true (some other guy must have taught her those sexy moves, because she never acted that way with him before) and her efforts backfired badly.Even more ironic, was Ginny doing something so out of character for a h: she was willing to fight for her man, even when she thought he cheated!! (Usually, they run off in angry tears, even when the guy's not guilty.) Leo, however, had deliberately made her think he was cheating so she'd leave, and then told her in no uncertain terms to go!He wanted nothing more to do with her...until he did!There are more ironies, like when Leo, who had refused to give Ginny any of the financial help she needed to pay her legal debts (which had led to her taking more modeling assignments) while they were married, suddenly offered to pay them when they were divorced! Also: Ginny's later willingness to compromise and take less long-term modeling assignments that required travel should have been something she did when they first got married, which would have saved a lot of trouble.Leo, on his part, should have told her the truth he had discovered about her identity from the start, instead of playing games to "protect" her, when all he did was make things worse.But what was worst of all, is that it appears these two are related, and shouldn't be married at all! The author never cleared that up, which leaves this book with an ICK!!! factor!No great loss if you skip this one.
