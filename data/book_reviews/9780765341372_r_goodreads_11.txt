Man and Wife was riveting to the end; to the point where I couldn't put it down for fear of missing what happened next. It is a very good evening thriller - digestable, easy-to-read, and well-thought. The characters were well thought and written - notably, the wife was permitted to be womanly without being demeaning to her sex. In a literary landscape where women are often men with alternate pronouns, or excluded from major roles entirely, her portrayal was refreshing. Of course, Klavan aficionados working backwards will note that the plot here was
stolen for his more recent When Christmas Comes novella, though I feel that it was put to much greater effect here than there. . That aside, it was refreshing and gripping, with a twist that felt at home. Don't expect a fresh plot - I've read enough Klavan that by the end of the first quarter, I knew (mostly) how the story was going to end, though there were still a few extra surprises thrown in here and there. Overall, the story was impressive by itself, and makes for a great fireside or beach reader - you won't want to put it down until the very end.

I also give Klavan credit here - he tied the story up very neatly, showing the reader not only how the story ended but also where the dust fell for all involved players - something frequently neglected in these sorts of novels, but much appreciated by readers like me.
