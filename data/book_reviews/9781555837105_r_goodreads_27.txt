The third in the series, with the most complex mystery so far. It's a good one, convoluted and with perhaps a few too many converging suspects to easily keep straight, but well-plotted. Once again it touches on the local gay community, in its resolution. It's an interesting look at the pre-AIDS LA-area scene.I love the character Hansen has created in Dave, and I wish there'd been a little more of the personal here. Conflict is clearly brewing between Dave, and Doug whom he lives with, but this story skates over the surface of a coming storm. And as usual, the book ends with that personal thread dangling.Hansen has a distinctive, slightly dry but clear, writing style, that matches the cool, laconic nature of his character. I really enjoy his descriptive turns of phrase, and vivid similes and metaphors that color but do not clutter the book. I've been binge reading this series, so I won't leave a detailed review. But I enjoyed them all, and was pulled along to the end, watching Dave age, and change, and grow, and yet remain essentially the same at heart. Not an emotional series, but by the end, I had fallen hard for Dave Brandstetter.
