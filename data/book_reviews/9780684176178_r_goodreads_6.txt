I can’t believe I haven’t read this book until now! More thoughts coming when I recuperate.I’m not sure what planet I’ve been living on to have made it 52 years of life without ever having read this Pulitzer Prize-winning novel (1939). The Yearling is a novel that will transport you to a time and place that does not exist anymore. Thinking about that is quite emotional, especially if you know what a concrete slab the state of Florida has become, it’s sad to think about the environment that used to be prevalent. It was a time of hunting for your dinner, fending off wildlife like bears, panthers, wolves, and a place where remedies for ailments were found from the fauna. It was a very different Florida.We meet Jody from the first page and naturally fall in love with his exuberance and love for nature. He is an only child of parents, Penny and Ora Baxter, who buried many others before him. Jody’s father is a man who takes his time with Jody’s innate curiosity and is patient with Jody for wanting to stay an adolescent and have no cares in the world. He’s a father but he’s also a friend. Jody’s mother is the opposite. She has no patience for Jody’s desire to wander about and enjoy life. She would rather Jody put all of the things of childhood away and focus on the grown-up things that a farm needs accomplishing. She appears at times to have less love for him than his father does. She certainly shows it differently than her husband does.Jody so desperately wants to raise a baby animal for his own. However, his mother cannot allow a wild creature into their home. Jody’s opportunity arises to take in a baby fawn and his father helps to convince Ma to allow it. Jody’s bond with this creature is amazing and endearing. Some find it sentimental, and it may be, however, I enjoyed Jody’s devotion. Jody becomes a sibling to Flag in a way.I won’t go into any details of the story because I fear ruining this delightful yet tearful story for anyone who still wants to read this. It is so much more than a coming-of-age story. It’s about loyalty, family, responsibility. It is about making that leap from childhood to manhood and realizing life is not always about fun and games. Adulthood brings a load of challenges and sometimes they can be harsh.Rawlings’ prose is exquisite even the dialect is beautiful to read. You read her descriptions of the landscape and can easily visualize its beauty and ruggedness.In a late afternoon toward the end of August, Jody went with the fawn to the sink-hole for fresh water for supper. The road was bright with flowers. The sumac was in bloom, and the colic root sent up tall stalks of white or orange orchid-like flowers. The French mulberries were beginning to ripen on slim stems. They were lavender in color, close-clustered, like snails’ eggs along lily stalks. Butterflies sat on the first purple buds of the fragrant deer-tongue, opening and closing their wings slowly, as though waiting for the buds to open and the nectar to be revealed.
