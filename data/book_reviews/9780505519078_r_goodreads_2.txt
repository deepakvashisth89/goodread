Phoebe Hampton stumbles out of the Pacific Northwest roadshow ofDeliveranceinto Harlan Garand's weekend fishing cabin. She's hungry, tired, and needs to report a crime to the police. He thinks she's been sent by his Aunt Flo (*snerk*) to crash his fishing weekend and "compromise" him into marriage...because Aunt Flo reads romance novels and thinks it's 1853 or something. (And that's the theme JAK is going to beat to death in this book. The hero isnota stereotypical romance hero, he's a real man. Apparently, in 1980 real men were douchebags who didn't believe women who claimed they had been kidnapped and assaulted. Sadly, that's kind of true.)Phoebe manipulates Harlan into letting her stay at the cabin until he's ready to drive back to civilization. In exchange, she has to do all the chores, including *shudder* cleaning the fish he catches. In between his unnecessary displays of alpha male power, the pair banter about the fact that they're not each other's type: he dates willowy blondes (Phoebe is a well-rounded brunette), while she's contemplating a marriage proposal from a tall, dark, and handsome business executive with a sterling family background (a Disney prince personified, while the redheaded doucheb--*ahem*--hero won't help a lady in distress).[One of the things that makes JAK's backlist so compelling for me is the repeated use of the same themes, scenarios, and tropes. "Not [his/her] type" is one of those themes. It's an annoying pebble in the otherwise brilliantFamily Man, and used most effectively inSerpent In Paradise, where the heroine is in fact exactly the hero's type, but he thinks he can't have his type because of tragic backstory reasons.]The following day, one of Phoebe's creepy captors shows up at the cabin looking for her, and Harlan is chagrined he didn't believe her in the first place. He trusses up the wannabe rapist and drives everyone down to the police at last. This demonstrates that he is, in fact, awesomely heroic when the situation is blatantly obvious and doesn't require any subtle nuances of trust.Safely back in civilization, Phoebe tries to get rid of Harlan because she's not his type and her Disney prince is dropping by her apartment that evening to receive a (negative) response to his marriage proposal. By now, Harlan has decided he wants Phoebe, so he's not leaving until the prince has received his congé. Which means he's there for the humiliating reveal that the Disney prince may have proposed because Phoebe's brother is some kind of wealthy genius inventor and the prince wanted the business connection. She then kicks them both out of her apartment, but the hero comes back the next day and proposes with one of those "I am a 35-year-old successful CEO with a family business and I need an heir" proposals that NEVER sweep a woman off her feet.Phoebe is concerned that he'll just see her as a challenge if she says no and afraid she'll surrender completely if he seduces her, so she manipulates Harlan into agreeing to a three week (presumably sexless) courtship. (Remember when she manipulated him into letting her stay at the cabin? Manipulating is Phoebe's thing, but I read it as her being clever enough to successfully negotiate against the patriarchy. '80s-era feminism at its finest.)During courtship date #1, they run into the rejected Disney prince's sister (who somehow manages to be a fragile blonde, even though her brother is tall, dark, and handsome), and Harlan embarrasses Phoebe by suggesting to the catty blonde that he is marrying Phoebe because he compromised her. Returning from their date, Phoebe is alarmed to discover her bedroom window is wide open. Oh, no! Wannabe rapist #2 is still on the loose and may be after her.Harlan decides to take Phoebe to Aunt Flo's (*snerk*) for safety, but first they need to drop by his house to pick up his dog and some clothes, since he'll be standing guard. When they get there, a gorgeous blonde OW is waiting in his living room, dropping little OW nuggets like "I let myself in with the key you keep hidden outside" and "You know I often spend the night here."Harlan efficiently dispatches the OW and returns to tell Phoebe that "she and I are through. You're not to concern yourself with her, Phoebe. Is that understood?" To which Phoebe gently replies, "You have lipstick on your cheek, Harlan." We never get an explanation or timeline for this relationship, which is just a bit icky. They had a fight and broke up before Harlan met Phoebe, but when? Was it on the Friday immediately before the fishing weekend?Now we're off to Aunt Flo's stately home, where Flo and Phoebe bond over their love of romance novels. Phoebe defiantly (and correctly) points out that admiring domineering heroes in romance doesn't mean the reader wants one in real life, while Aunt Flo states that of course women want a domineering man because they make good providers. (I want to say Aunt Flo is channeling the 1950s, but I think people may still have thought in terms of "providers" rather than "partners" in 1980.) Also, Aunt Flo is planning an engagement party for them on Saturday.The next day, the OW calls Phoebe at her office to kindly explain that she and Harlan were on a break and Phoebe will probably just get hurt if she sticks around, to which Phoebe replies that if Harlan had wanted to marry the OW, they would already be married, so buzz off.The OW buzzes straight to the engagement party, where Phoebe finds her chatting up Harlan alone in the garden. After some verbal sparring, Phoebe pushes the OW into the decorative fish pond. (She hadn't even lost the argument! She's just being retro-feisty. It's so cringeworthy.) Harlan gets angry at Phoebe for behaving like an adolescent, so Phoebe packs her bags and sneaks out into the night, but Harlan catches up with her in his Jag and takes her back to his home where they finally have sex and declare their mutual love.But wait, we're not done. Remember the backstory where Phoebe was kidnapped and nearly raped in the hills? Now the other wannabe rapist shows up at Harlan's home with a gun. In a one page action sequence, Harlan and his trusty black Lab Jinx take down the intruder, but not before the guy coldcocks Phoebe with the butt of the gun. She wakes up in the hospital with Harlan yelling at the doctor, then suddenly it's their wedding day and they're packing Jinx into the car for their honeymoon...which I fear is going to be at the fishing cabin and Phoebe's going to have to clean more fish. The End.That last chapter really illustrates JAK's inexperience as a writer at this point in her career. She should have wrapped everything up with the criminals before the protagonists had sex and declared their mutual love, but she had left a dangling hillbilly rapist and needed to tie that up, so we ricochet from action sequence to hospital to wedding in a way that contributes nothing to the relationship or the novel's themes.Yet, oddly, though the hero's interactions with the heroine are terribly dated (during an early attempt at seduction, he actually says, "Relax. You'll enjoy it." *cringe*) and the dialogue is stilted in its attempt to be clever, I can detect the chemistry between her protagonists that will eventually make Krentz one of the most popular romance authors of the 1990s. In fact, that growing popularity put this book into my hands when it was reprinted in 1989. Otherwise,Whirlwind Courtshipwould have been lost to the mists, like JAK's MacFadden titles.JAK checklistPacific Northwest setting:OregonFamiliar professions:She's in corporate data management; he has inherited the family shipping firm.What's in a name?Too many ARs and ANs, that's what.
