Jonathan Lethem is one of my favorite writers, but like a lot of favorite artists, I've went through periods of hot and cold on his work. My early initial enthusiasm was undermined by "A Gambler's Anatomy," the first of his books published after I discovered his work, and yet "Brooklyn Crime Novel" (his most recent) was a wonderful surprise, dazzling and inventive. So returning to his work, exploring books I haven't read yet, can be a bit of a crap shoot.

"Girl in Landscape" is a hybrid sci-fi/Western that doesn't quite work, but enough elements are here for me to appreciate the effort Lethem went to. Pella Marsh, whose widowed father has suffered political defeat on Earth, is a new colonist on a planet where she and her fellow humans are few and far between. There is a native alien species, called "Archbuilders," who inhabit the place, and there is an uneasy cohabitation among the species, with some transformative properties available to humans who are open to it. But all is not well, because a violent loner has imposed himself on the small group of settlers and he has designs on the teenage Pella.

Early Lethem (pre-"Fortress of Solitude") is heavily invested in blurring the lines between genres, and "Girl in Landscape" is no exception. Efram, the male protagonist, is John Wayne straight out of "The Searchers" (and true Lethem fans will know how much impact that film has had on his work), while the body-hopping abilities available to Pella and others is straight out of Philip K. Dick (another substantial influence on Lethem). The narrative is a bit too clunky to work overall (it definitely feels like an early novel), but Lethem's talent is evident and I found it a page-turner once I fell into its rhythms. What makes it interesting is how it prefigures his later, better work. It's not a hidden gem, but it is a good showcase for what he would be capable or, when he found his way. "Girl in Landscape" is, overall, a minor Lethem work, but a good one in many ways.
