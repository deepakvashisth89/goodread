So what's with the one star? Didn't I know what I was getting into when I started reading this thing? Yes and no. I thought that I had skimmed through some of "Chariot of the Gods" before, and I seemed to recall it being more entertaining than this book was. Unfortunately, you have to wallow through the first half of the “Gods Were Astronauts” to get to the fun stuff. The first chapter is spent railing against the Old Testament, using others' centuries old arguments about the inconsistencies of the God it portrays, while occasionally throwing similar jibes at the Hindu traditions. Chapter 2 is spent spewing venom at the Catholic Church and its ‘lies’ about the secrets of the ‘Virgin of Fatima.’ During these attacks, which take up 100 pages, Daniken hardly mentions his central thesis, that the old gods were not divine but extraterrestrials, i.e. the title of the book. Apparently he assumes that everybody has read his previous works, and that he needn’t bother to make his case, or even attempt to outline his argument in the most lazy fashion. I was about to give up reading at this point, but finally, after a 100-page anti-religion rant full of tangents on astrophysics, he gets to the goods. He spends the rest of the book looking at amazing passages, mostly, from Hindu scriptures, that discuss fantastic weapons and vehicles (Daniken claims that they describe anti-gravity/space ships, atomic weapons, etc.). However, by this point I was so tired of his book that I merely skimmed through it. Maybe he should have dumped the 100 page rant and stuck with the good stuff. Or, better yet, he could’ve incorporated the Old Testament passages into his discussion of weapons/vehicles, etc. I guess I can’t complain about the guy’s scholarship too much, he is writing about flying saucers.Show more
