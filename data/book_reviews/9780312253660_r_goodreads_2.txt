This novel just plods along. I am about 100 pages into it, and I have found myself in a reading quagmire.You know the feeling - you are getting nothing from the book, you can only bear to read about 8 pages a day, but you don't want to stop because you are almost in the middle of it and you are afraid there might actually be something good in it somewhere.I bought the book from a Manhattan thrift store for a dollar and it just goes to show you how much suffering a dollar can still buy these days.From what I can tell, the author wants to focus on the extent to which we can be adversely affected by horrific stories from the news or history. The central character - Joe - seems to have been deeply affected by the image of a dead Russian girl from World War 2. So affected, he travels to Russia as it is falling apart in the late 80s. Huh? That's not a stretch? Did I miss something? Why is this guy in Russia? Everything seems a bit melodramatic and overwrought in this book.So Joe accidentally, or is it so accidental (I may never know since I will probably stop reading the book), meets a sexy Russian lady and her coarse friend. There are intimations of kinkiness looming and abounding and if this just turns out to be some high-brow soft-porn s/m novel I am going to want my dollar back.Not recommended unless you have joined Oprah's Reading Quagmire Club and want to experience time as something ultra-slow-moving, to make you feel lethargic.
