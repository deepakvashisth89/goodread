All of the characters in the book should have gotten together and bored each other to death like they did to me.

This story had potential, but Wallace Stegner was not the best author to tell this story.

At the center of this novel is Lyman Ward, a cranky old man, who while not whining about this life (and everything under the sun), he tells of his grandmother Susan Burling who lived in the 1870’s.

However, Stegner clearly just throws out vocab words hoping to sound smart but fails to understand their meaning.

Susan is supposed to be a Quaker and a transcendentalist. Unfortunately, Stegner reduces her to “hubba hubba I just need a rugged man who looks like I-Can’t-Believe-It’s-Not-Butter” and “Oh, I had no idea about this problem until two seconds ago but I have an extremely strong opinion on this, something that will tilt my entire life upside down, but I shan’t give it another thought!”

Ugh! Transcendentalism is a philosophy where the person is trying to live out a certain set of ideals. These ideals can be fundamentally described as “high thinking and simple living.” At one point, Susan says something like, “How can I show my face again with a dress that is 8 years old?!” This is not in keeping with transcendentalism. At all. It only makes Susan appear petty.

Not only that, but the storytelling is abhorrent. Grandma would travel from place to place, but Stegner never developed other characters so when it came time to move it didn’t make a difference. It wasn’t as though Grandma actually cared about the locals. No tugging on heart strings.

Angle of Repose is light on plot, and the characters are only memorably boring.

Someone else could take this and rewrite it better.
