I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills.

It's been quite a while since I have felt romantic about anything, but this memoir has reminded me of the spell that love can cast on a person's spirit.

I have felt moony for weeks, all aglow, but if I've got you wondering if I'm going to start posting photos of a young Robert Redford or whatnot, I must stop you there and inform you that this ISN'T a memoir about a woman and a man. . . it's a memoir about a woman and Kenya.

Isak Dinesen was born Karen Dinesen, but, honestly, she went by so many pen names and nicknames and titles, I am going to refer to her as Ms. Dinesen throughout this review.

Ms. Dinesen was born Karen Dinesen (Blixen was through marriage), in Denmark, and she had the great misfortune of falling for her cousin (Helpful Hint #1: Folks, when you're , other than a post-Apocalyptic deserted island with only one other person, ALWAYS choose a romantic partner who is NOT related to you).



She and her cousin were sent, as exiles, to Kenya, where they got married without any Danish witnesses and were enticed to start a coffee plantation, paid for by their shared family, in return for them promising not to send an annual Christmas card to relatives.

Her husband, who will now be referred to in this review as “Scuz,” rather than “Cuz,” had this awful affliction that is sometimes known as “he-can't-keep-his-dick-in-his-pants,” and it wasn't long before Scuz gave his cuz syphilis and then fled the plantation to see if he could spread his sexually transmitted diseases to other parts of the world. (Helpful Hint #2: According to my gynecologist friend and my eye doctor, cases of syphilis are again on the rise, and, unless a person takes a “high risk” test for sexually transmitted disease, they are NOT being tested for syphilis).

(Helpful Hint #3: For the love of all that is holy, do NOT Google, “What are the advanced symptoms of syphilis?” You will never unsee what pops up in the images).

Turns out syphilis isn't funny, AT ALL, and it's still making people go blind, so for goodness' sake, don't have sex with any of your unfaithful cousins or anyone who has provided photos of their infected genitalia for Wikipedia.

But, Ms. Dinesen already KNEW that syphilis wasn't funny. Her father contracted it here in the states when he had a midlife crisis and decided to have international sex with strangers. Apparently his untreated syphilis caused him to suffer a crippling depression that led to suicide and, potentially, young Karen's confused perspective on what makes for a good husband.

.

(And, in case it's unclear. . . NONE of this is part of the memoir).

Please, stop distracting me!! Let's get back to KENYA.



So, it's 1915 (or so), and Ms. Dinesen is basically a lone woman, with a large staff of locals, running a huge coffee plantation, outside of Nairobi, and: she fell in love with Kenya. It became her forever home, and quickly.

.



Ms. Dinesen was a woman of great contradictions: she and her peers “sport-hunted,” at times, but she was also a fierce, outspoken protector of wildlife. She was often photographed wearing ridiculous furs and strings of pearls in the Great Outdoors, but she was truly loved and respected by her local staff. She was a devoted Christian, but she was also a sensualist and a naturalist.

I learned more about Africa, from this 400 page memoir, than from any other book I've ever read, set on that continent.



Truly, I was quite spellbound by her writing and descriptions and I cried, spontaneously, throughout this read. I can't even imagine the unique circumstances in which a single white woman was able to live in that part of Africa for 17 years, at the beginning of the twentieth century.



I can attest to the power this memoir possesses. It is easy to see how someone wanted to convert it to a film, but I would like to point out, for the record, that the famous movie with Robert Redford and Meryl Streep is considered only “loosely based” on this memoir, published in 1937, and Dinesen's memoir from 1960, SHADOWS ON THE GRASS.
