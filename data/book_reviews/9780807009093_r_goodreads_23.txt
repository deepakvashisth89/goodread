Plum Bun, by Jessie Redmon Faust is an exquisitely painful story that details the travails of African Americans who try to make their way in America by literally "crossing the line", and becoming someone else. Angela Murray, older fair skinned daughter of Junius and Mattie Murray does not want to be shut out of the opportunities that white people are able to benefit from with no effort. Her mother is also very fair and the two of them regularly go about their community in Philadelphia and "pass" enjoying shopping and excursions to tea rooms and other places that they would not be welcomed if it was known that they are Negro. Her father is black, and her sister Virginia are brown skinned which means the family members essentially live in different worlds. Angela moves from Philadelphia to New York City separating herself from her sister after the death of their parents. In New York she enters a glittering and yet treacherous world and learns many difficult lessons as she moves towards self acceptance at last becoming "at home" with herself. A gripping reading experience, harrowing, heartbreaking and at last healing. Recommended.
