John Steinbeck's first novel is a really good story about the imagined life of the welsh pirate Henry Morgan. History has decreed him to be a buccaneer or privateer who pirated with the approval of the English as long as he just attacked Spanish ships and the Spanish Main, the northern coast of South America including Maracaibo and Panama City.

I did laugh at the very first line which contained the description 'black Welsh glens'. No glens in Wales other than people with the first of Glen.

The Cup of Gold is Panama City and Henry Morgan leads his men across the isthmus for nine days in order to surprise the Spanish defenders of the city. However, the Spanish commander of the cavalry is expecting them and orders them to attack, but they ride into a marsh and take no further part in the defence. You would have thought the Spanish would have known about the marsh, but apparently not.

Morgan's main reason for attacking Panama City is to meet a woman he calls the Red Saint. He builds her up in his own mind into the total object of his desires and the most beautiful woman in the world. When he meets her, he is disappointed, but takes it out on his trusty sidekick and an English dwarf rather than coping with his own feelings like a mature adult.

Morgan was a poacher turned gamekeeper who became the governor of Jamaica and passed down sentences on people who'd been inspired by Morgan to take up his previous profession.
