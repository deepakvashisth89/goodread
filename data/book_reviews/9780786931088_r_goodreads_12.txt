I find it difficult to write a review on this book.I am not a fan of Scott Ciencin's way of writing BUT I am a fan of the story.Starting to play d&d you realize that you have to know about the story and deepen in some of the details. Time of Troubles was a cataclysmic time period in the chronology of Faerûn.It is an epic story, and a lot is going on, but it all goes a little bit too quickly for my taste.I do not like that Cyric just turns evil. Ok, he wants the power the Tablets of Faith can offer him. And I cannot believe how gullible people are when he tries to persuade them. I guess in turns of rolls they are all just critical successes, but, come on, even Bane falls for it.Kelemvor is just bad tempered. He is always somehow betrayed and then finds out that he wasn't betrayed at all. He finally gets rid of his curse (I do not want to go into detail about that because it is just uninspiring).Midnight is just gullible, she finds the best in people and hopes for the best (come on... Kelemvor just walked away form Bane with some scratches???)Adon... Adon has lost his faith in Sune because he has a scratch on his face. Ok it is bad for a priest of Sune to be "disformed" in some way, and you could say that he is in shock... I give him that.Oh! And you have the return of Elminster, who is just the greatest Mage there is and who you thought was dead. And he returns just in time to save the day.But I must say, I thought the battle of Bane versus Trom somehow epic!With that said, I am curious to see how the trilogy ends in Waterdeep.
