Beautiful, philosophically profound, and moving, Chaucer’s Troilus and Criseyde is one of the high points of medieval literature. The seminal example of medieval courtly love poetry, combined with epic style, and rich theological exploration in imitation of Boethius’ Consolation. A fascinating blend of Greco-Roman and medieval style and ideals, at once a tragedy and a comedy, both pagan and Christian.

I think it ought to be read in Chaucer’s original Middle English, and the Broadview Press edition makes it very feasible, with a keen gloss and generous footnotes. The introduction and appendices are a rich trove of insight into Chaucer’s context and sources. The best scholarly edition around.
