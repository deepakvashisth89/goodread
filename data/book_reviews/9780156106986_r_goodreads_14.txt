I originally bought this book without doing any research on it, picking it up only because I found it on a reading list for prospective converts. I was somewhat thrown off by the language at first and thought it was pretentious until I realized it was published in 1947. Because of this, some of the discourse, as well as the language, feels a little antiquated.That doesn't mean it doesn't have any merit, though! This doesn't feel like a typical basic judaism book and instead focuses primarily on the philosophy behind the religion/culture/practice, rather than on the religion/culture/practice itself. It doesn't linger on the Orthodox/Conservative/Reform trichotomy and just discusses the differences between traditional practice and more modern practice, regardless of your sect.It has a few good nuggets and is an interesting, fast read, but I wouldn't suggest this for people who are brand new to jewish concepts. It's not going to introduce you to prayers, rituals, explicit moral principles, anything concrete.
