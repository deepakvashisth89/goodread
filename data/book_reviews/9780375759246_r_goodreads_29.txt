Kama Sutra is not only a book about pleasure. Yes, they explained almost every inch of attitude regarding sexuality, profoundly over detailed sometimes, but it also holds the philosophical fundamental practice of Kama. Talking about the perseverance of principles in Dharma, Artha, and Kama without becoming the slave of passions and desires. It’s explained in terms of which kind of attitude we have to take relating to the situation. The 'how-to' approach: so we know what’s been told to be the best and the worst thing to do based on this book of Vatsyayana.

Back in the day, a long time ago, Kama Sutra lay on the mattress inside my house. So easy to grab, joyful to read, thus this book was my first introduction to one form of sexuality. And since then I've always been interested in the beauty of human unity. However, I was too young to learn something so vigorously vital without the presence of wisdom. It was so heavy on sensuality, and so my knowledge betrayed me; the pleasures rained on me every time. I escaped the world to the gate of heaven.

Despite all of my imperfections in my understanding of love and affection, wisdom slowly caught me, even after I ran hundreds of miles away from purity. What it meant to be defiant, slowly changed to finding its honesty in my experiences. There were times I treated pleasure as an equal enemy of me, while it’s always there in a heart of me; I abused my own. Say about anything, but I'm still a man of pleasures, an explorer, and an artist. This book was my milestone, my magician that turned me into a successful lover: a man of Moksha.
