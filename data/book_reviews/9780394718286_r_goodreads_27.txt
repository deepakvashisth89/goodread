Red Harvestfollows the misadventures of the Continental Op, a tough-minded gumshoe working for the Continental Detective Agency. A stocky, two-fisted man with no scruples, he’s one of the least likable fictional detectives - and one of the most compelling. In this saga, the Op arrives in the mining town of Poisonville, overrun by warring gangs, corrupt police and ruthless businessmen. The Op manipulates them all on a one-man crusade to clean up the town, with no shortage of fights, murders and double-crosses. Easily Hammett’s angriest book, fictionalizing his personal experiences with the Pinkertons into a savage indictment of capitalism; the gangsters are largely ex-strikebreakers who, finding murder profitable, decided to make it a profession, while the local tycoon rages about his inability to control the forces he’s unleashed. This story famously inspired Yojimbo, A Fistful of Dollars and myriad other films which borrowed Hammett’s “two sides against the middle” conceit and weary cynicism, though none go as far in their takedown of social corruption. Fast-moving, episodic and violent, it’s a brisk and entertaining read whose power remains undiminished.
