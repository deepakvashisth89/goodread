My rock and roll bible. In the early 80s, I devoured the wit and wisdom in this compilation of rock music knowledge. While it's a great historical document of rock and roll history, it's also a product of its time--this came out right at the end of 70s punk rock, but before post punk was really codified.There's a definite punk slant, and it also suffers from some of Dave Marsh's well known biases--he's always hated big rock heroes like Led Zeppelin and Queen and Kiss, and he looks down his nose at most prog rock bands like Yes and Rush and Pink Floyd.On the other hand, Marsh has a genuine love for rock and roll roots artists, blues and R&B artists who were its founding fathers, and he has his own heroes in Jimi Hendrix, the Beatles, Pete Townshend, Elvis Presley, and Chrissie Hynde, to name a few.Such a document could probably not be produced today, because the industry is not the cultural phenomenon it once was.Marsh's juvenile biases keep this from being a five star book, but oddly enough, those biases also make for some interesting editorial choices, and a lot of entertaining reading. Just keep that third eye open.
