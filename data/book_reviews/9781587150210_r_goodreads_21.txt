The Enchanted Island of Yew was fun enough. It was a pleasant children's story, though lacking in Baum's typical humor and wittiness. The setting, as a bland European inspired series of kingdoms, was not as engaging for readers as some of the locations in Baum's other children's novels set in Nonestica.This book likely holds interest mostly for adult fans of Baum who want to read more of his non Oz work. The structure and themes are easily identifiable as Baum's, even if the tone and humor are not. Notable to current audiences is the gender politics of a protagonist who changes gender. The commentary on beauty is also interesting, though in the end it seems the themes were less thought out than beautys' use in the narrative for drama. Highlights include a sassy dragon who shirks his official job, and the land of Twi which is shaped like a mirror and everyone has a double. Interesting for Baum scholars, and not a bad read, but ultimately unmemorable.
