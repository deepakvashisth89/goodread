Leavitt has keen insight where family is concerned, and in this story Nick is the contaminant without even realizing it. Orphaned at a young age, tragic circumstances seem to follow him into his first marriage. Hungry for the stability when he meets Dore, he has a fruitful, loving marriage only to see it crumble after another tragic event. He creates a second family, secret from Dore in the hopes it will be his salvation. But is it?At the start of the novel Nick's parents, Tom and Helen, are still alive. Tom's father is a bit of a wild card, losing money at card tables. Helen is the suffering but loving wife. His upbringing is more carefree than that of his peers and his parents attention available only when he is alone with one or the other. "In my house, every man is a king." his father Tom tells him, and a friend replies to Nick "In my house, it's my father." So much said in two lines. With the promise of a move to California, Nick accidentally let's Tom's cat slip out, this comes to be the cause of his parent's deaths in his eyes.We follow Nick into the Pittsburgh Home for Boys, where he doesn't quite fit in, and with his pride grows up independent earning his own money. He soon meets Dore and sees a chance at a family, love, and a full life. For a while it works, though the men and women eye Nick with suspicion for wanting to spend time with his family and enjoying caring for his child. But as he feared, he loses it all only to begin anew with a second family. Leslie soon has a child, Robin, with Nick but the damage in his past causes him to live on the periphery of their lives. For Robin, Nick's flickering presence in their lives leaves her feeling lost and unlovable. It leads her to make poor choices and find love in unhealthy places.This isn't a joyful story, but it is a very good character study. Leavitt burrows into her characters fears and hopes, and exposes the things we feel and do without even being aware of it. The reader is left to wonder just how much influence those we love have on our being, for better and for worse. I felt for everyone in the story, even when I hated what they were doing. I liked it.
