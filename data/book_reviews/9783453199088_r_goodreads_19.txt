__________________________“If you were not quite yourself . . . when you climbed the wall last night . . . then who were you—and why?”—Dirk GentlyAs the story begins, we meet the Electric Monk. The Monk is a horse-riding AI robot from a different dimension which was created to believe impossible things so that its owners wouldn’t have to. The Monk has somehow stumbled into a cross-dimensional passage to Earth. This 1987 creation of Doug Adams has resulted in nearly 20% of the U.S. population turning into Electric Monks who spend their time believing impossible things.OK, the previous sentence isn’t in the book, but just look around you. You know it’s true.Who is Dirk Gently? First of all, that’s not his real name. He changed his name because “Dirk” sounds like a Scottish dagger. I know, weird right. I used to occasionally work with a freelance writer who always wore a monocle, clicked his heels together, and bowed like a 1920s German baron. That kind of weird.Dirk believes that everything in the universe is connected and that many things we consider strange coincidences are caused by links that exist between us and everything else in the cosmos. Doug Adams borrowed this philosophy from Carl Jung, a real-life, highly respected psychiatrist who was a protégé of Sigmund Freud. Shame on you. He does possess some uncanny preternatural abilities. When he was in college, he made a list of 5 questions he thought would be on a final exam and offered to take bets on how many of the 5 questions he got right. It turned out that he got all 5 right—word-for-word, period-per period right. It surprised him, too. And as a result, he was hauled away and sent to prison.Now, ten years later, friends from university have asked for his help. A horse has mysteriously appeared in the upstairs bathroom of a professor, there’s a murderer afoot, and a ghost or so.🌟🌟🌟🌟Stars.This is not Douglas Adams’ funniest book, but then it is still hilarious, and may lead to some weird coincidences in your life.
