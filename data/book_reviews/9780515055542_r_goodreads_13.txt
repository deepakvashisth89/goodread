4.5 stars - very entertaining classic country house murder, it was good to have Inspector Alleyn back with Fox and Troy in England after wartime duty of three-plus years.As the novel opens, Troy has been asked to paint the portrait of legendary actor Sir Henry Ancred; Alleyn is due home anytime, but Troy is deeply tempted to take on the commission. She heads to the great actor’s country house and is soon pleasantly immersed in her work, despite being surrounded by the dysfunctional, overly dramatic Ancred family. I loved this part - Troy is a great character, so calm and collected, and her internal musings dealing with this emotional, dramatic family was quite amusing!Marsh gives us a wonderful cast of drama queens with the Ancreds - Sir Henry is also keeping company with a beautiful but clearly lower class actress who is, in the parlance of the era, “no better than she should be”. Yes, the old lion taken in by a beautiful young gold digger is a hackneyed device as old as the hills, but Marsh plays it brilliantly, pitting the scheming young woman against Sir Henry’s resentful, grasping family - quite Shakespearean, really, and fun!Adding to the drama, the old man constantly threatens to change his will; the inevitable happens, the old man is found dead, but was it his usual gastric trouble bringing on a heart attack after a too-rich birthday celebration and a raging temper tantrum brought on by practical jokes poking fun at him? His ego, of course, is immense and he is enraged by being poked about his young lady friend...Troy is present when the old man is found, but she can’t help wondering if someone decided to put an end to Sir Henry.When the family all receive letters suggesting Sir Henry was poisoned, Alleyn steps in to investigate. A very satisfying Golden Age mystery - so glad Alleyn is back in England, I look forward to continuing reading these books with the Reading the Detectives group!
