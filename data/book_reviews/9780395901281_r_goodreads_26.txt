I've only heard vaguely of David Leavitt so this was pretty much a blind read that I didn't know what I was expecting. The three novellas in this are well-written, sometimes really funny, but don't feel like they really go anywhere unexpected or come to some any profound points. My favourite of the bunch should beSaturn Streetsince AIDS-narratives usually reduce me to a blubbering mass of intense feels, but that one felt too short and constricted for the narrative growth or emotional turns to feel natural. I would have wanted it to be a novel, while the other two were entertaining if a little lightweight and probably should have themselves been short stories. My favourite was probablyThe Wooden Anniversarysince I love the concept of a woman and her gay best friend having a platonic relationship as dysfunctional as a real marriage.Also two of these three novellas are set in Los Angeles and feature first-person narrators who are both dating actors and who both ritually rent pornography from the real-life Circus of Books, but who are supposed to be different characters. I don't know but I feel like there should have been someone in the process of publishing this book who should have pointed out that these two stories share such specific details but are supposed to be unrelated. I just found that weird.
