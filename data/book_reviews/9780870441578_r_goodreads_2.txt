This narrative is about three Native American Children who are Creek, Cheyenne, and Nootka who also go through their everyday lives with universal qualities of childhood. The story describes their different housing structures, family rituals, and parental bonds as well as them playing the same way most children play during their free time. Stuart highlights the Native American’s respect for their elders as well as the roles everyone is to play within their villages. The main characters within the story are a Cheyenne boy who learns how to be a man by the games he plays with his friends, a little Creek girl who learns how to survive by watching her mother, and a little Nootka boy who enjoys watching his mother and grandmother and learning from his father. As a teacher, I will use this text because it is a great vocabulary builder in regards to the names of the different artifacts mentioned and housing structures, and how it illustrates great family values. The illustrations by Glanzman capture the realistic features of the children’s emotions with the colors and strokes when the children are learning and while they are playing with their friends. This story is a great piece to use when teaching children about diversity and how they may have the same qualities and beliefs of another culture.
