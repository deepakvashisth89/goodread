Since my cooking tends to be simple / "every day" (or "real fridge/pantry recipes" i use ketchup! and garlic powder!) I wanted help for the few times we have another vegan couple over for dinner on our patio. (Covid!)

My main complaint: this cookbooks calls for so many obscure ingredients.

For example:
gravy powder
Chinese leaves
smoked tofu
roasted buckwheat
Soya flour
cornflour (as far as I can tell, this is not cornmeal)
melba toast
demarara sugar (I think this is raw sugar?)
Cox's apple
mangetout
minature sweet corn
black onion seeds
swede
apricot-flavored liquor
"fresh fruit juice" <-- what fruit?!
malt extract
mincemeat <-- what? this is a vegan cookbook?
wholemeal self-rising flour (I WISH this existed)
sachey easy-blend yeast
custard powder
muscovado sugar
tangarine drink

I also saw "Eating apples" and "dessert dates" which was a real headscratcher, along with "light tahini" (there's a dark kind?) . Frozen chestnuts also made an appearance and I'm not sure I've ever seen those, even at Christmas... shrug... and I couldn't figure out what "2oz medium oatmeal" meant.

I knew this book was published outside of the U.S., so I was prepared for measurements in oz/g and "Dessertspoons" -- and I expected a few foreign things or foreign names for things---EXAMPLES:

*aubergine instead of eggplant
*sultanas instead of golden grapes
*almond essence instead of almond extract
*chevril instead of French parsley
*porridge instead of oatmeal
*cougarette instead of zucchini
*pine kernels (that's pine nuts, I hope)
*gherkins (a very specific pickle)
* "yeast extract" which I always assume is the 70s name for nutritional yeast... it is, right?

but oh my goodness!

Several recipes call for expensive/harder-to-find nuts such as hazelnuts and you'll see things like "vegan margarine" and "vegan ice cream" and "vegan cheese" as well as "pesto" which I've never found vegan anywhere, and "vegan fruit cake" as well as a number of liquors or drinks.

I struggled to find one recipe where I could reasonably find all the ingredients, and then I had to make substitutions so I'm not sure it even counted. Sigh.

On the upside: This book educated me on food terms/analogues because I googled A LOT.
