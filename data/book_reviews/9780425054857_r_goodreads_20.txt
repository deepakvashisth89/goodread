This book was unnecessary and underwhelming. T. H. White at this point had a sermon to preach and, rather than craft a good story that embodied the ideas he wanted to convey, he set about putting long, didactic speeches in the mouths of his characters to tell the readers what they were supposed to get from the previous books.

The thing is, there is nothing new being conveyed that wasn't already better portrayed in The Sword in the Stone, which was a fantastic book. All his ideas about might, force, war, government, etc. were already perfectly captured in the lessons that Wart learned from Merlyn. Books 2-4 in the series didn't really open up new political or philosophical ground that The Sword in the Stone didn't already cover, but I didn't mind because the characters were so humanely written that I loved reading about them, and as a fan of Malory's great work, I enjoyed seeing how White reworked it for his purposes.

This last book, however, was ham-fisted and mostly unpleasant. It seems like White was worried that some readers might just read his stories for fun and miss out on the Very Important Message that he wanted them to get. Sometimes fiction books do contain Very Important Message, but rarely does a writer who sets out primarily to convey a Very Important Message end up writing a good work of fiction. If you're going to read through the Once and Future King, just skip this one.
