Tolkien’s background as a philologist and myth-maker shone really bright in the description of The Two Towers. Here, the world feels ancient because every hill, river and ruined wall carries the ghost of a story. This book really deepens the world, fractures the fellowship and shows a kind of lovely quality that runs through the whole legendarium. What I've found out is that beyond the brilliant magic and sprawling worldbuilding is the true soul of this series- the characters. Tolkien breathed life into them with such rare passion. Every line of dialogue and action resonates with a meticulous care that made these characters deeply felt and unforgettable.

The introduction of Gollum as a central figure is perhaps Tolkien’s greatest touch of character genius in this book. He is not merely a monster to be slain but a tragic mirror for Frodo. Through Gollum, we see the terrifying, corrosive power of Besides that, the dynamic between Frodo’s immense empathy and Sam’s fierce & protective skepticism was heartbreakingly so beautiful.

It expands the world with breathtaking scale. Loved the iconic locations like Edoras, Isengard, Helm’s Deep and Osgiliath with some spectacular new characters like Theoden, Eowyn, Eomer, Faramir, Treebeard and Gollum. The reunion at Fangorn Forest, the redemption of Rohan, the siege of Helm’s Deep and the Last March of the Ents are all so deeply written into the fantasy history. Everything keeps dizzying and shifting seamlessly between the heartwarming and the heartbreaking. Epic!
