You know who true villain of The Vampire Chronicles is? Freaking Marius de Romanus. He is the scum of the earth and after reading The Vampire Armand no one can convince me otherwise. But I’ll return to that later because there are things that need to be addressed first. For example the genius that was the Queen herself, Anne Rice because right when she manages to make you hate a character she pulls a 180 on you and all of the sudden you find yourself questioning that hate and sympathizing with him.

The way I fucking hated Armand in was unreal. Like I detested him with every fiber of my being. I thought him a great villain even a remarkable one, but when he tried to make Lestat pick him over everyone else and told him the pity party that was his life, which I truly didn’t care for at the time, I wanted him GONE! FOREVER! (Not literally though). He was annoying because he was the my-way-or-the-highway type of character but instead of letting you go he would torture you and try to murder you while seducing you at the same time. And unlike Lestat who does all kinds of weird fuck shit because he is stubborn and a idiot, Armand is brilliant and calculating and there was always something sinister that crept at the edges of his personality. Then enter his and Daniel’s chapter in and although he did really disturbing things to Mr. Molloy, you could tell that Anne Rice had begun to make him grow as a character. Taking him out of the villain box and moving him to grayer areas.

Now let’s fast forward to me reading this book, which for the longest time I had been avoiding because I was still giving Armand the side eye. Like I said before, I truly liked the way Armand was growing as a character but I wasn’t sure I wanted to read his story, specially since I didn’t like the snippets he showed Lestat back in . Well lo and behold, I fucking loved it. So basically this is me asking for Armand’s forgiveness.

Armand’s story broke my heart not only because he went through some dark and sinister stuff but for the reason that as he told every detail of it to David, he constantly lets you know that for the shortest second he was happy, that being when he lived with Marius, somehow he knew darker times were around the corner. He was trafficked by the Turks then bought by an eccentric vampire who gave him a rotten life if you actually sit and think about it. He was never allowed to grow up as a normal young adult because Marius kept throwing him in weird and perverse situations that ultimately brought poor Armand to the end of his human life. What Armand lived in Venice was not a blissful life; values and morals were dashed out into the canals while Marius kept pushing on him a lot of philosophical bullshit. Did Marius do good things for Armand? Yes, he basically gave him an education and a lavish life but at the cost of his soul.

One of the true remarkable things in this novel is the fact that Armand is always contending with the idea of being worthy of God. Which I thought was brilliant, we’ve always known him as one of the many leaders of the satanic covens. But when I found out he was a prodigy ikon painter back in Kiev Rus I was flabbergasted. He was under the mentorship of this group of monks who wanted him to become one of them. Then he had his father who wanted him to become a star, because he knew his son was special. Then we have Marius who is an atheist wanting Armand to become more worldly. But more importantly we have Armand that through the trauma caused by all this dysfunction around his life manages to retain an admiration for Jesus Christ. Not one implemented by the monks, not one out of rebellion because of Marius pushing on Armand the staunch ideals of atheism but because he viewed Jesus Christ as someone closer to him than any of these people trying to influence him. A statement he nails when he wraps the novel when confronted about it by David and Marius, who still can’t understand why, after all those many centuries, his still has devotion to Christ deep in his heart. And that truly fascinated me.

There were scenes that touched my heart deeply and one of them was when he returned as a powerful vampire to Kiev Rus and visited his family. Both the scenes he had with his mother and his father were so remarkably done, they brought me to tears. The fact that he was able to let his family know he was alright and help them at the same time had be bawling. The second one was when he found Benjamin and Sybelle. So touching that he was able to help them and become to them their guardian angel rather than a demon of the night ready to suck their blood.

Here is a controversial take. I think Santino and Alessandra were better beings to Armand that Marius ever was, specially Alessandra. Yes, and I know what they did. Yes I know they indoctrinated him. But while Marius was cowering somewhere and after getting better never gave a flipping fuck about looking after Armand, Alessandra even went to him when he was sent to Paris. They comforted him in a terrible time. Are they good for that, no, they’re terrible creatures of the night. But for all the philosophical bullshit, Marius is always spewing, he is simply a coward and worse than any of the Coven wraiths.

Now let’s rip at Marius because he is an asshole and I can’t stand him any longer. Like I said before, he is scum. I hate that he always acts like he is a better vampire than anyone else just because he was Roman. Not only he is full of himself but he also thinks he is the greatest philosophical eminence in the world just because he studies history. Boo! Tomatoes! Tomatoes! Tomatoes! It truly infuriated me when Armand confronted Marius about all the philosophy he fed him during their time in Venice and he just flip-flopped and said ‘i don’t believe in that anymore’ like what? Bitch you can’t change your stance when you’re on the losing side. Admit that you were pulling ideas off your ass and take the L. What he did to Sybelle and Benji was so out of line and the most selfish shit he has done so far. And I was glad Armand punch his perverted face because he deserved it. The only thing that gave me satisfaction was that he had to admit he was a loser talking about things he didn’t understand and that he knew nothing. Akasha should have ended him in .

I’m giving this one 5/5 stars and recommend it to every fan of the Vampire Chronicles. It does deal with heavy themes but it is a necessary novel to understand Armand as a full fleshed character.
