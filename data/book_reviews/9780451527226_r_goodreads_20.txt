“It was no mature woman with a long dark vista of intrigue behind her who was tormented thus; but a girl of simple life, not yet one-and-twenty, who had been caught during her days of immaturity like a bird in a springe.”This book is special. It isn’t an enjoyable read, it isn’t a story that you fall in love with, it isn’t a narrative you find it easy to follow. It is intentionally difficult, intentionally bleak.The reader is subjected to the deterioration of a young girl’s innocence, her naivety entirely stripped by the brutality of a hypocritical society and sinister reality. A once hopeful young woman, with ambition, the security of ignorance, moulded into someone else, at the hands of men. Made to confront consequences to actions that are not her own, her creation into a societal understanding of an impure woman. A sinner. It is a frustrating and hopeless journey. The writing seems to envelope you in the downfall of Tess, you walk alongside her, through the lush valleys and hills, clinging to chance, the potential of a beautifully described love with a man you are mistaken to believe is good, the hope for a light at the end of the tunnel. A relief that you don’t get.Such a poignant discussion. A display of Victorian and religious values at their worst. The character of Tess will stick with me for a long time.
