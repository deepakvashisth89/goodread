I enjoyed this swirling tale set in the mists of myths and history in Romano/ Celtic Britain.

I had not read any Marion Zimmer Bradley; I was sort of aware of her from long ago but had not realised how prolific she was. Indeed I had forgotten this book was sitting on my to-be-read bookcase. Recently some modern authors have cited her as influential and the Mists of Avalon series was highly rated.

Part of me wanted stay in the early pseudo-idyllic “in the village” first section of the book, that felt almost Tolkeinish - back in the Shire.

The book has a brooding intensity as it moved inevitably towards the conclusion. It was a little slow in places in the middle, however picked up at the end as our tragic star-crossed lovers headed to their destiny, which also served as a metaphor for the wider country. I put the book down towards the end then read all the final chapters in one session. It did have a similar feel of doomed destiny and hidden secrets to that of the Arthurian tales.

I think I was rooting for our doomed Druidic high priestess heroine; not so sure about our slightly more ambiguous Romano-Briton hero.

Whilst chronologically a prequel to Mists of Avalon, I did not realise that it was actually written later than Mists of Avalon. I’ve not read MoA so maybe this is the right reading order. I’ve since read she started this book first at age 17 but only finished after MoA.

Not sure how much of historical elements were factual however they felt real and I enjoyed them.
