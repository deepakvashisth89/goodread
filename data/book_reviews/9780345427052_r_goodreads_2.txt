Moderately interesting and educational. The first hundred pages explore the importance of listening, a skill Monty excels at whether animals or people. Unlike his father, he 'starts' horses rather than 'breaks' them by observing and building trust. His antagonistic relationship with his father who ridicules and dwells in ego makes it difficult for a father son bond. Later in the book he develops a close relationship with owner of Harcourt publishing which begins well but ends poorly. What I found unusual was there's little if any mention of his work on the Redford award winning film, The Horse Whisperer, whose author based the story on his abilities. A memoir, I'd hoped to learn more about his 'listening', but the second half is focused on horses he's trained, sold and mated among numerous other things which had little interest where I'm concerned.
