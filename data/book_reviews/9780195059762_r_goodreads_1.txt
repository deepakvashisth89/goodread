Holy moly this is a beast of a book, jam-packed with information, and thank goodness it only goes through the Reagan presidency!Presidential Wivescovers every first lady from Martha W. to Nancy, and a handful of other wives, to boot. Only James Buchanan is left out of the book, having never married, but you would think there could be a little something about his niece, who filled the ceremonial role. Sadly, there is little biographical material that survives for many of the early first ladies, since it was not unusual for them to erase themselves from the narrative (or for their husbands to do it for them).Even when enough material exists, most of the first thirty tell a similar story, that of mother, wife, and hostess. Only in the mid-twentieth century do we begin to see first ladies not only with minds of their own but expressing themselves with those minds, having their own careers and interests, and finally influencing their husbands’ presidencies.As such, the later chapters get longer to the point of being very detailed biographies, yet the truth is each of these women deserves their own volume. The author’s political persuasion may peek through from time to time, seen also in the extensive detail for Nancy and Ronald Reagan (but then again, they were in the White House when the book was published).The editing misses also increase as the book progresses, not especially distracting but sloppy, nonetheless.While respectful overall, the unfortunate structure identifies the first ladies’ husbands by their last name and themselves as “Mrs.” last name, forcing the reader to identify the woman in relation to the man, but it does follow the standard writing style. Even the title does that: Not “First Ladies,” but “Presidential wives.” Still, Boller skillfully presents both the traditional, dutiful wife and the modern go-getter as worthy of respect.This one is a marathon, not a sprint. It’s American history from start to finish (1988).
