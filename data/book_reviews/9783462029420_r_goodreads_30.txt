Alienating, daring, weird and philosophical. They don't write debuts like this anymore, very impressive how ambitious, and for lack of a better word, weird, Faber manages to get here while not losing a "human" heart to the storyWe are all the same underneath the skinUnder the Skinis a singular novel, documenting the work of Isserly, who is on the lookout for muscular male hitchhikers. These hitchhikers, dubbed Vodsels, very near to the Dutch word for food, endure a fate that raises questions on factory farming and the comfortable unique position we accord to ourselves. How a debut writer asMichel Faberwas in 2000 would think of this, giving us a story that subverts multiple genres and leads us to care for Isserly despite her profession and inherent alien nature, is impressive. I was reminded of equally uncomfortableThe Cement GardenbyIan McEwan, a debut while very different, also unafraid of being nasty at times and wholly different.Sexism and gender relations clearly play a role, with many of the interior monologues of the hitchhikers being so misogynist one could argue they deserve what is coming to them. Meanwhile Isserly is not just a perpetrator but also a victim in order to be able to lure men into her car, which becomes particularly acute when the handsome son of the owner of the enterprise she works for comes along. The ending mirrors the intrinsic violence of the central plot, and subverts any expectations one might have.Deeply weird, unique and ambitious, I am very impressed and would argue this is a modern classic!
