This was a STRUGGLE for me! In fact, all the 18th century novels I've read have been a struggle. (Except, oddly, The Mysteries of Udolpho! Funny because I'm not usually a lover of the gothic.) I think this may be because I'm the opposite of an early adopter, so the novel as a form needed to be around longer before I could "adopt" it as my favorite form. I'm also thinking that Jane Austen really revolutionized novel writing. All the 18th century novels I've read, including Udolpho, have such outsized emotions and situations. It's not to my taste in a novel. I much prefer the subtext, the understatement, the internal play of emotions that contribute to the external movement of the plot. (Jane's so good at making the reader pay attention.) Except that I like Dickens... But his outsized emotions and situations are tempered by realism and more subtlety in the plot and characterization. So back to my first theory that novel writing had to evolve and deepen and experiment into the 19th century before it became Elizabeth's Literary Happy Place.There are some great characters in this: Tom Jones, Sophia Western, Mr. Allworthy, the Nightingales, and Mrs. Miller. There were characters I hated (Thwackum and Square--ugh, prosy bores!!). I like Tom's journey to wisdom over the course of the novel and his goodheartedness. The plot was very complex and it would take forever to write out step-by-step what happens in the novel. There are some funny scenes and lots of tedious ones (for me). I didn't see the final reveal of Tom's parentage, so that was fun to finally learn.For now, though, I think I'll vault over the 18th century in my reading and continue inching into the Medieval period while also delighting in the Victorian era and 20th century where my favorite novels benefit from all that was tried before.**I wanted to note that it was actually quite helpful for me to read Handel in London by Jane Glover as I was reading Tom Jones. Handel was involved in the same artistic scene as Henry Fielding in the early to mid-century 1700s London and so many of the plots of the operas Handel composed music for are just bizarre! Talk about outsize emotions/situations/characters, etc. It was clearly an era of experimentation.
