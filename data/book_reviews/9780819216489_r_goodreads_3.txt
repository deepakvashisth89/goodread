Another modern classic about a monastic way of life written by a Protestant. It's like Catholics don't find the Rule of St. Benedict as interesting.
Unfortunately, this is really not about the Benedictine way of life, it's about the author's idea of it, disconnected from any strong ideas about the nature of God or the church, which is combined with some Annie Dillard, Metta Bhavana, and Tao Te Ching. I'm a fan of Dillard, but I definitely would not call her a Benedictine. Benedict's emphasis on obedience to authority is lost here; the prayers that McQuiston includes aren't really directed to God, which makes the book more Buddhist than Benedictine, more Julia Cameron than Esther de Waal.
