“I jest want something all my own. Something to foller me and be mine.”A lot in this story resonates for me. I grew up in the south, living out in the woods with my parents and a few distant neighbors, on acres of land, with dogs and critters. I did plenty of hunting as a boy with my father. We never ate panther meat, nor fought with bears, nor did we depend on our crops for rations. Still,The Yearlingwas moving and captivating, being reminiscent of a life that is simpler, albeit harder, and that lacks city-based “intrusions on the individual spirit.”In the story, young Jody lives with his ma and pa on “Baxter’s Island” in the wild Florida scrub. His family must grow or hunt their food. Their nearest neighbors, four miles away, are a mixed bag, being hellraisers but also sometimes behaving friendly and helpful. Jody is lonely and longs for a pet. A mutual catastrophe finally brings him one, a young deer.“The fawn was alone in the night, as he had been alone.”Jody raises and loves the fawn, which he names “flag.” The story includes tension between neighbors, tension created by nature (e.g. a flood), and tension from the Baxter’s hard way of living—will they have sufficient resources to avoid starvation? Into this mix is thrown the fact that Jody’s ma doesn’t like the fawn, and the fact that all cute young things eventually grow up, creating mischief along the way.“How come you to grow up so unlawful?”Other Memorable Quotes:“A woman has got to love a bad man once or twice in her life, to be thankful for a good one.”“Well, boy, be glad you got encouragement. Most of us live our lives without it.”“Boy, life goes back on you.”“A man’s heart aches, seein’ his young’uns face the world.”“What’s he to do when he gits knocked down? Why, take it for his share and go on.”
