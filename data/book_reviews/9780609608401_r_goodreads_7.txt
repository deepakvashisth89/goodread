What's the difference between "liked it" and "really liked it" anyway? If I liked some of these short reflections, thought some were ok, and really liked others, does that average out to "liked it"? I'm going to say yes.

I picked this up because I lived for about four years on Cape Cod (over-the-bridge Cape Cod, not "wrong side of Buzzards Bay" Cape Cod) and the title, Swimming at Suppertime, made me remember those days when my kids and I, or my best bud and I, or my best bud and I and all our kids would go down to the beach at suppertime after all the crowds had left and enjoy a wonderful evening swim with the place all to ourselves. Those are some of my best memories of my old life on the Cape and I wanted to see what someone else's were.

These essays serve up little moments of quiet reflection on what it's like to live with loss, what it's like to be a year-rounder in a place where people come in season and leave when it ends, what it's like to make your own way in the world, and what it's like to get a little older. Wasserman's big-hearted gaze makes the best moments in this book shine like holy artifacts and salvages the weaker moments with a wise and sometimes earnest, sometimes wry, but always engaging voice. Little moments in daily life, little moments on the job, little moments in town - they all add up to a book that isn't - well it's not exactly big, but it's bigger than it looks.
