I love my neighbors. They remind me how much I love to read, and how much we love so many great authors.I had forgotten that I had read this book a while ago, until it was deposited recently as a donation at my Little Free Library Shed.I remember when I first read this, I had also done some background on James Baldwin and discovered that he had written this novel after he had given up being a youth preacher and left the church to become a writer.He said that it took him more than 10 years to write this, having spent time in Greenwich Village and Paris before moving to a small town in the Swiss Alps. He finally finished it in 1952.In a 1961 interview with American broadcaster and oral historian Studs Terkel, Baldwin shared that he didn’t think he would ever finish the novel because of the shame he felt inside for the life he had in a Negro church. (Many see this novel as semi-autobiographical.)He also felt “ashamed of my father, ashamed of the Blues, ashamed of Jazz, and of course, ashamed of watermelon: all of these stereotypes that the country inflicts on Negroes, that we all eat watermelon or we all do nothing but sing the Blues. Well, I was afraid of all that; and I ran from it.”Eventually he stopped running.The title of the book is actually a refrain of the African-American spiritual: “Go tell it on the mountain/over the hills and everywhere/go tell it on the mountain/that Jesus Christ is born.”The story is centered around 24 hours in the life of John Grime’s – and it is his 14th birthday. His mother gives him money which he uses to explore the city for the day.As readers we explore the city with him. We can feel his anger – because this is not his life. He is reflecting on the various demons ruling over his life: his violent preacher stepfather, his church, and the racist society into which he had the misfortune to be born. He does not get to experience the riches or splendors that he sees. Yet he dreams and hopes big for himself.As readers we are feeling the essential aspect of life in America, its contradictions and seductions, that bittersweet mix of love and hate that so many feel towards our country when we see this racial divide.And then…He finds himself at the church. And we can sense what that holy feeling is that takes over John.Is he transformed? Is he saved? Is he destroyed or renewed?Baldwin’s novel is well thought out, and well-crafted. A lyrical work.It can even be thought of as a protest chant.It definitely is a brilliant portrait of a young man struggling with complex issues. A landmark classic novel.
