Probably 3 or 3.5 but I felt that did unfair things to the book's average rating.This is a very assured, entertaining mystery. I read it after loving several Jude Morgan books, and was not disappointed. March (as I guess I will call him here...) has as usual an excellent grasp of the period. There's neither unrealistic finery nor unnecessary grit - unpleasant realities of life in 1760 are mentioned when they are relevant, but there's none of the relish for dirt or misery one so often finds in historical fiction; likewise the past is not treated as a halcyon, rarefied paradise of elegance.There's a similar avoidance of cliches with the characters. The naval captain isn't the stereotype of "naval captain" that springs to mind with the phrase, time is taken to draw the actors as real people, and Fairfax's relationship with his student is depicted naturally and realistically: they like and respect each other, but 19 year old Matthew sometimes treats the 30 year old Fairfax like an old man, and Fairfax sometimes gets annoyed.I was wary of Fairfax'stwagic pastat first, as I think such things are often misused as a substitute for characterization. However, Fairfax's past informs his character; it isn't used in place of one. It's still a hook for a novel series, but it's a well done one.I'll definitely be seeking out the other books in this series.
