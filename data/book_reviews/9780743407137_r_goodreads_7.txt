I discovered the Toby Peters novels decades ago when I would buy them through the, presumably now defunct, Mystery Book Club (a genre-specialty, discount-by-mail service similar to the Book-of-the-Month Club and a Science-Fiction/Fantasy “club” to which I subscribed). Since then, I’ve had to settle for pass-along copies from both of my younger brothers (this one from my middle brother) and the occasional score in a used bookstore. I rarely see them on shelves.

The Toby Peters schtick is to have a hard-boiled ‘40s detective (with both police and film studio security background) who keeps have motion picture celebrities (and others, including the Roosevelt White House) as clients. features an aging Bela Lugosi (not pictured as the addict of ) and a William Faulkner who is failing as a screenwriter as clients. Not only is entertaining, but is is one of those mysteries where I kick myself for intuitively discerning the perpetrator extremely early on and discounting said “suspect” until the revelation. It was so obvious that I couldn’t believe it, but allowing myself to float on the tides of clever situations and WWII Era nostalgia was worth the leisurely trip to find out what I instinctively knew. So much for clever armchair sleuthing.

Nostalgia-wise, being reminded of rubber rationing wasn’t necessary but it was embedded cleverly in a couple of scenes. The Monogram Studio, home of an incessant stream of “B” movies, seemed like the perfect venue for a meeting with the fading Lugosi. When Peters gets a suspect to make a false identification of Harry James, the famous trumpeter and orchestra leader, as William Faulkner, he hums “You Made Me Love You,” a tune successfully covered by James. He is somewhat obstructed by a Rainer (sic—actually, Rainier from Mt. Rainier and brewed in Seattle till 1999) Beer truck, and drinks from Horlick’s to fortify himself. I didn’t know Horlick’s, the UK version of Ovaltine licensed to a Wisconsin company which also made malted milk balls during the war), being an Ovaltine kid (whenever I couldn’t have Nestle’s Quick). I was also informed about the Chicago Bears vs. Pro All Stars game, an interesting factoid about the team that keeps breaking my heart that appears several times in the story.
