On my bookshelves I have a sizable section of Charles De Lint novels that I no longer read and reread obsessively. I'm rereading them and sending them off to be enjoyed by others because this is something I seem to have largely outgrown.Regarding a less personal description of my relationship wth this book it is a collection of three novellas, Cafe Purgatorium by Dana M Anderson,Ray Garton's Dr. Krusadian's Method the final one by Charles Delint, and deals extensively with death. Cafe Purgatorium is about a bored executive who buys a club in a mdlife crisis. He finds hmself hanging out with a crowd of lost souls, most of whom died close in within his new property. He has long dialogues with Satan's agent and then Satan himself. Anderson makes the connection between the fallen angel Lucifier and the sacrificial aspect of God, viz. Jesus. And in hopes of rescuing the girl he has jilted his fiance for, the ghost of a suicidal hooker, he does what Jesus would do-he won't leave until they all can go forth from Satan's clutches. He gets his girl briefly and then seems to reconcile the broken aspects of his life. I found the best part his conversation with Satan, perhaps because I have never bothered to really understand the Lucifer myth or read Milton.Dr. Krusadian's Method is about the death of an abused child's soul, which os redeemed by Dr. Krusadian. In many ways it is a classic revenge tale wherein Jasons parents are not exorcised, while Jason is. Instead they are gifted with his removed traumas, as seen through his eyes. At one point I felt like I was experiencing a racist rendition of a voodoo doctor, but kind of let go of that after Martin makes a bunch of racist accusations. Who better to understand the trauma of abuse than someone who has probably had more than their share of it. And Martin is gross and Dani is well rendered in her denial and doe in the headlights response, the deadening of alchol, etc.The last novella Death leaves an echo feels like a worn theme, a place this author, Charles DeLint has researched many times, perhaps because he is a haunted person himself. A man dreams of running into an elm on black ice and wakes up ina world here his wife seems to be erased from everyone's memory and his house shows no sign of her. He goes searching for his lost love and is laughed at by the police. Consoling himself with a beer he runs into a highschool acquaintence, who after some prodding he recalls as a looser. later he will recall how she died, but only after he's taken some consolation in helping her move into her new apartment. There he dreams of a different kind of relationship with Penny turned supermodel. And of his own coma. Turns out he's in her fantasy, her heaven where she invites him to stay rather than returning to a body hopelessly crushed against the elm. Good man that he is Dave Sheil decides reality is better than someone else's fantasy, (in part because he wants to kill Penny) and genuinely wakes up. He's also quite dead. t's a more cynical twist to his usual Bardo tales.
