I enjoyed this more than I expected to.

De Quincey was one of a number of significant literary figures in the Regency period associated with Wordsworth and the Romantic Movement. He is best remembered as the originator of Drug Lit with the account of his life and struggles with opium addiction (taken as tincture of laudanum).

Opium was a common ingredient in pharmacy products until 1868 (most parents would appreciate Mother Bailey's Quieting Syrup!).

The titular work is mainly autobiographical with, unsurprisingly, some fantastic elements. De Quincey fled Manchester Grammar School in the middle of the night whilst being prepared for Oxford, spent a year tramping around Wales and eventually matriculated in Oxford at Worcester College. While there he began opium use, perhaps to treat pain. He never completed his degree. He later met Wordsworth, married and raised a family, turning to journalism for work, though debt collectors were never far from him.

He spent quite a lot of time away from his family and would rent accommodation, working in it furiously, filling it with papers and abandoning it, without moving out, for somewhere new when it became too full of material to be habitable.

Also included is The English Mail-Coach, a visionary work clearly written under the influence. A remarkable piece.

An interesting collection of pieces from a minor literary figure who shaped attitudes to drug addiction through the nineteenth century.
