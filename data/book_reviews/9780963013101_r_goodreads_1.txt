This is not the "unwavering voice of a free spirit ". Free spirits aren't conceited, judgmental etc. I don't care if it was the 80's when this was published. This person fat shamed a model within the second page of her book!! Was this supposed to be the "humor" the blurb talks about?Here is the passage I refer to:"There was one model I drew early in who weighed upwards of 200 pounds. She was covered in quivering cellulite from ankles to hair roots. (Hair roots? Wow. Dumb). No wonder so many people squirm at the thought of drawing a naked model! I had to go outside and have what amounted to a therapy session with my instructor before I could face her behind. When the instructor moved the stand she was modeling on...I don't know how...he fell a little, and his right hand plunged deep into her fat, blubbery belly. I almost threw up. I wondered what my parents would think if they could see this fat woman? Eek! What was this warped new world I had entered?"" What in the the world did I just read??There are undertones throughout that hint she feels she is above others. Sometimes subtle, sometimes not. There is a distance between herself and empathy. She holds herself as special and aloof. At least, her writing surely comes across this way, and there is a slight juvenile tinge to it as well. Example, she didn't go to art school because she is special and beyond those soulless creators/students. Geeze.It's something in the way she presents her concepts and ideas that comes off as self absorbed and better than you.These illustrate some of the negatives I found within these pages.Positives: her connection to snakes, trees and nature.Her message that yes, we can make it as artists.Some of her descriptions of dreams, painting frenzies, etc.Not sure if I want to pick up book 2 in her journal series, Coyote Woman.
