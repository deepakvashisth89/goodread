Such a long and thorough epic novel deserves praise for its detail and ability to amass such information into a relatively short space. That said, Rutherfurd's inaugural novel was much too dense and perhaps 'thick' for me to thoroughly enjoy. I felt that it did drag on and left me wishing for a slightly abridged version of this 'James Michener-like' saga.I have great respect for Edward Rutherfurd and have nothing but praise for his attempts to gather such detailed information for the reader to learn about important spots around the world. Sarum takes thousands of years of history and tries to lay it out in such a way that the reader will connect with its characters and then extend that 'like' along generations as new stories sprout up with familial connections. While Rutherfurd did well in making those connections, I feel that each story could have been reduced and perhaps added stories added to the mix.I cannot help but wonder to myself if Rutherfurd does use James Michener's style in writing these multi-generational sagas, and will have to pick up one or two for myself to see if the pupil emulates the teacher with any great skill.Kudos, Mr. Rutherfurd. I have to offer them to you, since I know how great your books can be. This one, alas, did not leave me pining for anything but to reach the end.
