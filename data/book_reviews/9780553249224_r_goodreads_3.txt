Radigan tells the story of Tom Radigan, a tough, smart rancher in the New Mexico Territory sometime during the 1870s. In the mountains, valleys, and mesas west of Santa Fe, Radigan, with one trusty ranch hand named John Child, has spent 4 years getting to know the remote, difficult land, built a small but comfortable house, and started establishing a good herd of cattle and the horses he needs to do the work. But one day he discovers a trail that he didn’t make. A dangerous stranger is trespassing. Turns out this stranger works for a young woman named Angelina Foley who claims she owns Radigan’s land, that she inherited it from her father who received title to it in a grant from a Mexican governor. But Radigan knows he paid for the land fair and square. And so begins a brief (the book’s only 196 pages) but dangerous battle over land rights.I really wanted to enjoy the first book by Louis L’Amour that I’ve read but it must not be one of his best. Radigan himself is a fairly well-drawn character but the others are pretty sketchy, including the two women in the story, Angelina and Gretchen, John Child’s teenage but beautiful foster daughter who suddenly comes to live at the ranch. There’s tough talk, fist fights, and some shooting between the two sides, all of which Radigan wins, rough riding in an early winter snow storm, secret hideouts, and just not great writing (L’Amour seemed to insist on following that old and pointless rule of sentence structure: Don’t ever end a sentence with a preposition; drove me nuts). L’Amour’s descriptions of the land are bland at best but a map in my old paperback helped a lot (it seems the locations in the novel are real).I really hope I find some L’Amour books that I enjoy more than I did this one.
