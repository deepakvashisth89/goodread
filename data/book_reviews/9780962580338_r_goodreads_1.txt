This is a solid retelling of the Robin Hood folklore, especially for readers who never read any retelling. The emphasis on religious themes, highlighting the conflict between Christians and Pagans, adds an intriguing layer to the classic tale. All characters are given equal weight and are well-developed, making the ensemble cast feel balanced and engaging.While I enjoyed the overall story and its progression, Lorele’s pacing sometimes works against the tension. The buildup takes too long, and when the big finale arrives, it feels more like another Robin Hood escapade than a true climax. The epilogue, in particular, left me underwhelmed. After nearly 460 pages, I expected a satisfying resolution to Robin and Maerin’s long-awaited union, but instead, the ending felt abrupt.I was also left wondering about certain unresolved threads. What became of Maerin’s maid? Of King Richard? By the final page, I couldn’t help but feel slightly abandoned by a story I had invested so much time in.
