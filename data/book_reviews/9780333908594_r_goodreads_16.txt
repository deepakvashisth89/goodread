Perhaps 3.5 stars.
“Written on Glass” was on my re-read shelf. I don’t know why. It must be at least a decade since I first read it and maybe a bit longer. This time I found it overly dramatic and overdrawn. The title is somewhat puzzling, but one of the main characters, Topaz, does write a name on misted glass a couple of times, and there is a fair amount of staring through glass windows.
The book does, however, have some redeeming features.
It is set in Britain spanning the years after WWII from 1946 to 1953 and captures well the austerity of these years of continuing rationing as well as the struggle of men and women to adapt to peace time relationships and expectations.
The story is essentially a saga of romances among a couple of interlinked families that are upper middle class (with a few working class interlopers thrown in here and there). The group of characters are difficult to like because they are all given to weakness and naive behaviour. In fairness, this is probably a reasonable capture of that class in this time period, but I really found the characters tiresome.
The writing is quite good and it fulfills expectations for this genre, but it is long
