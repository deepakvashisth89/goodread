Okay to good

The biggest problem with this book was the h. Brenna refused to marry bc she was terrified of child labor. At 11years old she watched her mom bleed to death and later her raped bf died in child labor as well. While I could understand her fear the author took it to far.

Brenna (thanks to her Norman mother) inherited her mother's dowegery lands. King William is trying to marry her off to one of his Norman Knights. Brenna is able to scare most away.

Enter: Rye . Rye is intrigued by Brenna and arrogant enough to win her. Rye actually admires her spirit and doesn't want to tame her. More fool to him! I thought she was a spoiled brat.

Rye is actually a sweet, honorable man. He's a bastard that inherited his father's lands to the bitter disappointment of his stepmother. He takes care of the b**** and his sweet half sister. With all his problems he also has to deal with his new wife's Saxon brothers.


Problems: Again - Brenna's fear of dying from childbirth. What I don't understand is why she didn't visit a wise woman to prevent conception. Problem solved!

The stepmother was a bitter, evil woman. Rye should have gotten rid of the hag. I hate how H's keep villians around when there was an easy way to solve the threat. They kind of get what's coming to them.

All in all, this was an okay book. There is better Norman Conquest Hr out there. This book was nothing special.

Sidenote: I didn't know the first castles built didn't have chimneys.
