This disarmingly charming novel was published in 2000 and I’ve just discovered it now! Set in a Northern First Nations community, this book deals with serious issues such as addiction, poverty, land rights and homelessness, yet it is never depressing or preachy. There is a great deal of humour and warmth and likeable yet flawed characters. Haley delves into satire with her examinations of various levels of government that control and impede native communities – such as the hilarious outhouse incident in which the local bureaucrats demand the outhouse builder apply for a permit beforehand and submit architectural drawings. The author spent a number of years running a charter airline in the Northwest Territories so I assume many of the scenes in this novel come from personal experience. Overall this was a satisfying read; I cared about the characters and wanted them to succeed, and feel I learned a bit about life in the Canadian North.
