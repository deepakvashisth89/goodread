Both lazy and dull, and yet somehow the style still retains its charms. Vidal's wit remains miraculously intact, which is good because if 1876 were to rely on its characters, plot, or scenes, it would be unbareable.Not sure what inspired Vidal to write 1876. It's sandwiched between two of his masterpieces in the same series: Burr (1973) and Lincoln (1984). It depicts the year of the defeat of Reconstruction and the US Army at the Battle of the Little Bighorn, but isn't interested in the South or the West. Rather, it wishes to imagine New York and, at times, DC as the only living places in America. Fair enough, but when reading, you get the sneaking suspicion that something more interesting is happening literally anywhere else.
