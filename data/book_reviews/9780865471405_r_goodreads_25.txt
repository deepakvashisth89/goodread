I randomly selectedTotempolebySanford Friedmanas my next reader. This radical coming-of-age novel follows Stephen Wolfe, a young Jewish boy growing up in New York City with a fascination with nature, drama, and the arts, from childhood through maturation. Set between the Depression and war years, we meet Stephen as a two-year-old boy and follow him through his obsessions as a toddler, his summers spent at the beach and summer camp. We get to know his family, his young crushes, and all of the quiet parts of his soul.What a marvelous little book. To be honest, I hadn’t heard of it until last year, which was somewhat surprising to me. Clearly, I have some gaps in my reading to fill in. You know, it’s difficult not to fall in love with young Stevie. The wonderful way he sees the world, his attraction to nature. I really loved the early chapters ofTotempolebecause of the family dynamic. His parents, Harriet and Saul, were very interesting characters. They have a sort of toxic relationship, but I liked their separate relationships with Stevie. It was particularly nice to see the wealth of affection between Saul and his sons.“Loon” was probably the chapter that I could recognize most of myself in. The intense crush that Stephen develops on Uncle Hank was so reminiscent of my own first crush. The conflict, the confusion. The final chapters, “Lice” and “Rats” were both quite a bit longer than the rest, which gave the narrative an uneven feel, but I might have enjoyed them the most. There were elements of the final chapter dealing with the Korean War that might not have necessarily aged well, but the deeply spiritual journey that Stephen goes on to overcome his shame and to accept his sexuality has a universal appeal that I think still very much speaks to the gay/queer experience for many.
