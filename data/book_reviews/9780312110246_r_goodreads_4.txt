The editor has certainly cast a wide net in his interpretation of "Fantasy and Wonder," as the inclusion of stories by Mark Twain and Charles Dickens attest, since neither of them is likely to spring to your mind as someone you would otherwise place in that genre.At age 79, my reaction to this book is probably a lot more mixed than it would have been when I purchased it in 1994! This is probably because in these troubled and, indeed, grim and threatening times "fantasy" is about the last thing I am inclined to dip into.So, for a more general readership, I think it important to note that ALL of the stories in this ample volume -- which range from very brief vignettes of just a few pages to substantial short stories approaching 60 pages -- are, indeed, well-written. It's just the genre doesn't "grab" me as it once did, so the "fault" -- if such exists -- is more likely to lie with me and not with this book.This is perhaps sadder because for decades I was a fan of science-fiction, one of the more specific genres that fit within Hartwell's encompassing title. In fact, I was a member of the still-extant Science Fiction Club from 1956 -- when I was but 13 -- until sometime in the late 1980s or early 1990s. It was during this time that the more interesting type of science fiction (again, hard to escape a personal bias in such an observation) -- such as represented by Clarke, Heinlein, and other greats of the mid-century -- began to morph into both the equivalent of Westerns, with fighting and impossibly brave and wise heroes as their major contribution, as well as an influx of fantasy, with imagined worlds of fairies, dragons, and -- increasingly -- female models of the male superhero line increasingly featured in science fiction.It was not just that I preferred so-called "hard" science fiction to this, but also stories that focused on the human dilemmas of encountering the unexpected or, even more chillingly, what lay within hidden in themselves, more than the flashy spaceships or the derring-do of muscled, chin-chiseled, and handsome heroes and heroines increasingly crowding the shelves of science fiction sections of bookstores.So, when I discovered this book amongst others than I had long ago read, I succumbed to tip-toeing back into this area of, for want of a better term, "literature."For persons who really enjoy this genre, as well as for those just curious about and wish to dip their figurative toes into samples of it, I think this book would serve well.Sigh! I guess I just have to admit that I have encountered yet another proof that I am not as young as I once was -- nor, sadly, as hopeful!
