Florida attorney Matthew Hope returns in a new thriller by Ed McBain. "Snow White and Rose Red" is a modern-day story that really isn't a fairy tale.It's been over 18 months since the "Jack and the Beanstalk" case has been closed. While trying to catch a killer late one summer night, Matthew Hope received a bullet in his shoulder by the suspect. Matthew survived the ordeal with the shooter in custody and confessing to three murders. But it's all in a day's work!Now in mid-April, both Matthew's personal and professional lives are running parallel to one another. On a personal note, his ex-wife Susan wants to send their 14-year-old daughter Joanna to Simms Academy, a private school for girls up in Massachusetts. Both father and daughter object to Susan's idea, so Matthew has his law partner Frank Summerville look over the Hopes' separation agreement. As the "king's messenger", Frank ends up giving Matthew the bad news: Even though Susan must remain in Florida until Joanna turns 21, she can send her daughter to school anywhere she wants to.On a professional note, Matthew is contacted by a wealthy heiress named Sarah Whittaker. Against her will, Sarah has resided since the previous September at a private sanitorium called Knott's Retreat (dubbed "Nut's Retreat" by its patients). She wants Matthew to fight for her freedom as well as the $650,000 she inherited upon her father Horace's death. Her widowed mother Alice Whittaker sent her daughter to Knott's after she attempted suicide. Despite warnings from both Alice's attorney Mark Ritter and the two doctors who are dealing with Sarah, Matthew decides to take on Sarah's case.Matthew drives over to Calusa's Public Safety Building to find out with officer removed Sarah from the Whittaker family home to Knott's Retreat. The officer Randy Ruderman gives him the account of what happened. (Sarah told her side of the story in the previous chapter.) Matthew also finds his old friend, Detective Morris Bloom, at the station. Morrie briefly tells Matthew that the body of a young murdered woman was just found in the Sawgrass River at the nearby Bird Sanctuary. The victim only wore a red wraparound dress and her feet were eaten by alligators. She was shot in the throat and her tongue was removed.As Matthew continues to petition for Sarah's release from Knott's, Detective Bloom and his partner Cooper Rawles try to find out about the murdered woman, later identified as Tracy Kilbourne. A dry cleaner's mark inside her red dress is their first lead. Then a friend and co-worker of hers named Corrine Haley recognizes Tracy's dress. Rawles and Bloom are lead down a crooked path trying to find a killer before he strikes again.Meanwhile, Matthew is walking on a thin line between his personal and professional life. He discloses more about his family to Sarah than he should. When he meets with Sarah's doctors, both of them try to deter him from moving forward with her as his client. Dr. Silas Pearson, who runs Knott's Retreat, tries to explain to Matthew about Sarah having delusions and paranoid schizophrenia.Pearson also mentions how she refers to people by certain names--i.e. herself as "Snow White", Pearson as "Dr. Cyclops" (due to being blind in one eye) and Matthew as the "White Knight". Ironically, when Matthew later meets with Sarah's mother, Alice wants him to get her daughter out of the sanitorium.Sarah's so-called diagnosis seems to have many layers like an onion being peeled. Dr. Pearson compares it to different color skeins in a tapestry. But I'd compare it to the petals of a red rose--with blood at its epicenter. (See the cover photo of this book's edition.)As the story progresses, it soon becomes apparent that both subplots (Hope's and Bloom's) are perpendicular from one another's. When the two friends finally meet again, they need to try to put the pieces of their separate puzzles together. And the readers know that they're on an imminent collision course. One that could cost Matthew Hope dearly in both his personal and professional lives!Ed McBain continues his "fairy-tale" mysteries starring Matthew Hope. This story is one that won't provide a "happy ending" for those near and dear to him, but it's still worth a read for the fans of Matthew Hope and the 87th Precinct series!Rating: ***
