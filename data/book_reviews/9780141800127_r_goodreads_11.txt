I don’t know about this one. Stephen King’s coming of age stories are very much hit or miss for me, which honestly makes me slightly nervous about finally reading IT. Then again, I guess there's the terror that's pennywise so the dynamic is different.

This story follows four boys growing up in a small factory town. It explores their friendship, their families, and their aimless summer adventures, all centered on the search for the body of a local boy, Ray Brower.

Strangely, I find myself at a loss for words.
That almost never happens when I sit down to write a review. The problem is not that the story lacked meaning, but that it lacks cohesion. For me, there were too many moments and events that felt random, as if they never completely fused into one story. And as for the characters, I cared more about the dynamic between the boys than about the boys themselves. I probably should’ve felt more sympathy for Chris and Teddy considering their tragic and abusive backgrounds, yet I couldn’t really bring myself to.

King’s ability to make a reader physically recoil in revulsion is honestly unmatched.
My mind consistently circles back to the scene of the eating contest, which escalates into . I didn’t see it coming. Few writers can produce that level of visceral disgust so effortlessly. Oddly, I respect it. Feeling anything strongly, even revulsion, is still engagement.
