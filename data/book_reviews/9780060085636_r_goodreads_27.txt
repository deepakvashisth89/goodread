Kind of a wonky one for Leonard. Starts out introducing some really interesting bad guys but then gives over much of the book to Stick hanging out with a playboy stockbroker and their financial advisor Kyle who Stick courts. Lots of stock market stuff and country club Miami-set details (ground also well tread in Split Images from this same time, the last Leonard I read which I also found to be a bit off though I liked that one a bit more than this) and not enough about nutty Chucky the fat fidgety drug dealer or Nestor the Santeria practicing kingpin (though we get a good amount of Moke, Nestor’s shitkicker henchman). The smart, mature romance between Kyle and Stick is really well done and Stick’s struggles to figure out the next chapter post-incarceration is also really good, I just feel like we’re a bit low on juicy crime business for much of the middle of this thing. This is barely an issue because Leonard is nearly incapable of being boring as a writer, but the abrupt and kind of farcical nature of the climax underlined how long we were away from the bad guys. Stick even comes up with a pretty cool scam in the last section too, it just doesn’t lead to that satisfying of a final confrontation. Maybe if the rest of the book had been packed with showdowns and double crosses I would have liked the anticlimactic finale more, who knows? That said, where we leave the Kyle/Stick relationship is like Graham Greene-style cool and understated downbeat stuff and the ironic joke of the final chapter is good shit. Also, speaking of good jokes, love how Stick gets to bone down with the stockbroker boss’s wife and the boss’s mistress in the same night and that doesn’t leave him with enough in the tank to get it up with Kyle for the first time.
