The book is more about a journey the author takes with Ian Stevenson, a researcher on reincarnation, investigating cases in Lebanon and India.  They are exclusively children, once they can talk, speaking of former lives, denying who they are now, insisting they are someone else and don't belong in the home they are now riving.  In some instances they can speak foreign languages fluently (Hindi and Japanese given as examples).

In giving the background of the author and the researcher it takes a long time to get to the first case.  It becomes further confusing that we are given instances of when the child recalls a past life and then many years later recalling the different pasts.  It would make a better movie than a book, as there would be visual clues as to who is doing the speaking and which time period is being presented.

Interesting patterns emerge in that 50-60% of recollections are of violent deaths, when such deaths are only about 5-6% of demises.  Also that "moving" into another body seems to occur at time of birth. Although later on Schroder says the average length of time from one death to another is eight months.
