My review after reading all 48 volumes:

I found this to be one of the most influential pieces of fiction in my life. From what starts as a pretty typical battle shonen but with cars manga becomes a very deep personal reflection on how you can be changed through the pursuit of a craft. Every race is a battle of competitive morals on how one ought to pursue racing as an art, and I think the message of it can be best depicted in the Athlete vs Craftsman arc. At least, as a competitor myself, it's probably the most accurate description of how I feel about competition.

I compete in a world that cares a lot about arbitrary rankings, and there's a lot of discussion and debate, and people get fired up over achieving some level of skill that falls on that arbitrary ranking. I've always felt like an odd one out. Maybe it's from being an artist where you can chase after honing one's craft side by side with other artists, not necessarily needing to compare oneself to another, because in such a personal craft, do you have the right to take from someone else's personal journey by ranking it above or below yourself? Melee, like art, is something I do. To an outsider, it may be seen as a hobby, but when I'm doing these things, I want to feel like I'm in full pursuit of sharpening a craft with whatever limited time I have. I think athletes feel more inclined to do whatever is necessary to win in their chosen field, and that kind of mindset is why I admire athletes of all sports. But I find myself feeling more like a craftsman or an artist in my endeavors. A lot of my practice and curiosity feels more selfish and sometimes less utilitarian or practical in the world of competition, but helps me achieve my own personal happiness and shape my own voice.
