hey yeah! a big juicy adventure story, w/ the adventure in question being death, sir walter having royally screwed up in not securing a pardon from his death sentence before sailing for guiana. every bit as true to its time period as falstaff & faust but blessedly free of the skeeviness that blemished those two novels. infelicities were (a) the midsection of the book is a doldrums chock-full of these philosophical chitchats with the native dude christoval (enlivened admittedly by some wild psychedelic kokha-leaf-fueled nightmares); and (b) it's never quite established why sir walter is so dead-set on sailing back to certain death in england, tho i guess the q of whether that's courageous or suicidal is kinda central to ye olde narrative. that his attempted escape from england still manages to be suspenseful despite the obvious outcome is a testament to the fine storytelling up in here. (also dang, can any jacobean scholars confirm/deny the thing about james i and the deer carcasses?)
