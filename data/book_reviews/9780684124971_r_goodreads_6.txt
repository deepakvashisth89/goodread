I read the kindle version of this book because I was interested in the 50th Anniversary additions to it. My initial interest in this novel actually began a long time ago…Young Stephanie came upon a book while browsing in a used bookstore that caught her eye. On the cover was a line drawing of a little Asian girl, being hugged by a man who looked terribly upset. The jacket flap blurb was intriguing; this was a story about that girl and her family being sent to an “internment camp” during WW2. Upon asking my history-teacher father about what that was, I got my first lesson about Executive Order 9066. The order given by President Roosevelt to imprison all people of Japanese descent living on the West Coast, of all ages, just because they were Japanese… The bombing of Pearl Harbor was the trigger for this order; anyone who looked Japanese or had a Japanese surname became suspect, even those who had been born in the U.S. and were citizens.I’ve read many books since about those incarceration camps. I’ve also been to the museums at Heart Mountain and at Topaz. Everything about this “blot” carried out by our government, I learned on my own; never once was this subject ever touched on in any history class I ever attended. My husband and I made sure our two homeschooled sons were educated about this event early on in their educations…
