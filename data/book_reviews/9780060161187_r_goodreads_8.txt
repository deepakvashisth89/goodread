Another page-turner in the Leaphorn and Chee series by Hillerman. I have been reading these sporadically after watching and enjoying the TV series Dark Winds on AMC. The fourth season just debuted this week so I thought I should delve into the books again. This is the ninth book in the series and as usual I have been reading these very much out of sequence. However, the stories are always enjoyable and can definitely be read without knowing all of the backstories of the characters.

In this one, Leaphorn is contacted by the Gallup area FBI agent, Jay Kennedy, about a body that was found near a railroad track in New Mexico. Kennedy knows that Leaphorn is a good tracker and wants to know if he can determine how the body got there. When Leaphorn gets to the body, there are no tracks around and he can't figure out how it got there unless a train was stopped to place the body there. The body has no identification and even its false teeth are missing. In the mean time, Chee is put on a case to arrest a part Navajo man called Henry Highhawk for digging up some bones from a cemetery in Vermont. The bones are of prominent whites. . . Highhawk works for the Smithsonian in DC and is concerned that thousands of bones from native peoples have been dug up and stored in the Natural History museum there. He would like them returned to the native people for proper burial. Well these two cases lead both Leaphorn and Chee to Washington D.C. So are the two cases related? It appears so. And why is Highhawk being stalked by persons from the right-wing Chilean government?

This was another very enjoyable and compelling entry in the series. Although this one didn't totally take place in the desert Southwest, I really related to Leaphorn and Chee's stay in Washington DC. I worked there for the Federal Government in DC for 13 years and the places described in the book were very familiar. This one also was great in describing some of the Navajo ceremonies and superstitions including the various masks and fetishes used. I'll definitely be looking forward to reading more of the books I have missed so far.
