James Joyce is best known for his works of fiction, including the obscure Ulysses and Finnegans Wake, and is also the author of this collection of short stories, Dubliners. It would not be easy to speak of enhancement since Joyce does not idealize his compatriots. In rich, neat language, Joyce describes the Irish capital at the beginning of the 20th century through stories that plunge into the rampant daily life of the bourgeoisie.From schoolchildren who left to skip school to supporters of Charles Parnell, the hero of independence, Joyce is more attached to the inhabitants than the city itself. The author describes the frustrations of social conventions that are impossible to eliminate: lost loves and desires for elsewhere—a genuine literary portrait of the city.
