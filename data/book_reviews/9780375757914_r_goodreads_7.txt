Lucien Gregory, an anarchist poet, is in the midst of controversy with his colleague Gabriel Syme, the poet of order and reason. Mr. Syme plays a double game. It is an agent of the secret police whose mission is to infiltrate the Central Council of European Anarchists. This organization has more the air of a cenacle of strongly degraded, dressed-up people than a dispensary of dangerous nihilists. The chef's name is Sunday, and his colleagues, like him, bear the name of one of the days of the week. During a meeting with an agenda item on the election scheduled for a Thursday to replace the deceased, our infiltrator usurps the honorary title, much to the chagrin of Gregory, who had aspired to the post. An anarchist danger has settled a brigade of anti-anarchist philosophical detectives. I liked the book's first half, which is remarkable for its English humor and absurdity. Then the story sinks into the grotesque—an uneven book.
