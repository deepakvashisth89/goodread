This minor classic from the Harlem Renaissance (1927) is a satire about a guy who invents a serum that turns black people white. Everyone soon is, and we end up with the inevitable conclusion that "we're all n-----s." The prose is sometimes clunky, but the satire is well-crafted and effective. (Side note: this is the logical end point for a surprising number of books from the era about passing for white, including Nella Larsen'sPassingand Jessi Redmon Fauset'sPlum Bun.) I dug it pretty well, although I don't think its relegation to the margins is unjustified.Theme but not plot spoiled ahead:Be ready for a shockingly violent conclusion; it's a pretty drastic shift in tone, and not really an effective one.
