Now that I've read a few, I realise there is a distinct similar pattern to all of Mary Stewart books which I can understand some readers might find repetitive or predictable. I, however, continue to just find them comforting and charming.

We always get the story from the first person point of view of the heroine. This time around it's Vanessa, a vet, who is fairly newly (two years) married to Lewis, a salesman who frequently travels abroad for work. Lewis is supposedly in Stockholm when Vanessa sees him on a newsreel item of a circus fire in Austria, the footage showing him seemingly a little too chummy with a gorgeous blonde girl. So when a family friend needs someone to accompany her teenage son, Timothy, to Vienna, Vanessa obviously agrees, with the plan of finding the circus and Lewis.

After previously putting Crete and Corfu on readers’ bucket list, Stewart’s descriptive passages this time bring Austria to life. There’s castles and mountains and quaint villages and steam trains and far too many baked delights to assist with keeping my diet on track.

The title comes from the book’s focus on the Spanish Riding School of Austria where the stallions are trained in strict dressage including a jump with all four legs off the ground, thus creating ‘airs above the ground’. I read where Stewart actually consulted with and received permission from the director of the riding school, so these scenes and the mentions of the school’s history should be accurate. I’m not a huge horse fan myself but with Stewart making them dance across the page, she easily coaxed me into wanting to see the Lippizzaners perform in real life.

Stewart’s heroines are always able to look after themselves and usually they are the ones who need to rescue the men in their lives. Vanessa had her moments to shine but, out of all Stewart’s heroines I’ve ‘met’ so far, I did find her a little more reliant on the male characters than the others.

Even though Stewart changes things up in this book by having Vanessa and Lewis already married, I really enjoyed the romantic aspect of the book. It seemed a more mature romance and there’s a lot of almost an additional sexiness to some of the scenes.

Stewart too writes action so effortlessly. There’s a couple of her usual car chases, which are always done uniquely so that they still remain interesting, along with a tense and entertaining rooftop chase. This is probably where my rating dropped a little -- there seemed to be too much action. Rest assured, it's easy to follow and reads as if you're watching a movie. (I’ll repeat what I say every time I review one of Stewart’s book -- I’m so confused as to why Hollywood never adapted any of them other than The Moonspinners.)

There were signs of the book’s age now and then. I wouldn’t say it’s completely politically correct when it came to Vanessa’s reaction to certain circus members, for example. Timothy’s naivete also felt crazy compared to what a 17 year old would know these days. He’s a good character still though. Stewart also has a knack of making me like the kids she includes in her books.

As I said, this book follows Stewart’s strict pattern for a romantic suspense. But (apparently) that isn't a pattern I’m not going to complain any time soon. 4 1/2 out of 5
