Ali’s Trotsky for Beginners (he wrote the text, Evans did the illustrations) is a somewhat better book than the Rius volume on Marx. It is definitely less “hagiographical”; he admits that Trotsky (and Lenin) made many serious mistakes, which allowed the bureaucracy to come to power under Stalin. A number of his interpretations of events are very much his own opinions on debatable questions, which the reader who knows something about Soviet history may disagree with; this is probably unavoidable, and so a real “beginner” book may be impossible, at least for the period before the last exile. Even for the last period, I had one reservation; like virtually every book I’ve ever read on Trotsky (other than those published by Pathfinder Press) it almost entirely omits his work on the Fourth International, which only gets four or five sentences, other than the entry in the glossary at the end. This was only surprising because it was by Ali; otherwise I would simply have expected it. However, the book does serve its purpose in answering the question, who was Trotsky, which is all I needed from it.
