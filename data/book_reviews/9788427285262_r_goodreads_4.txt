Another twisty and entertaining Hercule Poirot novel. Ariadne Oliver and Superintendent Battle both make an appearance, and Poirot is as brilliant (and as egotistical) as ever. What sets this one apart, however, is that there is a short period of time where Poirot almost believes himself to be wrong about something. I know, I know, it's almost unbelievable and it probably goes without saying that Poirot is rather upset by this turn of events.

I know absolutely nothing about bridge so there are bits of this novel that went straight over my head, but overall this is probably one of my favorite Poirot mysteries to date. The ending is delightfully twisty-turny, and I once again had no idea who the murderer was until the big reveal. There are only four suspects in this one so you think I'd have a decent shot at it, but, well, let's just say that it's a good thing that I don't have to go around solving murders in real life.

And, as always, Hugh Fraser did a fantastic job narrating the audiobook. He might have played Captain Hastings in the TV series, but he is hands-down my favorite audiobook Poirot.

4.25 stars, rounded down.
