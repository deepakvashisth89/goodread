This book covers Diana's whole life, including her friendship with Hitler, in some detail. Much of it is interesting and it is written in an easy, engaging style, but it is curiously lacking in detail about Diana herself. We are told that she was beautiful (and there are photographs which show her at several ages throughout her life), but also that she was intelligent, well-educated and kind, for which no evidence is produced. We get a good sense of what Diana's life was like, what she did, where she lived, conditions during her wartime internment and so on, but little sense of what she was like.
Anne de Courcy makes it clear that Diana never lost her admiration for Hitler, but she fudges the politics somewhat by suggesting that Diana was 'tending Mosley's flame', when Mosley himself was nowhere near as close to Hitler as Diana or several of her family. Mosley agreed with Nazi and Fascist politics, including the repellent anti-Semitic ones, but did not like Hitler himself (his international links were more with Mussolini's Italy). He did not think Britain and its Empire should be involved in the war, but once in it he wanted the Allies to win it (and this became the official BUF/BU line). Diana, on the other hand, celebrated German victories, kept a photograph of Hitler beside her bed and failed to see why this attitude might be considered treasonous. Even the other fascist Mitfords adopted a less enthusiastic attitude: Unity shot herself when war was declared because the prospect appalled her, Tom joined up and fought against the Japanese (although he wanted to avoid fighting against Germany), others decamped to remote houses and kept out of it all, only Lady Redesdale might have shared some of her daughter's passion.
The Mitfords are an unusual family in their fondness for totalitarianism (even Jessica 'the communist' was a follower of Stalin rather than Marxist ideology; she broke with the Communist Party over Khruschev's policy of de-Stalinization), but their well-mannered arrogance and self-assurance is not unusual for their time and class. What singles Diana out from her family, class and times is her continuing adherence to Hitler and the Nazi cause, and I know or understand very little more about that now than I did before reading the book.
