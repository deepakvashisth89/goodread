It’s easy to think of war as “Good Guys Vs. Bad Guys”, where the good guys go in, kick the bad guys’ butts, and do it in a way that is moral and honorable. But it’s rarely ever that clear—there is so much grey area involved. Philip Caputo says his purpose for writing this book was to put the reader in Vietnam—and to force them to look in the mirror and ask, “What would I do I were in this situation?” His goal was to write it in an immersive, simple narrative style that was both vivid and readable. He wanted to show the good the bad and the ugly, and all the grey areas in between, that are all apart of war.Caputo details his time in Southeast Asia, as well as his time after the war, and isn’t afraid to question the motives of the politicians and generals that sent countless thousands of young Americans into the jungle. The memoir itself is stellar—wonderfully written, it reads almost more like a novel. It is highly detailed, from training and the people he served with, to various battles and firefights, to his time in Danang, and his experience with being court marshaled (which I did not know about and found extremely interesting). Caputo had a very interesting experience in Vietnam, and I’m grateful that he decided to write this book and tell his remarkable story.Highly recommended for people with an interest in Vietnam as well as war memoirs in general.
