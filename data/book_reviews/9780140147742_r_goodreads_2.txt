Stegner says in the foreword that the order of the stories is random. I'd prefer a chronological order—I like to see a writer's progression—but I doubt it's purely random. In the first story, the eponymous traveler looking back at a young boy he identifies with seems a fitting start for stories Stegner says do not form an autobiography, but are a sort of personal record.

Stegner is a brilliant writer. He writes beautifully and many of the stories are complex. But my rating is not for his great prose style: It’s for the collection itself. All in all, because of its non-chronological order and its 'slices' from what are or seem like novels, it made for a frustrating reading experience.
