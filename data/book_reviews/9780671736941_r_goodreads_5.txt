Honestly, I found this in my father's car and only popped in the first tape to have something to listen to on my 20 minutes drive to school. I was surprised to find myself engrossed in the novel about a young girl who was the daughter of a Polish immigrant who build his own American Deam in the form of the Baron hotels. Imagine my surprise in finding out that this was the sequel to another best seller, that I will be sure to pick up as soon as possible. The best part was the main character, Florentyna, and her unwavering honesty and diligence in not only her buisness life but also her politics. I found myself cursing the American political system at times for the unfairness in campaigning schemes and was heartbroken every time Florentyna was hit by a blow from the media during her efforts to make it into the political spotlight. I feel as if her character were a real person, she would be one of the only politicians I would admire. One thing I also loved was how the love story in the book did not take up a major amount of the reader's time, and rather was put in to help push the plot forward and added to the realness of the main character. Not only were the characters wonderful, but the historical facts in the book were added with subtlety and grace. I never knew much about the Polish and found myself engrossed in the telling of the Polish heritage from Florentyna's father. The connection to American politics throughout the whole book taught me more about the history of modern America than I could have ever learned in one of my college classes, all while intertwining it with the lives of the characters.I would reccomend this book to anyone who has knowledge of the American political system and likes fast paced historical fiction with empowering women as lead characters.The only thing about this book is if it were not for my recent class on Mass and the Federal System, I would have been completly lost in the political details of the book. Thankfully I learned what the Majority and Minority leaders, whips, and other political positions held in Congress. That would be my only warning for anyone who decided to read this book.
