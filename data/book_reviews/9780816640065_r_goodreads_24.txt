I really like the first half of this book. Levine challenges all our Puritanican notions about protecting children from sex. She forces us to ask "What IS the harm to minors?" when it comes to sex. We think we just instinctively know what kids should and shouldn't know, see, or do but where do these ideas come from? (Hint: The Christian Right.)To me, the most interesting theme that runs throughout this book is agency. At what point does a child have control and say so over his or her own body and when does this right belong with parents/schools/the law? I don't really know the answer but I know that more often than not we side with adults over kids and that this can have very damaging outcomes. If kids do not feel like they have sovereignty over their own bodies, how will they learn to protect themselves from predators, learn to give enthusiastic consent to sex, take charge of their lives outside the sexual realm?The second half is less effective for me. This half feels less organized and is more of an advice or "how-to" have good sex education, which is less interesting to me than her theoretical ideas.
