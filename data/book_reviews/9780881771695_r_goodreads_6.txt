I read this book for a class. It’s the text for what was described as a “heritage” class, so I thought it would be church history, but it’s more a doctrinal history. So, part of my “meh” reaction to it may just be that I was expecting something different - kind of like when you’re expecting a glass of water and blindly take a big gulp of sweet tea instead (you kind of want to spew it). The book has some factual errors our instructor had us correct before reading, so there’s also that against it. But I think the main thing I disliked about it is the amount of “church speak” the author uses. The author is a bishop in the United Methodist Church, so maybe he’s just been in the hierarchy of a religious organization so long that it has become unnatural for him to talk about God or religious matters in plain and simple terms, but the reason that bothers me is that this text (and class) is used to train lay preachers, and if you’re training your preachers to speak in terms that make sense only to other preachers, you’re probably not going to have much appeal to people who are not already regular church-goers, which seems like an ineffective way to “win souls.”
