Hogan brings the trilogy to a conclusion that is intellectually interesting but noticeably less satisfying than what came before. The ideas are still ambitious, and he remains committed to exploring the long-term implications of his speculative framework, but the sense of discovery that powered the earlier books is largely absent. The plot feels more procedural than revelatory, and the emotional stakes never quite rise to match the conceptual scale. It’s thoughtful and competent, but more subdued and uneven, an ending that completes the arc without recapturing the excitement that made the series stand out in the first place.
