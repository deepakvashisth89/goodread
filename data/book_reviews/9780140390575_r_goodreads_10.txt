I found this collection of stories difficult going, largely because of the archaic storytelling technique. I'm sure in Hawthorne's day these were easier to digest, but time has altered our expectations. Also, Hawthorne injects considerable allegory into these tales. In a few cases, the allegory seems to overwhelm the story itself. But again, that was likely par for the times in which they were told. The upshot, though, is that I don't feel entirely qualified to comment on them, except to say "Rappaccini's Daughter" is a brilliant tale, and "Ethan Brand" was my second favorite.
