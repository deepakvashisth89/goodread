First, I found this book at a book sale in the "Sports-->Cycling" section and thought it had potential, even though the book jacket hinted that it may have a faith-based leaning. Second, hats off to Mr. Trout for completing his cross-country bicycle journey. Third, this inspires me, if I ever undertake a similar adventure, to do the exact opposite of what the author did. Instead of indulging in the comforts and support offered by those who happen to share the same set of beliefs in invisible things (not to say they can't be real), I would prefer to explore the "heart of America" in a more Bourdain-esque way, cycling through some of the more alternative or marginalized communities out here.As a Jew, I'm a bit hardwired to cringe at every mention of Jesus, but the more I think about it, I find the exclamatory style pretty similar to ultra-orthodox Jews whose main thing often seems how many times you can name drop God into the conversation. In a parallel universe I could see a version of me wholesomely enjoying Mr. Trout's adventure more, but fortunately I live in this universe that has afforded me a little space to think more freely. (On a related note, God (or whoever is in charge) bless America!) Looking back, I feel the title to be vaguely offensive as I feel excepted from the author's conception of the "Heart of America," though I realize he was going for some wordplay there.Lastly, I wonder what it's like to be a somewhat famous personality with a unique name, only to have someone who looks like he may become one of the greatest baseball players of all time arrive on the scene with the exact same name.
