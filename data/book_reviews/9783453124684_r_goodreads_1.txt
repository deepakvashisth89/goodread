I see dead people.

Young Kerry McGrath is a fierce state prosecutor and loving mother of a beautiful child, Robin. When her teenage daughter suffers a mild car accident they visit a plastic surgeon, and she discovers not one but two patients with an uncanny resemblance to a murder victim killed several years back, Suzanne Reardon. The mystery further deepening when the supposedly already resolved case reveals ties with the local mafia, and a political conspiracy that may even jeopardize her aspirations to become a judge, not to mention her own life, or her daughter's!

Well I must say this was quite enjoyable on the whole; a nice amount of interesting characters, a fairly convincing and moderately gripping drama, a little bit of powerful sustained thriller, many red herrings, a few pow pows, and a not so great but decent enough ending. All in all quite a neat surprise, unputdownable even, sometimes. Given the right conditions I can see myself recommending it.

This was my first Mary Higgins Clark and hopefully not the last. I can definitely see why the author has amassed her well-deserved fame, and looking forward to reading more of her books someday. Which will happen for a fact since I see a few more RD used books bearing her name. But, for now... just content to have checked this author off my list.
