This is Schow's first novel, from 1988, a time when Vietnam veterans in pop culture, music videos, glamor bands and hip psychology were in vogue. Protagonist Lucas is a 40 something Thomas Magnum-like special ops soldier except he is an LA ad man whose teen daughter recently died at a rock concert disaster. His boss, Burt, also a vet, is no Jonathan Higgins but is a likable, sympathetic sort and his psychologist Sara, who is in love with him. All of the women in the novel are drop dead gorgeous.

The antagonist is Gabriel, former lead singer of Whip Hand, and described as a David Coverdale look-alike with a beautiful model girlfriend and large, mysterious Black bodyguard in tow. Lucas Ellington kills the former percussionist, bass player and lead guitarists before a final showdown with Gabriel who sees the violence as a PR opportunity, but barely survives as his hunter's would be lover and "serves" as his conscience nearly blows the singer's brains out.

The protagonist's seemingly simple revenge plot evolves becoming more bleak and unexpected as he eventually reveals a much darker and complex persona. Schow dives deeply into psychology jargon, arms and weapons and popular music especially rock music, making amusing commentary on the business. He brings up many of the big acts, like Pink Floyd, Zeppelin and even Wall of Voodoo, accurately. Our hero turns Gray and finally black as his Jekyll becomes Hyde.

Lucas is not just shell-shocked but is a psychotic, murderous and misogynistic timebomb that the reader slowly and shockingly discovers. He even rapes his daughter in a fit of familial horror apparently! wow! He is not as sophisticated as 80s icons Dr. Lector nor as raging as Patrick Bateman. But he has a subtle panache.

The final chapters are fun in a way with 1980s references to The Dukes of Hazzard with a Dodge Charger, and takes on California life. Strongly recommend.
