A couple of years ago I first heard of Patricia Highsmith and wanted to sample at least one of her titles. When I went on a used-book-buying-frenzy around that time, this was the one that caught my eye.I had no expectations, other than perhaps creepiness and suspense, based on what I had read about the author and her other works. I see that some reviewers were disappointed and others were bored, but I never was.The creepiness starts right off the bat when we meet a then unnamed blond girl on a street in New York, follow her into and out of a few shops, and then watch as she crosses the street to avoid a certain man with a dog. Why did she do that?The man was named Ralph Linderman and the dog was God. From the beginning and throughout the book this character reminded me of Robert DeNiro's in Taxi Driver. But would he at some point blow up the way that character did? Or would he turn out to be the upright, honest, slightly eccentric guy he seemed to be when he returned a wallet to our next character to be introduced, Jack Sutherland?Little by little we are drawn into Jack's life, which also little by little becomes enmeshed with Ralph's and the blond girl's (whose name turns out to be Elsie). What sort of intricate webs will be woven between these three people and others in the neighborhood, including Jack's wife Natalia?This is not a blood and guts splattered everywhere type of book. When the splatter does eventually happen, it was a bigger surprise to me than I expected. I also did not expect the victim to be who it was, which was silly of me because how else could this book have possibly ended?After this sample of her work, I plan to keep an eye out for other Highsmith titles when I am able to browse the library and their sale shelves again later this year.
