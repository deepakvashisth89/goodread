George Eliot and I have had a very strained relationship. I didn't enjoy both herThe Mill on the Floss and Silas Marner in school. When I returned to her after joining Goodreads, I found that my feelings are still unchanged on The Mill on The Floss. But Silas Marner pleasantly surprised me. From the first chapter it held my interest that I couldn't believe it was the same book that bored me to death and gave me nightmares in school. I think it is the kind of book that cater to mature audience, not one that could cater to teenage fantasies. :)

In any case, I found to be a beautiful and comforting story. It is well written. The principal story is centered on the protagonist, Silas Marner, but there was also a subplot on a secondary but an important character, Godfrey Cass. So, it was like reading two yet interlinked stories. George Eliot has a greater ability to bring provincial lives to life. In the thoughts, beliefs, superstitions, manners, and conduct of a provincial community are presented and explored. The simplicity of the working folk, the more complicated lives and relationships of the gentry, the secrets, the treacheries are all wonderfully weaved to the story making it quite an enchanting tale. The story proceeds very slowly, and rightly so, since Eliot was telling us a tale of a quiet community away from the city hustle and buzzle.

The story is centered on the themes of love and faith. Silas, who lost all faith in God and men due to a severe injustice that's done to him, who only lived for his work and gold, slowly returns to accepting and trusting in God and men through the mutual love he and his adopted daughter bears one another, and the kind attention paid to him by his neighbour Dolly. Eliot shows that how love can melt a heart that was made cold and hardened by life circumstances. She also shows how strong is the love Silas and his adopted daughter, Eppie, bears one another, even there is no blood tie between them. Even when the real father claims Eppie years later, the bond between Silas and Eppie was too thick to be broken by any such blood claim. Through the story of Silas and Eppie Eliot portrays a love so pure, a love that is unselfish, unstained, and unmaterialistic. Love is free, it is beyond petty claims like blood ties and materialistic benefits. I found this exposition on love to be so beautiful.

Reading this was quite a pleasant experience. The story had the ability to generate calm and relaxing vibes which I found very soothing. I've been reading some brain racking books these past days and felt grateful that this one was able to restore some of the lost equilibrium.
