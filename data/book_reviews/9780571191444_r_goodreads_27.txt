A sea voyage from England to Australia in the nineteenth century must have been a protracted and taxing enterprise.So I suppose it is the reason why "Rites of passage" is a protracted and taxing story.The book slipped down from my hands more than once and I never felt like stooping to pick it up.There are so many pages to go through only to be told in the end that seasickness is no party. Well I could have given you the same warning without committing it to a full book up to the brim with platitudes. No matter how many years of seafaring, you can still be caught unawares and the worst is when on an empty stomach. Do I need to go any further with graphic detail? No I don't, because I am not William Golding so I do not get a kick out of such rawness.Needless to say, I fast turned my back on this seamy trilogy. One single wretched book was more than I could take.
