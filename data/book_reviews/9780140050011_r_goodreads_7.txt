What do you get when you combine a small luncheonette, an automotive repair station, a charter bus, a bridge, a muddy old road, and a hodgepodge of strangers including, but not limited to, a drunk lady, an acne-covered tire jockey, a rich guy, a stripper, a delusional wanna-be actress, and a few perverts? I'll tell you what you get! One hell of a book! Steinbeck was on point when he wrote this one. He was coming off the high of his masterpiece, The Grapes of Wrath, and as far as I'm concerned, he hit it out of the park.

While the plot itself is minimal, like nonexistent – essentially a bus trip beset by delays and detours – the strength of The Wayward Bus lies in Steinbeck's observation of human behavior and his ability to imbue ordinary moments with symbolic weight. A character study at its finest! From the bus itself, to the environment, to the passengers, we experience love, loss, friendship, hate, disgust, acceptance, and everything in between. A story told less by words and actions and more by feelings and personal thoughts.

Well done, John!
