Exceptionally fine novel - surprisingly fine novel - I am amazed I enjoyed it so much because, for me, an almost guaranteed turn off is anything to do with the 'golden' Elizabethan era (I am talking about the first Elizabeth, surely nobody imagines the late Hanoverian Haus frau has given a name to anything?). As an Irish person the years of the Tudors/Stuarts were, for Irish people, anything but glorious (but then almost any period of Irish history for the past thousand years when England was in power were less than glorious) but I have a particular loathing for Elizabeth as she has to be one of the most extreme examples of indifference to her 'subjects' on record - please reference her treatment of the ordinary sailors who had won 'her' victory over the Spanish Armada. But then this novel is wonderfully jaundiced in its view of 'Gloriana' and the whole gang of 'heroes' she surrounded herself with.This is a sharp, powerful and splendidly written novel - I had read other things by Robert Nye (but not this novel) but that was over forty years ago and I had forgotten what a truly superb writer he was. I can only compare this novel to Ronald Blythe's 'Assassin' another splendid historical novel which I read recently (and have reviewed on Goodreads) and also set in the reign of James I & VI. It is of a quality that suggests comparisons with biographies rather then most other historical novels (and has any other period in English history inspired more bad novels then that of the Tudors and Stuarts) it is a damned sight better then the majority of histories on that period. Without indulging in grisly 'ye olde' English Robert Nye creates a voice for Walter Raleigh that is anything but a modern one dressed up in doublet and hose. It is a startlingly convincing portrait of Raleigh as a flawed man who gradually sees though his own lies, deceits and braggadocio to understand himself his era. For a writer to present a character, particularly a historical character, gaining insight and understanding of themselves is not easy and Nye handles it brilliantly.Having read this novel I am searching out Robert Nye's other works - all of which were extravagantly praised on publication - he is a writer of immense skill and is certainly in danger of becoming one of those forgotten classics.A wonderful start to 2024 reading.
