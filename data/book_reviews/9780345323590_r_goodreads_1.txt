In the twenty books that separate the first in the series from The Giant Horse of Oz the Good Witch of the North has been so forgotten that even Baum lost track of her and in The Emerald City of Oz (1910), the Gnome intelligence on Oz states that Glinda is the Good Witch of the North (as does the 1939 MGM film).

The only two magical Gillikins mentioned in those intervening volumes are Ozma and the witch who kept her captive, Mombi. Mombi though in her introduction is "at most a Wizardess" (Chapter 1). She does magic through tools and spells purchased via the black market. By the end of the book she is captured, forced to transform Tip into Ozma, and is striped of her knowledge of magic.

Yet, by the time Thompson is writing the Oz books, Mombi resurfaces as a greater threat than she ever was in The Marvelous Land of Oz. In The Lost King of Oz (1925) Mombi, still stripped of magic knowledge, is back as the main antagonist, this time while working as a cook for a minor kingdom up north. Now, she's back again, holding an island nation in a northern corner of Munchkinland, hostage with the aid of a sea serpent.
