I almost gave up on this book after a couple of chapters. It seemed sarcastic and full of outdated racial views (circa 1945). But the story became interesting when the white, Midwestern central character discovered that he was actually 1/32 "Negro" (which made him black in the eyes of his peers, and therefore immediately despised). There was a fair amount of subtlety in the author's portrayal of the main character's transformation to enlightenment, and the choices he faced along the way.
