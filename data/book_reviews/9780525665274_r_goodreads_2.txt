Three novellas by three women published in 1976 with an intro very "Gosh is sexism really a thing?" by Silverberg.The Crystal Ship by Vonda N. McIntyre is sooo 70s I could picture the costumes and sets from Logan's Run or Xardoz. The aliens are lovely, though, Kangaroo-like, and 1970s film would not have captured them. The ending felt abrupt.Megan's World by Marta Randall feels very Golden Age at first - the all-male human crew landing on a planet with intent to strip mine - but the story opens up with the introduction of Megan, who is a complex and compelling character. The ending also felt rushed. I think it's all there, she just needed to slow down a mite and get it all on the page.Screwtop by Joan D. Vinge is the best of the three, exquisitely evocative in a timeless way, it really got me into the setting and characters. She leaves the ending off the page, but it feels intentional? Anyway, maybe Silverberg as editor had a preference for open endings?Will definitely seek out more works by Vinge and Randall, who were new to me.
