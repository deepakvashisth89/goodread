I loved the little exposure I had to Jung as a psych major in college. Much later, when his name appeared in my reading of Joseph Campbell, I realized his theories were still with me. This is my first attempt to read his work for fun, and while I’m quite late to the game, I’m hoping it’s only the beginning.

Published in 1961, the year he died, this work focuses on the dangers facing us (understandably for the time, the atomic bomb was top of mind), and how to battle them. He seems to be saying that we must focus on understanding our shadow selves and conquering ourselves, rather than looking outside to blame “the other.”

He compares “religion” and “the state,” as ways we are grouped and both manipulated and inspired. He points out that the state looks at us as statistics, whereas in his view of religion (which emphasizes the symbolic, metaphorical side), we are guided to respect and promote our individuality, and he sees this path as preferable and more likely to promote the health of ourselves and our fellow beings.

He gives a brilliant discussion of the connection between archetypes--our sort of inherited mythology--and art; how we keep seeing these things repeated in life, and how they might be where wisdom lies and be the best deterrent to our superficial culture.
