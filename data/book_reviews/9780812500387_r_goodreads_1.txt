Systemic Shock is the first book in Ing's Quantrill trilogy, followed by Single Combat and Wild Country. Ted Quantrill is a fifteen-year-old preparing for a Boy Scout camping trip in August of 1996 when World War IV arrives. III occurred a decade before and resulted in new maps and political powers; the USSR collapsed and Russia became an ally of the US, Canada is the new Superpower, and the Mormons take control of the US. It's a survival story that incorporates a lot of political philosophy in the tradition of Mack Reynolds. It's kind of a YA coming-of-age story, but there's a lot of violence and rape and murder. The story branches out a lot along the way; when Ing stays with Ted it's pretty good, but some of the sub-threads are not so convincing. (For example, Israel packed up their country and moved it to L5.) It's an enjoyable if somewhat uneven story; I enjoyed it.
