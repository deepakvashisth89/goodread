I've seen the movie a half dozen times so thought "what the heck, I'll read the book". Into it 50 pages and it is quite good and true to the movie (or vice versa I guess) while adding extra insight.150 pages in and some more thoughts:1) The screenwriter for the movie has made really, really good choices. That's no surprise when you consider that the screenwriter was Ernest Lehman who wrote the novel and screenplay for "The Sweet Smell of Success" and the original screenplay "North by Northwest" as well as a half dozen other classics. The producer (John Houseman) and director (Robert Wise) picked an amazing cast that where they deviate from characters described in the book are uni-formally better -- for the movie.2) Hawley reminds me of Arthur Hailey but a better writer. You have the absolute pitch perfect research of Hailey but not as clunky in presentation. It's tighter than something like Hotel, but every bit as gripping.3) It really brings home that folks aged and died a lot more quickly back in 1952. Old man Bullard is only 56 when he kicks the bucket to kick off the action. One of the VPs on track to succeed him is 61 and really too old to take the presidents position (the hadn't come up with CEO at the time?). Another is 59 and planning to shockingly retire at 60 because he wants to get the most out of life in the few short years left to him. Hawley was 47 and newly retired when he wrote the book -- he would die at 62 years of age. Average lifespan then was 66 years in 1952 so three cheers for the medical researchers and doctors who are keeping us alive so much longer now.
