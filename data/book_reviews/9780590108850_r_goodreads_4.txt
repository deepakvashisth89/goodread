8.3/10

Nick Arnold's ability to make his books really informative and educational while still fun and entertaining is really incredible, and this is again on display in 'Chemical Chaos, which is especially impressive as chemistry is a rather mundane and often hard-to-grasp topic. Horrible Science as a series is not as popular as Horrible Histories, but in many ways may be more of an achievement, as the subjects Arnold is writing about just do not lend themselves to fun and comedy as well, but Arnold manages to make it work here. The formatting and organisation of Chemical Chaos and the series really makes the book a pleasure and a breeze to read through, as you never get bogged down by the short chapters relating to different topics, as well as the fun mix of segments within chapters - some fun science facts , a spotlight on a specific scientist, a short quiz, a try-it-yourself experiment, etc.
This variety really keeps you engaged as you read through this book.

The most entertaining part of 'Chemical Chaos' for me was the whacky and sometimes gruesome stories of the scientists and inventors and their historical experiences with chemicals - these parts really helped to liven the book up. I loved the sections told in prose or diary style which is then followed up by short fun facts. One of the great parts about this book is that Arnold throws in so much genuinely interesting tidbits of information, on every page you learn something new, even if you are already aware of the basic concepts Arnold is trying to explain, which he does very well, there is always a fun fact alongside it you never would have been aware of. Of course, some chapters are more fun than others - the chapters about discoveries, explosions, acids and crystals were particular highlights while perhaps chapters like the one of chemical bonding might not be quite as fascinating - however, I understand that it all needs to be there. Nick Arnold's writing style is really conversational, smooth and easy to read, and this works really nicely alongside the frequent comics, some of which are genuinely pretty funny, which are illustrated perfectly well by Tony de Saulles.

Ultimately, I think Chemical Chaos is as entertaining as a book about chemistry can be for me, and just about as educational as I'd like to ever read on the topic of chemistry before the details started to bore me. So, while I maybe wasn't persuaded to become the next Michael Faraday, Chemical Chaos was a really pleasant and informative read.
