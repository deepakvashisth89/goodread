Although the book’s title mentions “saga” it is not truly that. Intended for children, violence is threatened but there are no beheadings, grim blood feuds or thieving raids that you would find in a true saga. Speaking of grim, the bad guy is named Gorm the Grim. I feel it is suitable for older elementary age children and could also be read to younger children with less reading skills.This original tale by David Wisniewski does suggest a little light history of Iceland. That could have been developed a little more. In the story, the Haraldsson clan is recognized by the local Hidden Folk (Huldufolk in Icelandic lore) as good people and are helped to good land. Gorm the Grim and his clan are recognized by the Hidden Folk as bad people and kept from the best land. In his fury Gorm bites through his shield. This must be a reference to the Isle of Lewis chess set which has berserkers biting their shields. Because of this land dispute a curse is placed by Gorm on the good family. The good family’s daughter Elfwyn is born blind. With aid from the Hidden Folk she thrives and even saves her clan from the terrible crystal that Gorm has fixed on them.All ends well (I don’t think that is reallly a spoiler) and we find out how the Northern Lights came to be.The story could have been more developed and maybe even given more rhythm or feeling of the sagas.The paper cut illustrations are superb.
