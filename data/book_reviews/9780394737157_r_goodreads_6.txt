A decent book in the "for Beginners" series. Two things that bother me the most are 1) the constant attempts to distance Lenin from the supposedly tyrannical USSR that came after and the 2) the way Marxist understanding of historical progress is presented where it seems as if slavery, feudalism and capitalism all rely on same kind of regimes. But different disagreements and gripes with the author shouldn't be taken too harshly because the format of the book is a simplification and because it can still be useful as propaganda if not a serious text. I like how the incompetence of the tsarist regime is described, for instance significant attention is drawn to Russia's dependence on foreign investors, which should make anyone become more critical of the regime regardless of their political leanings. I also like how different factions are given attention, Lenin's rise to power is not presented as simply the socialists going against the monarchists, it was a great deal more complicated than that. This does mean however that the book has more text than most "for Beginners" books which could be seen as a good or a bad thing. Overall it is a book that could cause a lot of praise or a lot of frustration, a book with many flaws and positives, in the end it doesn't really stand out and I would recommend it to someone if they are interested in Lenin, but not if they wouldn't normally read it.
