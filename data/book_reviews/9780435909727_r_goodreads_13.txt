If there was one word to describe this book, it would be hard-hitting.

In many ways, it feels like a response to Achebe’s Things Fall Apart, but from a female perspective, this time focusing on Igbo society through the life of a woman. So if you’ve read or liked Things Fall Apart, I would definitely recommend picking this up as well.

When I turned the last page, my first thought was that this is really sad. And it is, because we follow Nnu Ego’s entire life, and it is full of hardship: trying to provide for her children and never feeling it is enough, being reduced to motherhood as her only identity, dealing with polygamy and tensions between wives, and watching sons be glorified while daughters are dismissed, even though it is the girls who give her one of the very few moments in the novel where she seems genuinely full of joy.

Then there is Nnaife. He is, in many ways, not a good husband. He is absent, often drunk, and claims his children only when things go well. At the same time, some of his behavior can be explained, though not entirely excused, by colonial influence, which reduces men in Nigeria to servants and sends them to fight wars that have nothing to do with them. That line, “I don’t know how I would feel if I was asked to kill people who had never offended me,” really stayed with me. Still, when you compare him to other Igbo husbands in the novel, he does not come out looking very good.

I think Nnu Ego is an incredibly strong woman who tries to do everything right. She sacrifices endlessly and manages to achieve a lot for her children, but never without suffering. I also truly celebrate Adaku, the second wife. She refuses to quietly accept her situation. She risks her reputation, chooses financial independence, and gives her daughters the chance at education and a life without constant hunger. In many ways, she is a true rebel against everything that is expected of her.

Overall, this was a great read. Even though I have read quite a lot of West African literature, I still learned many new things from this novel, especially in the depiction of Igbo village life and the transition to Lagos, and how drastically city life can change a person’s reality.

I marked so many powerful lines. It is very educational, very moving, and never once boring. Definitely a must read.
