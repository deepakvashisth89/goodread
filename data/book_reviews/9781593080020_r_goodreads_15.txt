3.5 🌟I want to start this off by saying that growing up, I was absolutely obsessed with Netflix’s animated White Fang (incredible movie btw) so I’d like to think I know what I’m talking about here.I’m very conflicted because the first half of these two books had me a little bored, but I actually found the second half really interesting and found myself wanting to pick it up.The Call of the Wild was a little underwhelming for me. The writing is beautiful, but the story was slightly flat. Obviously I was rooting for Buck, but not in the same way I root for White Fang. I like how the two stories compliment each other. The call of the wild is a domesticated dog returning to his wild roots and white fang is a wolf-dog slowly domesticating.One thing I really didn’t like is how painfully obvious it is that a white man wrote these stories. When Jack London started talking about how white people are superior to indigenous in white fang I genuinely had to write the words “please just shut up” on the page. These moments aren’t necessarily on every page, but that doesn’t take away from the damage they’re doing when they do pop up.Overall, I think these stories are very beautifully written and well thought-out. They’re far from perfect, and although I liked certain parts, if you’re looking for something low-commitment, just watch the Netflix white fang movie (did I mention I love it?)side note: I’m actually obsessed with white fang and Collie.🐺🐾🌲
