This is a short but beautiful book. It's been years since I read it. Each chapter is told from a different person's point of view, and most of them are from different cultures. I'm definitely going to use this somehow this year with my 7th grade students... just not exactly sure how at this point.

Reread: January, 2025. I'm using this book in my 7th grade ELA class right now, so I read it again quickly to solidify it in my head. I seriously adore this book. I even upped my rating to 5 stars. It is such a good story. Here's what I'm going to try: Each student has one chapter. 1. They're going to summarize their chapter. 2. They're going to meet with anyone else in the class who shares their chapter (all but one of my sections are big enough to do this) to discuss if they agree with the problems, resolutions, etc. 3. They're going to choose 2-3 objects from their chapter that represent their person and that person's story. They'll make a graphic essay about those objects; their summaries will go on the essays, too. No clue how this is going to play out, but this book fills my heart, and we all need a little heart-filling, right?
