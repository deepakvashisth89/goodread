Setting the title of the book aside, the Idiot's Guide to Buddhism by Bradley K. Hawkins is a very good starting point for those looking to get acquainted with the idiosyncrasies of one of the great religions of the world. To be blunt, this isn't a book for "idiots," but a means of foundation for the understanding of buddhism and its role in Asia historically, spiritually, and politically. Beginning with the roots taking place in India, the book expands upon key concepts like the story of Siddhartha, the goal of nirvana, the sub-branches of buddhism that sprang up, the expansion outside of India to places like Korea and Japan, and finally when Buddhism reaches the West and the contrast of its ideals set against capitalism and consumerism. The chapters are great and each end with several main talking points to make sure you understand the concepts being presented. The book is a good starting point for those interested in Buddhism and looking to expand their knowledge on the subject.
