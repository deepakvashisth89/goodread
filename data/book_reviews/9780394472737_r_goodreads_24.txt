Dear John Updike, did you really need to use that word or that word? And did you really need to create that sex scene, or omg that one?! How can you be so cruel? And why didn't you tell us how all of these crazy characters became that way? Why do some have seemingly normal parents?

I get it, you were slapping us around to get our attention. Well it worked. For me anyway. This book was not about each character's individual issues as much as it was the effect of insecurity from war, racial strife and other societal ills past and present on the entire nation. All of the characters represent more than just one person's problems.

I loved the first installment in this quartet, Rabbit Run, there I could really empathize with Harry (Rabbit), even though he was a jerk. I've had similar feelings like his where he felt the need to just get in the car and drive, to get away and think. I could see how that man, was feeling overwhelmed by life. He was in a dead end job, had a 2nd baby on the way and an alcoholic wife who just sat around drinking and watching TV. Then is selfishness eventually gets really ugly but I was along for the dangerous ride and took my licks. That's what you get for empathizing.

With this installment, It seemed like Janice and Harry were in a contest for who could be the worst parent. I disliked one or the other more in turns. My dislike never reached hate, because you could tell they wanted to do good, but just couldn't manage it. They were more pathetic than anything. They were still trying to process what happened in the first novel, and in this one they get hit with war, riots, and economic uncertainty. Harry, Janice and other people in their orbit were all trying to process so much.

Updike captured the tumultuous late 60's so well that I almost felt that I had lived them . I was too young at the time to know what was going on but I've read books and seen movies of the time. Nothing really captured how lost and confused people were as well as this. Updike shows off his skills at writing dialogue and creating vivid scenes. Even though the most shocking part of the novel comes around the 3/4 mark, I'm still reeling from it. I can't say this book is for everyone, but I found it hard to put down. It was a train wreck that I couldn't look away from. I'm glad I didn't look away because it was thought provoking and a novel I won't soon forget.
