Henry Reed, Inc. by Keith Robertson

I have an odd confession to make today. Last night, as I lay in bed falling asleep, my mind drifted back to fourth-grade English—a class taught by one of my favorite teachers ever, Mr. Whitehead. I attended Abingdon Elementary School during the 2002–2003 school year, so this was quite a while ago. However, for some reason, that year and that class have always stuck with me.

In that class, we read a children's novel of which I could only remember a few details. Last night, I asked an AI what that novel could be, and it pointed me to this book. Today, three-quarters of the way through it, I discovered it wasn't the right book after all. So, the lesson here is: don't trust AI.

But I will say that Henry Reed, Inc. is great. It's a classic, quintessential window into 20th-century American life. Taking place in the 1950s, it perfectly captures the elements of boyhood, adventure, creativity, and humor. If anything, it is a window into a simpler, happier, and better America (which, of course, never truly existed, much like "Merry Olde England").

Essentially, the book is written in an epistolary format, serving as a young boy's summer journal. Henry Reed, the son of an American diplomat, has been sent to the States to spend the summer with his aunt and uncle in New Jersey. He and his friend Midge get into a multitude of humorous, light-hearted adventures that will keep the reader smiling throughout.

I found this book by mistake, but I'm glad I did. It is charming, and it's one I'd like my children to read one day. Plus, I desperately need to pump up my Goodreads numbers!
