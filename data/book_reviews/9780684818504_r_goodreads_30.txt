It's important to bear in mind that this book is almost 100 years old, and therefore some of the author's attitudes are . . . narrow-minded, to say the least. However, Frazer is more open than usual for his time, I think, and his look at folkloric and religious customs is exhaustive. (I read the abridged version, which was over 800 pages long and meandered widely through numerous cultures, so I can only imagine what the unabridgedGolden Boughis like.) He ties a great many disparate ideas together, and for a poet and fiction writer who likes mythological themes, there's an embarrassment of richest to be had.
