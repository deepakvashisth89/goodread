This is a hard book to review for me - and I might point you out to much better versed reviews that can say how "Heinleinesque" it is and how it portrays a satire of bureaucracy, government and so on.

I unfortunately lack such depth and can share my shallow point of view.

The book should deserve a 5/5 rating. The general concepts and ideas are great. If you cut it to pieces and just take them out of context - they can be in the above average/great category. The majority of worldbuilding is fascinating. So, what doesn't work actually? Well, that is the hard question.

The book opens with interesting and tens elements of investigating an alien sighting. With terryfing aliens, uncomprehending of what are the constructions and things the main character is fiding and experiencing. Then, following main character reasearch background, some examples are being tested, and... and well now there is a jerking shift to class room years that are related to formation of goverments and responsibility and... well now there is a shift to a bi-sexual / homo-sexual encounter and... shift to actually not being in center of alien sightings but in center of a city exploring the dystopian wierd effect of how loosing 75% of population might have done to people and... jerking shift to political debates on what to do, should we do, are the aliens actually real... and...

And maybe that wouldn't be bad. You get the "whole picture" and unfortunatelly "a bit too much" fed to you how the world looks like, the "worldbuilding" is being sold to you, fed into you, in actually not totally bad way... but nothing "really" happens. Only a smither of plot brackets the novell actuall content which are lectures. Either a lecture in class, or a lecture during a political convention, or a lecture in form of a high ranked military official sitting in front of the main character with "you seem to be too stupid to get it, so I'll explain it too you". And all the "teachers" in guise of preachers, teachers, officers and scientists blend together until they feel like one and the same character - where even the main character remarks multiple times "he/she sounds just like my teacher during my studies" + of course flashback to mentioned studies.

Most of the time the "show don't tell" goes into "I'll show you how someone tells it".

And our main character is being bounced around places and settings, from teacher to preacher - sometimes with a very lousy excuse and explenation of why.

"Is anyone going to answer my question, or am I just going to be shoved from place to place until im out of everybody's way?" says main character.

And at the end - the "research" he was doing at the beginning of the book turns out to be uneventful watter drop in the ocean, where real fascinating stuff and technology development is done in the background, only to be (yes, you can guess) briefly explained to the main character by another incarnation of a "teacher".

For me this could have been a fantastic read - maybe if instead of bouncing the main character from one place to other, we sould have got a multi-pov story, where each person would be in the right place from the get go, and we could have experienced some of the more fascinating ideas in a actuall scenes not as part of someones lecture - then maybe it would have been better.
