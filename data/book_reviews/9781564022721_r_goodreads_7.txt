Diary EntryDeep in the heart of Dickon Woods there lives some very noisy but happy hedgehogs. I wonder how the rest of the forest feels about this musical band?Harry the hedgehog begins in the woods by banging his drum. Tum-tum-te-tum! Then his friend Helen joins in by making her own drum. Norbert and Billy then join the band and start drumming away. Diddle-diddle-dum! After all the drumming, they attract some other furry neighbours from the forest. All the other animals want them to STOP! It was too noisy in the forest. However, the friendly hedgehogs invite the other animals to play and they all joined in the band!I think that this is a lovely and innocent story about making friends and having fun through music. It would be ideally tailored towards Reception - Yr 2 children. Waddell’s use of personified animals is always captivating. I always find his characters very inviting and sweet, there’s often no maliciousness in the stories.The narrative is very rhythmic and flows using poetic sounds. This would be good for early KS1 to join in with to learn sounds and to follow the story. Although it is filled with ‘nonsense’ word sounds, the sounds add fun to the story.These sounds and interaction could help the children remember the story more. Often children remember stories through songs and poetic patterns.The book reminds us of similar word puzzles that Dr. Suess’ books present. Nonsense words and sounds are sometimes difficult for children to understand but it helps solidify their phonics knowledge. It also will help them exercise their phonics skills to help decode the book. The repetitiveness of the sounds will aid this.There are lots of interactive teaching activities you could do with this book. The main one that comes to mind would be to use musical instruments for the children to join in with the story (just like the other forest animals did!).
