3.5 stars -- This unique book will no doubt be used in studies of the Vietnam War for years to come. I do think the author could have polished the book a bit to more fully immerse the reader, such as asking the veterans to supply some photographs of their military days, and dispersing them throughout the text.This book grew out a simple question posed by a newspaper columnists to Vietnam veterans: Were you ever spat upon by people who opposed the war? The answers were wildly different: those who described, in detail, a spitting incident and those who just as forcefully denied ever being spat upon or knowing anyone who was.Readers' reactions to these stories will probably be just as politicized as the war itself. Some will angrily point out the spitting stories and announce that this was the real anti-war movement: ugly and vicious, and may tie it in with liberalism in general. Others will focus upon the stories written by vets who were sympathized with, and helped by, peace activists (including stereotypical-looking hippies) and those who joined groups like Vietnam Vets Against the War.As with anything else, both types of activists could no doubt be found among the throngs who opposed the Vietnam War and the draft. Teenagers and college-age people are fairly infamous for being immature, selfish bullies--and some no doubt used the anti-war movement as a convenient outlet for being a jerk or a criminal. There were cruel and nasty people in the cause just as there were level-headed and peaceful ones. Just like in every other place in society.One thing that had me consistently confused is that most of the abusive incidents happened in airports, most notably those in California. Now, I realize that in the pre-TSA era, airports were certainly not the lockdown atmosphere that we know today. However, why would airport employees allow large groups of people, who were most likely not paying customers, to gather to scream obscenities at, spit upon, and throw trash at returning soldiers? Why wouldn't they call the police to end this disruption? Also, did the demonstrators just hang around in the airports all day, waiting for returning jets containing soldiers in uniform? Certainly, the airports could have evicted them for loitering if they wanted to? There are so many questions, a word from former airport employees of the era would have been helpful.The behavior of some of the people described in this book should convince just about everyone how NOT to act if you are standing up for any sort of cause. In these stories you can also see the seeds of the modern culture wars that unfortunately define political discourse to this day.
