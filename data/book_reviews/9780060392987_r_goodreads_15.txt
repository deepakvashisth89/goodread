Really enjoyable read although seemingly less poignant somehow than other wrestling biographies I’ve read. It’s very interesting as always with these books to see the journey that the wrestler took to reach where he did.As someone from one of the biggest wrestling families of all, probably ever seeing as it continues to grow, it was fascinating seeing how he wanted to forge his own path but ultimately, he knew wrestling was his future and his calling.I really enjoyed how, unlike other books, matches were fully broken down and explained, how this was narrated in third person by The Rock in character, and they never lasted too long to be grating, and lasted just short enough for you to want another chapter. A really clever addition.I find it quite amusing how Johnson seems to take his natural athletic ability for granted: the man is a genetic freak, an absolute machine, but it’s all he knows, he doesn’t know what it’s like to not be 6’4” and jacked, and yes he works very, very hard to maintain his physique, but he was blessed with the right genetic mix from both sides of the family. He’s a once in a generation talent, and a once in a lifetime performer, and it comes as naturally to him as breathing.This is all part of the charm of The Rock, all arrogance, ego and charisma, but at his heart he is a genuine man who takes none of his fame for granted and fully understands and acknowledges everyone who helped him become who he is. And this book was released in 2000; since then he’s become an absolute megastar and he remains just as humble. I’d love for the Dwayne Johnson of 2000 to be able to see where and who he is now, and I bet while pleased, he wouldn’t be in the least bit surprised. And I’d love to think that Dwayne Johnson has read this book recently and smiles, and I hope he’s so proud of where that short tempered, rambunctious young man ended up. It’s a book like this that certainly makes the reader proud.
