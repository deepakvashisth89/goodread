Second book in the series. Second contact between the lion-like hani civilization and the Outsider, the intruder into the Compact Space who threatens the wobbly commercial alliance between competing species, both oxygen- and methane-breathing.
The book suffers from a serious middle-of-the-series syndrome in my opinion, expanding the themes from the first episode, but barely advancing the main plot. In fact, my biggest problem with the story is this main plot, a carbon-copy of , a step-by-step re-enactment of events already explained in detail there.
So I’m really having a hard time coming up with a synopsis for this second book. Captain Pyanfar Chanur is once again offering shelter to the same runaway Tully she helped in the first book, she is running into the same sort of troubles with the same Meetpoint Station and its stsho Personage, her ship once again runs into technical problems with its hyperspace drive and is forced to seek repairs on a hostile station while the kif are in hot pursuit of Tully.




While the plot is extremely familiar, the stakes are of course higher, as any space opera that respects itself will do for an encore. Instead of the fate of one human stowaway [Tully], a whole human fleet of spaceships is coming back to the Compact Space, offering either a commercial treaty or war in retaliation to the kif for killing the initial survey crews. Pyanfar Chanur, and through her, the whole hani sector of space will have to choose sides.

I still like the role reversals that made the first book such an interesting challenge: humans are the aliens, the intruders, the ugly ones in this Compact Space. Tully is not the apex of evolution, nor has he at his disposal the best technology and the best weapons. A more diplomatic approach is needed for his survival.

The second important reversal is the all female crew of the . In the hani society, males are bigger and stronger, but they have volatile tempers and short attention spans that makes them unfit for interstellar travel and for social compromises. They would rather fight for domination with other males down on their domains on the home planet. Which is why Pyanfar Chanur’s decision to save her own mate Khym Mahn from a suicidal frenzy and bring him aboard the ship as a crew member creates so many problems both for her team and for the rulers of the home planet, who value tradition above progress.

The next book is apparently heading towards Earth to negotiate the upcoming trade agreement between Earth and Compact Space. I think it will be interesting to find out how the hani/lions will view our own planet.
