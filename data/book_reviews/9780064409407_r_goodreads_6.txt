"Onward and Upward! To Narnia and the North!"

Shasta is a young boy who is in a poor living situation. He thinks he lives with his father (Arsheesh) but after a nobleman tries to buy Shasta (and Arsheesh sells him quite easily), Shasta realizes that most of what he's known is a lie.

Bree, the stallion of the nobleman, tells Shasta that the slaves are abused as well and the two of them agree to run away together.

They travel for weeks, eventually meeting other Narnians and people escaping bad situations, hoping for just a chance to reach the promised Narnia.



This one wasn't one of my favorite. I love the magic of the earlier books and these ones...I just kinda was glad it was over.

This one wasn't terrible from a plot standpoint but also... it just didn't draw me in like previous books. I think it's just cause I had no attachment to the characters.

The author keeps writing stories from different timeliness, with new characters and settings and jamming it into the Narnia series.

But to me, a series is a bunch of books that follow the same cast. I become invested in the original characters and want to see where they are for each book if the series. There are exceptions but also this is the third (fourth?) Time that we follow entirely new characters and I just feel over it.

Also, I didn't like that Shasta was from "Calorman" and how in the audiobook, it was pronounced color-men in the audiobook. And that they are all dark-skinned? I don't want to open that can of worms but i was side-eyeing Lewis for that particular naming choice.

Overall, I read it so I could complete the series but I don't think I'll be going in for a reread anytime soon.
