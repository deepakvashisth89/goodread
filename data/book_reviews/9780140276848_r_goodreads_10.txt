Very poetic, fascinating and original descriptions, very intense, but not happy.
The author is an unusual person as is her mother and her grandmother; maybe her sisters are too, I couldn't tell, but I don't think so. Mabel the grandmother has a mouth on her that reminds, fondly, of one of my relatives (can't tell you which one). This is quoted from one of Mabel's letters to Jacki (the author):
"Dearest Jack,
Who are some of these people you're down there with? Jesus, they make my hair stand on end like a spider crawled up my neck. Between you and your mother, it's a wonder I'm not dead yet. I made headcheese today. Does it ever stink! Viola next door complained she was gonna upchuck from the smell. Her ass! She cooks them TV dinners."
So grandma Mabel is this character who catches and cooks squirrels and other critters from the land, she speeds (not just a little) when she drives and of course she drinks and hides the bottles under the front porch and in the linen closet.
Then there is her daughter, the most beautiful woman in the room, a princess. But real life on Lake Puckawasay with her 3 young daughters, after her prosaic and nice husband falls off a roof and becomes deaf, is not enough. She divorces her husband and marries "the Doctor". He is rich and handsome, he punches the girls in the back of their heads when they are not looking and calls them pig and stupid. No one comes out of this new family quite the same. Dolores, the mother and subject of the book, slips out of her regular mania and becomes delusional. Manic and delusional, the Queen of Sheba, Marie Antionette. She is also very interesting with a sharp legal mind, a creativity that is amazing and energy that won't quit. Too bad she is nuts, it is hard on her family. But if she wasn't nuts she might not be nearly so interesting.
The Queen of Sheba's daughter, Jacki, the author, is oh so not dull herself. She goes to college in England, joins the rodeo back in the U.S. and eventually becomes senior correspondent for NPR and travels all over the world, much of that time in the middle east. .... A very very interesting family.
