Stirling wrote 2 books in the Brainship series by Anne McCaffrey: The City Who Fought and The Ship Avenged. Of the two, I liked this book better but its not without its problems. The City Who Fought was like a WWII adventure where the brave resistance fighters fought off the Gestapo SS in the form of the Kolnari. This book is like a post-WWII spy novel where the Kolnari are again back as the bad guys. I liked the cross and double cross of the spy novel crossed with a science fiction adventure. Despite having so much advanced technology, the vastness of space means that they have the same issues with communications as any spy in the 40's or 50's. I thought the timing of the various angents trying to work together and just missing the mark seemed realistic and added to the suspense.However, there are two main problems that made this novel just okay for me.First, the Kolnari are not interesting villains. Kolnari are descended from human colony ships but the author turned them into uber villains with no redeeming qualities whatsoever. They even eat their own babies! The result is that they have the personality of cardboard. Even the Daleks had more personality!Second, Joat didn't develop one bit between The City Who Fought and this book, although almost a decade have passed. Emotionally, she's still a twelve year old child abuse victim trying to deal with her trauma. In between the books, she manages to make zero friends and gets kicked out of school. Once she hits "adult" age, Simeon sends her off to make her own way which proved to me once and for all that Simeon was an idiot and an unfit parent. She had done nothing to show she had matured mentally or emotionally but Simeon sends her on her way regardless. Joat does take some baby steps towards maturity during this book, but it's a pity that she wasn't developed further as a character.
