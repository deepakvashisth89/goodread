This is the third consecutive book I've read where parents die at an early age, setting up the exploration of family and relationships. The characters in this novel all search for meaningful relationships. At first, the characters seemed rather flat, but by the end of the book, I really felt drawn into their longing for deeper connections with others. Nick, an orphan, keeps losing people. I admired the way he finally is able to relate to his teen-aged daughter. I really liked the image Leavitt uses to illuminate their relationship, when Nick tells about playing the game "red light, green-light" with his daughter as a little girl. It seems the game has continued, with the daughter finally seeing Nick as a green light worth responding to.Nick is a traveling book salesman. I found it interesting to see all the ways that books enter into the plot and show different facets of the characters.
