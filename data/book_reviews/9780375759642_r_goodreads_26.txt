“I don’t care about truth, I want some happiness.”So says Gloria Gilbert Patch in this 1922 publication, written when the author was 26 years old. Gloria was loosely based upon Fitzgerald’s wife Zelda and this story has some autobiographical elements as the reader learns of wild times with some idle rich in the Jazz Age a hundred years ago.I’m sure over the years I have angered and mystified English and Literature teachers with my takes on books (I know I have from the DMs - Ha! I do read those) and I will here again - I read this as a dark comedy.Yes, there is the cautionary tale of how idleness and debauchery and a certain nihilism will destroy lives, too much partying and drinking will bankrupt souls as quickly as a checking account; but Fitzgerald’s imaginative and innovative way with how he describes the events was golden and - made me laugh.Yes, I have a dark and twisted sense of humor but here I could see that Scott was my people, sharing for us a broken landscape with a ice cream truck riding through it, blaring children’s songs while driving over the wreckage of a once great civilization.But, try these quotes on for size and see how they fit -“Here's to alcohol, the rose colored glasses of life.”“I shall go on shining as a brilliantly meaningless figure in a meaningless world.”"A stout woman upholstered in velvet, her flabby cheeks too much massaged, swirled by with her poodle straining at its leash - the effect being given of a tug bringing in an ocean liner.”And more than just a dark comedy, there was some vitriolic satire, some acerbic irony that would make Vonnegut wince. We see the Patch’s shambling through life with a ridiculous bank account but with no moral compass, no sense of belonging and no happiness.Here’s the darkest comedic takeaway from this book - it’s terrifyingly relevant to our society now. And I’m not hating on rich folks, rather the idea that wealth can bring happiness and yet, almost without exception, Fitzgerald demonstrates that without purpose, there can be no real contentment and the abyss will not just stare back at you but will ooze over to the bar and mix white Russians.PS - also, as a Veteran I enjoyed the descriptions of basic training during WWI.Very good.
