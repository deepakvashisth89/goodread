I'm running out of her books, sadly. I've read most of them, so I'm down to the lesser known ones, or the ones that didn't necessarily appeal as much to me going by the publisher's descriptions.Thankfully, this is written by the brilliant Pat Highsmith, so even if it's lesser known, it isn't less compelling. I have had the most luxurious weekend finishing this oddly prescient novel that explores the dangers of Christian fundamentalism.Richard Alderman has two sons, Robbie and Arthur. Robbie ends up sick in hospital with an infection, and it's very touch-and-go. Richard says a prayer, and Robbie turns the corner. Robbie comes home from the hospital, a healthy fourteen year old boy. Richard is transformed too - into a "man of God".Arthur, his eldest son, doesn't see things the same way. While his father now reads magazines called "Plain Truth" and "The Waylighter", even donning slippers with criss-crossed leather straps (a biblical look?), Arthur studies the writings of Jacques Monod, Nobel prize winning French biologist.You know that this won't go well, and it doesn't.Now that Richard is "born again", there's an odd assortment of people who knock at the Alderman residence. Soon, Robbie is behaving strangely. Arthur just wants to be happy with his girlfriend Maggie, and looks forward to his first year at university, but there's an ominous storm brewing.If you've ever experienced the intensity of fundamentalism, or if you've noticed it seep into the current political arena, this book will ring a few familiar bells of warning.It's not a "thriller" (Pat didn't always write thrillers, so please adjust your expectations) but it's as engrossing and insightful as she can be. God, I love reading her words! :)
