The very audacity that a white person's voice is stronger and louder than the collective suffering of the underclass makes this a discomforting read. What annoys me most about this book is that there are many authentic 'Black' voices that have been silenced throughout the years, yet this poorly written book is one that is held up as a beacon of what repression, racism and discrimination feels like - a phenomenon that basically says that unless you are white and experience suffering, then your account is somehow diminished or of lesser value.
