I am attempting to do a Classics challenge mid year through, since I completed the British Lit course halfway through this year. So this book fulfills the category of Short Story collection and I chose Faulkner because this author has been recommended to me so many times and I have just been too intimidated to read his works.

I figured a short story collection should be a good starter and I was right. I had already read “Barn Burning” and “A Rose for Emily” previously for class of some sort, but the other stories were new to me. Here is the list of stories in this collection:
1) Barn Burning
2) Two Soldiers
3) A Rose for Emily
4) Dry September
5) That Evening Sun
6) Red Leaves
7) Lo!
8)Turnabout
9) Honor
10) There Was a Queen
11) Mountain Victory
12) Beyond
13) Race at Morning

Possibly because I was most familiar with it, “Barn Burning” was my favorite. But right alongside that was “Mountain Victory” and “Turnabout”. “Red Leaves” was disturbing, or rather more disturbing than the others for me, and I really disliked the protag for “Honor” so I just didn’t really get into it. I liked “There was a Queen” and “Race at Morning” too. So all in all, I enjoyed most of these stories.

In case you did not know, Faulkner is dark. Maybe because he is also a Southern writer, I felt a lot of the same “anxiety” that I felt when reading Flannery O’Connor. Possibly this is because Southern writers write about people of color and when people of color pop up in stories written before 1970, very rarely are there happy endings.

What did I learn/like about Faulkner? I loved his descriptive writing, his “deadpan” manner of delivering disturbing details and protag thoughts. I loved his use of dialog, despite the heavy use of the N word (this is the South after all) and the very realistic dialectal speech. I really loved the way he depicted characters truly having trouble absorbing new information or bad news. They would say the same thing over and over, unable to move forward. I loved the intensity of the prose, how as a reader, I could sense that Faulkner did not choose words as a matter of fact. He was very specific.

So will I tackle a Faulkner novel? Of course I will at some point. But sometimes, more often than not recently, I like to read about daisies and roses and pots of gold at the end of a rainbow. Thus, I will probably not even think about it until next year.

But this was a great collection. Heavier themes and topics, no happy endings. LOL HIgh recommendation as it is excellent literature. Four stars just because not every story was fantastic. 4/5
