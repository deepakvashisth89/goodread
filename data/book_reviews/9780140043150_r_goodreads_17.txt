This book was recommended to me because of my love of the naturalist-essayist John Burroughs, a sort of eastern John Muir. But I think the reason I love Burroughs is what I didn't love about this book--which keeps its eye on the sea and the sky and very little on the human beings and their activities. It's an exercise in minimalism--how much can you write about the observable landscape. I found little bits of it to be interesting, but mostly looking at a deserted beach in various weathers.Whereas Burrough's essays are rich in the human response to nature as well as nature itself, in the doings of humans as well as the birds and animals, streams and woods. I didn't make it to spring.
