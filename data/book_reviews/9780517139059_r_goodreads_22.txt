Rating: 3 stars

I went into this book completely blind. Not knowing the premise, what characters it entailed, or even what era of Star Trek it was focusing on. (You could say that Kirk on the cover was a dead giveaway, but Star Trek is notorious for slapping well known characters on the covers of books they have little or nothing to do with.) Having gone in with no expectations, I come out the other end feeling exactly whelmed. Not overwhelmed, not underwhelmed, just whelmed. This book wasn't bad by any stretch of the imagination, but it also isn't up there with some of the best the novels have to offer. More often than not, that is how I come away from media tie in novels. Most of them are not bad, but each opinion may be affected by my fondness of the franchise as a whole, so it has to be particularly terrible for me to give it anything under 3 stars.

I have another comment that doesn't fit anywhere but I want to mention it because it stood out to me. This book was a little more gory than the regular novels tend to be. A couple of scenes were particularly descriptive involving body mutilation and near death experiences with said injuries. Yes, people were "wounded" in (classic) all the time, but it usually involved paint-red blood, palm punches, and dramatic collapsing, not severed arms with fleshy tendrils. The character in this book is so beaten up Kirk says it looks like a corpse is speaking. But oddly, this is the only violently descriptive part of the book. There are no other instances of brutality this intense in any other situation. I have no idea why that is.
