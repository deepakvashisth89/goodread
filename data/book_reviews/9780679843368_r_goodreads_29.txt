"A Wolf and Little Daughter" is an African American folktale set in a cabin in the woods where papa, as a worried father, warned Little Daughter not to go out the gate where there are wolfs, Little Daughter never did. One day papa had to leave to do something, Little Daughter thought it wouldn't harm anyone if she went outside the gate to get some flowers, so she did. Little Daughter heard a noise and saw a big bad wolf. The wolf would tell Little Daughter to sing the sweet song she sang when she was picking flowers, so Little Daughter did.

Every time Little Daughter sang the wolf left and Little Daughter tip-toed towards the gate. Little Daughter got to the point were she was so close to the gate that when the wolf was ooming she slide right into the gate and shut it, crack, right into the wolfs big fat face. Little Daughter was sweetest goodest safe.

As I read through "A Wolf and Little Daughter" I noticed it was a folktale as it includes the four elements of a folktale. First it includes the supernatural of a talking wolf: "The wolf said to her, in a low, gruff voice´Sing that sweetest goodest song again" (Page 61). It does not have an specific author: "None of these stories were ever written for children. They were just told; so I re-did them" (Author Story). Then its based on African Americans' during slavery: "In 1985 she published The People could Fly (were A Wolf and Little Daughter is) a collection of African American folktales dating from the time of slavery" (About the Author). Last but not least it teaches the theme of obeying your parents when they tell you not to do something: "Papa told Little Daughter never to go out the gate alone" (Page 60).
