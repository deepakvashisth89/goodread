This is a beautifully illustrated, coffee table book about the history of Arctic exploration in search of the Northwest Passage. The text is fairly simple, and will not offer much new for people familiar with the subject. It is, however an excellent introductory text for those with only a passing familiarity with the subject. Recent archeological discoveries in the Arctic have of course made the chapters on the Franklin Expedition somewhat outdated, but host is not the fault of the author and the book I still worth reading nonetheless. The real joy of this volume comes from the illustrations. Portraits of the men involved in the exploration are combined with examples of drawings by explorers and paintings and other works of art about the locations and events of explorations, and with photographs of relevant artifacts make for an engaging presentation of the subject matter. My one complaint is that the Canadian Arctic archipelago is a large and not very well known landscape. The book would have benefited from more and better detailed maps to help the reader orient themselves in the region. All in all, however, Delgado has presented a great introduction to the search for the Northwest Passage.
