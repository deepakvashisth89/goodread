So probably about 45 years ago, one year for Christmas Dad gave me a copy of this hardcover book that I'm pretty sure he'd picked up from a remainder table at the local K-Mart or Waldenbooks or some such.



And I thanked him for it, put it on my shelf, and never got around to reading it; and now that copy is long since lost to the ages, but I've gotten around to actually reading it.

And I can see why I didn't make any headway with it when I was young, and it's probably just as well that I didn't read it back then, but now I'm very glad to have done so.

This is a sort of brief coda or finale to White's ; but apparently it was never completed to his satisfaction and wasn't published until the 1970s, a full decade and more after his death; and, importantly, I don't think it stands alone.

Events in this book overlap the events in the final section of Once & Future King -- Arthur and his surviving knights are preparing to fight Mordred and his supporters (as per the end of Once & Future King) when Merlyn (who'd been seduced and imprisoned by Nimue many years ago) reappears to have another series of chats with Arthur about the duties & responsibilities of kingship, and again sends him off to learn from animals by being transformed to join them.

And if parts of this book -- Arthur's time with the ants and with the geese -- seem familiar, it's because White ended up repurposing them into The Sword in the Stone when he was dissatisfied with how Book of Merlyn was turning out. And honestly, those sojourns in particular might be impactful when they take place at the of Arthur's reign (and in the aftermath of Guinevere's infidelity with Lancelot) rather than preceding it.

And while it's a shame that White never managed to hammer this into the shape he wanted, I'm glad that it's available to be read.
