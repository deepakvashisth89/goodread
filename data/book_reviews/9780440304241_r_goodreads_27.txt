3.5, rounded up.

I struggled to connect with MacArthur as a subject, but I think that's more of an issue with MacArthur as a human than Manchester as a biographer. Hard to get into his inner mind.

What I particularly enjoyed was building some new knowledge about the role of the Philippines in WW2 and the "Pacific Theatre" in general. Also, MacArthur's role in the recovery of Japan was fascinating.

This book seemed to have a quality gradient where it was weaker in the start, stronger in the end. Likely true of all biographies (who really cares that much about what a subject's parents did before they were born) but it was particularly true in this one.

Still, many bright spots and I love Grover Gardner's narration of anything.
