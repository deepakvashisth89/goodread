“It wasn’t until 1987 that the United Nations released its full list of fugitive Nazi and Japanese war criminals, forty thousand of them .”

The hunt for a brutal Chechen gangster trafficking prostitutes and sex slaves out of Bosnia into Edinburgh crosses the paper trail of the interminably slow but relentless investigation into WW II war criminals. A clever and intriguing seed around which one might imagine a thoroughly engrossing police procedural cum suspense thriller plot, wouldn’t you say? It certainly caught my attention!

Brilliant character development? Check!
Convincing, credible, ofttimes humorous and sarcastic dialogue? Check!
Atmospheric settings? Check!

Great execution of a compelling plot? Not so much, I’m afraid. For my tastes, a much more accurate description would be tortuous, byzantine, and, all too often, plodding and over-extended.

Notwithstanding the obvious positives related to Rankin’s writing, this is now the third time I’ve experienced that reaction with the DI John Rebus series (as opposed to my almost universally positive reaction with the likes of Michael Connelly’s Harry Bosch or Lisa Gardner’s Detective DD Warren). So I’m officially declaring an end to my efforts with Ian Rankin and clearing my TBR shelf with a multi-volume visit to the local Little Free Library box.
