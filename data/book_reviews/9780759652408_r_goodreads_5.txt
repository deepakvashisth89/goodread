Crack this to journey back to the early ‘90s when Rita Rudner ruled stand-up comedy. Most of us caught her on HBO or A&E. This book compiles 45 short essays on everything from the uselessness of bikinis to the perils of cooking. One essay has her yearning for a scenario where others’ exercise benefits her.

In a flight of fancy, she describes gym promotions she’d love: “Join now! Get last year free!” Other favorites include: “Sign up today and get a free racquetball membership for any friend you have who’s over ninety.” Another gem: “No money down! You won’t come anyway.”

She imagines how much easier it would be to ride in a car with a man if the car had separate bedrooms. She recounts cooking a massive Thanksgiving turkey one year. If it turned out tough, she planned to trick her British-born husband into thinking the pilgrims liked their turkey chewy.

Admittedly, this book won’t leave you screaming with laughter or gasping for breath in every essay. Still, despite its 33-year run, it delivers plenty of smiles and a few laugh-out-loud moments. My favorite is her take on labor and delivery:

“I know there are all these wonderful birthing methods where you put the baby in water or lay it on your stomach and pour warm mushroom barley soup over it to ease the poor unsuspecting thing into the real world. I’m in favor of a more straightforward approach to life. I think the delivery room should have traffic noise and pollution, and you should immediately put the baby on the phone and have somebody be rude to it. The baby should then have the option to go back in.”

Much of this still feels four-star fresh after nearly a third of a century. If you read it, you’ll likely find plenty of reasons to smile and a few to laugh out loud.
