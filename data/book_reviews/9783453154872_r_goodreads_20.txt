Highly recommend. Not the finest piece of literature, but I think Wangerin accomplishes exactly what he set out to do, communicate the Bible as one linear narrative without excessive embellishment. I was often struck by how faithful he was to the Biblical text, which I genuinely appreciated. For a creative and gifted writer, it must have been difficult to essentially transcribe someone else's words, but at many points that's what was required.The Gospels is likely where he takes the most creative license, but I think he does it in a wise way, and really he does the astoundingly monumental task of trying to integrate John's Gospel with the Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke), which the average Bible reader might not realize the difficulty of such a task. I thought it was absolutely genius how he weaved events and teachings from earlier in Jesus ministry into whatever was going on in the present. Especially when characters or themes were overlapping. Brilliant.I also greatly appreciated the prophetic section of the Old Testament. Maybe it's because I'm currently on a kick regarding the prophets, but those chapters alone are worth the price of admission. He masterfully sets both the narrative of the two kingdoms as well as the specific prophets and their teachings within the larger, global historical/political machinations that are mostly implicit in the Bible, but are fascinating in their own right, as well as invaluable in understanding much of those texts (not to mention instructive for our own day and age).I think I will make it a habit to photocopy pertinent sections of the OT from this book as supplementary reading for any Bible Studies I lead.
