Ludwig Wittgenstein was a major figure of contemporary philosophy. Two of his works were salient, one being the Philosophical Investigations. It's about the limits of algorithmical thinking and the values of context, rhetoric, and flexible rules. Thus, it's recommended for anyone interested in questions of logic, meaning, and adapting to an age where machines are everywhere.

Philosophical Investigations is composed of short thoughts, ordered by numbers and reunited in two parts. The reader goes back and forward between them as if revising Lego pieces. Unfortunately, Ludwig Wittgenstein didn't finish it, and he avoided formalism, which makes it supple and processual. The Romanian translation follows the English one, not the German original.

The deliberative and critical perspective is intriguing. He invented situations and used an imaginary ethnography to investigate the meaning of worlds. Semantics and practice were linked, for Ludwig Wittgenstein. His approach was tentative and illustrative. In , the author avoided any rigid new theory (Adrian-Paul Iliescu, 2004).

Often, the meaning is how we use words in certain contexts. These cases are called language games, they are relational and rule-dependent. Between them, for Ludwig Wittgenstein, there are family resemblances, though not universal categories. A general theory is to be avoided, an important premise of (Adrian-Paul Iliescu, 2004).

The result may be to gain a detachment from difficult-to-solve questions, such as how to be good and how to act well. There is no general answer for Ludwig Wittgenstein; only vaguely related situations. While we can sharpen our personality, there is always the risk of relativism and abolishing all rules. Be as it may, I found that is always an interesting book to read.
