“Unit 731” was a heavy, informative read. I knew some of the experiments that took place, but I had no idea about the amount of people killed with diseases outside the unit. I was also shocked but also not truly surprised to learn that the US knew a lot of what was happening and just did nothing.

I think the first 2/3 of the book is a lot more interesting, partially because it was a lot more understandable. The last 1/3 is mainly describing the Tokyo Trial and the lies of the US government. It’s confirmed that they were aware of the biological warfare, but not the exact extent. I wouldn’t doubt that the US knew everything but decided to grant most of the war criminals immunity anyway. With that said, the trial chapters are almost entirely different statements from US officials where they’re using loopholes and semantics to try and claim they had no idea without lying. It was intentionally confusing, but I wish the authors would have done a better job of explaining what maneuvering was done in each statement. Typically the statement would kind of be left to stand on its own with the lie/omission supposed to be this sort of blatant wow moment, but I know so little about legal documents that it didn’t read that way to me.

I do appreciate that the authors didn’t use the experiments as a sort of shock factor. Obviously they were all horrific—literally crimes against humanity—but the authors don’t include fictionalized descriptions of what the victims went through. I’d be lying if I said I didn’t have some morbid curiosity about the human experimentation though. It’s hard to conceive that people could be so evil that I feel like I need to see/read proof to believe it. Despite my curiosity, I did appreciate the focus on how Unit 731 came to be, how it operated, how it was hidden, and how the perpetrators largely got off with no consequences (and were even rewarded in some cases).

If you’re looking for a dark, informative book to read then you’ll probably enjoy this. It’s not the easiest, most comprehensive nonfiction book I’ve read, but it’s also on a very secretive part of Japan’s history so I think they did a good job with what they had. It was also written in 1989 so I imagine more has come to light since then.
