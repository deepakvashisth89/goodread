I read tons of horsey books as a kid. If it had anything to do with horses, I read it.* Somebody's Horse was one of my favorites. Jenny takes responsibility for Farfalla, spends her money on him, spends her time with him, nurses him, cares for him, trains him...in short, everything I wished I could do!Jenny is a great character. She's hot-tempered, headstrong, and sometimes selfish, a combination that often lands her in trouble. But she's also great with her cousin's young kids and willing to work hard. She faces up to several challenges including making new friends and competing in barrel racing. As a kid I loved living through Jenny, forging ahead on this adventure with a horse all her own. As an adult I thought that Jenny's tantrums were genuine without making her unlikeable. I can still identify with being thirteen and thinking the world was unfair. I enjoyed that Jenny doesn't succeed at everything she tries, and that she does the right thing knowing there's a good chance she'll lose Farfalla.Minor note: Jenny spends the summer with her cousin Lorelei, "the one whose husband got killed." Lorelei has two young children, works at a newspaper she and her late husband founded, writes books in her spare evenings, and just bought a ramshackle house she's enthusiastic about fixing up. I wonder why the author chose to remove the husband from the story? A husband whom Lorelei doesn't spend any time mourning on the page. I mean, I applaud the confident, optimistic, go-getting single mother character because I imagine there weren't (aren't?) many of those in juvenile fiction, but what was the purpose? Or maybe that was the purpose, all by itself?*Though I never got into the Saddle Club series for some reason. Maybe because I automatically lumped it into the same bucket with the Babysitter's Club books, which I didn't like at all.
