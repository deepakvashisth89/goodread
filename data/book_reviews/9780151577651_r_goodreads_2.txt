Who is the mysterious myrrhophore Mary Magdalen? Haskins explains and explores the way Mary’s misidentification stresses the concerns and comforts of western culture across the ages.

In the roles of ascetic contemplator, dignified patroness, or sensual penitent, Mary attracts respective admirers. Culture, scripture, faith, myth, and metaphor interlace to create a woman so arresting, so compelling that her name elicits an instant and vivid mental picture. Haskins traces the saint’s depictions in art and literature, connecting these to contemporary dogma.

The point-missing and confusion that has surrounded Mary’s reputation overlays neatly, painfully, with the feminine Christian dilemma between Madonna or whore. Haskins carries this thread through the final chapter, where she finally addresses her personal understanding of Mary Magdalen and ponders a future where her true identity is celebrated.

(Also, wherever something entirely sinister and bizarre is afoot, the Victorians are always at the scene of the crime.)
