This is quite an epic! The story of poet Lucien Chardon and his friend David Sechard, son of a printer. Lucien is a really annoying character in many ways, good looking and full of himself, easily led and quite able to use those closest to him when it suits, leading to disadvantage for David, who marries Lucien’s sister, Eve. There’s three parts to this novel. It begins with Two Poets, Lucien and David’s lives in Angouleme. The second part is A Distinguished Provincial in Paris, Lucien’s move to Paris and his attempts at a career as a writer then in journalism. The third is called Eve and David, the couple have been ruined by Lucien borrowing money from them in Paris. Much of this reminded me of Dickens in a way, innocent people being used by moneylenders and lawyers and other corrupt devious businessmen in a never ending effort of exploitation. Much snobbery from the old aristocracy looking down their noses at working people and the middle classes. I did get a bit bogged down in the printing and paper making details and some of the money stuff is dry in the detail. Overall an enjoyable read.
