This is a very clear, surprisingly readable account of the early stages of the French Revolution. It covers the financial crisis, the calling of the Estates-General and events up to the confinement of the royal family in the Tuileries. It is, though, a product of its time. It was published in 1939 when the prospect of war was becoming a gloomy certainty for France. This may explain the slightly incongruous decision to focus in the conclusion on the Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen.
