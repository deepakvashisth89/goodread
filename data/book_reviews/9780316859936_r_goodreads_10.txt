To me this isn't one of Linda Grant's best books although there is a lot of fine writing which looks forward to her later books. The two narrators were convincing and struck the right balance of funny and sad. The book told us a lot about the experience of Jews who had to uproot and make the best out of their sometimes random landing-placea, and about the never-resolved feelings about loss and dislocation which are passed on to the next generation. The experience of the male narrator in the Israeli army I thought was particularly powerful and also his reasons for never ever speaking about it to anybody. However I found it a bit patchy, with some passages feeling a bit like being lectured at with sociological and historical data, and there was a bit too much sex for my liking. (I'm not prudish - I just think it's hard to write about well, and is often better left to the imagination.)
