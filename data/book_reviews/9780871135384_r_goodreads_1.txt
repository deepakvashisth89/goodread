Well, T***p is president so I wanted to read something funny and feminist to lift my spirits. I found this in a used bookstore and thought it would be fun to read some '90s feminism. I was just a babe back then, and I was raised in a Rush Limbaugh loving household, so I thought it would be cool to go live the '90s through a feminist lens.Oof. It was really cringeworthy at times. I'm grateful that feminists have worked harder now to be a little more inclusive and insightful. (Though there's still work to be done feminists!) What I got out of this was that feminism in the 1990s meant white, straight, and rich and still bound to some patriarchal anchors.For one, she has a whole chapter on dieting and exercise and hating her body. MASSIVE SIDE EYE: women are more than a number on a scale and stretch marks. Sure, we are, as a whole, still struggling with body image, but we definitely don't need our liberated feminists reinforcing that.She also has a overly sympathetic chapter on Woody Allen. While she is skeeved he married his step daughter, she casually dismisses child abuse claims as coming from a deranged jilted ex wife. Yeah, no. Now that his kids are grown, it's pretty clear he's a child rapist, as his daughter has so painfully written about.She has a chapter about the misogyny of gay men. And I agree there's casual misogyny in the gay community. But then she goes on to stereotype them really badly, feminize them, and go on to say that it is just in men's natures to be promiscuous and see what happened with AIDS? Ummmm. Perhaps men, both gay and straight, are affected by patriarchal ideas of men and sexuality--I mean, COME ON.And she loves Bill Clinton, her hero. He can do no wrong. He's here to rescue us from the decades of Republican rule. Ok, I can kind of forgive it. I'm sure it seemed very hopeful and people have a tendency to back a candidate and then never, ever question his decisions. Still happens. But damn-- the war on drugs, DOMA, Don't ask don't tell, Bosnia, Somalia....the man was no liberal savior.The chapter on the LA Riots was the worst. It's like, rich people are saying racist things and we are watching this from our comfy homes and it's terrible, but I'll crack some jokes about it. Great ally work there! That's probably why my generation is still having to deal with this crap.She talks about being poor, briefly, but then it's about being rich. She disparages women who wear makeup as insecure. She says it's in our nature to want babies (I still don't want one!) and is there any mention of women of color? Nope. Not really. Though Anita Hill is mentioned favorably, but her race is never considered in how the country demonized her.So why did I give it any stars? It was amusing at times. It's not totally off the mark. It was easy to read. It IS a slice of history. And ultimately, it's a reminder that we have advanced in the movement, but we still have a lot of work to do to make sure we squash these attitudes for good.
