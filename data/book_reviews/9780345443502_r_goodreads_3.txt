This was really great. The voice of Pilate was so authentic, I totally believed it was his actual memoirs. Such a fresh and interesting look at the life of Jesus Christ through the eyes of the man who sentenced him to death by crucifixion.

The author weaves into the memoirs, reports from eyewitnesses to Pontius Pilate, and letters he received about Jesus. Pilate was not too pleased with his posting as governor of Judea, and encountered frequent troubles with the people who resented Rome's presence in their land, and Pilate as the representative of Rome.

In this book, Pilate comes across as a very sympathetic character who did his best to avoid becoming involved in the affairs of the Carpenter from Galilee, and came to regret the part he eventually played in the fulfillment of prophecies contained in a book in which he did not believe, about the arrival of God's messiah.

Mills does a great job of imagining around the historical facts, and I would recommend this to not only to Christians, but to anyone interested in history. Jesus Christ was and always will be a very polarizing figure. Mills avoided being preachy and drawing conclusions, instead allowing the events and the characters to speak for themselves. Really great. I'll probably read it again.
