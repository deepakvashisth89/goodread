This edition is probably of most interest to the one interested in the narratives from the Silmarillion as it contains in their nascent forms the three key stories of that cycle. Once again, I find Christopher’s commentary valuable in piecing together the meta-narrative of his father’s life and time while he wrote these stories first and then fitted them into the frame of the Lost Tales told to Eriol/Ælfwine. Of most intrigue to me were the last two chapters devoted to Eärendel’s story— at least, what poems and outlines there were— and to the link of the faerie stories to England, specifically the Angles. Much to think about after and one can see how, by 1937, this certainly was not the world written in The Hobbit, but the germ of that connection is revealed.
