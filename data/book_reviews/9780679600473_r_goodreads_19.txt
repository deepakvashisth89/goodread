I now understand why Jane Jacobs is a legend in the urban planning space.Jacobs thoroughly outlines what she believes to the problem in cities nowadays and presents a beautiful vision of what cities should look like. She argues for diversity and liveliness which make cities sustainable, interesting, and safe. This sharply contrasts the dull, segregated layouts of many North American suburbs. It is fascinating that this book was written in the 1960s yet feels so relevant and fresh. Many of the problems facing planners in the past still persist today. I also really enjoyed Jacobs conversational and witty writing style; it made the book an easy read.I did find the book a bit long, and I preferred the first half over the second. The second half became a bit more technical as to Jacobs different proposal on how to address common problems in planning, such as unslumming or city governing. I found it a bit hard to understand though it may be because this is my first book on urban planning. I also found Jacob's vision for a successful city to be quite unrealistic. Though each aspect of her solutions made sense, I would find it so impossibly difficult to implement. At least she addresses how complex of a problem it is to build a city in the last chapter. Good thing I'm not an urban planner! ;)I loved this book and would recommend it to anyone who has an urban planning itch to scratch and willing to commit to a nearly 500 page read. Watch as Jane Jacobs masterfully changes how you view cities.
