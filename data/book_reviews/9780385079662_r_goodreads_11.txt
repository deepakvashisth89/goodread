After Doris Lessing’s death, The Guardian published an article about the contents of her library. One of her bookshelves held many works by Idries Shah, an author I hadn’t heard of before. I stand in awe of Lessing's sharp mind, and since she seemed to be a fan of his, I decided to add this book to my TBR.Here’s what Lessing said about it:"I met Idries Shah because ofThe Sufis, which seemed to me the most surprising book I had read, and yet it was as if I had been waiting to read just that book all my life.""The Sufis" is a very interesting read. It explores Sufism, the mystical side of Islam, including its practices and philosophy, while also tracing how it spread into Europe through Spain and Italy. Shah sheds light on alchemy, the troubadours, chivalric stories, witchcraft, and other medieval practices, as well as Freemasonry, Rosicrucianism, and other (more or less secret) organizations and movements. He posits that these are remnants of the Saracen influence in Europe, originating from Sufism but losing some of the Sufi essence due to the conditions in which they were propagated.This is not an easy read (the points Shah makes are often very elusive), but it’s an enlightening one, and I can now see why Lessing felt as she did.
