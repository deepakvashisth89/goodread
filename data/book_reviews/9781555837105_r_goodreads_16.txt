This is the first in the Dave Brandstetter mysteries, and eventually, I'll read more of them. Dave is like all those Humphrey Bogart PIs -- noir, but on the light side, and with a '60s/'70s bent. And, he's gay in an era when being out isn't done.Brandstetter is an insurance claims guy in a California beach community working in his father's firm, and his job in this case is to look into a death before the company pays out on the insurance. It's clearly a murder, the police haven't dug deep enough, and Dave wants to get to the bottom of it before his company cuts a check. Because the victim was also gay and owned a popular gay bar, Dave can call upon many friends in the community to help him solve the crime, capturing that world in that era in what is now a perfect amber for a reader today. He also works with local police, who clearly know his work and respect him. I loved that the ones who knew Dave was gay didn't seem to care. I also loved that while his father wasn't happy about it, he handled it, partly because he clearly had an ethic that you love your kid no matter what, and partially because he knew Dave was good at what he did.What seems to be a cut-and-dry shooting by a "rent boy" is, of course, not so cut and dry, but along the way, the author manages to point the finger at everyone from the rent boy's former young wife to the victim's mother and almost everyone in between, and it's fun watching Dave piece together the clues in a story that ends up in a "beauty contest" in the area's biggest gay bar.For a book written in 1975, I'm pretty sure this was a rather daring publication. It was well worth it then, and it still is.
