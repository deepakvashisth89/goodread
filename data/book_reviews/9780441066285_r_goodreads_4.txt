“Black Knight in Red Square” is #2 in Stuart Kaminski’s long Rostnikov series. Like previous episodes and subsequent ones, it’s chock full of wry observations of life in communist Moscow, gentle and dry Russian humor, and solid police work colored (and hampered) by politics.The story begins with the deaths of several foreign attendees of an international film festival held in a Moscow hotel. Inspector Rostnikov is given the case to investigate, mainly because his boss has it out for him, it’s politically charged, and the chance of it being solved to anyone’s satisfaction is remote. Concurrently, the terrorist group responsible for the murders has something even bigger in mind, and it’s up to the old, burly inspector with the bad leg to stop them. With able help from his second in command, the ghoul-like Karpo, and young detective Tkach, Rostnikov figures it out and Moscow is saved.I’ve become addicted to this series, even though I’ve had to bounce around in it due to availability issues. The stories are always interesting, I enjoy the dry humor that results from interactions between Rostnikov and his politically oriented superiors, and the ensemble cast is excellent. Black Knght is a fine addition to the series.
