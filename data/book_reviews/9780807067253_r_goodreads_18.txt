When this book was published in 1976, it may have been revealing truths about motherhood no one was talking about, but the blurb from Adrienne Rich is a testament to the lack of literature on the subject at the time. While Lazarre's message is an important one, it can be summed up as follows: Along with the joy that a baby brings comes anger and impatience with baby's needs which eradicate mothers' personal space and time, a loss of self, and struggle with partners over roles and responsibilities. I wish there had been a well written story to go along with this message, but this book would make any English teacher write in red pen, "Show, don't tell."
