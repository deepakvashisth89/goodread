This is book number 32 in the Oz series, and by this point there’s very much a blueprint for success that Thompson is following. Although there are also a few unique bits and bobs that are worth a mention, too.You can expect to see a bunch of old friends here, most notably Randy and Kabumbo, who end up meeting the titular silver princess and her fire-breathing horse. The main quest here is the attempt to get them back home, because if they’re away for more than a week then they’ll stop moving.It’s a pretty good story, and I liked Princess Planetty and the fact that there’s an extra-terrestrial vibe was also a nice touch. Then we have the fact that my particular edition was quite an interesting one, a spin on the whole public domain thing from Empty Grave Publishing.From what I understand, the guy who runs the press was upset that some of the Oz books that he remembered from his youth were no longer in print, so he decided to print them himself. While doing so, he also made some edits to the content to “change some distracting and very non-Oz words and phrases”. Basically, he took out references to characters being black.Oh, he also added a short story of his own, which was terrible. But I won’t let that affect the rating that I gave the story itself, because that was all I was interested in anyway. Yeah…
