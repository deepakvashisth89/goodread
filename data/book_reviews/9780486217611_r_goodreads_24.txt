Schopenhauer offers a surprisingly coherent philosophical worldview and a perfect example of German idealism from 19th century. He takes most of the Kantian philosophy - including the world as our representation - and then adds “Will” as the thing-in-itself about which he says a lot, Plato's ideas, Indian and Buddhist philosophies, romanticism, pessimism, Goethe, dislike of Hegel, and so on, and creates the “worst of possible worlds”.To me Schopenhauer represents the transition from Kant to Nietzsche. The young Nietzsche was deeply impressed by Schopenhauer when he discovered him by chance. Even if not exactly the same, the “Will” transforms into “Will to Power”, the cyclical/reincarnation into the “eternal return of the same”, and pessimism into nihilism. On the other side, it seems that Schopenhauer's approach to Plato and Kant, along with some positivism, also passed to Nietzsche.This book is an easy and a nice read. It offers an interesting, tempting, and coherent worldview for anyone interested in philosophical ideas and a practical treaty on how to live one's life.
