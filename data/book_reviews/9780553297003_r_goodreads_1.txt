This was so-so to reread years later. The author had, at times, rather a flair for dialog, but for the most part the writing was about standard. The anthropological aspect and even the women's volleyball had potential as topics, but Laura, the heroine, was singularly naive and tedious. Her past affair with the not-so-nice murdered professor was not beyond belief, given that grad students do have affairs with less than admirable faculty (in fact the Chronicle of Higher Education just wrote up a murder-suicide of that ilk), but it was hard to believe that she could be on the one hand conventionally gorgeous (tall, blonde, thin) and nonetheless be convinced that her height made her unattractive. I mean, I could believe this about a shy high-school student, but not about a former sports star in her late twenties. So... Laura goes about being a stereotypically dumb blonde right and left and naively pining for any male over 6' as if men of that height were rare or something. Thus: not a dreadful book, but one with numerous flaws.
