The blurb describes Lyons as "rightful heir" to Chandler and Macdonald. However,False Pretensesreads as if it was written by somebody who'd never actually read those authors, but instead wrote what they thought was a "typical" hard boiled PI novel based on cliches they gleamed from formula TV crime dramas and buddy cop films. The basic premise is actually pretty good: One day PI Jacob Asch is hired by a man to follow his wife for a day, because the man says he thinks she's cheating on him. He does so but observes no hanky panky, and when he returns to his office he finds the man who'd hired him that morning sitting behind his desk with his brains blown out.Alas, the plot of the mystery doesn't really take off much from there. About half the novel is taken up with Asch's infatuation with a lady police detective who is drawn like an inane male fantasy: the mythical statuesque blonde with a smokin' bod and [figurative] balls of steel, who can drink and hang with all the boys, but is still somehow totally interested in getting into bed with our schlubby PI hero and doing it all night long with him (trust me, we get to hear all about it). Oh, and she makes a mean lasagna!Whereas Chandler and Macdonald excelled at rich characterization, Lyons treats us to a parade of two-dimensional stock "types": Cynical reporters, cynical DA's, cynical bail bondsmen, cynical medical examiners, cynical secretaries, cynical escort service workers, not to mention scores of cynical cops, cynical retired cops, cynical deceased cops' cynical widows... do you see a pattern? All of these people speak in paragraphs of wooden, cliched dialog about "scumbags" and "the System." Even Asch's cynical sarcasm sounds like little more than an obligatory character trait imposed upon him by the conventions of the genre.Do yourself a favor: If you're looking for a good, noirish PI novel in the vein of Hammett, Chandler, or Macdonald, skip pale imitators likeFalse Pretensesand treat yourself to one of the originals.
