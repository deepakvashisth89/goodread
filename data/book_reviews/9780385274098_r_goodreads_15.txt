In War’s Dark Shadowis a popular history that contextualizes Russian society during the twilight years of Romanov rule during the twentieth century. W. Bruce Lincoln, late scholar of Russian history, describes a Russia riven with all elements of impending social unrest and revolutionary froth, including an impoverished and disease-stricken peasantry, exploited urban factory hands, persecuted Jews, and pondering and increasingly unrestful social revolutionaries. Lincoln parses the crumbling edifices of Tsarist rule, where the outward harmony and enforced order of Alexander III suddenly gave way to the weak and distanced command of Nicholas II, as well as a nation teeming with conflict and constant confrontation. As the twentieth century’s first global conflict commenced, a Russian civil society was already in the throes of civil discontent, and an overthrow of the Tsar looked increasingly in the offing.
