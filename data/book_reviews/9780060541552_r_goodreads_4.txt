Nirvana's Children is a gift to the YA category of novels. Where so many works feel and read the same, Ranulfo has concocted something truly unique and bizarre. A style all its own, with vivid language and imagery unlike anything else in the coming-of-age cannon, Napoleon may be the closest thing to an actually disturbed, unreliable narrator in fiction targeted at young people. Parents are quick to call out things like The Perks of Being a Wallflower or Eleanor and Park for their perceived offenses, so perhaps it is a good thing that Nirvana's Children has largely flown under the radar, as it offers an unflinching portrait of young grief, confusion, experimentation, emotional turmoil, and troubled family life, the likes of which none of its contemporaries can touch.
