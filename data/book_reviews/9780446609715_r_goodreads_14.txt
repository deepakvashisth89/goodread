David Foster Wallace listed this 1960s police procedural as one of his ten favorite books of all time. He also counted the Thomas Harris novels, "Red Dragon," and "Silence of the Lambs." I figured I'd be in for a gripping read, not so. The author, Ed McBain wrote more than 50 novels about the 87th Precinct in NYC. This one features a "tall, blond man with a hearing aid," who goes by the name Mort Orecchio (dead ear). He's a man with a plan. The police receive short notes warning a prominent person will be killed unless a ransom is paid. The ransom goes unpaid, the note's warning is fulfilled. The 87th goes on high alert. Unfortunately they have other problems too. There are two kids splashing gasoline on homeless people and lighting them up. There is a pistol-packing duo planning to knock off a tailor shop. The novel concludes with an impossible deux ex machina that resolves all the problems quickly. DFW found magic in it, but for me it was a waste of time.
