I picked-up this pretty 1988 children’s book at our local thrift store recently. The artwork byWendy Edelsonis exceedingly lush, detailed, and beautiful; leading me to consider four-star feedback. The story was dismal, although I acknowledge thatStephen Cosgrovehad a tough task, to demonstrate bad decisions and attitudes. I see he is a long-standing and well-loved author, with numerous books to his name. “Derby Downs: The Value Of Respect” teaches a highly essential moral and maybe it is hard to find new ways of expressing old, common foibles. Nevertheless, I felt that this story’s circumstances and outcome were predictable and shown a million times before.A youngster is warned against something; in this case, about seasonal storms and about the reason rabbit warrens are made underground. Those tunnels are dark and not as fresh and lovely as the environs of their outdoor excursions. Thus,Derby Downswondered why they should not live aboveground, like many animals. As one would venture to guess, the aboveground houses do not work and seasonal storms turn out to be as furious as elders thought they might be. The foremost thing that bothered me, is brushing off council with such a lack of consideration for a Grandpa’s intelligence and experience, that he sheds a tear. I am a sensitive lady and the child I was, I could not have borne tears.There is nothing wrong with wanting to try something different and discussing how it might work but the rebuffs of these elders was needlessly drastic. Subtly and showing, rather than in-your-face telling, translates well among all ages. Particularly concerning a storm, it is implausible for anyone to reject a warning. ThankfullyDerbyapologized and appreciated his Grandparents anew. I can’t believeWendyis a self-taught artist. Her drawings are elegant and luscious.
