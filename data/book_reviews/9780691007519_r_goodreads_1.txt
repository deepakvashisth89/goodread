“Dismayed by popular excesses, the bourgeoisie tried to blame them on provocative agents, foreigners, ‘‘brigands” and crimi­nals such as inevitably mingled with the insurgents. It is true that men who are the dregs of society are not the last to take part in mobs. But the allegations of the Assembly and the bour­geois authorities have a note of apology. The ordinary people neither condemned nor repudiated the murders of July, nor did Barnave or Mme. Roland. The elements in the revolution­ary complex cannot be taken apart. In this sense Clemenceau was right: the Revolution is a bloc, a single thing. The moralist must praise heroism and condemn cruelty, but the moralist does not explain events.”
