Jack is to Ozma as the Scarecrow is to Dorothy — being that first traveling companion before anything else is known about the trouble at hand. Except of course, Jack has known Ozma since she was Tip. In the Baum books, Jack always referred to Ozma as "my dear father." For the most part, Thompson has glossed over or outright ignored Ozma's origin story, so having a book centered on Jack was a base move.Seeing Ozma's story talked about so bluntly too was again, a pivot for Thompson. But it ends with Jack suggesting that Tip might have preferred to stay a boy. Some day I would love to do some research into Thompson to maybe get to the heart of that comment — because it's in keeping with how male protagonist focused her books are compared to Baum's.Taken, though, in the context of the books themselves, I see Jack's observation as an understanding of what Ozma has given up by becoming the ruler of Oz. Although no Oz book (yet) has ever put emphasis on feminine deportment above all else, there have been hints throughout of Ozma's boredom. She's expected to spend most of her time in the palace, and that has become more and more the case with each passing book. Rarely does she get to gallivant across the countryside like she did in Ozma of Oz (1907). So poor Ozma has been stuck being a pretty-pretty princess (albeit an omnipotent one) for 22 years!
