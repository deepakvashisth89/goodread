Mrs McGinty was an old woman who was murdered by by her lodger. But the investigating officer isn’t convinced the lodger did it. So he asks Poirot for help.The book starts with Poirot feeling old. He’s glad he’s still able to enjoy some pleasures of life like good food, but he also misses his best friend Hastings. As much as he admires himself, he’d rather enjoy some human contact than spend more time all by himself. That’s one of the reasons why he decides to take a look at Mrs McGinty’s case, as he was actually not interested in a modern case of meaningless brutality. This explains why Poirot keeps taking on cases despite being retired, apart from wanting to challenge his superior little grey cells.This story feels a bit different from the normal Poirot mysteries. It feels more like a cozy mystery than other Poirot stories, with the story being set in a small village, the pacing being a bit slower and the stakes being so low in the beginning. But putting Poirot in such a cozy setting does have some benefits as it allows his character to tickle our funny bone on occasion. It’s clear he doesn’t really feel at home there, because of the lack of luxury. Though perhaps even worse for Poirot’s vain personality is that most people in this village don’t seem to know who he is when he introduces himself.Another character worth mentioning is of course Ariadne Oliver, who is basically a fictional representation of Agatha Christie herself in her own books. She actually plays an interesting part in the plot. She boasts to everyone that Poirot probably already knows who did it, he’s just trying to collect enough evidence for the big reveal.It starts as a solid and amusing cozy mystery that has a bit of fun before it turns into an interesting story with a good twist.
