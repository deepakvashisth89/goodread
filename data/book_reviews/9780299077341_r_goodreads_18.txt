Book Review: Son of the WildernessEver since I was a wee lass and my dad took me to see the country via literal Great American road trips, I’ve been a fan of John Muir and his contribution to the creation of the National Parks System. I’m finally on the verge of revisiting one of my favorite places, so naturally I wanted to brush up on my Muir lore.This book was a bit of a slog, though — and according to academia, there may never be a better biography of Muir than this one. (It was published just 31 years after his death, which gave the author access to interviews with people who had actually known Muir personally.)The problem is that Muir the man was dynamic, alive, and exciting, while this book felt comprehensive in details that didn’t really matter or stick out as memorable. Much of this very long book has already evaporated from my brain 😭What I can remember:Muir was born in Scotland to a religiously fanatical father who beat John and his siblings if they failed to memorize Bible verses and frequently starved them.John was incredibly sickly anytime he was not adventuring. He could practically be on his deathbed, but if someone let him walk out into the mountains, he would almost instantly recover and become capable of feats younger and healthier men wouldn’t dare attempt.He was a devoted family man, though often absent. (Lucky for him, his wife wanted to keep him alive and therefore encouraged his long explorations.) Still, if he ever got word — or even a premonition — of a wedding, illness, or death, he would stop whatever he was doing and go to whichever family member needed him.He was maligned during his lifetime through political maneuvering, though that does not appear to have become his lasting legacy.He absolutely would have written more books if he had lived longer than 76 years.God rest his soul! ⛰️2.5/5⭐️
