While there were a few points from this book that led me to deeper reflection on my life and last experiences, much of the book feels pretty obvious—things I’ve already learned on my journey. If you already know you are an ACOA and have started even the slightest in acknowledging what has happened to you, I don’t recommend this book. Besides it’s pretty dull and cliché information, the cheesy princess antidotes, the heteronormative takes on romantic partnership, and the general fact that this book is not written by an adult daughter are all horrible. The author does not understand the experience of adult daughters and compensated for it by using gender norms and stereotypes to try and relate to women...I mean the book is hot pink for gosh sakes. Not to mention the new edition’s first chapter of letters from adult daughters which doesn’t really teach you anything but instead is just a chapter of thank you letters kissing the author’s ass...pretty unnecessary. I’ll give him a little credit though as much of the information is taken from a study on adult daughters...I just think it’s from the wrong perspective.
