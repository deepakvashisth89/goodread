Although she died in 1995,Patricia Highsmithhas created in Ralph Linderman a precursor of the MAGA-hatted Republican who believes in things because hefeelsthem to be true, not because theyaretrue.Found in the Streetbegins with a wallet found in the street, with cash and credit cards. He returns them, cash included, to the owner, Jack Sutherland.From here on in, we see Jack's life interleaved with Ralph's. It seems that both are entranced by a lovely young blonde waitress named Elsie. With the help of Jack and his wife, Elsie is introduced to the right people and becomes a successful model. Ralph is afraid she is living a life of total depravity, not suspecting that she is a lesbian (and even that she has a fling with Jack's wife).In the end, Elsie comes to a bad end; and Ralph is convinced that Jack was responsible (he isn't). He continues weaving his conspiracies and refuses he believe they are wrong.This is an interesting novel which deals with homosexuality circa 1986 as if it were, at that early date, an unspectacular fact of life.
