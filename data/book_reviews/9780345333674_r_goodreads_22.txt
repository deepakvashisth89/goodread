Good news! Someone has kidnapped Ozma and stolen (almost) all of thedei ex machinaused to solve the plot of every single book! Unfortunately, the other characters want to find them. Like, Dorothy, you know you're somehow a Princess of Oz too, right? So with Ozma gone, you could be queen now. Y'know, just saying.Beginning with the positives: our characters have a set goal, which is surprisingly rare in this series, so even as they wander from one random subplot to another, it does feel like the plot is progressing instead of totally wasting time. And these random episodes are as creative as usual. I actually did like most of the book, but as always, there were those little problems that niggled at me until the end, when Baum pulled out thebigproblems that made me slam my head into a wall.The first of those niggling issues is that we have too many characters. The group we follow consists of Dorothy, Toto, Betsy, Hank, Trot, the Wizard, Scraps (who, by the way, is still Best Girl and seriously smarter than everyone else put together), Button-Bright, the Sawhorse, the Woozy and the Cowardly Lion. Except that we also have a B-plot about a baker named Cayke accompanied by the Frogman, who then team up with the Bear King and the ambiguously sapient Pink Bear. That is14.5main characters, not counting the villain. You probably won't be surprised to learn that Baum cannot, in fact, find something for all of them to do. (In particular I was curious if he could make Dorothy, Betsy and Trot feel more distinct. Not really, though Betsy still feels a tad gentler than the others.)Cayke and Frogman's whole story is...odd. The same night that Ozma et al. got stolen, Cayke lost her dishpan. She's on a quest to find it, because it magically makes her able to bake better. I feel like the pettiness of this goal could be funny, but Baum isn't really playing it up enough. Frogman's thing is that he's pompous and acts smarter than the Yips whom he lives with, and then he magically is compelled to only tell the truth, so he has to admit that he's not smarter than the Yips, even though I'd argue that he actually is because the Yips are idiots...I don't know what Baum was going for with this subplot, and it feels like eventually he just forgot about it himself, because nothing really comes of it.There's also this dumb subplot about how Toto "lost his growl," so that every chapter or so he can speak up and remind us that he's "lost his growl" and then at the end he "finds it." There's no clever angle here, nothing actuallyhappensto cause the supposed loss or find, it's just all wasting time. I liked Toto better when he didn't talk.The heroes explore some parts of Oz that outsiders don't ever go to. They're warned about stuff that supposedly happens in these cities, but reasonably ask how anyone could know, if nobody has ever gone there? Sure enough, the rumors about the first city turn out to be exaggerated. In the second city, however, the rumors are perfectly accurate. Again, I don't know what Baum was going for here.Anyway, it turns out that everything was stolen by Ugu the Shoemaker, who inherited a book whichsomehowtells him about all this magical stuff even though Ozma and the Wizard should not be written in a book that was once owned by this old man's grandfather. Maybe the book alters itself over time, like the Book of Records? Anyway, he stole the dishpan becauseapparentlyit not only makes good cookies, but can teleport people? An odd set of powers to have, especially since there are other magical ways to teleport that we've seen in other books. Like the Silver Slippers, which, given that his name is Uguthe Shoemaker...yeah, feels like Baum missed a cool opportunity to tie things together there.Anyway, time to discuss the ending, by which I mean, time for this review'sDorothy Ex Machinasegment. Now, ostensibly this book shouldn't have a problem with this, because the whole premise is that the heroes have lost all of their usualdei ex machina.Unfortunately, Ugu didn't steal the Magic Belt. (This gets a somewhat questionable explanation that the book he read only talked about magic from Oz, while the Belt comes from the Nome Kingdom; but again, said book talked about the Wizard knowing magic, which he didn't learn until Ozma already had the Belt.) But apparently only Ozma knows how to use the Belt, but then Dorothy figures it out, but we've retconned in a new rule that you can use it only once per day, but then, literallyright afterestablishing that rule, Dorothy uses it three times in a row to insta-solve the plot.[image error]You can really feel Baum fighting with himself, can't you? The whole genesis of this book, as described in his opening note, was that a fan wrote in and basically said, "Wow, if Ozma et al. couldn't solve the plot with no effort then the characters sure would need to put in effort!" And Baum seems to have realized that yeah, he needs some kind of limitations and rules so that we can come up with more creative, varied endings to these books. But on the other hand, that'shard.It would involve, like, thinking and stuff. So he gives up immediately, and somehow even his editor didn't point out that he broke the rule that he literally just retconned in. Easy come, easy go, I guess.I think this bothers me so much because theOzbooks really are very creative; just about anything can happen in this setting. A character in this book is a giant living teddy bear with illusion powers. So why not solve the plot with some kind of illusion? But no, instead he throws that character into an overstuffed plot that doesn't need him, and then just bashes one of the WIN INSTANTLY buttons like always.Conversely, we also gain a newdeus ex machinain the form of Pink Bear, who can tell you anything that has already happened. In this case, however, they don't use it enough, or correctly. There's a part where Pink Bear insists that Ozma is in a certain place, but she doesn't seem to be, and I'm like...obviously she's been transformed into something, our heroes getting shapeshifted has happened multiple times before, why don't you try asking "why can't we see Ozma?" or something? I will say, however, that Ozmabeing in the pearwas actually clever. I feel kind of dumb for forgetting that plot point.I didn't like Ugu's rushed redemption at the end. He's our most effectual antagonist, I want to see him kidnap Ozma again.Anyway, like I said, I did enjoy most of the book (though, as I recount all these issues, I'm sort of questioning that in retrospect). It just seems like the endings always go off the rails. I wish that Baum would take some of the imagination that he puts into creating weird characters and gimmicks and redirected it into his plot outline.
