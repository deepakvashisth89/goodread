I really wanted to give this novel more than 3 stars (I would give it 3.5 stars if that were possible), since I really like the set-up for this mystery. Some of the details of academic life were so deliciously perfect. What kept the novel from being better was its tendency to hammer at its premise. I like a little subtlety in the development of a novel's themes, and there wasn't any of that here. The language may have been drawn from some of the most elusive writers in the English language (Gertrude Stein and Henry James as epigraphs for writing about scholars of the 17th century), but the knife sliced home repeatedly. Or perhaps the hammer.
