Imperial Legendreviews the persistent legend that Tsar Alexander I faked his own death and lived the later part of his life as the SiberianstaretzFeodor Kuzmich. Alexis Troubetzkoy, says that he began as a skeptic (who wouldn't?) but gradually came around to belief in the truth of the story. Here he makes his case. In many ways it's pretty convincing: there are many oddities, inconsistencies and loose ends in the accounts of the Tsar's death, and even of the disposal of his body. When I finished the book I was much more willing to accept the story, though it was still difficult for me to see how the Tsar and his circle could have engineered the massive cover-up that would have been required. Maybe most convincing is the fact that the Russian Royal Family, including Tsar Nicholas II, the last Tsar, themselves accepted the story as true.Imperial Legendis a quick, gripping read, worthwhile not just for its detective work but for its coverage of an eventful period in Russian history — from the accession of Katherine the Great to the death(?) of Alexander — that included Russia's rise to the status of a world power and Napoleon's invasion of Russia and catastrophic defeat, in which Tsar Alexander played a crucial role.I've read this book more than once, and enjoyed it greatly each time. It's a lively, responsible, mystery story and window into Russian history.
