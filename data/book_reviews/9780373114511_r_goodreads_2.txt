Sometimes I wonder if these Harlequin writers and editors take a spinning wheel, spin it to see what kind of story/characters they'll pop out: arrogant Greek tycoon, malevolent arm twisting psychopath, Mary Sue Martyr, weepy doormat, secret baby, young love gone sour... Every now and then, we as readers get lucky with a fun and sassy heroine, perhaps a strong elegant one, a charming rogue, or a gentleman in over his head.Today's dish is the cranky, know-it-all, kinda bitchy friend we all have somewhere. The h has come down from London, furious, as she thinks her father is planning on selling the family home. The family home that she hasn't been to in six or seven months, but she's there to make sure her father doesn't sell. England is NOT that big. We're not talking New York to Houston or something. Dad's in his early fifties, HORRORS, and must have been manipulated by his new neighbor, a hot shot businessman.On her way home, she almost hits the adorable plot moppet wandering on the road, the H's doppelgänger charge. Carole Mortimer did a very good job with Sam as he is one of the more enjoyable characters. Maybe THE enjoyable character. The h unleashes the hounds of guilty hell on the H when he runs out looking for the toddler. Turns out that this is the man who's befriended her father. He's also the man who has apparently lost his mind as she is such a bitch to him at any given meeting it's pretty shocking he would want to call on her. Oh well.The h stonewalls him and her own attraction to him for more than one reason. An evil OM from her past pops up, and what little affection I had for the heroine dropped even more. This was a man she had dropped because of his immoral dealings in business The OM has just been hired by the H, but she never warns him of the evil doers ways. She thinks about telling, but never does.Although basically a decent sort, the h was a problem as was her dad. Turns out he made up the story about selling the house to get the heroine to meet the H. Obviously the apple didn't fall far from theI-know-what's-best manipulative tree. However the hunky hero and cute kid made up for them.HEA, and the heroine makes the hero and Sam happy so all is forgiven. Makes 3 stars but barely.
