Do not ever, I mean EVER, play an impromptu drinking game with Marilyn Harris’ characters’ show of insanity. You will lose. You will be pickled in a matter of moments, and you’ll be on a crying drunk. Don’t be that person. Nobody likes that person. Don’t do it.

This book has been reviewed by Karla here. She says it best, but I wanted to add a couple little thoughts I had along the dreaded journey. John Murrey Eden is a head-case and I just love observing him; waiting and watching for broken and misshaped parts to come flying off.

This review is a first-hand glimpse into The Making of an Asshole, John Murrey Eden.




While, for the most part, aside from general adolescent selfishness, JME seems the victim of a lot of circumstances that befell him. I sympathized with a lot of the uncontrollable parts of his life and the unfairness he was forced to endure; how he crawled through the world looking for parts of himself that are jaded, stunted and permanently obscured by life’s .gif’d FUCK YOU marquee spam & coming to the realization that other people could be thoughtlessly used to fill those myriad of voids.

As the story progresses, I was simply waiting for the powder keg of his many traumas to compound his adulthood. And boy does it ever. I think I had a lot of hope for him in the beginning, even the fact that he endured a lot of low times, that should have humbled and corrected the childhood selfishness, but the entitlement endured. Sometimes a parent can’t beat the bad out of a kid, well, in this case – the world couldn’t bitchslap JME enough to unfuck him properly. Instead, we’re left with an emotional and life survivor, who gleaned and siphoned from the harmless, innocent, and adoration his saintly father manifested.

Oddly, with this series, where JME is concerned, it has a ‘from the cradle to a casket’ ominous feel, because his victimhood started in book #2 and continued through #3 as well. As oftentimes with Thomas Eden, the selfishness won over… and I feel JMEs going to see such trifling self-indulgence and reface that asshole moniker to a power that will send lowly egotists into a proverbial hole in the wall. Why? Because victimhood that’s celebrated, glorified and forever undaunted throughout a lifetime and also self-designed, can only imply that the ‘victimhood’ ride can only last so long before the façade is lost and we’re introduced to the real conductor behind it. He’ll go to any length to prove he is in the right – even doing wrong in the process.

Somewhere along the way he hit adulthood, but people still lived in the shroud &shrine of his father’s good graces, and thereby encouraged his bad behavior. JME is simply his own moving target. Scrims of humanity kept him afloat in his youth, but the moment he was given what he truly wanted, money (and lots of it), the power & opportunity to overreach his victimhood came and he chose the path that allowed his domineering jackassery to be cleverly disguised as a businessman’s front.

JME admitted that the people, particularly the women who played a role in his life, were simply placeholders to fill the void of Harriett’s abrupt departure.

Dhari = His passion for Harriett not to interfere with his other persona of innocence.

Lila = His lost innocence. Major attractors being: Innocence & an inability to tell him no. Whether through love/affection, or the mental disability she obviously enjoys.

Elizabeth = A mother he never had. He modeled Elizabeth as a saintly figure and when Elizabeth reverted to her courtesan ways, he no longer holds her sacred, nor considers women to be other than objects; his own personal fuckpawn.

Maybe, just maybe, this may have worked in a merciful world, but fate didn’t have it that way. What’s in store for book #4? I’m not certain, but I have a little whimsical theory, though.

JME could have possibly survived himself on the parts of others fitted to his nullification that substitutes for his whole parts; only if the person he was substituting for wasn’t still capable of reminding him that those other people, and their living/breathing parts, aren’t her. Once JME begins to view other people for who they are, aside from those parts he no longer requires; those parts will begin to tear him apart from the inside out for their refusal to play a role; a one dimension to his needs, and as we saw in a few glimpses of him in power; he doesn’t control his emotions and plainly moves people in his life like chess pieces when he thinks they’re pliable enough, and at his worst, the emotional cripplings can manifest themselves to a murderous degree; even if they’re deserving of it.

I’m quite terrified that everything that held JME back from his dark fruition is now within his grips and we’re about to witness the masticating force of a man so severely disrupted, he’d rather loving figures to be deceased(his own father) than remind him of his own shortcomings.

Good luck to those moving onto book #4.
