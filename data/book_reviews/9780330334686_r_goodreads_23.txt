Eco is undoubtedly smart. But that intelligence doesn't necessarily translate to humour. And unfortunately, Eco is really trying to be funny here. And so unfortunately again, most of these short pieces don't have appeal beyond the "Oh, I see what you're doing there" moment. Stories with all the subtlety of a political cartoon.There are a few kinds of pieces here. Many are parodies. They ask that you're familiar with the work they're talking about. This means that if you haven't read or heard Diderot or Tasso or Manzoni or more, whole sections of this book have literally no appeal. Which is the point of parody, but still. It would help if they were funny.There are other types of stories that don't parody anything. These rely on the cleverness and the fanciness of the prose to carry the story through, rather than relying on an intrinsically interesting premise. The only of this type that I like was "Letter to my Son", which hits a little close to home (re: current fascist insanity here in North America), and which actually made me laugh a little. Other ones are written in a tone that is heavily ironic and smug sounding, or are otherwise unreadable. Even as somebody who historically likes that type of writing, it was tough to get through. Worst was "Equisse d'un nouveau chat". Avoid.In summary, you have to be an Eco superfan to like this one (and that's coming from somebody who gave five stars to The Name of the Rose earlier in 2017). Still, it didn't take long to read, so it's not like you'll be wasting too much time on it.3/10
