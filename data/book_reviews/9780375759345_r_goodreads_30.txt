Although I didn’t understand most of the poems, there were many lines that left me thinking (or wishing I knew what they meant), and there were a few lines that left me awe-struck. Here are examples of the latter two (i.e., left thinking or awe-struck):

“Neither fear nor courage saves us. Unnatural vices || Are fathered by our heroism. || Virtues || Are forced upon us by our impudent crimes.”



I know that “The Waste Land” is ’s most famous poem, and is considered his masterpiece. Still, I didn’t enjoy it as much as some of his other poems, such as “Gerontion” and “The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock.”





“April is the cruellest month, breeding || Lilacs out of the dead land, mixing || Memory and desire, stirring || Dull roots with spring rain.”

“There will be time, there will be time || To prepare a face to meet the faces that you meet; || There will be time to murder and create”

“I should have been a pair of ragged claws || Scuttling across the floors of silent seas.”

“I would meet you upon this honestly. || I that was near your heart was removed therefrom || To lose beauty in terror, terror in inquisition. || I have lost my passion: why should I need to keep it || Since what is kept must be adulterated?”

“Because I know that time is always time || And place is always and only place || And what is actual is actual only for one time || And only for one place.”
