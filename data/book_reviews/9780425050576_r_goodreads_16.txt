I thoroughly enjoyed this novel, as to be honest, I thought I would. Having high expectations from such a distinguished author as Thomas Keneally, (I mean he did the book "Schindler's List" was based on) sometimes falls short, but not in this case. It reminded me of E.L Doctorow's, "The March," another historical fiction account of the Civil War which I thoroughly enjoyed. "Confederates" lacks some of the hauntingly descriptive visions of the hellish landscape of war that "the March" had, but was superior in the remarkable character development and the simultaneous stories that the author expertly laid out. In fact, I was so absorbed by a couple of the subplots, I started to dislike the main protagonist, but in the end, it all came around full circle. One nit pick is that I would have liked to have seen some of the slaves portrayed in the story play a more important role than simply serving as background to the main characters. I especially enjoyed to portrayal of some of the main historical figures such as Stonewall Jackson and even a couple of brief appearances from Abe Lincoln. A+++
