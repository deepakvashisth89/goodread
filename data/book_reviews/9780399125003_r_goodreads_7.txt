Having read Mr. Sanders' Seventh Commandment, I already knew he had a soft spot for shorter people, not naming anyone in particular. What amazed me most was the character of Josh Bigg: an easy-going man with a sharp mind, brilliant in the most unassuming way. Reading Mr. Sanders day after day has a way of lifting your spirits and genuinely makes you feel good about yourself.Beyond the many book characters pleasantly described by other authors, Mr. Sanders offers something different: a cast with both inner beauty and brains. And to keep things exciting, the antagonists are attractive people hiding behind charming masks.I couldn't help but laugh imagining Tippi Kipper's expression as Josh peeled away her façade with his deceptively innocent questions. Then there's Father Knurr, good-looking, dangerous, and utterly convincing, whose charm cost some people their lives and broke many hearts. His bewildered look when Josh and the police tracked him down to his hideout on a boat was deeply satisfying.What I also loved was how Mr. Sanders highlighted the role of ordinary people in solving a case. It's a quiet but powerful reminder that brilliance doesn't always wear a badge.This novel had me asking "How could she do that?", "How did they manage it?", "Why?", "What did they see in him?" and that's what makes it so compelling. It's less about who committed the crime and more about why, and that's far more interesting.As Wanda wisely put it: "Hate is a poison."
