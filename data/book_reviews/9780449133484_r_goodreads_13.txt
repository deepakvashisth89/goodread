Cleeves' Vera series has enjoyed tremendous success (despite it's 40w cinematography) as well as the Perez series despite the miss-casting of a ginger for the main character...wasn't he supposed to 'look' Mediterranean/Spanish or whatever? No matter. The casting director's fault.But I had sworn off Cleeves' Shetland series when I was so disgusted with her gratuitous execution of a major character...I figured this earlier work might not be so highhanded, and it was not. In fact it emerged as a clear (and thus truly dated) depiction of Devon society in the late 80s which I clearly recall from personal experience and when this title was originally written .The classic plot construction was pretty standard (George being a sort-of amateur detective and Molly tossed in rather pointlessly at the last) but I found more entertainment in the small side descriptions that firmly place the story line in 1987: female stereotypes of that generation, the limited introduction of 'a computer' in an office, the total absence of instant communication devices and the diets like the proliferation of bacon sandwiches(!) while typical of the period would rarely be part of the gastronomic scene 40 years later. Funny how that really shows.Further, there is little SOCO activity described and the actual protection of victim's personal items is incredibly absent...along with a completely inadequate police presence and wide open opportunities for compromised evidence in crime scenes in the immediate aftermath of the murders. Compared to today, it all reads as a rather amateurish understanding of police procedures.The detailed and repeated descriptions of bird 'ringing', netting, capture etc etc however was EXHAUSTIVE!...and beyond me but obviously Cleeves is an authority with a background in those pursuits that perhaps allowed her to dwell on it a bit much for my taste. But then the entire scenario is based on these pursuits so generally as I plodded along, I felt it suited my mood for a quiet minimally stimulating read.
