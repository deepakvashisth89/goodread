The Mother Tonguewas the fourth in Teri Holbrook's series that began withA Far and Deadly Cry, which was nominated for the Agatha and the Anthony Awards for Best First Novel. I'm glad I read this short series all the way through because each book not only solves a mystery but takes the reader through phases of the main characters' lives beginning with a personally significant event in England. Each book takes place alternately in England and in north Georgia.The mysteries are not what you would expect, given that Gale Grayson is not a detective but rather a historian who gave up her shot at a career to follow her husband to England. And as the years go along and life events unfold in unexpected ways (with murders to be solved in each book), she eventually finds herself back at her great-aunt's house in Georgia determined to find a way to raise her child hand-in-hand with carving out an independent career.The writing is solid and the characters believable. I have only one question: Why is Teri Holbrook not writing more?
