Although Heyer is famous for her Regency romances, those were her bread and butter writing, not the books that she longed to write. What she really wanted to work on was a sweeping, detailed historic "quintogy" of the House of Lancaster. Sadly, only one, "My Lord John" was published. The character list goes on for pages.Her mysteries, while few in number, are much less formulaic than the romances. Penhallow is reminiscent of Hardy or Bronte in its ever-expanded atmosphere of despair and its lack of the neat and tidy solution.
