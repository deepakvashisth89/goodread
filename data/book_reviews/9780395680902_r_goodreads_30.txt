Having lived in Africa for a couple of years in the late 70s and having seen elephants, lions, wildebeasts by the thousands, roaming freely, when I came across this book, I was curious. I'm glad I found it.The writing is descriptive and inclusive. The conditions, customs and corruption were accurately portrayed. The friendly, infectious enthusiasm of the always smiling people was pervasive. The description of the interpersonal relationships between the authors, their employees, the villagers and even the officials was compelling and usually entertaining. The Owens' love of their work and the animals is obvious and heart-warming.I suspect that anyone who would consider picking up this book, is already on the side of animal preservation. Unfortunately, there are many times in the book when I felt that the authors were trying to persuade me to their viewpoint, which wasn't necessary. I have 40 year old memories of African plains covered by massive herds of animals. At the end of the book with the statistics of how many animals have been killed, I was left wondering if those images really are just memories now.
