A journal entry that I wrote at least 12 years ago says that this is:

....a somewhat untidy historical mystery set in 1846 New York, involving grisly murders, international intrigue and the number 13. Technically, the writing is acceptable but contextually it all feels a bit weak. Meyer tells us that his main characters are nicely dynamic, but he doesn’t actually show us that they are. Poe, we hear, is a lascivious, strange, drug-addled troublemaker, who is also damned smart and deductive. What we see instead is a self conscious poor man who adds little in terms of aid. The book quotes his work, but as the story itself is not really macabre the quotes seem out of place.

The story itself is not too bad, but may have fared better as a straight forward murder mystery. There is a small, one page-ish intro which speaks of high intrigue, which is then disregarded until the last chapter. This adds not so much a surprising twist, and more a “Wtf, mate?”

I like mystery, and I like history, and I love Poe. This had all of that, so I can’t be too terribly sad about having read it. I honestly did enjoy it, but I’ll likely not go back for a second run-through.
