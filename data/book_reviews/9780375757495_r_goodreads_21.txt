I have a curious story behind my reading of this, almost 60 years in the making. When I was a sophomore in high school, around 1968 or so, I of course had a best friend. As a matter of fact we're still friends and keep in touch although we live in different states. I was a reader then, as I am now, but Barbara only read what was required for school. She was a music lover and had no use for books. In 10th grade we ended up in separate English classes, and her class read Silas Marner that year. To my surprise, she not only loved it, she couldn't stop talking about it! This had never happened before and I was in hopes that it would send her into the literature lane, but alas, it did not. It was just this one book, and to this day she claims it's the best book she ever read.

My class never did get around to reading it, but every time I saw the title I would think that I needed to see what was so great about this book, and yet never picked it up. I ran across a copy at a used book sale a few years ago and brought it home to languish on my shelves, but at least I was one step closer. So this year I put it on my list of goals and made it a priority.

It was a Victorian novel to warm the heart, with poor Silas being treated wrongly and misjudged for years until fate landed an orphan girl on his hearth. He was a miser whose money had been stolen and this golden haired little girl took the place of gold sovereigns as an object of love, which also improved the opinion of the villagers toward him when they saw his devotion and care. Of course Barabara would have loved this, as she is a kind and caring person who loves to see justice and happy endings.

And yes, back in the day we read classics in high school, lots of Dickens and Shakespeare, mythology, the Iliad and the Odyssey. Not only that, but the books were handed out to us from the school and we turned them in after the final exam.

So there you go Barbara. At long last, I get it.
