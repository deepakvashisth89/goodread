A story of futile resistance against a dystopian world government develops - after our young protagonist and her family get arrested - into a story of kids developing a society by themselves in pioneering conditions. Either of these stories could be fun by itself, but the juxtaposition is awkward. Hughes spends a lot of focus on our protagonist poking around that juxtaposition, but it still doesn't ring solid to me - either in-universe or tonally. The setup still feels too convenient.

Besides, this doesn't leave enough focus for exploring so many interesting questions of what the kids' new society might look like! We're left with a vision of what might be, but will it be that glorious way? Or will it fall apart as soon as the kids hit puberty? How will our protagonist's unique role look as it continues to develop? I'd like to see a story like that! Sadly, we don't get anything like it here.
