It’s 3:39 am and here I am writing a book review. I’ve been re-reading Straley’s books and am now on The Music of What Happens. I’m writing this to share my opinion that out of the 10,000 or so books I’ve read in my life, this is the best. Straley is gifted beyond belief. This book is pretty much perfect. It involves a private investigator in Sitka, Alaska who lives in a ramshackle house on the shore with his friend Todd, a man his age who has autism and is additionally the kindest, most intelligent, most honest person I’ve met in literature. That’s not to say Cecil, the investigator, is not great shakes as well, just more apt to make mistakes and get into messes, typically while he’s trying to help someone out and make a few bucks, if possible. Cecil’s a (for now) sober alcoholic who represents Priscilla, mom of a 4 year old boy who’s become the rope in a tug of war between Priscilla and her ex Bob. Priscilla and Bob are both basically nuts and both are determined to maintain sole custody of the boy, whatever it takes. Priscilla has hired Cecil to assist in her quest to get Young Bob back from his dad, who has more resources and is just as determined to acquire possession of the boy. In the process of this, there are kidnappings, assaults, break-ins, a senator’s murder, and Cecil’s coming back into contact with Priscilla’s sister Jane Marie, whom he’s been in love with since high school. There are wild boat trips involving attempts to broker an agreement for Jane Marie to be given guardianship of Young Bob and the absolutely insane things that happen in pursuit of this goal. There are a number of other fascinating characters including a determined biker hired by Bob who gets hold of Young Bob but has little interest in anything but the $10,000 he’s been promised to help Teller, a brilliant but substance-impaired local attorney, as he tries to help accomplish agreement to Jane Marie’s guardianship of the boy. Straley’s depiction of the situation and characters is a joy to read, by turns funny, moving, thrilling and heart-breaking. The characters are genuine folks who have come to the frontier outpost and former Russian colony of Sitka to enjoy the natural beauty, wildlife, freedom and access to guns and drugs for which Alaska and specifically this small town are noted. This easy access to guns, drugs and booze makes an already dangerously charged situation even more fraught, and watching this unfold is both affecting and great fun. I can’t recommend this book highly enough.
