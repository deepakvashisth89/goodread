Some picture books have animal characters that are thinly disguised humans.

Other picture books are meant to help tiny children with Self-Help. Character. Growth, Can-Do Spirit.

This Goodreads reviewer is usually fine with characters who are thinly disguised humans, looking like cute little animals.

(Except when an animal of some kind is depicted as doing something, or thinking in some way, that Or else the animal physically does something that )

Likewise, I love the spirit of Self-Improvement. Character. Growth, Can-Do Spirit.

Why wouldn't I? Most of my books are nonfiction, aiming to help my readers gain useful skills for Self-Help, Spirituality, Personal Growth.

(Except I'm not such a fan when an animal character for kids is shown -- preposterously -- doing what a human would not be able to do.)

HMMM, CAN YOU TELL WHERE I'M GOING WITH THIS BOOK REVIEW?

HERE ARE THREE THINGS YOU GOODREADERS WILL FIND IF YOU READ THIS PARTICULAR BOOK

* Meet Dudley. He was born small.
(Oooh, just like a human baby. Just like the picture book reader, possibly.)

* Supposedly, Dudley has started his own program of "weight lifting."
(How hard would it have been to tell the truth? Such as, say that this puppy played a lot by doing things like carrying big sticks. Then explain that exercise this made the puppy's muscles stronger.)

In case any of you Goodreaders are wondering, photos in this book do NOT, actually, picture Dudley at the gym, grunting under his bench press.

* Also, "Dudley" went for his regular checkups.
(As if he would find his own vet, then drive himself there, then pay for his wellness visit?)

Yeah, this is what I mean by preposterous!

I REALLY, REALLY DON'T LIKE PICTURE BOOKS FOR KIDS THAT STRETCH THE TRUTH

Imply false promises. Or deceive readers through false comparisons.

To me, "Dudley: The Little Terrier That Could" implies that if you exercise and get your regular checkups, you'll be able to "fly."

How, exactly? Maybe grow dog legs?
