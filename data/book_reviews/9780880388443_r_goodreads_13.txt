A writeup of pantheons from across the world (plus one fantasy pantheon from Fritz Leiber novels). There are some interesting new spells and magical items scattered throughout, plus the stats for a host of avatars, heroes, and mythological monsters. Like many AD&D 2e products, it has a gorgeous cover and there is some nice interior art as well.

The individual entries themselves are pretty shallow and, honestly, nowadays you can find MUCH better information on Wikipedia. Let's take a look at the entry for Dionysus, for example: it says that priests of Dionysus worship him because they like to get drunk and party, and their only duty is to make and sell wine. That's about it. Nothing on Orphism or the Dionysian mysteries, nothing on theater and the arts, nothing on the maenads...just "all his priests do is get drunk all day". That is about the level of knowledge of a Disney cartoon rendition of Dionysus. Now, I understand that they had a page count that they had to hit and A LOT of gods to talk about, but I just can't see anyone using this book unless they wanted the most dumbed down version of real world mythology and ancient religion.
