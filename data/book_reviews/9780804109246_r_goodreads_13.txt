I've read several detective series before discovering Abe Lieberman. From my experience, about ninety percent of the detective novel is dedicated to the crime and its subsequent investigation with maybe a few pages at the beginning and end where we get a glimpse into the detective's personal life. There's nothing wrong with that, but Liberman's Folly going to be a bit different because Abe Lieberman's personal life is emphasized as much as his detective work. He's investigating a prostitute's murder with his partner, but he's also playing marriage counselor at home and navigating drama at the synagogue. It might be odd at first, but as you progress, you start to wonder how his personal problems play out as much as the murder investigation.By the end of Lieberman's Folly, I felt the same way as I did when I finished Michael Connelly's "Black Echo" with Harry Bosch. I look forward to reading Abe Lieberman as he's thrown into criminal investigations with higher intensity. Having read all twenty books of the core "Harry Bosch" series, I can say that Abe Lieberman is like an older Harry Bosch. More cerebral but is willing to pull out his gun when necessary. It's a shame that Stuart Kaminsky's no longer with us, because it'd be interesting to see the Abe Lieberman series grow to a level on par with "Harry Bosch" or "Dave Robicheaux," but nevertheless, Abe Lieberman is still a unique detective that deserves his own place in the pantheon of detectives within modern crime fiction.
