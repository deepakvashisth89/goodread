This is a not terribly thrilling novel blending ancient history and New Age mysticism. The whole thing revolves around a cache of manuscripts and a woman who knows nothing about it or her family. The format of the novel is similar to the author's earlier (and far better) novel 'The Eight' - a blend of mysticism, mystery, history, ancient artifacts, old knowledge, and side stories. Somehow, 'The Magic Circle' just never got off the ground. Perhaps the New Age mysticism was just too esoteric - too philosophical and woowoo - for me; or perhaps the tired and worn-out trope of the "13 treasures", Hitler, and the biblical scenes just wasn't interesting enough, or the lack of interesting locations? The protagonist, Ariel Behn, wasn't much of a main character - she showed no agency, everything happened to her or was dictated by others, or had to be explained to her. Her drip-fed family connections got many eye-rolls from this reader (her family tree could give the Hapsburgs a run for their money! and just made my eyes glaze over - it got boring really fast) - so did the insta-lust (because turquoise eyes and a heroic Macedonian profile just automatically make women all warm and squishy and wanting to leap into bed with this guy!?) Leaps of logic abound, while plot points are left dangling. The manuscripts and why they were important didn't get nearly enough page time. I got the impression that the author tried to stuff too many nifty historical stories, myths and esoterica into one novel, while the plot was left languishing and ignored. The very convenient water-loving cat good at retrieving important bits of paper is rather more interesting than the important and mysterious manuscripts, characters or plot. The ending was a complete let down. I loved 'The Eight' and 'The Fire' was pretty good too, but 'The Magic Circle' was a complete disappointment.
