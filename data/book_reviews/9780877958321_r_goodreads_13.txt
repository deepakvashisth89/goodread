This is a book of three parts, although there is a unifying theme. The first is a fictionalized biography of Burgess's own father, a cinema pianoplayer, and this is by far the best of the three parts. Here Burgess captures a bygone age with his erudite and fluent prose. The second, a bit of light whoring, seems almost cursory in comparison. And the last is a long anecdote; it would be hilarious if told to you down the pub, but here it just seems out of place.Still, this has reminded me of the pleasures of Burgess's work and I shall be re-reading more in the future.
