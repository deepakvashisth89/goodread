BOREDOM!!! SHOOTING!!! SHELLING!!! PEOPLE BEING KILLED!!! DESPAIR!!! HUNGER!!! MISERY!!! FEAR!!!That’s my life! The life of an innocent eleven-year-old schoolgirl!! A schoolgirl without a school, without the fun and excitement of school. A child without games, without friends, without the sun, without birds, without nature, without fruit, without chocolate or sweets, with just a little powdered milk. In short, a child without a childhood. A wartime child.Reading this account of an 11-year-old girl thrown into nightly bombing and plenty of deprivation, death, despair and destruction is of course heart-breaking in its own right. But knowing that we keep repeating the wars of ethnic cleansing just adds to the heartache. The Armenian genocide, the Holocaust, theNakba, the various Congo civil wars, the Bosnian ethnic cleansing, the Rwandan Civil War and now the Palestinian genocide, it’s really too much even for adults; how much worse for children? Even the children get inured to the bombing, electrical and gas outages, the death.That’s nature at work. Children grow, the elderly age. That’s how it is with those of us who are still alive. And there are lots and lots of people and children in Sarajevo who are no longer among the living.How heart breaking is that?What does help is that Zlata Filipović’s diary made the world pay attention, forcing the international community to intervene. (She was 13 when her family was able to escape to Paris, but the diary’s publication made a big difference to her remaining friends and neighbors.) One little girl who had no idea what she was launching. One person made a difference. Here’s to hoping that the YouTube videos by Palestinian young people will continue to persuade the citizens of the world to implore their own governments for an end to the ethnic cleansing there.
