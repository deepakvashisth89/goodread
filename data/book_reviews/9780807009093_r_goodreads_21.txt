One of the things that I noticed about Plum Bun by Jessie Redmon Fauset is the comparison to Jane Austen, which makes me wonder if Jane was an influence on Fauset. Right off the bat, I understood why people draw the comparisons. Plum Bun, subtitled "A Novel Without a Moral," seems to be more of a social observation piece about race in America as well as the Philadelphia Black Elite that Jessie Redmon Fauset comes from. Books about passing are pretty common for this time period; most notably, Nella Larsen's Passing is an example that a lot of people think of. I enjoyed this more than Passing, even though I do genuinely love both. The reason why works for me more is the sheer refinement in Jessie Fauset's writing. It's more in-depth, and it gives us more about the psychology of passing, even though I'd say that is the more thrilling novel. I enjoyed the wit of this novel. I'd say that the tone is very keen and sharp without cutting people down. What Fauset says here is without offense, but it is a funny look into the life of someone who has a lot of self-hate. However, I certainly don't pass but I find myself frustrated at how certain things for me appear out of reach because of the simple fact that I am Black. So, I don't think Angela is an unsympathetic character.
