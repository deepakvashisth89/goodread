I liked the premise better than the actual book. The pairing of a French Surete cop with a Gestapo agent to work solving crimes in Vichy France is a clever idea, and those two characters are interesting and fairly well developed.The plot lines are hopelessly tangled - the cops are investigating the murder of a farm wife whose missing/dead husband was an ancient historian, the discoverer of a cave that rivals Lascaux. There is a Nazi movie crew that is filming in the cave, to produce a documentary that will prove that the Aryan super race goes back to the Neanderthals because there is a swastika scratched on an ancient artifact. If the cave and its art are forged, the political implications will cause major headaches for all involved. There is a whole caveful of terribly degenerate film types who are sexing and drugging all over the place, none of whom are very interesting.The writing is mixed. Some of the historic stuff about Occupied France and the descriptions of the creation of the art and artifact forgeries is interesting and well done. But there are long passages scattered throughout the book that sound as though they are literal translations from French phraseology, and read very awkwardly, especially in the light of the fact that the book was written in English. Or Canadian.
