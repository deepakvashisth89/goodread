Despite being a bit dated at this point, I think Dancing in the Dark is a great introduction to the notion of critical media studies, with a special emphasis on culture and its relation to morality and religion. One of the book's unique features is that it's written by a group of professing Christians who nonetheless do not force a religious perspective into the conversation at points where it maybe would not prove illuminating. When it does come in, it is to provide insightful commentary on how matters of faith relate to the predicaments of youth and youth culture in contemporary media and society. The specific examples in this text are somewhat dated, but the overall message and philosophy is as ripe for conversation as ever, and I believe that the authors do a phenomenal job of walking through and demonstrating the importance of having the kinds of conversations about media, youth, and culture that this book provokes. In that sense, this book definitely taps into something timeless and essential for Christians and discerning media consumers everywhere.
