“When this is over, Hap, what you say we make something of our lives?”

“I’d like that.”

“I mean it this time.”

“I mean it every time.”

“But it don’t change.”

“I mean for it to.”

“We got to do more than mean it this time. It’s got to happen.”

“Maybe I don’t know how to change.”

“We’re going to learn how. Got me?”

I mean, how can I give a book that gives Leonard an armadillo buddy named Bob less than 5 ⭐️s?

But even beyond that, man, oh man, did this one move like a bullet. Let not my slight pokeiness finishing this dissuade you from that motion, I almost had to parse it out as to not finish it in one go. Once it picks up it never once stops and it keeps juicing the character beats and the oddballs and mercenaries Hap, Leonard, and Brett, new arrival and instant scene-stealer, right up until the very end. I had a total blast with it.

And I think too, these genuinely might be the most authentically Texan sounding things I’ve ever encountered in fiction? So much of the time it’s always so affected and hacky, and Lansdale just nails the patter and the roughness of it (as well as the openly vile racism and homophobia of a lot of “good ‘ol” people that I really appreciate). It gives these such an edge beyond usual southern crime fiction that is always so caught up in tinniness or just open parody. It’s been refreshing to see my home state so naturally (warts and all) reflected back in the page.

I ALSO continue to appreciate JRL’s willingness to keep pushing Hap and Leonard’s worldviews into friction. This one has a fun domestic hook with them having now to live together once more and that strains them, obviously, but then the big plot brings back into the focus their stances on violence and morality and sense of duty, both as a citizen and has a person operating in the world with deep connections to others. It’s genuinely really engaging and makes them more than just these hinges around which plots happen. It makes them real people and I feel like their connection is earned and stressed in interesting ways. I truly love them a lot.

And Bob and Brett. This seems like the rare occasion where the cast of a series grows and I actually ENJOY seeing the branches. So much so I’m excited to eventually backtrack and see how they were introduced. But yeah! Super fun. Still insane to think this came out in NINETY-EIGHT. Absolutely mind blowing to me.
