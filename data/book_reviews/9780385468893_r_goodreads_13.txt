An oral history of race relations in the U.S. that despite its flaws should probably be read by everyone.It's not perfect: it's a little dated and a little too focused on the civil rights backlash, black-white relations and the consequences of de-industrialization in the Midwest. In other words it's very Chicago. An entirely different book would have arisen out of an oral history of the Los Angeles riots -- which went down the same year "Race" was published (1992).That said, Studs Terkel is a fabulous historian. The stories here are endlessly surprising and touchingly human. There are no saints or sinners here. And each time you think you know one of Terkel's speakers they twist back and contradict themselves, circling round and round a constant, but uncertain, human decency.Be warned, it's not a page-turner. I slogged through it over the course of many months, but I'm glad I kept at it.
