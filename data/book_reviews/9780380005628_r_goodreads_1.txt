Really enjoyed this one. I wasn't expecting much, but it surprised me. :)First, the good:Egyptian-set romances don't always agree with me. This is because many authors spend an inordinate amount of time infodump'ing the reader into a stupor as they wallow from one index card to another. Verrette, mercifully, does not. The setting is made clear, & while she describes some architecture, clothing, & customs when relevant, they're not lingered over for pages & pages; more often she expects the reader to pan over details in their mind's eye. The plot, likewise, is slow to build (the first 50 pages are packed with insta-lust, though it's somewhat believable given the circumstances)...but then a higher gear kicks in & things romp merrily from one obstacle to another. Meanwhile the characters, despite being black & white, attach quite firmly in the reader's mind; small details like Nefrytatanen's unashamed mysticism, Amenemehet's dry sarcasm, & Kheti's unspoken loneliness balance out their Mary Sue/Gary Stu auras & cheesy declarations of lurve.On the downside, there's the aforementioned black & white characterization & slow opener. I liked the way they tied in Atlantis (yes, really), but I wanted more details of that other culture. And there could've been a better build-up of tension where the villainy was concerned. There's a turncoat priest & skanky priestess & poison-wielding daughter & murderous thief, all of which are great, but there wasn't much lead-in.Overall, though, a fun read. Two thumbs up & a strong 4 stars. :) It shows nice hints of future adventures to be had (I've already ordered the sequel), but offers plenty of closure as-is.NB.: this novel features occasional supernatural intervention from Egyptian gods & priestesses & whatnot. Being someone who favors the WTFery of Old Skool, I only mention it now to be a good romance-reading citizen. ;)
