I don’t know why this felt more poignant and melancholic to read than it did to watch. The film is powerful, undeniably so, but reading the story is a different kind of experience. I couldn’t help but cry. I cried and laughed at the iconic quote :“Oh shit it’s shit”.I cried for Andy and Red. Maybe it really is just the power of words, the intimacy that comes with reading. It pulls you uncomfortably close. You’re not simply observing events; you’re sitting inside the thoughts and the fear, the slow wearing down of hope.I truly had no business watching the movie at the age I did because it mostly justtraumatised me. I remember having nightmares afterward and sitting down with my dad to ask him questions about prison, more specifically the surest ways to avoid it. Reading it now, though, I can finally appreciate the sheer ingenuity of it.This is not simply a story about imprisonment. It’s about survival inside a system that seems designed to erode dignity. A corrupt system that prioritizes convictions over justice or truth. One that will put an innocent man in prison as long as someone is made to pay for the crime. We witness the realities of prison politics, the quiet hierarchies of power that govern daily life, and the everyday cruelty and inhumane conditions that shape life behind bars. The story also reaches beyond imprisonment itself, confronting the unsettling question of life after prison. Can a person truly reintegrate into a world that has moved on without them? Can they ever feel free of the identity that prison imposed, and become an independent, functioning member of society again?❝ That’s what a whole life in prison does for you, young man. It turns everyone in a position of authority into a master, and you into every master’s dog. Maybe you know you’ve become a dog, even in prison, but since everyone else in gray is a dog, too, it doesn’t seem to matter so much. Outside, it does. ❞Andyis such a fascinating and compelling character. He is quietly intelligent and rebellious, observant and endlessly patient. He does not rely on brute force or bravado but on wit, restraint, and an almost dangerous sense of composure. He uses his financial knowledge to carve out a place for himself, to build a kind of protection in a space where vulnerability is currency. He remains hopeful despite the hopelessness of his situation. I loveAndyandRed’srelationship. It’s interesting how I didn’t fully grasp its significance, or the imprint Andy leaves on Red, until Red is finally out of prison. Only then did I realise how profound and lasting that influence was, how knowing Andy positively shaped the rest of his life.I also love how the story is told through Red’s voice. So much of it is conjecture, his careful observations and logical reasoning piecing together what he can never fully know. The fact that the story is not told with perfect certainty makes it feel more intimate, almost haunting. It invites the reader to sit in the gaps, to wonder, to draw their own conclusions. Did Andy make it to the promised land? What was his first experience outside prison like? Did he find the world unrecognisable, or did he slip back in as if he had never left? Did Andy and Red ever make their way back to each other?If the central question of the story is about imprisonment, the answer ishope. The story is a meditation on hope. How fragile it is, how rebellious it can be, and how, in the wrong environment, it can feel almost like a liability. Above all, whatever hope may be, it is always heavy with promise and the potential for change.❝ I find I am excited, so excited I can hardly hold the pencil in my trembling hand. I think it is the excitement that only a free man can feel, a free man starting a long journey whose conclusion is uncertain.I hope Andy is down there.I hope I can make it across the border.I hope to see my friend and shake his hand.I hope the Pacific is as blue as it has been in my dreams.I hope.❞
