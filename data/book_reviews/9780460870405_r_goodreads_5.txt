This edition ofPoe’s Tales of Mystery and Imagination, illustrated by Arthur Rackham, was first published in 1935. It is a beautiful edition containing 25 of Edgar Allan Poe’s most famous stories, and is complemented by 12 of Arthur Rackham’s full‑colour plates, plus numerous line drawings. This is one of the most famous illustrated Poe editions of the 20th century and was reissued in 1978. My book club edition is from 1979, and replicates the 1978 one. At 19cm x 26cm it is larger than a standard hardback novel, but not large enough to qualify as oversize.The best word to describe the edition is “sumptuous”. The pages are of high-grade, heavy cream paper, and the print is a clear font. It is not technically Large Print, but is dense and black enough to be easily enlarged. The cover by Arthur Rackham is stunning. This shows it opened out, as the illustration is a wraparound one:Arthur Rackham was a golden age illustrator, and is still regarded as one of our greatest illustrators, particularly of fantasy works. Rackham’s style for this book is unusually dark and expressionistic, even compared with his illustrations for the Brothers Grimm and those for“Peter Pan”.This distinctive dust‑jacket plate is unique, and does not illustrate any specific story. ForPoe's Tales of Mystery and ImaginationArthur Rackham created an image of a gaunt, skeletal figure in a long cloak, looming over a crumbling Gothic archway, with gnarled, twisted trees.Arthur Rackham has also created silhouettes for the inside cover illustrations front and back, cleverly worked to show skeletons and branches in a repeating pattern, so that some are cream and some are black:Rackham usually used watercolours, but at least one of his books of fairy tales is worked all in silhouette. This inside illustration looks as if it is a woodcut, but the black and white illustrations inside are pen and ink. Most of them take a whole page, just as the “tipped in” colour plates on glossy paper do, and the extra size shows them at their best:The Pit and the PendulumHere are the stories which are included in the 1935 edition, with links to their reviews as I write them. (Please note that although usually it is a separate review, sometimes a story's review is within a review of a collection.)The “Rackham edition” as it was referred to was designed around 12 colour plates, so the publisher selected stories that suited Rackham’s visual style. This original 1935 edition (now classed as rare) separated them into three thematic sections, as the headings indicate, but the later edition which I have, just lists them together and rearranges the order (the number before the story title indicates the order in my edition). Those with an asterisk are the ones for which there are colour plates.
