Found out about this book through reading A walk in the woods by bill Bryson. Overall an important read that I would read again. It’s interesting to read this in 2026. It brought a lot of insight into how far we really have come and what has changed since this story.It stuck out how there wasn’t necessarily fear of being shot yet a fear of anti gay violence (with words or maybe some physicality), yet now as a woman in her mid 20’s, all I’ve really ever known is a fear and normalcy of gun violence and acceptance and normalcy of the LGBT+ community. Obviously things aren’t perfect (especially under this administration), and I’m lucky to have grown up in an area that has fought for gay rights and that the youth exploring themselves is accepted in whatever way it means. I know social media has played a big part in that as well as spreading hatred and violence. A double edged sword I could go on about.My only issues with the book are similar to others. I found it confusing and hard to follow along in some spots. Lots of names that I couldn’t seem to figure out who was who and where they came from or why they were there, other than the officers, lawyers, and 2 of the friends. Would have loved more detail or reminders to keep track of people and places. More detail in most places would’ve been nice. Sometimes was given a blip of information, that I feel could’ve been expanded on somewhat. Was also confused by the second author and who she was/when she’d pop up. I liked the aspect of the story having other perspectives added, but felt like it was just popped in there sometimes.
