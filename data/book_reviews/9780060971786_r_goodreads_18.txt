This is a masterpiece. Miller has such a depth of understanding of what was going on in the 20th century and he explores all the elements he grew up with that he later transformed into his plays.As an observant and sensitive little boy, he absorbed lessons from his family, his neighborhood, his Jewish heritage, and the class system that helped create the Great Depression. He illuminates these themes with dazzling insight and self-awareness. It's heartening, too, for the rest of us flawed human beings to see that failure after failure ultimately led to his becoming one of our very best playwrights.He felt his book wasn't as well received in America as it was in Europe, partly because he refused to write more about his relationship with Marilyn Monroe. I respect him for this but, sure enough, some of the other reviewers here criticize him for not telling all!I only regret that Miller didn't write more novels so that he could have been more accessible to more readers. But his plays make good reading, as well. He had the gift of being able to write plays with profound messages that were also entertaining and humane. Few can do that without sounding preachy.Some of these reviews criticize the way he jumps around in his timeline but the book is called "Timebends": Isn't that the way we remember things? Not in chronological order but impressionistically, as one things reminds us of another. He is truly an American gem.
