So hard to rate this.As a Christie fan, I absolutely love to read anything she's written. However, this is my least favorite of herMary Westmacott romances. It somehow manages to be really interesting and deadly boring at the same time.Honestly, it's the characters. And the subject matter. Maybe if I were more into composers and music, I would have liked this, but as it is? I thought the premise was so dumb.Fortunately, even a dumb premise had some sparkle by the time Christie was done with it.The skinny gist is that this sympathetic weird kid grows up to be an unsympathetic weird adult.He's terrified of music growing up. The family's piano is a monster to him, and even well into adulthood, he thinks music is just a cacophony of noise.Until, that is, when he's almost done with college and has to go to a concert to cover for his favorite cousin's shenanigans. Then he decides he's overwhelmed with the desire to write the future of music, which includes breaking glass and all kinds of other nonsense.With the music thing running throughout the background, the rest of the story is about his relationships with his over-dramatic mother, philandering/absentee father, the estate that he loved, his balsy cousin, their lifelong friendship with the Jewish boy next door, the woman of his dreams, and the sexy singer/actress who loves him.It's a great character study of what English society looked like before, during, and after WWI & WWII. I just simplycould notgive less of a shit about the artistic crowd, though.And like a lot of theseromances, you get less of a happily ever after and more of a look at personal growth. This is about what you desire vs what you can have.Like the Rolling Stones said, "You can't always get what you want, but if you try sometimes, well, you might find you get what you need."Recommended for hardcore Christie fans.
