Competently researched and written, no doubt, but morally bankrupt, treating its Fascist subject as though she were an ordinary celebrity, like Liz Taylor or somebody. What in Mitford's psychology and in that of her famous husband and sister, drove them to Fascism? The book offers no insight. A great deal of time and energy is spent on depicting the Mosleys' ordeal in prison, but none is spent reminding us of the ordeal of millions in concentration camps on the continent, which would very much put things in perspective, don't you think? And a great deal of energy is spent putting to rest decades-old rumors about the Mosley's posh conditions in jail. Fair enough, but that seems a curious area in which to place all your emphasis, to put it mildly. When the hated Mosleys are let out of jail, they leave during cover of the night, in the word of the author "as though they had committed some deep, dark crime". They HAD! They were traitors whose mission was to create a Fascist England! But that obvious point seems lost on the starry-eyed author, who seems as smitten with Hitler and his movement as Diana and Unity Mitford were. I'd only recommend this book if I knew there were no other books about Mosley and the Mitfords but there are several, of course, so I won't.
