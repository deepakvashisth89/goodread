"He leads them to a monastery hidden in "the valley of the blue moon" -- a land of mystery and matchless beauty where life is lived in tranquil wonder, beyond the grasp of a doomed world." Says the blurb. Maybe James Hilton based his idea on the hidden valley of Phobjikha, high up, 1.8 miles up, in the Himalayas of Bhutan.

Maybe the monastery is Gantey where girls and women, nuns, dance and sing the sacred dances of Buddhism and it looks like the whole world is bathed in a clear light from an immense blue sky where the wind sings through bells and the pines, and there is a kind of purity in this place untouched by commercialism that is hard to describe but easy to feel.
