I listened to this on audiobook and had no idea it was published in 1938. I see only 27 other people have troubled to rate it here on GR. Yet, somehow, the library app tells me "someone is waiting" for the audio.

Franklin’s life was long, and so was this book. The age of 84 years and 3 months doesn’t seem so very old by today’s standards, but in the book it seemed like he was highly conscious of his declining status for the last two decades of his life. In the first years of the Revolution he travelled to visit the Continental Army group that was invading Canada, and that trip seemed to almost be the end of him. Subsequently he went to Paris as a kind of American goodwill ambassador, and stayed there for nine years. He successfully schmoozed the French into a treaty of mutual aid. It was complicated – he was one of a 3-person legation, there were British spies everywhere, and nothing happened in a straightforward manner. Through it all, Franklin’s esteem only increased.

Prior to that, Franklin had lived in London for fully 16 years ending in 1775. It was very interesting to see how much effort Franklin put into the idea of securing the rights of Englishmen to the colonists. He was convinced that political liberty within the comforting umbrella of the British Empire would have been good for both Britain and for the colonies. Here again, through 16 years, little happened in a straightforward manner. When open rebellion broke out, he was unjustly pilloried as the cause of it all, which was very far from the actual case. He was widely reviled in England, almost to the extent that he was later hailed in France.

His wife Dorothy died in Philadelphia while he was in London. That was sad. In those days one did not lightly cross the Atlantic, and even in his London days it seems he felt that his declining robustness was a factor in limiting his ability to stand up to the rigors of crossing.

It seems he is the one who, during the Constitutional Convention, proposed the compromise that gave each State equal representation in the Senate, while having representation by population in the House. By that time he was super-old and for the most part he just lent gravitas to the proceedings by his very presence.

What a life he had. A true self-made man. There’s a reason for him to be an icon all these centuries later.
