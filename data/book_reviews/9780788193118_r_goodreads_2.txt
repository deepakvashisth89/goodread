I have my own problems with Starbucks. Some of it is due to its globalization efforts and how it has conducted its business and some is how they shaped the American tastes for coffee.

There is no doubt that they raised the level of coffee experience for North America. I am grateful that they were able to do that. It is kind of like saying great band but hate their fans.

In terms of their politics and doing business, they have changed and adjusted. I begrudgingly have to give them that. I still don't like that they are perceived as an indicator of gentrification, and that their fans will forsake a local, and better quality place because of the name. They have become a symbol for me, rightly or wrongly.

This book is similar. It gives the basics of most coffee preparations and a more or less standard definition where it suits the Starbucks brand. There are gaps and then there are places where they add in the Starbucks way without saying that it is a Starbucks standard and not a general standard. An example is the coffee sizes or their way of roasting.

The recipes are pretty standard and good examples can be found in most general cookbooks. Remember, Starbucks is in the business of selling baked goods and coffee. This is a simple pleasure that many places can give. Even in your own home.

I am clearing my shelves of older cookbooks and coffee related books. This one does not make the cut even though it is a decent starter book, mainly due to my own biases.
