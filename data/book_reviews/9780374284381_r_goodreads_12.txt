"obviously, doctor, you've never been a thirteen-year-old girl.”full review here:https://postcardsbyelle.substack.com/...the thing i always simultaneously love and am horrified by is the atmosphere that eugenides builds in this book. the suburb that the book is set in is so insular, residents so obsessed with themselves and their neighbors that it feels claustrophobic from start to finish. for the duration you read it, you will feel like this neighborhood is the only one in the entire world. add the suffocating male gaze into the mix, and you get: breathing problems.i don’t have a cohesive review but what i found the most difficult yet interesting was the dichotomy between the mythologization of the lisbon girls in the boys’ minds until they were well past adolescence, and the reality of who they were: girls who never got the help they needed. because the sisters all die before they are adults, it is almost as if they are immortalized along with the boys’ disturbing fascination with them. in a way, not only has the lisbon girls perpetually remained adolescents, but the boys have as well, unable to escape the clutches of their infatuation with girls and their subsequent suicides.dark and disturbing but hauntingly beautiful.
