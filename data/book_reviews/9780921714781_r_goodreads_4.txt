Not worth it. Definitely written for people who already agree with everything Jeffrey says within the book, and the author fails to realize that his "proofs" are more along the lines of clues or support but cannot definitively prove anything, thereby resulting in the same sort of fully-accepting reaction toward the data he accuses evolutionists of. His writing creates the impression he believes everyone who believes in evolution is a simpleton who cannot grasp complex concepts (he actually says at one point that anybody who believes the human eye developed through evolution probably also believes in Santa Claus). Many of his examples of advanced science in the Bible could just as easily be read as metaphors, and his examples and evidence throughout the rest of the book often seem as if they were simply regurgitated from his sources.Overall, this book isn't going to convince anybody new to reject evolution, and is far more likely to alienate them. If you're looking for a book to examine scientific evidence of special creation, I'd recommend you read Phillip Johnson instead- after all, Jeffrey gets a good deal of his own material directly from Johnson.
