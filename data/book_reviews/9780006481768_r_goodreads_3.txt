This is a tricky one. I first read this in elementary school, and something about it really stuck with me, and then I spent years keeping an eye out for it, but I never saw it again and had forgotten all but a few scant details and the title until one really late night spent on google.

The ideas are new and challenging... for an 8-year-old, which I happened to be when I first read it. I think it's a very friendly and safe introduction to the world of dystopian fiction for a child. There are some possibly disturbing ideas for a kid: the limits of mom and dad's protection, untrustworthy adult/authority figures, friends disappearing mysteriously, etc. But the overall message is one of kid empowerment and the importance of community and taking care of each other. It also skips merrily over some unsettling implications (that I completely missed in the first read at 8) in favour of a neat happy ending.

I very much think this is children's lit rather than young adult. What's challenging for an 8-yr-old is trite for an actual teenager, let alone an adult, but YMMV.
