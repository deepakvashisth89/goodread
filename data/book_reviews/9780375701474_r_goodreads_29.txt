"Mostly, I have been impressed by how little value our society puts on saving the lives of those who are in such despair as to want to end them. It is a societal illusion that suicide is rare"3.5 stars. This specific book will not be for everyone, but the topic itself is something everyone should look into, read and learn more about. Each case is highly individual and complex, and is not something most people will ever really understand. The destructive ways in which some have chosen to end their lives is truly harrowing and ultimately speaks to the catastrophic consequences of mental illness, especially when paired with alcohol and drug abuse.Much of this book is science and research based and includes lots of data, statistics and figures. This, although important, is difficult for me to read. I just do not enjoy it (not that this book should be necessarily enjoyable). The personal anecdotes and stories, such as the chapter on Meriwether Lewis is what I prefer to read and that is typically where the information and the key points stick in my mind. Numbers and data is often just lost on me.Jamison writes with passion, knowledge and personal experience. She does a great job of driving home the point that suicide is widely misunderstood and stigmatized. Historically, taking ones own life is seen as cowardice and sinful and the idea of it being a selfish act still exists today.
