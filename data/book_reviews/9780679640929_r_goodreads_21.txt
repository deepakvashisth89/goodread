Don't be fooled by the short length of this book, or the background of the author. Kung packs a hell of a lot into 200+ pages.

It may be tempting to question a Catholic's partiality and ask whether one can be truly evenhanded or fairly critical of an institution they're part of. But Kung succeeds there too, pulling no punches about what he sees as the sins of the church over the centuries. You won't finish this knowing everything about the Catholic Church, but you'll understand a great deal more, and probably want to know more. That makes this book a profound success.

If you do read it, and want to know more about a pope he devotes some space to with good reason - Pope Pius XII, whose diplomatic efforts enabled Nazi Germany and whose failure to condemn the treatment of the Jews is a lasting shame for him - I highly recommend Hitler's Pope by John Cornwell. It goes into great detail about Eugenio Pacelli (Pius XII)'s accomplishments, and the appalling consequences, for which he never took responsibility.
