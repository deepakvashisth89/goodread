The salience and prescience of the core claim of the book has grown in my estimation over time; that is, the argument that public discourse has been debased because of the medium on which it occurs, when that medium is predominant a medium of entertainment.

Now now, some will say, "Stetson, you're an old curmudgeon grumbling about kids these days." or some such, but this isn't to say that public discourse doesn't exist or that intelligible arguments cannot be made on all kinds of forms. It that the dominant medium of public discourse has become increasingly hostile to the pre-requisites of intelligent and thoughtful exchange: brevity, intensity, emotionality, falsity, etc. Simply, you can't have a conversation at dinner about the finer points Plato's while in front of the AMP cranked to 11 blaring screamo. Nonetheless, we insist upon jamming the insights of public intellectuals next to all kinds of junk. Maybe we'll adapt to this, but I think this is an insurmountable ask for a large portion of citizens.

And if anything, Postman's thinking is too interested in television, which should have been understood as a brief stop on the way to many more screens streaming and beaming stuff into the brains of more and more and more people until the entire purpose of public discourse is impossible, even unthinkable. No one believes, and perhaps it was always thus, that persuasion using reason and evidence is sufficient to solve human problems. The only solutions arise from technological innovation, spontaneous order (markets), and extremely motivated and coordinated elite formations.
