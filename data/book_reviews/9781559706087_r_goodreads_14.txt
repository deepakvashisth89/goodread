I thought it was a very light, mildly interesting read. I love learning about Russian history. I'm not so big on conspiracy theories. The author does put out his case fairly well though, and I'm interested to see if there ever will be any DNA testing on the body (what's left of it) that is in Tsar Alexander I's tomb.I wish the author had fleshed out the characters a bit more. Most people come off as neither likeable or unlikeable. Just bland. I'm sure this wasn't the intent, but Tsar Paul comes off as the most interesting, at least compared to his son. I wonder if his son really was this sort of self-pitying, indecisive, narcissistic man who really thought the world's sufferings were through one act of his, and if he really was the coward who fled his wife and country, to live in obscurity (but not total) out in Siberia as a sort of Holy Man. Interesting stuff. I probably won't be reading more books about Alexander I any time soon though, unless someone convinces me that he's a lot more than an ineffectual depressive who moped more than he ruled.
