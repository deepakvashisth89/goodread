4.5 stars - This is the best Ngaio Marsh mystery I have read, I think.Blackmail is the crime, and Scotland Yard Inspector Roderick Alleyn, Marsh’s upper class detective, asks a beloved family friend - invited everywhere, and known to everyone - to watch for suspicious activity at social functions. Lord Robert is loved by all, the sort of charming, kind, friendly “universal uncle” who asks the awkward wallflowers to dance, draws them into sparkling conversations, introduces them around, amuses everyone with his stories from his Foreign Office days. He calls Alleyn from a debutante ball very late one evening to report he’s found the blackmailer, and will come to discuss at the Yard - but the call is interrupted when an unknown person walks into the room. Robert changes topics quickly and hangs up, but when he a taxi pulls up at the Yard, Lord Robert is in it, but he’s dead. Now Alleyn has to investigate the murder of an old friend.All of the investigation takes place over the next couple days, and Marsh does an excellent job showing the detailed, meticulous and often dreary police work that goes into police work in the 1930s, before all of the modern technology of today. It was also a fascinating dive into a typical 1930s debutante season in London, with some wonderful characters and suspects. There is also a resolution of Alleyn’s romantic resolution with Agatha Troy, the painter from the previous case (Artists in Crime), which I read many years ago, but don’t remember! I want to reread that, along with her other 1930s mysteries.
