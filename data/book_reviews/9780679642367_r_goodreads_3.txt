This is not my favourite Verne novel-- probably because I can count on one hand the 700+ page books that actually needed to be that long --but I can see why it forms an essential part of his Voyages Extraordinaires series. Read after Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea for the best experience.

Long it may be, but there's no denying The Mysterious Island is an epic survival story. If I were ever stranded on an island, this would be a handy manual to have with me. It's like The Martian on steroids.

The five men and a dog who find themselves marooned were Northern prisoners in the American Civil War before they used a hot air balloon to escape. They crash-land on an unknown island in the Pacific and proceed to try to keep themselves alive whilst discovering the island's secrets. Why do weird things keep happening? Could others be on the island with them?

The pace meanders but it's not unpleasant, especially if you have an interest in nature, wildlife and survival skills (though I'd add a caveat for animal lovers that there's a LOT of hunting and animal death). It was quite fun to discover the island and all its mysteries with the characters, and it was an easy book to dip in and out of alongside my other reads.

In the later chapters, though, it becomes more emotionally intense and reveals itself to be deeper than just an adventure story. Despite how it first appears-- with the five men declaring themselves colonists --Verne offers critiques of imperialism. And my takeaway was that no matter how much science, technology and ingenuity humans bring, the power of nature will ultimately trump their efforts.
