The best thing that can be said about this book is that you get a lot of Poirot. He is present in almost every page where there are no notes that had been written by the characters. We are privy to his thoughts.Not only that, but the Poirot we see is almost human. He is normal, foreign, but normal. He isn't the eccentric walking bag of tics that a few generations of fans have grown to laugh at/deride. Despite that, I gave the book 3 stars.Oh, the story is cleverly written. But we get the same multiple accounts of the one event, that of murder of Amyas Crale. It quickly becomes grating and tiring until numbness takes over the mind. The murderer for once looks to have escaped the law. That's a first. Otherwise, I can only shrug.
