Left this for a day to stew before attempting a review.Warning: there may be spoilers lurking ahead.The experience of reading the title story was of something to be endured rather than enjoyed. It was a "stunt narrative" written in working class voices and I suspect this was Kipling showing off (or rather, attempting to do so). The need to struggle with a story composed of 99% dialect / vernacular / truncated words —every sentence is littered with apostrophes indicated missing letters— is reflected in my 2 star rating.A British reader might get more out of this. I struggled to make sense out of it, and had no ideas what on earth a "whisk-drive" could be.However, after an overnight simmering, I wondered if perhaps this story is actually about the notion of "service." Mrs. Ashcroft was in service (as they say) as a cook. Might the other elements of this story also indicate a life of service?{Note: this review is for "The Wish House" story only, not the collection}
