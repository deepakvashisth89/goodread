Metamorphosis and Other Stories by Franz Kafka (1883-1924) contains a handful of short stories and a larger number of very short ones. Frankly, most of them didn’t move me — many of Kafka’s writings read more like observations than narratives. I was especially interested in the title story, Metamorphosis, as well as in The Stoker and In the Penal Colony. In each of them, the author explores power and the value of life, themes that would resurface in The Trial and The Castle .

is an enigmatic story. When Gregor Samsa awakes one morning from troubled dreams to find himself changed into a monstrous cockroach, the reader cannot help wondering what brought about this change, and what it signifies. Gregor himself, however, seems concerned only with his absence from work — the chief clerk (!) does indeed appear at a remarkably early hour to check on him at home. In Gregor’s family, for whom economic survival — a cockroach scaring off tenants doesn’t help — matters most, there is little room for love and affection.
