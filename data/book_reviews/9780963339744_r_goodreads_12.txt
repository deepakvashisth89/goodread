4.0 stars —- Have you ever started a book on your “Want to Read” list not knowing one thing about it. Unfortunately, “The Girl Next Door” was that book for me. Needless to say —if you are someone triggered by detailed descriptions of graphic violence and child abuse— you do not want to read this book. This is the first time I’ve read a Jack Ketchum novel, and I am not sure if I want to ever read another one. Why (you ask)would you say that about a 4 star book? The answer is — I can’t ever remember being more stressed while reading a book. The entire book was so well written and realistic, I felt like I was a witness to the torture the book described in such detail. These realistic and intense scenes of violence really got to me. I wanted to put the book down several times, but I was determined to find out what the outcome would be for the victim of this abuse, Meg. Although, the book is written from the perspective of Meg’s young friend and neighbor, David, I could only think of Meg’s perspective of what was happening to her. The book did have an impact on me in that it really made me think about the hesitancy to act when you suspect or even know that someone else is suffering. It also really digs deep into the senseless and cruel actions of those who suffer from extreme untreated mental illness and the effect someone suffering from this could have on young children under their influence. This book will stay with me for a long time. Its only saving grace was that it wasn’t true, but while you reading about the plight of the poor child in the center of it, you sure will think it is. The book really makes you think of the level of depravity that some people are capable of and what you can do to better help others get out of these situations. To summarize—“The Girl Next Door” is a very well written but torturous read about extreme child abuse and how inaction in these circumstances is almost as bad as committing the act yourself. This book is not for the faint of heart.
