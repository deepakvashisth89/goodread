I checked this book out of the library because I wanted to read fishing stories and also because I was expecting to find some writing by Horace Kephart. Unfortunately there is no Kephart in the book, but that does not diminish the quality of the read in any way. True enough I found many enjoyable stories of fishing at the beginning of the book, but once I progressed to the stories of trapping and hunting I found I still couldn't put it down to save my life. I discovered in these pages a romantic vision of the outdoors I didn't even know I was longing for. One of the reasons I've always been somewhat turned off by hunting is all the gadget geekery that goes into the hardware and accessories. It has become a very technical sport focused on the most efficient, technological limiting-out methods rather than creating an enjoyable experience based on respect and love of the outdoors. The stories in this book are simply dripping with that nostalgic outdoorsman's spirit that even as a non-hunter I have always felt any time I set foot in the woods. After all, hunting and fishing aren't about collecting a bunch of toys and racing around the back of yonder, they're about respect for the natural world that no matter how hard we try to fool ourselves we are still apart of, and about being able to rely on yourself when so much of the focus of civilization is luring you into dependency of one form or another. If only Field & Stream still had writing of this caliber, but alas a browse of the most recent issues shows it too has fallen prey to the times and given over mostly to gadgets, gizmos, and the hypertechnicalization of what should be the most un-technical experiences in a person's life. Then again, there aren't too many Kepharts and Zerns around anymore either.
