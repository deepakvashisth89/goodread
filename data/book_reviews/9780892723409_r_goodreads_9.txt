Fun collection of horror stories set in and around lighthouses. A lot of them skew older, because a century or so ago lighthouses weren't automated and so there was a lot more scope for various things - a lot related, in practice or in metaphor, to isolation - to wreak terrifying havoc. Nowadays, of course, most lighthouses don't have anyone living there; they're just appealing structures standing off on their own but because they're so abandoned it's harder to set a story there.It's almost a shame. There's something very appealing about the job of a lighthouse keeper - although I say that as someone who enjoys isolation more than most. I suspect that I'm romanticising it, though, and that I wouldn't enjoy it nearly so much if I was stuck in one now. Not without internet, anyway!The best story here, for me at least, is "The Fog Horn" by Ray Bradbury. It's just so sad... poor old sea monster.
