This book consists of letters to the author, a columnist from the '80s, from Vietnam vets, sometimes their wives, with an introduction and some additional commentary by the author - letters in response to a question the author asked in a column: Is it true that vets were spat upon in American when they returned from Vietnam? The hundreds of responses the author - Greene - received were compiled/edited/organized into the extremely interesting, even mesmerizing volume. For anyone who lived through the era, it's really more than a trip down memory lane: It fills in numerous gaps - at least for me it did - in memories of the time. I at least had no idea of the amount of rancor vs the soldiers out West - that they were received with hostility in SF, Seattle, etc. My memories of the war were that I couldn't deal with the it after a while and deliberately stopped reading the paper etc. I remember doing this in HS - I turned to poetry, films, reading etc. instead. It was enough arguing about the war with the parents at dinner every night. Or watching it on TV every night. This book for me at least filled in a lot of gaps - provided insight into what the soldiers went through, in Nam and when they returned. I definitely recommend it to anyone interested in finding out more about the war.A few quotes:"[Letter from G.M.
