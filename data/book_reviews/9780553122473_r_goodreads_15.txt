Title: The Secret AdversaryAuthor: Agatha ChristieGenre: MysteryPublication Year: 1922Review: Before my favored authors list began with King, Koontz, and Cross, there was Christie. I was ten and my brother was nine. Although it was my name on the library card, I would let him grab one book out of my three per week. One for him, a Christie murder mystery, and something out of horror or science fiction. It was Heinlein or Bradbury that usually found themselves in my bookbag.The Secret Adversary (Tommy and Tuppence #1) was one of the first Christie books I ever read. I was Obsessive Compulsive, even way back then, starting with the first of a series, unable to continue unless I read the second next. Here, Thomas Beresford and Tuppence Cowley are both looking for work after World War I, when they meet up for lunch. They have known each other since they were children and there is an immediate chemistry between the characters.The mystery itself is a bit of a MacGuffin that belies logic. Although both young people were involved in the war effort, would the British government hire them to work as agents of espionage? Whatever the case, indubitably Tommy and Tuppence work well together and the verbal jousting is such that you want to experience more of the quips. Clearly, I was not the only person to feel this way, since there was a series of Tommy and Tuppence books. Only five books, but still a great series except for the last book.
