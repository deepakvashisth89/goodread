Stories are dark and moody. I could often feel some Charles Bukowski’s motifs though the tales are told with quite original bleakness. It was probably the atmosphere of the time.
Ian McEwan seems to be in search of his own literary style and he often manages to find it.
In Melton Mowbray in 1875 at an auction of articles of ‘curiosity and worth’, my great-grandfather, in the company of M his friend, bid for the penis of Captain Nicholls who died in Horsemonger jail in 1873. It was bottled in a glass twelve inches long, and, noted my great-grandfather in his diary that night, ‘in a beautiful state of preservation’.

Angst of the youth… Fantasies hidden deep in minds… Strange events and occurances… Life showing its odd sides… First love… Last rites…

There is a time when everything starts… And there is a time when everything ends…
