Thematically,Breederis about abortion, but what it has to say about the subject is as murky as the cursed house that serves as the novel’s setting. Still, I had fun reading the book. The story centers on a young couple, Rachel and Hugh, both lawyers. Rachel is Hugh’s second wife- the first one having suddenly died. At the beginning of the novel, we learn that Rachel has just suffered a miscarriage. It’s her grief over this event that draws you into the book. Or at least me, anyway. Hugh’s family is rich, and his father gifts them an ancient house in Washington D.C. The place is derelict, full of rats and mice and roaches, a real fixer-upper. The place also has a sordid past.Children were killed there. We also learn that Hugh’s family was mixed up in the house’s history.Anyway, the book’s not deep. It is enjoyable, however: a mash-up ofRosemary’s BabyandCandyman, with a touch ofThe Haunting of Hill House.
