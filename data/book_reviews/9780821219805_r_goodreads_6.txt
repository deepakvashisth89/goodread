As you might expect a lot of the images are stunning, even if color was not Ansel Adams’ preferred medium. Still, his black and white work is undoubtedly superior, even with modern color printing techniques.I thought the sections on Adams’ history with color was interesting, especially the excerpts and letters from the man himself. Especially insightful is his note on the “trap of reality” in color photography, the idea that color photography falsely implies a “record of reality”, even though “the accuracy of the color photography implies is more a matter of belief than of actuality”.Incredible and hilarious how loosely Adams wrote in some of his more informal letters, given that he was so clearly well spoken / written. An example: “(No discount if there ain’t no rainboo [sic]!) some new experimental film. HUshhhhhhhhhh.”
