This is one of the most disturbing books I've read. In the early 1920s, the Osage had the highest per capita income in the world and the highest murder rate in the world. The tragedy of the Osage reign of terror is something that is exceptionally well hidden and buried away. It needs to be told.This is a frightening story but I must say the writing leaves much to be desired. It's very difficult to follow the meandering branches of the overlapping family trees and the heavy freight of sidetracks to the author's personal life are distracting. Nonetheless, it is a book everyone would benefit from reading.
