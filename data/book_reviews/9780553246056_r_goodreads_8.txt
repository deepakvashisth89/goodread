Another favorite, I definitely enjoyed this one more than the last. Clara Gamadge is off caring for a sick aunt when Henry, stuck in NYC doing war work (1943) is invited by an old school chum to the family estate in upstate New York.

Sylvanus Hutter’s (“Syl”) rather flighty, temperamental Aunt Florence has decamped with her entire household to the country to avoid Nazi bombs. This was an interesting history lesson, as I did not realize they had blackout conditions in NYC during WWII.

The back of my paperback puts it very well: “Gamadge is unable to refuse the tug of an old school tie…Aunt Florence – rather less Auntie Em than Auntie Mame. A giddy socialite, terrified of Nazi bombs, Florence has moved her extensive household of hangers-on to the family mansion in upstate New York. But menace seems to have followed them, in the form of threatening messages, inserted into the manuscript of Florence‘s painfully bad novel in progress. Several members of the household are convinced the messages are emanating from Another World, but the politely pragmatic Gamadge suspects a culprit rather closer to home.”

This was great fun, once I got straight who was who among the household, and it’s a doozy. Flo married a hunky sportsman type many years previously; he’s MUCH younger and an obvious suspect, as he plays around. Flo likes to keep people around her, dancing to her tune, but also tires of people and casts them off if they don’t do as she wants. Everyone (among the hangers-on) had family money at one time, but now haven’t a bean (I’m assuming the stock market crash of 1929, followed by the Great Depression took its toll - again, more interesting history).

I really like Gamadge, and he’s in his element here; he spent many childhood summers and holidays at Underhill, the Hutter family estate. Aunt Florence was always loving and kind, but he knows she could be controlling and temperamental. Among the household are three women who tried to serve as her ‘secretary’, and the current one, who’s managed to hang on for four years and become quite a favorite - and is typing the manuscript that’s been tampered with, making her a prime suspect as well. Aunt Florence and Syl share the estate, but when one dies, the family millions are unlocked and go to the survivor. So quite a satisfying powderkeg!

I can’t say Daly always plays fair, in the traditional GA sense. I suspected a character right away, then didn’t. But turns out I was right - for reasons that aren’t revealed until the big climax, after two people are killed - the second with a houseful of police responding to the first murder! But still, fun, complex puzzle I truly enjoyed.
