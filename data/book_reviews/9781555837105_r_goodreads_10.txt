In Troublemaker, we get a murder right from the get-go. Brandstetter's investigating the death of a policyholder, which is pretty clearly murder, and the police already have a suspect in custody. Only, Brandstetter thinks they've got the wrong person.This book has a way higher gay character ratio than the previous two novels. The victim owns a gay bar, the man the police have arrested is at least a one night stand, possibly more, who's been living with a well known architect, and the victim's business partner is busy training one of twelve competitors for a sort of talent competition between a bunch of the local gay bars. On top of Brandstetter's own circle of friends.There's not as much personal stuff in this one either. We do see Dave's partner, Doug, Doug's mother, an unwelcome house guest, and a couple of their friends, but the book doesn't linger on them, which suits me fine.I noticed at least one reviewer complaining about the lack of sex. To which I would like to point out,this book came out in1975. That, and the fact that it portrays gay people doing normal things, having normal lives, and not as sex-fiends, or as if being gay is some kind offate worse than death,is hugely important, and if you can't fathom that, just please never speak to me.I really don't know why I never tried these when I was younger. I've been vagely aware of Hansen for decades at least. More than likely it was because by the time I had heard of him, I'd already developed the very strong impression that all gay fiction was depressing, woe-is-we kind of BS where the best you could hope for was to die pretty.Anyway, this was awesome, and I look forward to the rest of the series.
