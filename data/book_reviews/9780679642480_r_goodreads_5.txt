It is Faulkner's first book, which I read long ago; I have dazzling memories of it. Thanks to this novel, I became passionate about this author and feared disappointment.
It is undoubtedly Faulkner's most constructed novel, coming closest to the status of a great classic. The book opens and closes with Lena, a young pregnant woman at the beginning of the story, who has crossed several states in search of the father of her child. Lena is an embodiment of absolute and serene femininity; she reminds me of those prehistoric fertility goddesses; nothing seems to disturb her deep tranquility.
And between this beginning and this end, which radiates this light present in the title, there is violence, injustice, stupidity, and the suffering of beings who cannot find their place. In the center, Joe Christmas, whose terrible story we are gradually discovering, highlights all the flaws and violence of this southern society. Puritanical, racist, not accepting otherness, neither between races nor the sexes, based on hatred of the other and ultimately contempt of oneself.
For me, it is one of the most beautiful books ever written that marks us forever.
