A serial killer, clumsily named Jack de Paris, is at work in literary Paris in 1925. Although his presence adds a frisson of tension to the artists' hard-drinking gatherings, he is an afterthought, and hardly places this story in the murder mystery category. When one of their own gang is killed, the motive is so flimsy, and the culprit so quickly caught as to make the murder incidental to the plot.

The real story is a roman a clef within a roman a clef in which the Hemingway character, Jason Waddington, threatens to reveal embarrassing stories about his literary friends in an upcoming book, thus causing tension within the group. A reader must be content to follow Toronto native Mike Ward around for drinks at Paris cafes where he introduces you to writers, painters and colorful characters. It's not an unpleasant way to pass the time.
