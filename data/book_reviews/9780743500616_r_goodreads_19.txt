Uch... honestly, I just wanted a light read, a thriller type deal that would keep me gripped over a journey I was making, but this was too damned stupid to enjoy it on that level.The four women from Sex and the City make a list of what makes a Perfect Man. The list is exactly what you would imagine such a list would contain, but for some reason it makes actual national news. Even before it did so, and the women receive scorn and mocking and teasing in response to it leaking into the office, I found it an overreaction, so when actual news agencies became interested I wondered if this was supposed to be science fiction. I suppose if this were set in the mid 19th century I could see it making the newspapers, but the year 2000!? If the list were at least original, contained anything surprising or were particularly witty, but honest to goodness it isn't any of those things. It's things like: handsome and faithful. I mean, seriously. No. Seriously. Just... seriously.The thriller/mystery part is not very good. You can't work it out for yourself, because there isn't a puzzle to work out. Tension is created by people behaving too stupid for actual normal humans to comprehendTJ knows she is on the killer's hit list. Two out of her four friends are dead. She knows the killer works where she works. What does she do? She goes to work. And then a co-worker asks her to go down to the dark basement with her, and what does she do? Of course she goes along. I mean, Jesus, at this point the blame for the murder is 50/50. Or Jaine is staying with her sister, stalked by a ruthless murderer, her only way of knowing what's up is her phone. What does she do? She turns the phone off, of course. To be fair, it's her sister that does it, but seriously, how bloody dumb can you get before you have trouble breathing?I wasn't aware, before I bought this book, that the author was a romance author, so I was surprised about the sex scenes, and the amount of attention on Sam's body. But honestly, I found the romance part really badly done. There was no romance, actually. There was just sex. They have sex and then they decide to get married, after a week. Sam is a personality-less walking erection. How exactly am I supposed to feel about him, if I know nothing about him, but that he wants to have sex all the time. Hmpf.There are a couple of things which are ridiculous and over-the-top but I can't be bothered to list themThe things that come to mind off the top of my head: Sam deciding to have sex with Jaine while she's on the phone with her sister - gross! The girls being so upset about their friend's death that they actually laugh at her funeral... yeah, way to set the tone, author! One of the 'friends' comes to her funeral dressed in bright red. I don't know, maybe that's a cultural thing, but it would be considered absolutely outrageous if it were done where I come from. Apart from the inappropriate colour for a funeral, the attention-grabbing sexy dress would simply be inappropriate full stop. Apparently the logic behind the choice of dress was that Marcy, the dead woman, liked the colour. She also liked sex with younger men, why don't you invite the Chippendales, you moron?! Ugh, those people. I mean, has the author actually met people? I think someone should introduce her. She'd be surprised. She'd learn, for example, not to make Jaine react to the nightmare unfolding around her by extreme horniness. Are you sure the villain is the mentally deranged one, author? Jaine is stalked by a psycho-killer but all she can think about is her wedding? I mean, come on! They know the killer works where her victims work, and the police has the list of all the workers and somehow their search never flags up a person who had been in a mental institution for several years after brutally killing their own mother!? What kind of police force is this, precisely?. On the whole, and on every level this was a disappointment. Luckily, it's the sort of thing that I will forget tomorrow.
