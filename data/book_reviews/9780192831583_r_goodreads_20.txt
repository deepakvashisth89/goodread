Tacitus's The Histories is a compelling yet dull series of books that primarily go over the year 69 AD, where four Roman emperors competed for the throne. His detailed accounts of the emperors (Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and Vespasian) shows the good, the bad, and the ugly sides of each. He goes into great detail for the politics and battles behind the civil wars. Tacitus even gives a glimpse into how common Romans were affected by the wars; all of this information was very interesting to say the least!
Not everything was perfect though, some passages read like clusters of words that felt meaningless. New names and locations are introduced frequently to the point where you lose track of their purpose easily. Many of the people are only mentioned once and not brought up again.
A good portion of my complaints however are with the translator/revisionist. It could be their fault for the text feeling lackluster and clustered due to translation. I understand that notes are necessary for all history books, but in this one they feel excessive. A good chunk of the notes are useless, as the reader can figure out what Tacitus is describing by simply reading the book.
It's a shame that the rest of the books in The Histories have been lost to time, I'm sure they would've shed more light onto the Flavian era of the Roman Empire!
