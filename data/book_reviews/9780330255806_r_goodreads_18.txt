All Things Bright and Beautiful#3-4)by James Herriot (1973, 1974 edition, hardcover), 378 pages.If you are a veterinarian, you will definitely want to read, and even have this series sitting on your bookshelf. You may find it very interesting to read how they tried to cure different illnesses, especially pertaining to cattle, compared to the medicines of today.Herriot’s stories are wonderfully written. Such a compassionate man who really seemed to love and care for all his patients through all hours of the day or night.In this book, he relates how he ends up in a serious relationship with his wife, Helen. She was introduced in the first book with some cute stories of by chance encounters. Here, they are married. It must be tough to be a man in love and trying to make that first good impression on the woman of interest. He flunked every time. But, maybe that’s what drew her to him.You get more wonderful stories of his crazy adventures in doctoring all sorts of farm animals. During the ‘30’s and 40’s, these were the years when science was just evolving and true vaccines and antibiotics that actually worked were being developed. He was living and working in the crux of progress.Then, there was the draft for World War II. His friend and business partner, Siegfried, left first. They stayed up all night drinking and reminiscing. James was the second to leave…very sad. Then, Tristan, Siegfried’s brother, was last to go. By this time, the war was in full swing and they actually needed veterinarians to care for all the animals involved in the war. Tristan became qualified just in time.Onto book #5-6, All Things Wise and Wonderful. I can’t imagine how he could continue writing at this level of interesting and humorous stories.
