Vanessa March has had a fight with her husband, and he has gone off to Stockholm for work. Or so she thinks, but a friend. Mrs. Lacy, tells her she saw him on a newsreel report about a fire in a traveling circus in Austria. Vanessa checks out the newsreel and sure enough, there’s her husband, comforting a pretty blonde in front of the disaster.

Mrs. Lacy has asked Vanessa to escort her seventeen-year-old son, Timothy Lacy, to Vienna, and in her rage at her husband’s apparent deception, Vanessa agrees. Timothy turns out to be a sweet young man, and there is some very poignant stuff about him, caught between two parents who don’t really care about him. He has an ambition to become a trainer at the famous training school for Lipizzaner horses in Austria, but first he and Vanessa get caught up in the search for her husband, which draws them inevitably into adventure. The husband’s situation is not what it appeared to be on the newsreel, and he becomes part of the action. The hero of the story may be one of my favorites in Stewart’s books, and at least the heroine doesn’t have to go running off into danger alone.

The dynamics of this story are unusual—married heroine instead of single woman trying to decide between two suspect men; young male sidekick instead of a female one; the circus setting; and the horses. It is slow to pick up suspense but I didn’t mind at all: I loved the setting and the characters, and there’s enough of the horse-mad little girl in me to adore a touching and magical horse story. The last half of the book gets busy enough with villains and chases and powerful cars, the sort of excitement Mary Stewart fans are looking for, all of which I enjoyed all the more because it was well grounded in likable characters who trusted one another. I came away fully satisfied.
