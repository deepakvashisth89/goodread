A solar phenomenon causes people and animals (mammals) to get significantly more intelligent.

The story shows the world-wide effects but focuses on two separate storylines. One about a previously barely literate farmhand who discovers a new role for himself after the change. In the other, a brilliant physicist and fellow scientists work on the problems and opportunities presented by the change.

Part of the issue that humanity encounters is that while everyone is much smarter, personalities and emotions don’t change much.

Anderson did a great job of telling a few personal, individual stories as well as the broader social, political, economic consequences of his premise.

There is some philosophical and intellectual discussion balanced out with tension, drama, and action sequences. The ending is pretty satisfying and Anderson doesn’t go on too long with any of the story arcs.

Some of the ideas explored in Brain Wave:

🧠The question of whether the world be better if everyone was more intelligent and thoughtful

🧠That what you apply your thoughts too is just as important as being smarter

🧠Man’s potential for scientific discovery and role in the wider universe

A few of things I didn’t like as much:

🧠People doing manual labor are suddenly bored with it. This is an oversimplification, which might be necessary for the scope of a novel but felt a bit elitist.

🧠The Characters are not developed very deeply.

🧠The psychological effects generalized, even for one of the main characters who suffers consequences.
