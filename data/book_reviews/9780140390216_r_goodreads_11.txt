I was glad to stumble across this classic in the library. It's a bravura work of imagination, tracing a soldier's mercurial thoughts and anxieties during wartime. In style, not content, it reminded me of the part of 'In Cold Blood' where Truman Capote imagines himself deeply into the mind of one of the murderers. It wasn't always an easy read, but there were some startling passages of exceptional writing, for example:Once the line encountered the body of a dead soldier. He lay upon his back staring at the sky. He was dressed in an awkward suit of yellowish brown. The youth could see that the soles of his shoes had been worn to the thinness of writing paper, and from a great rent in one the dead foot projected piteously. And it was as if fate had betrayed the soldier. In death it exposed to his enemies that poverty which in life he had perhaps concealed from his friends.(Ch.3)
