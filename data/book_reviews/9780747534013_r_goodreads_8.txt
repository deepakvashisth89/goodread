Five Stars of the Canadian Scale.

Having been born in the same mining townsite as Jane Urquhart (i.e. Little Longlac) and having lived near Silver Islet where much of the action takes place, I can say that in “The Underpainter” Urquhart gives a masterful portrait of the history and society of Northern Ontario’s mining communities in the 20th Century. Currently residing near and frequently working in Cobourg (a.k.a. Davenport), I can say that she does a similarly splendid job with Northumberland County, Ontario. Sadly, I know essentially nothing about the “Ashcan” school of painting and thus am unable to say if her treatment of this artistic movement is as good although I would not be surprised to learn that it is.
In terms of its character conflict and plotting, “The Underpainter” is a tour-de-force. Austin Fraser the protagonist is a totally abject character who through the skill of Urquhart nonetheless holds the reader’s interest the very end. Despite Urquhart’s warnings that he will not, the reader still hopes to the end that Fraser will seize the opportunity to redeem himself once he understands the depth of his iniquity. Urquhart shows comparable skill in the way that she connects a seemingly tangential sub-plot involving Austin’s childhood friend George to the defining crisis in Austin’s life. Finally, Urquhart brilliantly links her reflection on the Ashcan school of painting to Austin’s fundamental problem in life.
“The Underpainter” is a magnificent novel about being an English Canadian and a very worthy winner of the 1997 Governor General’s prize.
