📚👨‍⚖️NOVEL THOUGHTS👨‍⚖️📚

2.5⭐⭐

This is a startling account of a young woman who is a slave of Robert Newsom in Missouri in 1855, who, when bought by Newsom, was 14 years old and on their way back to his property, raped her. Being that Newsom had been a widower for many years, he used Celia as his concubine and fathered her two children. When Celia became involved in a relationship with George, another of Newsom's slaves, he told her that she had to quit being bedded by Newsom if she wanted to be with him (not like she had any choice). On a night when Newsom had told Celia she should expect him, she bludgeoned him with a large stick and with only two swings, killed him. A trial would ensue once she was finally pressured to tell the family what had happened.
The interesting slant to this story is while she was actually taken to court for trial, not just hung for the crime, the author explains all the nuances of the slavery matter in Missouri, the pressures by the Abolitionists movement, the possible prejudice by the legal teams on both sides and this also included the judge. Could Celia receive a fair trial? How does the fact that she was Newsom's property and he could do anything he wanted to do to her play in the trial? There could be no denying that the children she had were his and another one on the way which she would have in jail and then after which, her sentence would be carried out because after all, this baby was worth more to the property than to lose it when the mother's sentence was carried out.
While this really was an interesting true story if you could get through the exhausting details, it felt more like a dissertation for a Doctorate than a novel. Interestingly though, her story was actually turned into three plays. There remains though, Celia protected herself from Newsom and hit him in self defense. The defense attorneys tried to make that happen, but in a slave state, as Missouri was, would a jury see past the idea that this was a woman taken advantage of and therefore hit him in self defense or that she had no rights at all because she was at the mercy of her Master.
And since I listened to this one, the narrator had one octave which never varied or changed. It was hard to listen to.
