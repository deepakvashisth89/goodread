Somehow I spent the first month of the year reading almost exclusively short stories. Hunh. The syntax of that sentence looks way off - an older New York Jewess speaking through me; wait, I am an older NY Jewess! - but that sounds like one of Grace Paley's characters must have noodled into my brain and camped out there. Noodled? What noodled? Let's try this again.
Feh. A review is unnecessary. I'd read enough Paley stories to know I was one of the adoring throng long before reading the collection. But oh - how I loved having all of these stories bound together, long gone pockets of New York rushing through the pages ("There is a certain place where dumbwaiters boom, doors slam, dishes crash; every window is a mother's mouth bidding the street to shut up, go skate somewhere else, come home.") How fast she gets it down on the page, how quickly the scene is set, the narrator alive and talking, laughing and complaining in your ear. She's above no one and beneath no one ("When I went to the playground in the afternoon I met eleven unwed mothers on relief. Only four of them were whores, the rest of them were unwed on principle or because some creep had ditched them.") Her feminism is matter of fact, unconcerned with political correctness, just there, in dialogue thrown over her shoulder, a sigh of irritation (the playground ? Go have children), fond, close looks at aging women the outside world no longer sees. Literature, poetry and politics breathe in and out of every day, along with the laundry, neighbourhood walks, hospital visits, an occasional man.

It's interesting to see such a strong divide in the reviews here; Grace Paley's very particular voice obviously doesn't resonate with everyone. But I can't help wondering if any of those readers who chucked this collection because they were put off by the language, or in more cases say they found the stories dull, are Bernie supporters. Reading Paley in January of 2016 reminded me of why I sometimes get misty listening to Bernie Sanders - like Paley, he's mishpocheh, family I knew well and miss keenly, another Vietnam War era NY Democrat. Nu, so a Socialist Democrat, from that period, same difference. It's a last act performance, but he's out there giving it a shot. I wish Grace Paley was here to see him, to see Hillary, to write about the year we're faced with choosing between the first Jewish and the first female presidential candidate. What she'd have to say about that catastrophic person beneath the orange thatched weave, oy, I can't imagine.
