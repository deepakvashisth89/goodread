This is so much a book that I wanted to like more, but I did find a lot of it to be a real slog. Unfinished at the author’s death it was edited by Toni Morrison but, even though it uses the experience of one family to highlight the murder of dozens Black children in Atlanta in the early 80s, the narrative is really quite disjointed. For example the action often moves from one thing to another over the course of a couple of paragraphs so that it takes a while to realise that you’ve moved on to a completely new scene with new/different characters and there are lots of random vignettes that show the huge amount of research that Bambara has done but that can confuse the reader trying to piece together how everything fits. It also needs to be read in big chunks as it absolutely needs its readers’ full attention.It’s not all negative of course. Bambara writes beautifully and the book portrays a huge slice of Black Atlanta life in the early 80s- its politics, its social environment, its law enforcement. And the story of the murders itself is a hugely apt, if depressing, illustration of all of these things melded together, especially poignant in the fact that it is not something that seems to have stuck in the national psyche at all. So that is how I personally would recommend someone read this book - not necessarily as a ‘story’ but as a huge tableau of life in a particular place and time, with no need to try and map the often confusing and convoluted relationships (or not) between its characters. It’s a real shame that there was not a chance for the author to complete it as she would have wanted or planned.
