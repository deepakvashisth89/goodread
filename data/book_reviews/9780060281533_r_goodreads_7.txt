A delightful story set within 1920s Harlem, this early book by Gail Carson Levine might be short on fantasy, but it makes up for it with interesting depictions of 20th century Jewish American and African American culture. Some parts seemed a little implausible. The villain, in particular, was rather cartoonish for an otherwise very realistic book. Also, I'm not sure why Irma Lee was up all night at parties. Especially since her mother seemed so overprotective of her, wouldn't she make sure she was safe in bed before hosting her extravagant shindigs?Besides these minor quibbles, I enjoyed the story immensely. It might not have ended "happily ever after," but it ended hopeful. And for millions of orphans, including Carson Levine's own father, whose real story inspired this book, that's not a bad place to be.
