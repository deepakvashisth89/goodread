My four stars are not an indication of my agreement on everything with the author. Actually, I was given this book several years ago as an anniversary gift, and after previewing the first chapter, it was shelved until recently. It is outdated (as I think the giver warned). Some may find some of these outdated notions offensive. However, if you can interact with that which you don't wholeheartedly agree with, you can still tend to find wisdom. My advice to a reader would be to understand the cultural context in which it was written and read with discernment, but not condemnation. Despite our modern sensitivities, it's clear that Shedd and his wife had something special. For me, reading this was really an interesting experience, because despite some tentativeness or disagreement, some shades of give-to-receive, and what most would see as outdated in regard to marriage roles; there is a love underneath that may put our modern lovers to shame. In a chapter I could take issue with something he says, and still end up nearly tearing up, because it's obvious that that he is truly, deeply considering how to love and honor his wife.
