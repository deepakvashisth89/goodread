There's a great thesis throughout: that left-wing agitation in the Americas results from a colonial and provincial tendency towards Messianic thinking. That individuals can change the world is taken as a given, and mass hysterical narcissism arises—whether in a cult setting (Jonestown) or in political protest (Berkeley during the 1960s).The main and irritating problem with this book is that like a grumpy racist grandpa Shiva Naipaul constantly digresses to talking about race: quite literally stating that black people and white people are almost separate species (like the divergent species of humankind from HG Wells' The Time Traveller).As a result, a lot of his great points are rendered facile. Black people are, in his words, variously violent, stupid, illiterate, ugly, slaves... and so it goes on. The white college students are misguided in trying to solve this ever-present racial divide.In other words, it's often reduced to Clash of Civilisations or The Camp of the Saints style racism. Which is a shame because if the author could have had a little more empathy for his subjects (who are by the way constantly irritated by his rudeness and Oxbridge arrogance) he would have been able to weave his Messiah theory into something greater.Shiva clearly hated black people.
