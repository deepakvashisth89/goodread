Among all the ghost story books I've read, this was fairly unique in that the included tales were more about specific people being haunted than a *place* being haunted, as is common in US mainland tales. Grant states in the Preface that these are retellings/composites of the tales he's collected from locals - it allows for more narrative story-telling by combining threads of different experiences. Most of the stories seem to be republishings of his Halloween contributions to the Hawaii Herald, including 2 that are noir-style about a fictional private investigator. It does seem like the last story is based on his own experience in investigating a ghost story on the UH-Manoa campus.

Most of the included stories are in/around Honolulu or Oahu, so the geographic scope is limited, but it had really good insight about the Japanese-American community of the area. This was a great look about how the Japanese immigrants' culture and beliefs mixed with those of the native Hawaiians and Western immigrants to create something very unique to Hawaii.
