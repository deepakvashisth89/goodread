Made it to Page 700. Cannot Read One More Page of Telling (Compared to Showing)Reading this felt like sawing at an overcooked steak with a plastic fork and butter knife. After months of slogging, I finally put my finger on why: Eliot keeps stopping the story to explain what her characters mean, what they feel, what they might have felt if something else had been said or done. It’s commentary on commentary, a running lecture about how we ought to interpret every glance and word.What I craved was story. Dialogue that stands on its own. Scenes that let the reader draw meaning without a narrator leaning in to spell it out. Instead, at page seven hundred, I feel like not much has actually happened. The weight of the telling smothers the drive.I understand this book is admired and often called a masterpiece. I don’t dispute its ambition. But for me, pushing through nearly a thousand pages of that style has been about as enjoyable as a long day at the dentist or filling out tax forms.
