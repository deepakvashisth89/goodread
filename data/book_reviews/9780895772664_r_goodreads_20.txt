Yes, well…What we have in O. Henry is the master of a certain kind of story: small-scale, economical, with no loose-ends, and which hides its efficiency under a conversational tone. That’s a deeply enjoyable thing in small doses – there’s a charm to his voice, and the satisfaction of familiar pattern. That’s real craftsmanship, and it deserves more stars than I’m going to give it. That’s my fault, not his.In large doses – because what we have here are twenty-nine of the things, all bound together – I have problem. In large doses, personally, I go from appreciation of a living thing, to appreciation of a well-built automaton, to the observation of a skeleton clock. The cogs all fit together beautifully, the plots are exquisitely engineered, but you know exactly where they’re going and how they’ll operate. You’re looking at the same model each time: situation – development – reversal. Efficiency becomes the enemy of surprise.It’s my fault for reading too many stories in too close proximity. My fault.So what I’m going to do is recommend O. Henry, without question, but dipping in and out, not as a lump. And there we are.
