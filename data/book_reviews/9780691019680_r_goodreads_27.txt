Popper's book is a radical re-reading of Plato whom he argues provides, in his authoritarian vision of the perfect state, a template for twentieth-century totalitarianism oppression (Nazism, Stalinism). Rather than a champion of rational social organisation, Plato is therefore in fact the enemy of true political freedom. Perhaps the most unsettling claim - for philosophers anyway - is that Plato's use of the character of Socrates in his dialogues is a complete distortion of what the real Socrates stood for - the opposite, in fact. The book is well-argued, clear, and - apart from some sections which will be challenging to non-philosophers - a pleasure to read. I'm still not sure about all of what Popper says, mind you, but it's definitely a must read for anyone interested in Plato or political theory.Gareth Southwell is aphilosopher, writer and illustrator.
