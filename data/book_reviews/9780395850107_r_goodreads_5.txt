A wonderful history of women who lived in partnership with other women and whom we would today call lesbian, though that identity (and identity politics in general) post-dated their lives. Faderman wrote this around the year 2000 and so her summary of "current achievements" feels a bit dated at the end. I loved getting to learn the stories of all these important, accomplished women and all that they achieved for future generations. I was surprised at the numbers of women I'd never heard of -- scads of them -- and how they prospered in the face of society's resistance and, after the 1930's, active suppression of their ambitions & life choices. I especially enjoyed reading the primary source quotes from letters, biographies, and articles by and about these special women.Other reviewers pointed out that the coverage of the lives/loves of woman of color is pretty lacking. There are several portraits, however, and these are deeply interesting. Twenty years after its publication, it's not a surprising complaint when we are clamoring for more equity in representation. However, Faderman was way ahead of her time in researching and including these stories of lesbian women of color -- and it is all the more compelling to learn about their radical lives as teachers, ministers, lawyers and political activists.I had a difficult time reading this through Hoopla as an e-book. The platform repeatedly lost my place and it was annoying reading it on my phone. I put this on my Wishlist to own as a paper book, so that I can more easily refer to this classic. Also, the tiny photos in the e-format were frustrating. I definitely want this history on my IRL library shelves.
