Straight on from The World Is Not Enough to another novel with associated turn of the century indie earworm. When Robert Irwin died earlier this year, it reminded me I should read more of his stuff, and yes, the Haines association helped push this to the front of the queue, but it's my kind of thing anyway, a novel presented as the diary of a hapless sociology student in sixties London, who joins an occult society in a spirit of enquiry and inevitably finds himself in too deep. The blurbs on this edition* make much of its comic side, and at times it is a hoot (especially the bit where the narrator has taken what he suspects is some dud acid and is just keeping himself occupied while he waits for the trip to start). More than that, there's the gentle bathos any British fictional diary can easily borrow from Mr Pooter and Adrian Mole. But, if it's not on a par with Irwin's gruesome Arabian Nightmare, there are still some truly horrific stretches too. I don't just mean the dark visions, cult brainwashing, or stoner attempts at trepanation, but the digging into the very idea of keeping a diary, the way that the more comprehensive it is, the more it consumes the life it's intended to record – and that's before the automatic writing kicks in, or the complications of one's superiors in the Black Book Lodge being entitled to read one's diary, including one's record of the previous times they read one's diary... The combined result is one of those awkward books where I feel I should have liked it more for how good I think it is, if that makes sense, though it doesn't help that the one real misstep comes right at the end, a death of the sixties dream moment that was already old hat on first publication, a decade after Withnail, and which feels doubly so when I've not long since read Robyn Hitchcock's memoir, with its much stranger and more nuanced reckoning with the nearly as much time again now elapsed since the heady year of 1967.*An edition which is part of Dedalus' classics imprint, but still looks worse than any of its predecessors, suggesting more than anything that the Prince of Darkness has a passion for graphic design. Still, at least the photo establishes Irwin's own 1967 credentials.
