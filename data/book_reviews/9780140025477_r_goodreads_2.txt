While I'm a huge fan of the Blandings books and Jeeves, Wodehouse's standalones can be a bit of a struggle for me, and that's the case here. In some ways this replays typical PGW motifs: the irascible baronet, his modern daughter buzzing around in her motor car, the secretary (who is both female and
appears to know Jujitsu - and demonstrates it with immaculate timing!), broken lovers, failed courtships. There are love triangles and law suits - but there are also odd missteps: it's weird that the big house also takes in paying guests who clutter up the narrative; the long-lost brother who is also a kind of bailiff chasing another guest for breach of contract; extra-farcical elements that leave swimmers running around naked.

There are also some vicious characters which is unusual in sunny PGW land, with even the hero, Joe, being both tougher and sexier than is usually the case.

Maybe because we're not with familiar characters, the writing didn't make me snigger and laugh out loud as is often the case with the farcical Blandings books - the absurdities here somehow didn't tickle me.

Still, a mid-level PGW is still a sunny delight but this plods in places whereas PGW usually glides and dances lightfootedly and with immaculate comic timing.

I swapped between the book and audio: the latter read by Jonathan Cecil wasn't to my taste - Cecil makes all the young men sound like 70 year old colonial colonels, and the women like Penelope Keith!
