This is the final book in Moon's Serrano Legacy series. The first three books featured Haris Serrano, the next two shifted more to Esmay Suiza, and the final two throw everybody into the mix. This one is actually the second half of a very long conclusion, and in fact overlaps at the beginning with the prior book, Change of Command. In fact, I doubt that this one would have much impact if that one was not read first. It's a very complex space-opera, with lots of political conspiracies and familial machinations and military procedure. I think it needs to be read with large blocks of time, not in faits and starts. Moon does an excellent job of drawing sympathetic characters and leaving you unsure as to how good are the good guys and how rotten the bad guys really are. The ending doesn't wrap up all of the loose ends entirely, but it's a satisfactory and feel-good kind of farewell to characters that you've spent a long time with. As before, I'm not sure who the subject of the Gary Ruddell cover painting is. It's a good, captivating read.
