Got 75% of the way through. While I love the uplifting nature of this book, doesn’t it feel incomplete? It reminds me of Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, which in my view has very limited efficacy.

This book and therapy modality both propose that by gaining awareness of our own cognitive distortions we can simply remove them. However, that belies a deeper essential truth: that for the vast majority of us with cognitive distortions or limiting beliefs as it were, those beliefs serve a very specific and valuable protective purpose for us, usually one hidden away from our own awareness by deep shame, and only brought up gently, through acceptance and compassion, leading to a deeper understanding of our own true narrative of WHY we haven’t operated this “one true distortion-free way” all along, and oftentimes enlightening us to what we truly DESIRE in a much deeper way than simply wealth and romance. Perhaps not the house on top of the hill, but the ability to truly accept ourselves as we are and see ourselves as worthy of joy! Or both! All of which to say, this book and CBT ignore the deeper self and thus following their advice can lead to a papering-over and backsliding in the longer term.
