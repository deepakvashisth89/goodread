The strangest thing aboutRobert Gover'sOne Hundred Dollar Misunderstandingis that it somehow sneaked by the censors back in 1961. It is a book with considerable charm and remarkably little prurient sex considering the sexual situations of a college student with a 14-year-old Black prostitute.Misunderstanding is the key word. Both James C Holland, an Eastern college student, and Kitty, the prostitute are strangely clueless. The misunderstanding in question relates to payment for sex from a prostitute. The college student somehow assumes that a single $10 covers a whole weekend of whoopee, which it never did. Kitty speaks in a thick dialect which (I surmise) protects her from the censors.While I liked the general situation, I felt the book goes on too long multiplying unlikely situations.
