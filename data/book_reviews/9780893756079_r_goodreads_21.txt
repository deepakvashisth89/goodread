I was recently given this book as a gift. I thought something was off when I looked at the cover. I googled Captain Courageous and found a picture of the first edition of Rudyard Kipling's CaptainS Courageous. Some people shouldn't be allowed to be publishers. I would think getting the title correct would be the first thing a publisher would check. I hope none of the text was altered or left out.

In spite of getting off on the wrong foot with the title, I enjoyed the story. A rich, spoiled 15-year old boy, Harvey, falls overboard from the luxury liner he and his mother are taking from America to Europe. He is recovered from the flotsam by a crew member of a cod fishing schooner named the "We're Here". The captain doesn't believe Harvey's story of his father's wealth and wouldn't take him back to Boston. Harvey had to stay on board until the We're Here's hull was full of fish and to earn his keep in the meantime. The crew took a liking to Harvey and he to them. The rigors of the journey and his pride in a job well done turned Harvey's bad attitude around. He was transformed into a nice kid.

Kipling wrote this as a children's book. As so, and being only 103 pages long, I thought I would breeze through it in a day or maybe two if I got busy with other things. Not so. It took me better than a week. I was slowed by the sailor's jargon. For instance, "Jimmy Christmas! She'll etch loose in a flat ca'am." It would take me a moment to figure out ca'am meant calm. From the context I knew what she was etched loose from. I think.

Since Kipling was an Englishman I admire his ability learn the New England sailor's dialogue. I guess he just had a good ear for language. It couldn't have been easy.
