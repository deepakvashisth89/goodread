My favorite teacher read the Narnia books aloud to us in fourth grade, and I enjoyed them enough to read several on my own. I don't recall this particular book, though.

Reading these stories nearly fifty years later, I wasn't sure what to expect—but they're still wonderful! Since the Kindle editions have long wait times at the library, I decided to listen to the audiobook instead.

This installment takes place in Narnia, Calormen, and the lands between during the Golden Age, when Peter reigns as High King and his brother and two sisters serve as King and Queens.

Shasta is a young boy living with a fisherman he believes to be his father. When a stranger arrives, Shasta overhears the fisherman reveal that he found Shasta as a baby and wants to sell him. Though Shasta's life is difficult, slavery isn't an option he'll accept. He wonders aloud about this stranger's intentions—until the stranger's horse responds. The horse is a talking creature from Narnia!

Shasta manages to mount the large horse, and together they begin their journey to Narnia. Along the way, they meet Hwin, another talking horse, and a girl named Aravis, who's also escaping to Narnia to avoid a forced marriage.

As they travel, they discover that Narnia faces an imminent attack and must race to warn the kingdom.

I loved reconnecting with the children from the wardrobe and meeting Aslan again! We even get to see Tumnus make an appearance.

While I adore these stories, they contain elements you wouldn't expect in a children's book today—but remember, this was written in 1955. Shasta faces enslavement, and the narrative includes references to beheadings and torture. It's worth considering if your child is reading independently.
