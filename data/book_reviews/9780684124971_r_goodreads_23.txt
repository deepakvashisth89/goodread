Journey to Topaz is a novel about the injustice committed against Japanese-Americans during WW2 after the attack on Pearl Harbor. 120,000 people of Japanese ancestry were sent to concentration camps. The author, Yoshiko Uchida, experienced living in a camp firsthand and has for years written books calling this an injustice and a violation of the American Constitution.Yoshiko Uchida and many others view this as an unnecessary act by the United States government. After the attack on Pearl Harbor, anyone with Japanese ancestry was targeted and sent to concentration camps. The novel shows that fear and panic (caused by war) can make people do harsh things they will regret later.In the novel, Yuki is hopeful and tries to make the best of her situation, no matter how much she doesn’t like it. We see her trying to meet new friends such as the Kuriharas, specifically Emi. She isn’t optimistic like her mother, but tries to see a better outcome.Kenichi, however, grows more distant and angrier at their situation. He tries, somewhat, to replace his father as the leader of the family, but gives up and spends more time with his friends. He begins to make decisions with them instead of confronting, which he does only after he makes up his mind. He generally also follows whatever decision his friends make. However he still cares about his family.Mrs. Sakane is dignified and optimistic. She seeks the best outcome and tries to comfort her children. Even when Mr. Sakane was taken, she gave the FBI the same treats she would give to guests. She did not try to rage against the government but let her faith carry her and wished for the best. We learn that she knew her husband very little, put her faith in him, and moved to America, never looking back since.Mr. Sakane did not appear much in the novel, so I don’t have much to say about him; however, he genuinely cares about his family and endured a lot before he was able to meet them again. He fully supports his children’s decisions and tries hard to be a good father.There are other characters I could talk about like the Kuriharas and Mr. Toda, but I decided to analyse only the Sakane family instead.
