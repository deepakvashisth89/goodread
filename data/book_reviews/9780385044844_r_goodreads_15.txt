MacLean seems to venture out of his depth in Puppet on a Chain, which results in a presentation of a drug culture that fits more with paranoid anxiety like Reefer Madness than with real drug economy. This story is full of over-top crazies, and the main investigator with his two super beautiful assistants reads like a silly male fantasy. This is redeemed somewhat by the investigator's incompetence -- he makes mistake after mistake, stumbling around Amsterdam -- eventually resulting in the most lurid nightmarish death sequence I've found in MacLean's work so far. The writing is also rather sloppy here, and MacLean's reliance on his go-to sentence structure of "it was a very _____ indeed" is so frequent that it really calls attention to itself.That withstanding, I still found it highly readable, and its very unreality leads to some very striking imagery indeed.
