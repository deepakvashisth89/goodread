This book was first published 40 years ago, in 1986. As one of Krentz's earlier novels, it is a bit dated, especially in the technology area, but I enjoyed it all the same, even though I don't think I'll ever reread it.The problem is not the lack of cell phones. The problem of this book is that the author hasn't hit her stride yet. Her best novels would have come the following decades, in the 1990s and 2000s. What bothered me in this story was the lack of focus.The author vacillated between 2 themes: trusting in love and romance or establishing your professional identity at the cost of relationships. Seeking love or seeking professional fulfillment. Some lucky people can have both, but the heroine and the hero (to a lesser degree) struggle to find balance between the two. And the author struggles, too, to find the balance between the two themes - something she had become an adept at later on in her writing career.The protagonists, Hannah and Gideon, are also not the best she could write. While the hero, Gideon, is a complex man, a business shark who is learning humanity through his unfolding love for Hannah, the heroine is going in the opposite direction. She resists her love for Gideon and contemplates instead becoming harder, more self-reliant and less compassionate. She wants to become a female version of Gideon. Or she thinks she does, and it takes her awhile to meet him halfway and accept their burgeoning love.Another detail that made me disapprove of Hannah: she wants Gideon to change, to become less jaded, more receptive to love and kindness - and he is changing for her - while she herself is striving for an opposite transformation. Somewhat hypocritical of her, and I really dislike hypocrites.Nevertheless, it was still a good, solid romance, immensely readable.
