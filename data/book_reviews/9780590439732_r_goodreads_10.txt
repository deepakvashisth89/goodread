2.5 rounded up to 3 stars for GR.

Odd to read a winter book in the middle of summer but it was the next one in my BSC pile.

This was the third Super Special book and there isn't really anything too special about it except that it is written by Ann M. Martin. It isn't really like a vacation IMHO even if the events are supposed to be a week.

I guess I have just now gotten used to winter break now from a parent point of view than a teenager.

I know that when I was in middle school, we didn't have a place inviting us to come and stay. Field trips were a dime a dozen, but SMS gets to be invited to the Leicester Lodge in Vermont along with some other schools. All the grades and I guess the parents are just cool with it to get their kids out of their hair for a while.

Unless you are cool like Logan's family and you get to go to ARUBA on your winter break!

Most of the book is Mary Anne working on a project of creating a book about the winter vacation where she asks the other BSC members to pitch in so she can give it to Logan. This is Jessi and Mal's first time going on the trip, but all of the other girls went in seventh grade the year before.

Even Dawn who is not a fan of winter weather, but we'll get to that...

There is going to be a sort of color war between the red team and blue team from SMS with many events and a dance held at the end for all of the different kids from the middle schools to mingle.

An elementary school is also coming to stay but the bus with the kids gets into an accident with the driver hurt, one teacher chaperone with a broken arm and another having walked two miles in the snow to get help. The owners of the lodge, The Georges, go with the BSC to take care of the kids after getting permission from their own chaperones.

The BSC members decide to look after the kids until their own chaperones are cleared from the hospital to return. Dawn bunks with Mary Anne, Mal with Jessi, Stacey with Claudia and Kristy on her own alongside the kids. Before Mal and Jessi would have been with their own grade so they enjoy this better.

A few things wind around other plots:

A girl named Priscilla nicknamed Pinky from the elementary school ends up with a hurt leg and a bad attitude that no one wants to bunk with her. Jessi tries to be nice to her but finds that Pinky doesn't seem to like her much and for a while she believes it is because she may be racist.

She isn't but Jessi's family haven't been treated well by most of the people in Stoneybrook and Pinky is actually homesick and trying to be tough so no one will think she is a baby. Jessi even has Pinky help her with her duties of putting together a talent show at the lodge.

Kids and even the teachers join in singing 1950s songs.

Kristy is all about taking care of the kids and making sure her team wins the challenges.

Claudia is to judge a snow sculpture contest alongside a teacher and her choice for the winner doesn't sit well with Kristy since it puts the blue team ahead. The two of them are also going against each other in a skiing part of the competition and Claudia falls for her instructor.

He is at least in his mid-twenties, and his name is Guy (pronounced GEE) and he is from France.

Claudia is his star pupil and tries to keep Claudia focused on not being psyched up by Kristy, but she daydreams about a romance between the two. We already know that the crush will not be returned but mostly because Guy ends up being married with a wife and two children and not because he is a jerk leading Claudia on.

Stacey also happens to find herself falling IN LUV with her own French crush. He is a boy named Pierre in middle school from Vermont, born in America but with French parents, when they crash into each other getting to the chairlift.

Mallory decides to be all Harriet the Spy and imagines crazy things about the guests and staff at the lodge. All of her crazy things turn out to not be true, and Mal gets flustered about the dance since she has never been to one. She hopes that a snowstorm will hit the lodge and cancel the dance by sending everyone home but no such luck.

Dawn does not have a good time trying to ice skate or do any sort of snow activities due to her enormous mittens not good for grabbing things or making snowballs. She doesn't help her team out (she is on Kristy's team BTW) and decides not to take part in the skiing.

Dawn tries to talk to Mary Anne about how embarrassed she feels but Mary Anne is more concerned with wondering whether Logan might be having some sort of fling with another girl in Aruba. One who isn't as shy or clumsy (which is why Mary Anne decided to do a report on the lodge and make a book for Logan instead.)

She and Dawn end up fighting with each other, but the other BSC members tell them to try and act mature around the elementary school kids. Dawn decides to forgive Mary Anne when she finds Pinky crying in the bathroom out of homesickness (something Dawn knows too well). Mary Anne also agrees when she finds out that the seventh-grade gym teacher is missing her fiancé back in Stoneybrook.

Baby-Sitters' Weekend Vacation isn't completely boring but didn't really have a lot of conflict either to make a lasting impression.
