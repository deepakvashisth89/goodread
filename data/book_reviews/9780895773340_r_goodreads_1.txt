Oh wow... did I not see this coming! First published in 1924 and set in India in the 1920s the time when the British Raj was under observation, critique and ultimately the threat of the Indian Independence Movement, this drama centred around a woman seeking a more fulfilling life in India as she seeks a relationship with a high ranking Englishman juxtaposed with the story of a Muslim doctor who dared to have a real friendship with a (liberal) Englishman pulls no punches at looking at the failings of not just the British Empire, but Britons themselves as they seek to maintain a hold over 'British-India'.

Even read through a 21st century lens this book stands tall, leaving no stone unturned at the despicable, but normalised attitudes, behaviours and actions of most of the Britons in India. It also casts unflattering eyes at the treatment of women, and how they're perceived and ultimately abused in both communities; notwithstanding all that good work, Forster manages to give insight in to the complexity of the many religions, castes, creeds etc. in India itself.

The story itself is a compelling and interesting read, and most of the characters, with the omniscient narrator playing a strong part, are fully fleshed out and multi-faceted. Forster moves to another level as a writer for me, as even though he is immensely critical of the Empire, he does not make the main Indian cast angels at all, as each Indian protagonist has several layers, both good and bad as well.

I never understood the defence of people doing / promoting / normalising really unpleasant behaviour because of the age they lived / were published in, and the likes of E.M. Forster shows that right is right, and wrong is wrong, regardless of the supposed status quo. A truly surprisingly good read. I am a Forster fan for life, off of the back of my first venture into his work. 8.5 out of 12 formidable Four Star read!
