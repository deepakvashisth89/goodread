I loved this! I think it may be my new favourite Ngaio Marsh.I must have read this before, but I had no memory of it. I picked out a suspect early on and thought 'I must have read this before, because X is obviously guilty.' And the story continued and more and more clues pointing to X were found--and then the rug was pulled completely out from under me. I did not see the solution--and it fell together perfectly.Death and the Dancing Footman is also interesting because it is a book written during the war, and it manages not to be hysteric or propaganda, something that does show up in other writers of this era, and a little bit in Ngaio Marsh's other books--the ones where Alleyn is in NZ doing spy stuff. This is a solid mystery set at a time on the home front where things were grim, but still business as usual.
