This book is light, bright, and breezy. There are several wonderful baseball stories, many of them very humorous. The book is written more as a memoir of sorts about the experiences of this famousßaav major league baseball catcher. There are several stories about the lives and habits of famous baseball major leaguers. There are long discussions about how a pitcher and catcher together can work to strike out a batter. Some of the topics covered in the book are: enduring long traveling dates, a few notable pitchers in the major leagues, handling pressure during close games, the usefulness of pep talks to motivate baseball players, the reality of baseball and finding stability after a baseball career ends, and becoming an announcer for baseball games. There is a lengthy chapter on the activities of the Mets in 1986. Unfortunately having such a large chapter devoted to one team in one year dates the book unnecessarily. There is a glossary of baseball pitches that may interest readers who are also baseball fans.
