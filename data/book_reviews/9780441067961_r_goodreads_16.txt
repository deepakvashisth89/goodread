I don’t write reviews.What’s the show about?That’s the beauty of it; it’s about nothing.Nothing?That’s right. Nothing.Okay, this book was not actually about nothing, but it meanders quite a bit. It’s a really good idea for a story; a slightly mad (and a bit misanthropic) scientist uses his own lymphocytes to create intelligent life. When his job finds out that he’s doing unauthorized, and very dangerous, experiments, he’s forced to discontinue his work. The problem is, he sees his creations as his children and, reluctant to kill them, he injects them into himself for safekeeping. What could possibly go wrong? As it turns out, many things.Now the problem is, I expected all of this to lead somewhere. Things happen, but they’re vague. Characters are introduced without any follow through. There’s not any sort of an ending. Though I don’t regret the time I’ve spent in Bear’s imagination, I would have appreciated a more decisive “The End.”
