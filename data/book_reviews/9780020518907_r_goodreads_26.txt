another hemingway book to add to my don't-like-list. i am in a toxic parasocial relationship with him and i do not think it is ending here. the one reason i DID like this book is because i think it gave me a better understanding of why i don't like hemingway's writing in general. i watched a video essay recently about afro-surrealism in the tv show "atlanta" -- the idea that black americans live inherently surreal lives as a result of systemic discrimination. atlanta does a great job transforming that idea into beautiful media, in a way that makes it seem relatable and makes me think about how other marginalized identities also experience the world in a very surreal way. in media like atlanta, where the most mundane events and experiences become mind-bending and thought provoking, i have no problem with slice-of-life style stories. with hemingway, he takes boring plots and writes them as they are experienced by straight, cis, middle class white men who i simply cannot force myself to care about. there is no intrigue to their stories. if more exciting things happened, or if the language was more poetic and flowery, i might be able to get into it. but alas, that is not the case. i think the short story format makes things even worse since i can't even trick myself into thinking i'm invested in the characters because i've been stuck with them so long. not my first hemingway, nor my last. not giving up yet
