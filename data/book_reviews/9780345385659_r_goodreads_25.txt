I have owned this volume for ages, as I dreamt of my own journey to England. I finally got to read it as I now finally get to plan that journey. I refused to do so any sooner, because I knew it would be too bittersweet.

I thoroughly enjoyed almost every one of these memoirs. Just one left me cold, illustrating the reality that single working motherhood stinks, without the author seeming to notice the systemic influences impacting her situation. But then, it is the mission of an English professor and memoirist to look backward, not forward into new ways of thinking which we have developed since the 1992 publication date. And her authenticity and vulnerability in all her writing is one of the great strengths of this work.

Thanks to the author, I have several additional Travel pins on my Google map, many simply annotated, "Susan," for her mere mention, or slightly more meaningfully, "Susan recommends this." I deeply appreciated how we share so many common interests -- history, gardens, literature -- so that, though the book is decades old, her recommendations are still relevant to me. And it's not like England ages as quickly as we do here. Or so I'm told.
