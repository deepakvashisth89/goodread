Demetrio Macías had a reputation. Two soldiers demanded food and drink from his wife. They were drunk and just shot her barking dog. When Demetrio walked in, they skulked away. Why didn’t you kill them, demanded his wife? I was sure it wasn’t their turn yet, he replied. He knew.Demetrio sent his wife and child to stay with his father. Since he disliked the government forces, los Federales, he joined the Revolutionaries, led by Pancho Villa in northern Mexico. It was said that Pancho Villa was Mexico’s Napoleon. Villa had appeal but the rag tag army was under equipped and out gunned. However what they lacked, they had the drive to fight.Also joining the band was Luis Cervantes, a medical student and journalist. He was a bit of a dreamer, fed up with the Federales and now favoured the revolutionaries. Along the way, Luis meets Camila and Demetrio is bewitched by La Pintada (the painted woman). It was common for women to join the brigades and it gave these men something else.At first things weren’t going well but finally they won a battle. The moral boost was good but it did go to the men’s heads. Getting drunk, boasting, and fighting over women was part of the scene. It reveals their inner selves, and let’s just say they aren’t angels (nor are the women).After two years fighting, Demetrio has climbed in ranks and decides that he wants to see his wife and child. The men aren’t sure that is a good idea but Demetrio knows best. Of course, that was also his greatest flaw.The title translates to “The Underdogs” and as one expects, it shows the common person fighting in a war against the rich elites to make political and societal change in Mexico. This book was published in 1916 during the Mexican Revolution (1910-1920). Hailed as a classic of Mexican literature it shows how war changes people and how fate is a quickly changing thing. It is a short book (136 pages) but packs a punch with the ending.This is my second time reading this book. First in English and second en Español.
