A relatively short book, Vermeer's World is about a few selected works by mystery artist Johannes Vermeer. Like other introductory books on Vermeer, the biographical portion is relatively short. However, while it doesn't go in depth on the artistic technique on a few of his paintings which are: A View of Delft, Allegory of Faith, The Procuress, The Art of Painting, and A Woman Holding a Balance. These are just some though, there a few more works the author delves into analysis, but definitely, A View of Delft takes most of the pages on analyzing the process and symbolism behind this iconic piece.

What I did enjoy more of were the portions where Vermeer is also compared to some of his contemporaries, but it is not biased. Instead, the author suggests how much the artists take influences from each other, rather not copy exactly, but makes some notable changes that distinguishes their works from others. Additionally, a last chapter is devoted to the Golden Age of the Netherlands and how that period of success for the country helped launched Vermeer's artistic career. It's just unfortunate that when that period ended, Vermeer's success dried up and he fell into obscurity for a long period until the two centuries later. Vermeer's World is a nice little annotated book on Vermeer with high quality scans of his prominent paintings that is good for an introductory to both his works and the rich period in Dutch history.
