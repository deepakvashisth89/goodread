More gruesome and cruel than 'Night', which is still my favourite Edna O'Brien book. Reading this (published 1990) reminded me of reading Angela Carter's 'The Bloody Chamber and other stories' (published 1979). Haunted by a kind of clinging, cloying, angry, gory sadness/rage to do with being a woman in the world. I don't know how I feel about this and am not sure how relevant and helpful it always is for women now.But I shouldn't deny that it provokes something in me. And she's certainly a hell of a writer. And funny.Also, it gets history points. Reading fiction is the closest I normally get to reading history so this was a good glimpse into the past.
