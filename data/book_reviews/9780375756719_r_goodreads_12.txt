(Note: Rating and review only for the novella The Country of the Pointed Firs)

"There may be restrictions to such a summer's happiness, but the ease that belongs to simplicity is charming enough to make up for whatever a simple life may lack, and the gifts of peace are not for those who live in the thick of battle."

An utterly charming time capsule of summer in a coastal Maine fishing village at the very end of the 19th century, Sarah Orne Jewett's novella captures the resilience and nobility of the small community of Dunnet's Landing and the people who live there. The book evokes the bucolic setting and beauty of sea and land so completely that I was transported from the dreary grey winter of my current surroundings to a peaceful summer world full of sunshine and light, warm sea breezes. I look forward to reading more of Jewett's work in the future, and I'm glad I finally picked this up.
