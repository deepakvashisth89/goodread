If you are an Indian, this book is one of the most difficult things you can read. The difficulty arises from the undeniable truth in what Mr Naipaul writes. You resist that truth but are forced to acknowledge it as well. It is uncomfortable: that someone (an outsider?) can see that overwhelming reality of circadian Indian existence—and what lies beneath it—and articulate it so well is not easy to accept perhaps. But one is moved to accept it. Widely criticized for its negativity, this book actually shines in darkness. There is anger, there is resentment, stupefaction, disbelief, disgust, yet there is awe. It is not a hands-off review of a weird and hopeless nation but a concerned critique of a staggering civilization. Reading this book is a starting point of understanding India. It is highly recommended to those who are interested in India, and absolutely essential for those wholoveIndia.
