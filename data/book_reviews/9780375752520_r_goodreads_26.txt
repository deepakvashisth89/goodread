I was absorbed by this novel. It renders smoggy London as a drab, sepulchral pit of spies, terrorists, political fanatics, and lazy, ineffectual anarchists. Many characters are sleazy people living in drudgery and/or fear. Even the protagonist is a hefty man, a bad secret agent who runs a bric-a-brac shop on the side, selling illicit pornographic materials and contraceptives. Conrad seethes against the "perverse unreason" of violent revolutionary types who seem to have no meaning in their personal lives, resorting to fatuous brutality against their flimsy perception of "society."

My favourite character was the Professor, for sure. He is the only truly dangerous person in the anarchist group (he always has a suicide bomb strapped to his body). The novel is mysterious, claustrophobic, and intensely atmospheric. I’m not surprised many didn’t like it; it’s definitely a more conservative novel in terms of political sensibility, and the style can be darkly bizarre. But I really liked it for all these reasons. A great study of fanaticism and modern decay.

P.S. This novel would pair well with The Third Generation by Fassbinder. Similar themes & outlook, different eras & mediums.
