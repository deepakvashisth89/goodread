Personal ResponseI likedThe Shining Onesbecause there are more magical creatures than in the first book, and there is more action. I also like how it transitions into this book from the first one-it is a very smooth transition. I also like the mystery of trying to find out who is the evil force in the book, and I was very surprised when I found out who it was.Plot SummaryThis book begins with Sparhawk venturing to retrieve the Bhelliom, a magical force capable of destroying the world as well as taking down their enemy. Of course Ehlana did not know the true reason of Sparhawk's trip, and Aphrael had to split her form so Danae would stay with Ehlana so Flute could go with Sparhawk. On their trip back to Tamul after retrieving the Bhelliom, they encounter the magical beings of nightmares called the Shining Ones. The Shining Ones have the ability to melt flesh with a single touch, and they can also glow brightly when they wish. Sephrenia, a Styric, has a large dislike for the Shining Ones, and refuses to work with them at first. But after Emperor Sarabian and Queen Ehlana overthrow Sarabian's corrupt government, Sephrenia finds out that Zalasta has been feeding her lies about the Shining Ones, and wishes to kill her sister, the Goddess Aphrael.RecommendationI recommend this book to higher level readers because the language from the first book continues on into this book, and if you don't understand it, you will miss almost all of the plot. This book has more of the magical properties than the first book, and there is more action. Readers that like magic and knights will like this book as well.
