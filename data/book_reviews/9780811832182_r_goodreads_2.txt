As a librarian, I am always interested in the circuitous path that gets readers from one book to another. Why did I read Black & White? Because I had discovered Stephen Guarnaccia’s visual style through his two architecturally based fairy tale pastiches—The Three Little Pigs, an architectural tele and Goldilocks and the 3 Bears, a tale moderne. [And I found out about those books in Architectural Digest]. Black & White is a visual compendium of all things bxw in our lives. A small sample includes: stripes, spots, fashion, playing cards. In some cases the visuals stand on their own; in others, the author provides some history, as he does with dice and the 8-ball. Back & White is a great ‘browsing book” for waiting rooms, but it also makes you think about how we see.Steven Guarnaccia is an illustrator, designer, and author. His illustrations appear regularly in the New York Times, Esquire, and Abitare, as well as on greeting cards for the Museum of Modern Art in New York and on clothing for Swatch. He lives in New Jersey with his co-author, Susan Hochbaum, and their son.
