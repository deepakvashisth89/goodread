I first encountered this book in my Buddhism course in university. Our professor had us read about 40 pages of it; we read the chapters having to do with bearing witness on the streets. When I first read these pages, I had to read it very slowly, because I felt I was being transformed from the inside out. I knew I was encountering something beyond myself.So I ordered the book on Thriftbooks.What I discovered in those 40 pages that day was none other than a direct invitation to suffer for the good of others.For the past two years, I've had an active meditation practice, but it has been almost entirely comprised of silent sitting, the "wisdom" practices of Buddhism. The other wing of meditation, compassion, usually shows up as Metta practice: a deep, prayerful wishing for another to be free from suffering. From this, a certain healing can arise.But Bearing Witness has shown me another kind of Metta that I had never considered: one where you voluntarily put yourself inside of the suffering in order to help heal the world.He writes of bearing witness in the streets. In Auschwitz. In the stories of Claude Thomas and Fleet Maull. In revitalizing School 6.Reading these stories did something to me that I am having a very difficult time putting to words. It moved me to dedicate myself to stepping into suffering, to bear witness to the pain of this world. To live in service to others. And I feel deeply, deeply inspired to commit myself to that. In a way I never have.Thank you so much, Bernie. What a gift.(and thank you Dr. Wilson for assigning this reading. :)
