They say: wherever you go, there you are. The same, it seems, would apply across time. Published in 1984 in response to the growing corporate culture which led to movies such as American Psycho and books such as this one, the Yuppie Handbook shows both contempt for the systems that keep Americans following new trends in a never-ending monetary search for status as well as an awkward disdain for changing norms. Therapy, divorce, and using your money to enjoy the good things in life is portrayed as silly and emotionally empty. This portrayal is not entirely wrong when these benefits are a by-product of a corporate system that convinces its employees to worship money and status over emotional fulfillment and peace. But its desire to point and laugh at the people finding success in the ways it was sold to them seems equally silly.The root of the issue was, and still is, a system that encourages productivity and profit, leading to things like gentrification and the personal prioritization of physical beauty and physical objects. These are poor replacements for emotional connection and human relationships, but it’s what corporate America asks us to trade in for the other. In 2025, not much has changed: those who cannot find their unique purpose will find themselves, with the right amount of money, surrounded by the same objects that others tell them are trendy, undergoing the same procedures that others do to maintain their looks (an important task if you have no higher purpose to focus on), and taking the same vacations that others take. How bleak.
