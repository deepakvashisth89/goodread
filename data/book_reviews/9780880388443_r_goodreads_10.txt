One of my favorite 2nd Ed. AD&D books, this book influenced how I played clerics. Prior to this book, I looked at clerics and their patron deities in more general terms, i.e. worships a god of storms and here are the appropriate spell spheres he gives access to. When this book came out with its listing of historical deities from several cultures, the organization of what areas of concern they had, their alignments, and the rules for Specialty Priests, I was intrigued. I know that this book was not the first of the AD&D books to do this, but for whatever reason, it did not click for me until reading this book (maybe it was the presentation format or maybe it was because it was 2nd Ed.). My gaming group got excited about deities in our campaign world- which ones should we include as having a presence, which ones would we like to play clerics of? We were extremely excited about the Specialty Priests. Perhaps this was due to our mainly sticking with the generic cleric options found in the Player’s Handbook. Specialty Priests, with their additional class abilities, restrictions, etc. made your cleric of Ares seem unique and different than your friend’s cleric of Tyr, even though they were similar in both having a focus on war and battle.
