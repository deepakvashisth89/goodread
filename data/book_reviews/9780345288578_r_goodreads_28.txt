I am a great fan of The Big Sleep but I found this disappointing. I fully accept that writers of 50s noir often wrote testosterone-heavy dialogue but the dialogue in Playback was frequently juvenile and stupid, unless it was creepy, sounding more like sexual blackmail or coercion. Some of the scenes between Marlowe and Betty Mayfield read uncomfortably in our modern context:

She started for the door, but I caught her by the wrist and spun her around. The torn blouse didn't reveal any startling nakedness, merely some skin and part of a brassière. You'd see more on the beach, far more, but you wouldn't see it through a torn blouse.

I must have been leering a little, because she suddenly culred her fingers and tried to claw me.

"I'm no bitch in heat," she said between tight teeth. "Take your paws off me."

I got the other wrist and started to pull her closer...

And later in the novel:





Everybody's moral compass is skewed here: Marlowe betrays his paying client in order to "protect" a woman he knows nothing about and for whom his services are unwanted. Betty's willingness to use sex and money to get what she needs would definitely warrant a 50s "tramp" qualification, although she never really seems into Marlow - she just seems to acquiese in the face of pressure.

I get it: Chandler was writing for male readers. Whilst I think holds up well, I'm not really sure this one does.
