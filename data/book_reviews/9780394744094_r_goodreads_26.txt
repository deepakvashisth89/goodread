This book takes place in an upstate New York town which is dying as are many of the characters. The time is over a period of six years. The story begins at the Mohawk Grill and ends at the Mohawk Grill.

This is Richard Russo's first book. I read "Empire Falls" and the "Bridge of Sighs", "Empire Falls" won the Pulitzer Prize and is in a way something like this novel. I found this book at a library sale. The reason I bought this book is because a lady I know talked about how enjoyable and funny Russo's books are. The book is interesting, a good read, never boring. The characters and the setting is well told. Richard Russo and Kent Haruf are among the best at writing about blue collar lives and towns.

Harry is the owner of the Mohawk Grill. He looks out for a strange man, Wild Bill, who wanders around the town, dirty, unkempt. Harry gives Wild Bill food, shelter. At one time, it is said that Wild Bill was a regular teenager. Noone knows what happened in his life to make him a homeless outcast.

There are two elderly sisters, the oldest, eighty, the youngest, sixty five. From a large family of daughters, the middle four are deceased. Milly lives with her daughter and son-in-law in a wealthy home. Mrs Grouse lives with her husband, Mather, a good old New England name, her divorced daughter, Anne, her grandson, Randall. Her house is modest and small.

Anne is a beautiful woman, her cousin, Diane Wood, is several years older. Anne's ex-husband is a clown, never working, drinking too much. he does so many foolish things, that is irritating to read about, not funny. These characters all do drink a good bit. Anne has always been in love with Dan, her cousin's husband. Dan loves his wife, his mother-in-law is irritating. Anne marries Dallas Younger, young and stupid. It didn't last long.

Mather Grouse is so different than the men working in the tanning mills which are closing down. His only bad habit is smoking. He worked at his job, liked cold beer, had only one, did not become friends and cohorts with any of his co-workers, did his job well, is so quiet.

When the old hospital was under demolition, people stood around watching one exciting event in this town.

There are so many interesting characters, so many interesting places, so blue collar. Stealing from the tannery by some, a bookie coming around taking cash from those who hoped to do well. So many rough, tough, uneducated characters.

Russo brings up the closing of the tanneries for polluting Cayuga Creek. This was popular news about pollution in this part of the country several years ago.
