The first book in the series was thematically interesting and had one touching subplot that carried it for me. The second book felt like too much of a retread of the first, and it never really clicked. I thought the series needed a shot in the arm, and I think Benford agreed, because this book has an unusual and beautifully realized setting that's actually important to the story's themes and characters, a compelling protagonist with complex relationships, more high-octane action scenes to spice up the story, an arc that sets up future stories while simultaneously being satisfying all by itself, and one of the best villains in a science fiction book I've ever seen.I mean, Iguessthat'll do it.
