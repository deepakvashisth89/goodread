For those not familiar with this series: Sir John Fielding was a real person, the brother of author Henry Fielding. John Fielding was in real life a blind magistrate on Bow Street and the creator of the Bow Street Runners. The Bow Street Runners were the first constabulary in London and maybe in the world (not sure about this last). I have said this in previous reviews and I usually stipulate that all other characters are fictional. Imagine my surprise in this volume when Oliver Goldsmith makes a cameo appearance, including young Jeremy Proctor coming into possession of Goldsmith'sThe Vicar of Wakefield.This is a murder mystery series. In this installment, someone has been murdering prostitutes. Sir John has been named temporary coroner and convenes a jury to review the facts of the first murder. The verdict of the jury - at Fielding's prompting - is that the woman was murdered byPerson or Persons Unknown. Note above I used the plural of prostitutes, so this coroner's verdict was issued more than once.The Runners play more of a part in this installment than I remember from those previous, but my memory could be faulty. Jeremy Proctor, a young lad unofficially adopted by Fielding in the first installment, is the first person narrator. He, of course, is also prominent and actually does play a part in helping gather evidence.I love this series and wonder why it has taken me more than a couple of years to get to this volume. While in the middle of it, I ordered the next! It is a disappointment that only the last couple of installments are available in a Kindle edition. I rarely give this genre 5 stars, but it is so well written and the characters so "alive" that I cannot resist - yet again!
