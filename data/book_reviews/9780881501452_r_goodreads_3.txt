BOTTOM LINE: Another wonderfully old-fashioned visit with Asey Mayo and other New England-y types, circa 1930, mixed into a carefully wrought - and stylized - murder mystery, complete with unusual murder method and intricate timetable. Not for those who enjoy fast-paced action, that's for sure, but one of my favorite things to enjoy on a lazy afternoon when a trip into the past seems very welcoming.Adelbert Stires is a wealthy, ruthless businessman, with a reputation for beligerancy. He's built a nifty new house nearby Miss Prudence Whitsby's friend Ramona's cottage on the Cape, and they're invited to come over and spend a day or two at/in the new house with some mutual friends. Seems Bert's a mite late in arriving, though, and when he does finally show up he has a very fishy story to tell. But everyone is so relieved to finally see him that nobody really gets to the bottom of it, just then. Unfortunately Bert is murdered that very night, using a most unusual murder method, in a nearly-locked room, in his new, opulent house filled with friends and enemies.Asey Mayo is called in - recently voted local sheriff, he has the sharpest brains around for solving that sort of mystery anyway, but before he can do much a big snowstorm is rolling in, and everyone soon becomes trapped in the remote house, buried in feet of snow, no phone, no electricity, and there appears to be a lot of poison about. And there's that dead body, too. Lots of motives are explored, along with a good deal of personal histories of the participants, and they're a motley crew, as befits a classic mystery: a business partner who'd recently been done out of a good deal, an ex-lady friend, with whom he'd badly quarreled decades ago and never made up, a peculiar niece/ward that he'd never seen before, a local historian who's digging into the Squires family past, some odd servants (including a cook that can't cook...). And they all have motives for wanting to do away with Bert too. Well, except for Asey and Miss Prudence Whitsby, the ostentible narrator of the story.Some stereotypes typical of the time-period and setting/style of mystery, but if you can get by that and don't mind the leisurely pace this is a wonderfully enjoyable story, with minimal romance - unusual for the time - but there is a happy ending. Taylor is one of my all-time favorite writers, and IMO is a sadly neglected Golden Age mystery author. Her Asey Mayo series starts out gently humorous, and becomes downright farcical in a couple of the upcoming stories, but this second in series is still quite a nice mystery, laced with some wry humor that's just to my taste.
