I finished, I thought, "The Education of Henry Adams" sitting in a small park 2 or 3 blocks from the White House. I had just bought an affordable wedding ring for my future bride arriving from Greece in just a few days. We had met only 4 months before in Athens and proposed to each other at the same moment 6 weeks later, which meant that I had to find a job that would sustain us. So, I flew back to the U.S. and found a good one in Alexandria, Virginia, the day I arrived in DC. I would be working on a language training project associated with the Peace Corps which later took me on research trips from Quebec to Colorado including 2 Indian reservations. The professor who hired me then is still a good friend, and I am still grateful to him. (He lent us his car to get to the church in Franconia to get married.)

Due to my own peregrinations in an attempt to become “educated", I was hoping to finally find a straight path to knowledge, understanding and finally wisdom, as King Solomon had counseled us. I did had not found it in this book necessarily and concentrated on the matter at hand.

We should be forewarned of seeking paths of others who have different origins than we do. I was not destined to be hidebound, not that Henry was; in fact, if he followed his instincts he would have made, I truly believe, an admirable pirate or Arctic explorer. He was a Boston Brahmin, but I think in his heart of hearts he was probably more of a Brahmin Brahms, in that although he was Bostonian, no changing that, but he was creative and pushed outward at the boundaries unafraid to take risks.

Having several times looked at the worn cover of my old paperback copy that I had read that long-ago day distractedly in heated anticipation and having let what I remember of the book gestate within me with time and experience, I should go back now and read more of this scion of American political aristocracy, including "The Education of Henry Adams”.
