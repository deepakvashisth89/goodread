As a TV character once said, I’ve lost the will to scold.

(Temporarily, of course. I can always muster some effort to talk smack. This book knows what it did, and I’m fighting back.)

There must be two editions of this book - the one Nastya and I buddy read and the one everyone else did. Because you gotta be kidding me, right? Because the only good thing I can say about it is that despite seeming endless it actually managed to reach the finish line right when I was losing hope that I’d ever see the last page.

This supposedly beloved classic simply exhausted me via terminally annoyed irritation.

It’s so disjointed both in plot and narrative style, like a poorly done surrealist where a bicycle ends up with rabbit legs and a flowerpot for a head. And no, I’m not talking about anachronisms and author interjections - I actually got a kick out of it. . The parts of the story are not as much loosely connected as haphazardly thrown together, dull and tedious with occasional clashing in tone unfunny comic relief here and there. Major events happen off-page, recapped by other characters; we learn of deaths and quests and battles in a few sentences of stilted dialogue that sound rather like a middle school book report or “what I did on my summer vacation” essay style.



The characters are not much better. Most characterization is flat, and the ones that initially stand out - Arthur/Wart and Lancelot - really become unrecognizable later on, as though written by completely different people. Losing idealism and falling prey to human weakness is fine, but it really shouldn’t read like our characters were replaced by fairy changelings somewhere between different chapters. . And I wonder if women hurt White in any way given how cruelly they are written.


The tangents about the politics and human nature were somewhat interesting but there was enough of them for a decent size essay and not for a 600-plus page book. And slowly but surely I went from being somewhat amused by simplistic and charming whimsy of the first novel (the reason, I suspect, many readers love this book - since they have the fond childhood memories of the first part, ) to progressive irritation to finally dejected resolution to finish it despite pervasive confused boredom. And even that relatively charming first part kept me wondering who it was aimed at, kids or adults, with its odd combination of childlike cuteness and very simple themes (basically “war is not good” and “might doesn't equal right” - things you’d want to teach quite young kids about) jarring with occasional inclusion of quite adult content.

Now, I do get the points and the messages that White, with varying success, was trying to make (and it’s hard not to get them when he constantly interrupts whatever little of the story there is to lecture us and critique Arthurian legends), so that’s not the issue.

(Now, even in my frustration I’m happy to admit that the very last chapter of the book was alright in its melancholic contemplation, even if it had to rely on a few paragraphs explaining to the reader what the book was about 🙄. I just wish I didn’t have to suffer through 600 pages of this doorstopper to get there. Seriously, it was basically an essay summarizing the book and I should have only read that since by that point I’ve had enough of summarizing).

Anyway, that’s it. It’s over, and I accept that I’ve read a defective version of this beloved classic, and no dare in the world can make me suffer through this again.
