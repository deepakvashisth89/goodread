Some high level observations of my four favourite detectives. Harry Bosch, Harry Hole, John Rebus and Kurt Wallender. They are all male. All apart from Hole have daughters, he has a son. None have a cheery disposition. All are introspective. They are divided by continents but united in leading sad and lonely lives. They all have a propensity for violence, although Wallender is by far the most restrained. They are all fiercely focused and driven. They are all ageing. Wallender with his fear of his father’s dementia. Rebus with his COPD. Bosch with hismyeloid leukemia and Hole just …getting older. All four like a drink or two. With Hole even out drinking Rebus, which is difficult to imagine. I am sure you can all add additional points of departure for these characters. But Kurt Wallender I feel most sorry for.This story appears to begin with a normal death in front of a cash machine. It spirals from there in only a Mankell way. Gruesome deaths. A possible conspiracy to attack the global markets from a simple cash machine. Wallender is betrayed and as usual feels helpless against the vicissitudes of life. Trying to link events is mind destroying.I remember this book on tv but not all the details or which actor played Wallender. I think I have a couple of Wallender books left - all read out of order. Oh and his daughter Linda makes a career choice at the end. Mankell is a super writer. That said the new Harry Hole series on Netflix has just kicked off.😉👍
