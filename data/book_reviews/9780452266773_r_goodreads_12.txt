Second time reading this, and I loved it all over again. Lucy, the protagonist of this story, is a young lady from the West Indies who has immigrated to America to work as an au-pair. Lucy is a fascinating character because she's so very gloomy despite what might be perceived as an improvement from her previous situation in her island: a liberal boss who is generous with her, four children who don't give her a hard time, an income, distance between herself and her family. We later find out Lucy is mourning the loss of love and the sense of betrayal she feel towards her mother, who didn't dream more ambitious dreams for her, who had had been a bright student, and instead gave more focus to her brothers simply because Lucy was a girl. This is a romance story in that case, but one that has soured.

But it's more than just that. It's a beautiful meditation on love, friendship, history, life. The last quarter of this book is some of the most moving and stunning writing I've read. I know that, if I have any say in it, I'll be reading Lucy again in the future.
