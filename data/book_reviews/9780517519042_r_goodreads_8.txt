Wasn't sure what to expect from this, but it's a very impressive work, as Baum takes familiar simple nursery rhymes and concocts a full parable for each to retroactively be based on. They're snappy and fun for kids, but also sophisticated and thematic for adult, as they explore classism, poverty, politics, complex relations between humans and animals. My favorite was "Mistress Mary, Quite Contrary", where he crafts a longing and anxious tale where the eponymous girl is using the growth of her garden as a way to count down the days until her father and brothers return from a long expedition at sea. Really, they're all lovely and witty and impressively moving, "The Man in the Moon" probably being the weakest, though even that has an interesting hot air balloon climax that presages Wizard of Oz.
