Nobody moves from serious to silly and back again with the quicksilver grace of Steve Martin. You expect it if you know his work, and he still manages to surprise you. This collection of essays, mostly written for the New Yorker magazine and New York Times Magazine in the 1990s represent Martin's attempts to get back in touch with his original love of comedy after decades of hard work and success. He was on a self-imposed hiatus to recharge, but ended up producing a substantial body of work in that time.A few of the essays are dated, in the you-had-to-be-there category, some made me smile and some made me laugh out loud, but I think my personal reaction should not be used as a guide for anyone else because Martin's work will strike everyone differently, at different times, like touching different keys on a piano. Sometimes the silly hits you in the right place, sometimes his irony makes you think and sometimes he just makes you feel happy to be alive, and watching him dance with Lily Tomlin. Pure Drivel is happy and thoughtful and sarcastic and a delight, just like the man who wrote it.
