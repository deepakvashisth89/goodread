A Dortmunder short story is naturally different from a Dortmunder novel. There’s no time for the slow-burn buildup to a big heist, no room for a complex villain, no chance to give each of the members of Dortmunder’s gang the space they deserve. Instead, these stories resemble the stories of failed thefts that often introduce the novels. Usually they only feature Dortmunder, though Kelp makes an occasional appearance and the last story is an exception, and usually Dortmunder is dealing with something that has gone wrong, though unlike the novels he usually ends up on top. To compensate for the lack of depth — excepting Dortmunder himself, whom Westlake had written often enough that he can bring him to life with a quick sketch — these stories are almost uniformly hilarious, some of Westlake’s funniest work. The last story is the exception, as it features all the core members of the gang, except that it’s not really them but alternate-universe versions, apparently because Westlake was at one point worried that he would lose the rights to them to a movie studio. The result is that they get stuck in a kind of uncanny valley, too similar to the characters we know to make sense. Plus, this story isn’t very funny at all. Feel free to skip it, but definitely read the rest of them.
