Baum's work is often cited as the point where children's literature became 'safe' and lacking in genuine peril. He even states that this was his intention in the foreword to 'The Wonderful Wizard of Oz' (you've probably heard of that one).I think he must've forgotten about this agenda in this book as the protagonists spend much of the book at risk of (a) being made slaves, (b) being thrown to their deaths off the side of the titular island and (c) beingsliced in half by a bizarre full body guillotine!I've read adult horror novels where the characters weren't in as much peril! :-)
