What a gloriously over the top read by the mysterious Trevanian! I first read this back in the mid 80s when I was in college and have held onto my battered copy since then.Shibumialso really captures the late 70s Zeitgeist, when the Western world (and particularly the US) became infatuated with Japan (just like today with China), the rise of OPEC, and the 'deep state' within the US government.Our lead, Nicholai Hel, in the 'now' lives in Basque France, where he recently retired from a career of world-class assassin. The novel, however, starts off with a busted 'op' on three Israeli agents in Rome, where some US government agency orchestrated their murder, but missed the third. The director of the agency, a Mr. Diamond, after viewing a video of the op, realized that the third agent got away.Now, this agency Diamond works for, 'The Mother Company', was founded by petro and other energy companies and other transnational corporations, along with members of OPEC, to control the profits of this lucrative sector of the economy. Basically, Mother has become the 'deep state': it controls the CIA, basically runs the US government (among others), and definitely dictates US foreign policy. The reason behind assassinating the Israelis concerns how these agents were going to kill some members of the PLO who were behind the Munich Olympic massacre of Israeli athletes at the games there. Why would Mother want to save some PLO terrorists? Well, it would help keep the OPEC contingent happy, and well, oil runs the world don't you know!Nicholai Hel comes into the picture as the remaining Israeli agent (an amateur really) finds her way to Hel's villa; that was their destination in the first place (they were planning on going to London to hit the PLO villains after visiting Hel). Well, Diamond, our antagonist, figures out that the Israelis were going to visit Hel via his massive computer named 'Fat boy' and aims to stop Hel from doing anything...So much for the plot, but this is really not a plot driven novel; the real story concerns Hel, his life and his life's philosophy. Trevanian takes us on a deep dive into the origins of Hel (born in Shanghai in the 20s/30s when it was still a European enclave), raised in part in Japan and then later cast on the world stage. Via Hel's life philosophy, Trevanian manages to brutally challenge just about every facet of the West, and indeed, the 'corrupted' Japan that emerged from WWII. America, just a bunch of mongrel merchants; a land of mediocracy. Nothing and no one is really spared from the cutting critique of just about everything in the modern world here. I do not think I appreciated it as much back in the 80s, but this aspect was brilliant!Shibumialso possesses long sections where Hel and a Basque buddy go caving near Hel's villa and I really dug this section; I was a member of the spelunking club in college and once a month we would head to Southeast Ohio or Kentucky to cave.Over all, great fun and really glad I reread this. 4.5 stars, only rounding down as the pacing and story were rather erratic at times.
