I have read this novel more than once, the first time when it was published, but I find that over the years my opinion has declined as Hollinghurst reputation has climbed with every book he published. This is the "the book that launched this great reputation and set Hollinghurst up as the voice of gay England!" (to paraphrase Abraham Lincoln to Harriet Beecher Stowe). For me, now, it is the book that dropped a stinking cow pat onto the lively, varied and interesting nascent UK gay literary scene and reduced it to sex, history and interior decoration. With his lack of insights or relevance he might as well have been writing 'Downtown Abbey' scripts. Although Hollinghurst talks of exploring the hidden or overlooked 'gay' past what he produces is the National Trust/Laura Ashley pastiche of the past were the toffs, flawed though they may be are in charge and doing their best and we should doff our caps and remind ourselves how lucky we are that we never made the mistake of the French, Russians and others of slaughtering our over breed, over privileged upper classes.

It is amazing to think that there was a time when there was a lively literary scene in the UK dragging down and coruscating the idiocies of the establishment. Who today would write a novel like 'A Significant Experience' by Gwyn Griffin demolishing the entire mystique of Britain's officer class in WWII (of course you will not have read it, you won't have heard of it!). What is even more extraordinary is that Hollinghurst is seen as the UK's Edmund White, the great pajandrum of, almost the inventor of, 'Gay' literature. In fact what he and 'The Swimming Pool Library' was the equivalent of Churchill's famous quote about the Germans in WWI sending Lenin to Russia:

"The German leaders... turned upon Russia the most grisly of all weapons. They transported Lenin in a sealed truck like a plague bacillus from Switzerland into Russia".

That is exactly what the UK's closeted public school & Oxbridge educated etiolated minions did when they found in Hollinghurst someone from above the salt they could promote as 'gay'. A whole generation of 'gay' writers remained ghettoised and then forgotten and there wouldn't be a decent gay novel published in the UK until 'At Swim Two Boys' by Jamie O'Neill (see my footnote *1 below). If we are honest Hollinghurst has had no successor because his writing is cul de sac which says nothing about the past or present - if you want writing in English that can use the past to illuminate the present you need to look to writers like Tash Aw.

But aside from my loathing of what Hollinghurst represents I find so much of the praise heaped on novels like this inexplicable. The whole setting in 1983 and the 'loving' evocation of London's very gimcrack and second rate copy of Studio 54 both pathetic and annoying. There is not an ounce of honesty or realism, except for realistic evocation of being absurdly rich, self absorbed and privileged, but you can get that by watching the Khardasians or any of their imitators.

This is the first of Hollinghursts endless succession of novels which use the past as a way avoiding reality - in the same way all of the UK's 'heritage' providers use the past as soporific to ensure the easants don't question why they are soending their good money to keep the 'haves' having everything and bitching about the very little the 'have nots' still have.

*1 But he is Irish I hear you say - but of course he is who won the battle of Waterloo? Irish soldiers; and I think the Irish Nobel literature laureates William Butler Yeats and Samuel Beckett have contributed more to 2oth century literature in the English language than Winston Spencer Churchill.
