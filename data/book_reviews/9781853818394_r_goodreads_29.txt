3.5 rounded up.Part of Angela Carter’s so-called Bristol Trilogy, ‘Several Perceptions’ follows the nihilistic university drop-out, Joseph Harker who, heartbroken and haunted by bad dreams, unsuccessfully attempts to explode himself via gas cooker. Through a series of vignettes, Carter charts Joseph’s unstable status at the fringes of, but not quite fitting in with, the sixties counterculture, and his various liaisons with a cast of quirky characters. Carter is one of my favourite writers, but I’m more familiar with her more explicitly, feminist and magical realist work; while I think I prefer that style overall, this was interesting to read as an embryonic piece of her early fiction, bearing the traits—both allusive and flush with figurative flourishes—that would come to their full expression later on.
