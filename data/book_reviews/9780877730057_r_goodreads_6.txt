I'm not sure if I've already read some of this, because I once had a copy of The Buddhist Scriptures (I believe it was a Penguin edition?). It meant a lot to me; it kept me sane while commuting hours per day to and from a punishing boot camp of an engineering school that was making me get very physically sick (along with my terrible diet and dirty NYC), and I've been sick ever since, with what's most likely chronic fatigue syndrome.I would read The Buddhist Scriptures on the train a lot back then, especially where it said how painful life is and how the world's on fire. I would be feeling and seeing it right in front of me, the mess and the poverty.I barely had enough money around that time, but I got an audiovisual job making about $15 a week at the college--and I used to leave my knapsack with my books behind a door. I had no idea that a thief was sneaking around the school--a thief who finally stole my knapsack with my scientific calculator, my meager money, and my Buddhist Scriptures inside. Thankfully, he at least left me my copy of the Lao-Tzu's Tao Te Ching piled up with a notebook where my knapsack had been.*What kind of person steals a copy of The Buddhist Scriptures? A fucking creep.I never bought another copy; it just wouldn't have meant the same to me. I've always kept the book's message somewhere in my mind anyway.(*I've been an atheist since I was a little kid, but I had taken a comparative religions course at that college--and I really enjoyed reading both books.)
