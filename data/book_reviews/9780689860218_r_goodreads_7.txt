(The beginning of this book made me nervous as it started out quite slow. However, after about 40 or 50 pages, I started to notice my interest level changing and my desire to put the book down became less and less).This is such a great dual-perspective story about a boy named Spence and a boy named Dieter. As the story progresses, you come to care greatly for both of the characters, even when you want to shake some sense into Dieter.Dean Hughesdid an incredible job weaving a tale that ultimately leaves you sad and heart-wrenched but withjustthe right amount of distress. It is not hard to imagine that tales like this happened over and over again in WWII and that gives this text a feeling of authenticity.I would absolutely recommend this book to both adults and young adults, and specifically those who don't typically like historical fiction. I feel as though this text could [ever so slightly] change their mind.
