3.5 to 3.75.Well, I didn't know about mid 90s noir, but here it is. Abe Lieberman is a cop, a bit past retirement at early 60s, partnered with an on-again, sober- again Irish cop with the name of Hanarhan. Just as his partner is dyed in the wool Irish, Abe is dyed in the whatever the ceremonial cloth is of Jewish-American, Chicagoean. He eats lox on bagels, knows everyone and anyone, can spin a yarn, and everything hurts and he's tired.But like all good noir/ detective story, it feels timeless. Doesn't feel like he needs the latest technology to solve a crime, he'll just keep doggedly pursuing it. He mediates his family life, he kvetches with the old timers at the deli and he listens and watches the Cubs.I supposed it would be like me writing about Boston and layering in every single street name and location just to get it known I was talking about Boston.My complaints is the specificity and the confusing tone of it some times. I had to re read a few passages to make sure I knew what was going on. And there was a great amount of coincidences and an easy tie up that I didn't see what was coming but felt almost like a, pardon the pun, cop out.The dialogue is realistic and paced, no zingers to find out, just two real people plodding along. A lot of " so and so told them" and we have to figure out what was told or it's written later. Some of the stereotypes didn't age well and some of the big city didnt age well. Gentrification was mentioned and was like a fifth character.I think I found an easy bingable reading series.
