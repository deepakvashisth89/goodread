I’m not gonna lie: the prose is at times dense, and the sentences feel over-thought, but from the start I had the sensation that I was reading Salinger or Capote. I think the secret is that Fitzgerald wrote about the supposed antiheroes, the socialites Anthony and Gloria, with affection. They don’t deserve our sympathy, but they are so honest with us and with each other that I found myself rooting for them to remain the spoiled brats they are and to never have to work a day in their lives. It’s a sentiment that, as a society, we celebrate more than ever, which makes it surprising that Fitzgerald is no longer fashionable, with the exception of The Great Gatsby. Well, I prefer him to Hemingway, and I’m gonna read more from him.
