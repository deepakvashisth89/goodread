An interesting read more so as an exploration—and expansion—of Kathryn Janeway’s character, rather than the presentation of the plot line set in Voyager’s native twenty-fourth century. I found myself much less interested in the plot set in the present rather than all the flashbacks of Janeway’s past experiences, though I must admit the latter was advertised to me as the main draw of the book, and was my primary source of motivation for picking it up in the first place.

Canonically, it ties in well with the Star Trek: Voyager I am familiar with. Figures, as the author was one of the producers for the first portion of Voyager’s run; this book takes place sometime near the end of the second season of the show. The characters are identical to their show counterparts, which is much appreciated and always something of a fear of mine when interacting with licensed media.

A plethora of words that I wasn’t familiar with, but nothing too distracting from the reading itself, and nothing the built-in dictionary of my eReader couldn’t handle. A bit of a slow read (particularly the non-flashbacks sections of the book), but nothing unlike watching an episode (or two) of any twentieth-century Star Trek series.
