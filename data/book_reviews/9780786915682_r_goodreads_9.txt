“Arrrr, ever been to sea, Billy?”Welcome to my review of the anthology ‘Realms of the Deep’ written by various authors conversant with a Dungeons and Dragons shared world setting called Forgotten Realms.Evil is boiling up from the seas and overrunning the Sword coast. It is up to humanity and her sea faring allies to stop the hordes from destroying all.The evil is in the form of the Sahuagin, and they are using dark magic and countless aquatic troops to destroy the landlubbers . . .If you wonder what a Sahuagin is let me tell you.*Consults Monster Manual* - Wow, they are a challenge rating of 2 - pretty nasty little sea creatures. Physical description (just see book cover art) although the one depicted thereon has four arms. Only about one in two hundred have four arms. I would bet these guys are a challenge rating 3, not 2 since they get two extra attacks per round . . .I know, enough with the gaming geek speak, what about the stories?I just can’t help it though. I read these Dungeons and Dragons anthologies with an eye towards my own campaign and the other two I participate in.Well, to be honest, I found the stories adequate but not exceptional. The only two which really stood out for me (maybe because they were not just sort of the same) were “Fire by Fire” by Elaine Cunningham. Inside that tale was a great battle between a Kraken and an animated statue . . .The second cool (eye towards gaming) story was by “The Crystal Reef” by Troy Denning. In this tale we see a giant (a sea giant) protecting his precious island’s reef against invasion. I won’t give it away, but I thought it was imaginative and well done.That is not to say the other stories were inferior or didn’t have really cool bits in them, they just didn’t appeal to me as much.Actually, this collection is a kind of add-on to a three book Forgotten Realm series called “The Threat from the Sea” by Mel Odom. I have not read this series, but the short stories in “Realms of the Deep” all take place more or less at the same time as the huge sweeping war written about in Mel’s three books.And Mel has a short story in this collection too. If you like Mel. I don’t recall too many details from his story.So, should you rush out and find this work and read it? Hmm. Well, if you are like me and enjoy a good foray into a fantasy gaming world through fiction, and you like tales of the deep and sea creatures, then I would say yes. If you are just looking for great short stories, well then, this may not be the best.On a 20 sided die, this anthology would roll about a 12. Of course, if you have the appropriate skill modifiers, the number would be adjusted higher.Arrrrr!
