This is the first novel in Grass’s Danzig Trilogy, set during and immediately after WW2 in Germany, mostly in Danzig (now Gdańsk in Poland). Oskar Matzerath is the main protagonist in the magical realism story. His intellectual development is complete already as an infant and he refuses to grow up. He beats his drum against the injustices and general madness of his time and place. In some ways he represents the unreliable observer of society’s path to -and perhaps beyond- the brink, but there are also elements of the fallen angel and the little tyrant to his character. Eerily grotesque and apocalyptic, this superbly conceived and written novel is not comfortable to read, but it is important that we do, as this book in some ways defines the 20th century.
