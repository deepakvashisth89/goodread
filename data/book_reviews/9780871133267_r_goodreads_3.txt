"I don't know what's worse, a solitary creep or a married creep,"-- a character ponders in this novel. Oh, a married creep is worse, I think. But not here, not in this novel. There's a married guy, but he's not a creep at all; instead, he's open-minded, patient, smart, loving and caring. It's the solitary guy who's the creep, very creepy indeed. But, is he the killer?Because you have to have a killer in a work ofnoir, don't you? It may be a rule. And there is a killing in this story finally, but not until Chapter 31, around page 286.This is classic Highsmith, where the killing is not as important as the characters. Hell, anyone can kill, but who really is the creep? It's also classic Highsmith in that the ensemble cast is half Gay and Lesbian. Maybe one of them is the killer. I won't tell.
