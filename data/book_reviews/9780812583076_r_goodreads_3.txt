Kathleen O'Neal Gear is one of my "comfort reads," an author I know I'm going to enjoy reading just about every time I pick up one of her books. This book has the same forceful honesty and high adventure as her others. 5 stars because I can't find any reason not to.Based on true events and historical figures, this is the story of a Jesuit mission to Quebec ("New France") in the early 1600s. A troubled priest meets Andiora, a Huron woman, and faith-testing hijinx ensue.The cover might have you thinking this is a romance novel, but (a) it's a much broader story than any romance novel I've seen, i.e. it's about more than a man and a woman falling in love (although it is about that); and (b) it has all of the Gears' usual honesty about human behavior. If you've read any of their books, you know they do not mince words in depicting the brutality of life in Native American culture. There are people getting hacked to pieces and eaten alive (by people and animals) in this story. Sometimes, but not always, in that order. If you want a nice little story about people making out, this is NOT it.
