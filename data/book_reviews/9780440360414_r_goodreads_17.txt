When I first moved to Portland, I wandered the city and usually ended up at Powell’s, the world's largest independent bookstore, where this book kept turning up as a staff pick. I bought it despite my poor track record with "funny" books, outside of Woodhouse, Benson, and some of Waugh. Usually, the famously "funny" books like A Confederacy of Dunces leave me cold. Perhaps I’m deficient, but I find them cloying.

At first, I enjoyed this book. The plotting was tighter than comic fiction usually bothers with, thanks to some theft from Henry James's , James being where half of literature seems to steal its plot outlines.

Larry David was such a devotee of that he famously pressed copies on the entire writers’ room. You can feel why: Elliot Weiner has that same strain of people-hatred as Larry David, plus there’s a dance scene (the funniest in the book) that seems to have prefigured Elaine from ’s famously catastrophic shimmy.

The fat jokes are a problem, though. There’s homophobia and racism too, and we’re not meant to like the narrator, but the fat jokes carry a glee the rest of the cruelty doesn’t. I guess it’s an artifact of its time (it was published in the early 1980s, forgotten about, and is now being rediscovered), but I'm not sure how valid an excuse that is. Being a bully is just being a bully.
