I discovered this teenage book in my parents' library. I finally read it and was amazed at the beauty of this classic novel.
I loved - A book to recommend.
I wanted to rekindle the hazy memories of Hitchcock's movie from my childhood and understand why it scared me so much.
Suppose Daphné du Maurier succeeds in keeping us going from start to finish. In that case, I believe this has a lot to do with the angle of narration chosen, namely a story told in the first person by the frail and shy heroine, who is naive yet lucid about her status as a victim, and whose tragedy will reveal her personality throughout the intrigue.
More Hitchcockian, you die! No wonder the great master appropriated this work in a symbiotic way. From the first scenes on the summer roads of the Riviera to the insidious weirdness of Manderley, I felt his presence behind every stage, with the unsettling feeling of reading a movie or seeing a book that was thirty years older. Late always scares!
