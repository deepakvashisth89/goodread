Not a bad “ Star Trek” book; certainly not the worst I have read. The characterization of the “big three” do feel a little off - but part of that is (presumably) plot based, since Spock is injured and not in his right mind, and losing 1/3 of the “trinity” could be cause for the remaining two to be out of sorts - this is something that you see in TOS as well (“The Tholian Web” springs to mind) - so it feels less like dodgy writing than it does a narrative choice.

When the three are reunited, the characterizations of each seem to be quickly restored to proper “Star Trek” equilibrium. The requisite “guest star” is handled not too badly, as well, and while the nature of her role does dictate that she is (for plot reasons) not all that she initially seems, the ultimate (and final) reveal is sufficiently twisty that it feels both somewhat unexpected, and yet simultaneously also a reasonable end result - as opposed to just being a “final twist” for the sake of having a last “gotcha” moment, from the author.

It is a nice enough story, told capably. It does not wholly stand out as one of the best books (or episodes) but it is also far from the worst. It is solid, if unspectacular, “Star Trek”.
