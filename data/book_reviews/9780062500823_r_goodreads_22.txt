Despite being a Greek mythology aficionado with a psychology degree from Sigmund Freud’s alma mater, I never would’ve gravitated towards this pop-science book on my own… yet it got my vote, and was ultimately the winning selection, for my monthly book club. I figured that having just gone through a difficult and major shift in my personal life, I’d probably be more receptive to what’s essentially a feminist, Jungian self-help book than I’d usually be.Jean Shinoda Bolen, Jungian analyst and psychiatrist, made waves in the mid-80’s with this feminist revision of Jung’s concept of archetypes—Gloria Steinem, arguably the world’s most famous feminist activist, even provided an introduction. The underlying idea is quite simple: Bolen uses seven Greek goddesses to describe women’s behavioral patterns and personality traits. Building on the theories of Jungian analysis, she claims that these unconscious yet powerful archetypal forces influence how we feel and act from birth, and that by understanding how these archetypes manifest, we can reflect on what drives us, and be empowered to grow beyond the restrictive dichotomies of what (Western) society teaches a woman to be.Bolen separates the seven goddesses into three groups: Artemis, Athena, and Hestia make up the independent and self-sufficient ‘virgin goddesses’, Hera, Demeter, and Persephone represent the relationship-oriented ‘vulnerable goddesses’, and Aphrodite is the only transformative ‘alchemical goddess’ who shares similarities with both groups, yet is intrinsically different, and thus gets to stand in a league all of her own. She would love that very much.“The true cost of anything is what we give up in order to have it. It is the path not taken. To take the responsibility of making the choice is crucial and not always easy.”Each goddess gets her own chapter, wherein Bolen relates her myth, and describes what a typical childhood, adolescence, and adult years may look like if a particular goddess is solely in charge of a woman’s psyche, as well as the strengths and weaknesses related to each archetype… but throughout the book she makes it clear that we’re all made up of an interplay of these archetypes, and that at different stages of life, a different goddess may be more prominent. Right now, I see myself most in Artemis, but I’d say that Athena dominated in my twenties, I used to embody Hestia as a child, and Hera’s presence was strongly felt in my late adolescence… a lot of it really resonated with me very much, but that’s also where the weakness of Bolen’s theory lies: It’s ultimately too generic to be truly helpful? Like a horoscope, its worded in such a way that just about everyone is likely to find something that fits their lived experience in every chapter, because she applies a lot of traits—many of them contradictory—to each goddess. If we’re all on a spectrum, why make up constricting categories at all?I can’t deny that despite getting a little repetitive in parts, this was very readable and well-written, but I was hoping for the Greek myths to be more of a metaphorical framing device; I suppose that I expected something more scientifically based from someone with Bolen’s credentials, but since I don’t really hold with psychoanalysis, I should’ve known better. The goddesses don’t feel like metaphors, and she makes definitive, sweeping statements without citing any empirical studies or at least statistics to support her theories. I guess what I’m trying to say that it’s rather New Agey… at one point, Bolen claims that women with a strong Persephone archetype are likely to develop ESP, and that’s where she lost me. To be fair, I gleaned enough insight from this book to have something to talk about in my following therapy session, but I wouldn’t take any of it too seriously—it is ultimately the equivalent of the personality-type quizzes you find all over the internet. Just like those, it can be helpful inspiration to take a self-aware look inward—a way to recognize patterns of behavior that perhaps don’t serve you well, and consciously try and nurture other, neglected sides of your personality instead.—————★★★½ ·May 2025 selectionfor...Like a Good Book Club
