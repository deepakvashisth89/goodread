This is a terrific collection of poems, stories, and performance pieces. While the core emotion from which these texts of incest, trauma, rape, sex work, and recovery come is more than enough to draw a reader in and hold them riveted, Sapphire also proves herself a consummate artist in this collection. Having seen her perform and read her texts a few times back in NYC in the '90s, I was happy to come across this collection over the summer in a second hand shop, and to finally take the time to read her work on the page, and to consider it more carefully. The first few texts, while obviously mind-blowing and powerful, are perhaps a bit pedestrian in terms of form, or use the techniques of the spoken word troubadours of the period--part rap, part theater--stuff that works live but is a bit obvious maybe on the page. But, as the book goes on, the subject matter expands to more nuanced evocations of people, events, interactions, jobs, family relations, and social problems, the techniques similarly deepen in subtlety, complexity, and grow more bravely ambiguous and open ended, allowing the reader in more and more to share the experience. It's a bit like watching a writer mature and perfect her art as she goes. The raw pain of the early texts draws us in and the better and better writing takes us eventually deep into the heart of the USA's deepest wounds: abuse, racism, and our utter loneliness in the dog-eat-dog capitalist marketplace we have made of our country. But it's not really a didactic or polemic journey, rather it's pure poetry all the way.
