This work is a fascinating glimpse and unintentional warning about misunderstanding the past. Both in how people today and Plutarch himself (and people of his time) fail to understand and over glorify the past. This work cuts deep against some of the silly machismo many people today ascribe to Sparta but at the same time, many of the exaggerated qualities that Spartas martial slave society are ascribed directly inspired fascists and eugenicists of the 20th century. The debatable history in this work and ones like it has an immense body count to it. but this cannot be directly blamed just on Plutarch. It looks to be that for centuries people had glorified Sparta to a ridiculous extent before Plutarch and he show'd some awareness of this and does point how how cruel elements such as the Crypteia are.Furthermore, since Plutarch is one of few sources for some of these Kings (such as with Agis). Reading this work can help to deconstruct alot of the modern propaganda around Sparta. if a fascist uses Plutarchs description of Spartas practice of eugenics, you can also use Plutarch to mention that the last thing a king of Sparta ever did was kiss a man, and that a aspect as to why Sparta lost Leuctra and with it its empire was that the opposing Thebans were gayer. Breaking their silly mythos with their own sources they've pretended to read. Even if it's not the most accurate history to begin with.All this aside, these are some beautiful biographies where you can really feel and connect with the lives and struggles of these men. The Life of Cleomenes is an especially moving read that I'd highly recommend on its own.The copy I read also contained the Sayings of Spartan men and women, which was a hilarious read in and of its self and I'd highly recommend it.
