Book title: Love, Sara

Personal Response:
I really liked Love, Sara. What made the story even more interesting is that it is a true story. In my opinion, all high schoolers should be required to read this book because a majority of students experience something that these characters have and it could make them talk about their problems. I like how the author leaves the story and characters on a cliffhanger with the deaths of Dulcie and Jon. Also, personal letters are a great way to understand what the characters were thinking. Overall, I really liked .

Plot Summary:
Sara is troubled high school foster child. She was sexually abused by her dad and was shuffled from home to home. She has been at a family for 4 years with her foster parents and their 2 kids. She is feeling grounded at her new home but is still a little troubled. She writes her problems as stories in her honors English class as well as in her journal and as emails to her best friend, Dulcie. As Sara and Dulcie get comfortable at school, Dulcie meets a boy. Dulcie and Jon have a Romeo and Juliet like relationship. Dulcie finds out she is pregnant and she and Jon run away together only to want to kill themselves. Sara decides to join in on the suicide but then realizes she does not want to die and doesn’t know to go to get help. She decides to tell their parents and it seems that Jon and Dulcie might not kill themselves. But it’s too late. Jon and Dulcie die from a train crash. Readers are left on cliffhanger hanger if it was on purpose or not.

Characterization:
Sara had a difficult, and dramatic role in this book. In the beginning, she is a ruthless, troubled teenage girl. She feels as if no one but Dulcie understands her. She is depressed and over thinks every situation to make it terrible for her. Over time in the book, she comes to realize that Carol (her foster mom) only did everything to benefit Sara. Going from a suicidal teen to wanting to save her friends is a lot to take in. She was also strong and brave. She raised herself for many years. She also looked out for Dulcie and tried to help her situation when she became pregnant. Sara was a troubled youth, but she grew brave. At the end of the book, Sara came to understand that she was worth it.

Recommendation:
I recommend this book to anyone who thinks that there is nothing else left to do with their life. It has mature content on depression, sex, and drugs but High Schoolers who are going depression because of that, or if they just are interested in the read, should read this. It is in 2 girls perspective so girls may enjoy it more. I highly recommend this book to all high schoolers.
