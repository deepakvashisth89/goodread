Also known as “Five Little Pigs” and "Murder in Retrospect"“The accepted version of certain facts is not necessarily the true one.”One of the best Christie's I've read, sorry to see it's not mentioned more. Christie changed it up a bit by having Poirot having to take apart a crime that happened decades ago. Digging into the past and focusing on interviews instead of actual occurrence and eye witness details. A lot has been forgotten in the small details, and some of it has been skewed by memory and deceit. Like some of her other novels, the bulk of this one is interviews. In between that is the written accounts Poirot asked suspects to write, and when he compares them at the end it starts making sense.One thing that did bug me was the flatness of the daughter who is seeking answers. I figured she’d….care more on a personal level. I know she doesn’t remember much, but still. What I found the most interesting was the villain and everyone who was in love with everyone – it was like a soap opera or mysteries and most people wore fake faces. Interesting reveal and impossible for me to guess the actual villain since clues could have pointed in multiple directions.“It’s no use resenting a thing that you’ve no power to stop.”This is one of those mysteries where the crime is solved more by studying the psychological of the characters and what turns out plausible because of that, instead of following actual evidence and “clues”. There are some of those that help point the sign in the right direction, but it comes down to understanding and studying people in the end, as Poirot often says.I know she uses staple characters but I found the governess hatred of men to be taken to an even higher level than usual. The daughter came across as flat, but the other characters were convincing in their hatred or loyalty. There wasn’t as much humor in this one as some of Poirot’s books – it felt like a somber casting over the entire thing – but it fit the story. What impressed me the most with the psychology of the characters was Christie showing how demented romantic pairings can actually make some couples happy, even if they seem miserable to the outside world.NOTE BEFORE RE-READ 2025My mother and I got into the TV show, Poirot. We have favorites among the "humorous" episodes and the serious ones. This is my favorite of the serious, they did an exceptional job bringing this to the screen with Suchet donning the mustache and hat. Reading this one makes me want to re-watch the show episode again. This one shows the depths of obsessive love in ways many Christie works don't, romantic love that is unusual relationships but also that still exists when things are unconventionally happening. It also covers not wanting the future of your life and children overshadowed by "killer DNA" that is covered in books such as The Bad Seed. Finally, it starts with a warning that seeking the truth of the past may unsettle the present and to determine in advance if knowing the truth is actually worth it when so much time has passed.
