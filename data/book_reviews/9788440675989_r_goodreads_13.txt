4.5 ⭐'s“And it was like magic, too, like a gift from the gods, the ease with which a solution came to me, a simple plan, a way to keep the money without fear of getting caught.”Initial ThoughtsI've not been as full on with my reading in 2024. Not saying I've fell out of love with the written word, but I've had other things filling my time. But that's OK. Variety is the spice of life and all that.But I needed a book to take on holiday to Orlando and give me something to do on that long flight and while waiting in those theme park queues. Step forward Mr Scott Smith and his first novel...A Simple Plan.I had already heard of him due to being a big horror fan and the buzz around his other book...he's only written two...The Ruins. Not read that one yet, but if I enjoy this one I'll definitely give it a whirl. So let's see if I do...The StoryThis one kicks off on New Year's Eve with brothers,HankandJacob Mitchell, and the latter's best friendLou. They're out for a drive when Jacob's dog,Mary Beth, who jumps out the truck and bolts into the nearby woods to chase a fox.What the men discover in that forrest is a crashed aircraft containing dead men and a bag full of cash. 4.4 million to be exact. They then do what any upstanding citizen would in the circumstances...everything in their power to keep the money.Hank does have reservations about keeping the millions, but it doesn't take him long to get on board with Lou and Jacob, who are desperate to keep it. Greed will do that to you. However, he has a few conditions for them that they are compelled to agree to.Will it be enough to see them through without being caught...well who am I to spoil the surprise. It's a simple premise but the outcome is anything but. You've got one hell of a ride in store for you.The WritingWhat you have with A Simple Plan is a pretty basic story told very, very effectively. If I could pick one thing to focus on with the plot, out of many positives, it's the pacing. The way Smith slowly cranks up the pressure throughout is nothing short of exceptional. Expertly done.The narrative is fast paced and has you on the edge of your seat (or toilet if you do your reading on there). It's one hell of a page turner. Once it gets rolling it's absolutely relentless and takes you to some very dark places. I've already said that I've not been reading as heavily in 2024 and this was just what I needed. I couldn't put it down.Smith also paints a brilliant picture of small town life and the people who inhabit it with his words. For an author with only two novels to his name, I was shocked at his ability to create atmosphere, which is constantly tense. I recently finished a David Baladacci book and Smith is in a different league from him based on this performance.The CharactersWith so many fatal errors, it's not hard to put yourself in the characters shoes and relate to all the stress that hits them. Would you kill an acquaintance for a million dollars? How about to protect a loved one? Now you begin to understand, it's not as clear cuts as you first thought. And this is certainly the moral conundrum the author wants his reader to consider.The depiction and level of depth of the three main characters (four counting Sarah) made them so compelling and real that they worked their way inside my head and actually had me feeling sorry for them. Despite the evil things that they do.The story is told from Hank's perspective, i.e. in first person, and he's portrayed as your average, family orientated, likeable, generic type of guy. And this is a great choice by Smith, as it allows you to put yourself in his place. The way he remains a pretty sympathetic character throughout the story is a real achievement. Because his descent into savagery and madness, with the drive and direction of his good lady wife, is almost on a MacBeth level. I bet you didn't know I've read Shakespeare!You start off rooting for him to get away with it and it's hard to shake that feeling, even when things get progressively worse. And then you start to feel slightly disappointed when bad things happen to him. Every time Smith's characters thought they were doing something to solve their problems they only made things worse.Is Hank a victim of circumstance or a truly evil person? Does he have a choice? He may do some horrific things, but he’s still a normal guy caught up in extraordinary circumstances. He constant tries to justify the monstrous acts by convincing himself he has a choice. But I'm not buying it. The questions is...will you?Hanks relationship with his brother Jacob is also intriguing. They share a dysfunctional relationship, not having a hell of a lot in common. That's despite living in the same town. I hope my brother doesn't read this but it reminded me a lot of our relationship. Jacob is one of life's under achievers, while Hank is doing OK in his life. However, the only real bright spark in his life is his pregnant wife Sarah.Final ThoughtsA Simple Plan is more than you’re average crime novel. Much more. It's a tale of greed, fear, betrayal and murder. It really is like one of those Shakespearean tragedies for the modern audience. I know there's a movie starring Billy Bob Thornton and Jim Paxton and after enjoying this so much, I'm severely tempted to watch it.A simple Plan definitely added a little bit extra to an amazing holiday. And for that I am thankful. A big shout out to Scott Smith. I'm definitely going to read his other novel, The Ruins, at some point. But after that where do I go? I really wish he would write more crime novels. You can't have too much of a good thing, despite what they tell you.And on that note. Thanks for reading and...cheers!
