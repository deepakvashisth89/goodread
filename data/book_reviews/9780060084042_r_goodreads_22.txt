Leonard is the master of many things, but the two that shine here above all others: dialogue, and men with ethics who end up on the wrong side of the law.I wish he would write more historical fiction -- this is set in Cuba during the Spanish American war -- because he could give McMurtry a run for his money.There are whole sections here where the scene comes to life because of the absolute perfect pitch of the dialog. It might be said that there is a lull in the structure of the plot towards the middle, but not much of one.
