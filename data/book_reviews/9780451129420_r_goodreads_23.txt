You're a firestarter honey... just one big zippo lighterFirestarter is the 49th Stephen King book I have read and it took me this long to realise something. Some of the premises of his books can seem a bit lame, as in something a kid dreams up and not overly imaginative. Take the deadlights for example, those three lights that form the eyes and mouth of Pennywise. They just came from space? Admittedly, when tied in with the turtle and the strings it protects and the revelations in the Dark Tower, there is more of a story.In Firestarter it all starts with an experiment by scientists in a college where they inject patients with a formula. Not a lot of imagination in that but it's the backstory that King creates, the way he pits good against evil between characters, the peripheral characters that become mainstays. It would have been so easy to write a book where the firestarter just blows everything up, kills all of her adversaries and the thrill is all about the pyrotechnics.But King writes about emotion in this book. A young girl, a scared girl, a girl who is struggling to understand. The 'talent' horrifying, but at times, necessary. On the run because of something she can't control and she never asked for.Although it is a well known book, Firestarter is probably rarely mentioned when King's best books are collated. Perhaps it has something to do with the movies which leave a lot out but it is genuinely a good read.
