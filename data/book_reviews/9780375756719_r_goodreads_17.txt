I started the novella, The Country of Pointed Firs, before my August trip to Maine coast but then, when I picked it up again to finish after I got back, I enjoyed it even more because of my own memories of a beautiful, rough coast and a landscape different from my home.

Author was born and raised in a town on the Maine coast; she is considered the best of the “local color” New England writers. But her stories of Maine have many levels.

It is the story of a summer visitor, a lone woman writer, who wishes for time to concentrate on her work but is drawn to the people of the village and exploring it (a coastal area with nearby rocky tiny islands). We especially get to know and like the widowed landlady, Mrs. Todd, a local herbalist and her very elderly mother and brother who live alone on one of the islands.

The visitor befriends an old sea captain and old widowed fisherman/sailor and hears their stories. One can feel the loneliness and self reliance in these lives. One theme that keeps coming up is how their lives have changed from their youth or previous generations. The sailing industry has disappeared and the village has grown much more insular and less educated as a result. (Educated not the right word but I can’t think of a better one.). The old sea captain and fisherman went all over the world in their sailing days, exposed to many different people and places. A very old widow relates stories of accompanying her husband for 2 year voyages, bringing their young children along. These people were passionate readers, too — long sea voyages gave them the time and need for the entertainment books could give. But, now, the visitor finds that the long distance sea trading is just memories among the old and for younger people, life is hard work trying to make a living fishing or lobstering in the rough sea.

Confession — I only finished the novella itself but I want to go back and read the short stories, too. Lovely, slow paced writing and stories of forgotten lives here.
