Spiralling into an end-of-summer reading and viewing slump, where my otherwise robust capacity for studied appreciation snaps into tethersnapping irritation. This novel abandonment preceded a narked abandonment of Bèla Tarr’s Werckmeister Harmonies, one of those tedious, lugubrious, Romanian black and white arthouse films that make people hate arthouse films, and cineastes consider life-changing masterpieces kissed by Yahweh. It seems that others found What Maisie Knew as unappealing as me, with the bizarre cast of mamas, papas, stepmamas, steppapas, and the lovers of stepmamas and steppapas, all revolving round a poor little bore with the fortitude of a tardigrade. The prose is weapons-grade James, all exhausting longueurs and microscopic detail, which is tremendous when the reader cares. In this case, shoot me. Read up to Ch.21. [P.S. If you recommend Bèla Tarr’s seven-hour I will hurt you].
