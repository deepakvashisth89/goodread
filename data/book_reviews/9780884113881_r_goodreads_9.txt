Early Poirots or Deja Vu All Over AgainA review of the Blackmore Dennett eBook (April 2019) of the original Collins Crime Club hardcover (1974) collecting stories first published in The Sketch magazine in 1923.“Have you any clue to where the boy has been taken?”Poirot handed him a piece of paper. “Here is the address.”“But this is a blank sheet.”“Because I am waiting for you to write it down for me.”[3.5 average rating for the 13 stories, rounded up to a GR 4 star]This late issue of Hercule Poirot short stories from 1923 supplements the collection which was first released inPoirot Investigates(1924). Presumably the then publisher did not want to collect further stories at the time and insisted on novels. My lede header is due to some of these appearing in novella form inMurder in the Mews(Hercule Poirot #16 UK 1937) or in an earlier collectionThe Under Dog and Other Stories(Hercule Poirot #31 US 1951). So there is a definite sense of having read them previously.The early Poirots are very much in the format of those for Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes. Poirot is a consulting detective with Captain Hastings acting as his "Watson" and documenting the cases. Many of them had titles similar to Holmes's "The Adventure of ...", "The Case of ..." or "The Mystery of ...". Miss Lemon does not yet appear. Inspector Japp is the regular contact with Scotland Yard. There is even a hint of an equivalent to Holmes's Irene Adler, i.e. a female that Poirot admires for her criminal talent.The front cover of the original Collins Crime Club 1974 edition. Image sourced from Wikipedia.All of these were delightful in their own way. Some of the solutions were simple and straight forward (3 rating) and others more complex and entertaining (4 rating). Poirot and Hastings deliver their usual banter.The following story synopses provide only individual ratings, setup details and source information so I have not spoiler blocked them.1.The Affair at the Victory Ball**** Japp asks Poirot to assist in solving a murder and concurrent suicide. Hastings writes it up and refers to the 1st Poirot novel, The Mysterious Affair at Styles (1920). Story first published in The Sketch March 7, 1923 – Issue 1571. It was Christie’s 3rd story ever published.2.The Adventure of Clapham Cook**** A matron asks Poirot to find her missing cook, meanwhile £50,000 has disappeared from a London bank along with a bank clerk. Story first published in The Sketch November 14, 1923 – Issue 1607.3.The Cornish Mystery**** A woman from Cornwall comes to London and asks Poirot to look into her husband whom she suspects is poisoning her. Poirot & Hastings arrive in Cornwall only to learn that the woman is dead from poisoning. The husband is arrested but Poirot has doubts about it. Story first published in The Sketch November 28, 1923 – Issue 1609.4.The Adventure of Johnnie Waverly**** A young boy is kidnapped from the Waverly Estate and the parents ask Poirot to investigate. He suspects an inside job. Story first published asThe Kidnapping of Johnnie Waverlyin The Sketch on October 10, 1923 – Issue 1602.5.The Double Clue*** Poirot is asked to look into a jewelry theft where there are only 4 suspects but two clues are left behind which implicate different people. Also, Poirot meets his equivalent of “the woman” (i.e. a Sherlock Holmes allusion)? Story first published in The Sketch on December 5, 1923 – Issue 1610.6.The King of Clubs*** A dancer escapes from a murder and bursts into a house where the family is playing bridge. The Crown Prince who plans to marry her asks Poirot to assure him that the dancer herself is innocent. Story first published asThe Adventure of the King of Clubsin The Sketch on 21 March 21, 1923 – Issue 1573.7.The Lemesurier Inheritance*** The Lemesurier family is haunted by a curse that the first-born will never inherit the family title and estate. A mother asks Poirot to intervene in order to save her first-born son. Story first published in The Sketch on December 18, 1923 – Issue 1612.8.The Lost Mine*** Poirot tells Hastings the story of how he recovered the map of a lost mine after its owner was found murdered. Story first published in The Sketch on November 21, 1923 – Issue 1608.9.The Plymouth Express**** Poirot is asked by an American steel magnate to solve the murder of his daughter whose body was found on a train. Story first published asThe Mystery of the Plymouth Expressin The Sketch on April 4, 1923 – Issue 1575. The story was later expanded into the novelThe Mystery of the Blue Train(Hercule Poirot #6 1928).10.The Chocolate Box**** Poirot tells Hastings the story of his only failure, which occurred when he was with the Belgian police. Story first published asThe Clue of the Chocolate Boxin The Sketch on May 23, 1923 – Issue 1582.11.The Veiled Lady*** Poirot is asked to retrieve a compromising letter from the hands of a blackmailer. Story first published asThe Case of the Veiled Ladyin The Sketch on 3 October 3, 1923 – Issue 1601.12.The Submarine Plans*** The plans for a new submarine design for the Admiralty go missing at the home of a rising political star and Poirot is called in. Story first published in The Sketch on November 7, 1923 – Issue 1606. The story was later expanded into the novellaThe Incredible Theftpublished in the collectionMurder in the Mews(Hercule Poirot #16 1937).13.The Market Basing Mystery*** Poirot and Hastings join Japp on a vacation excursion to Market Basing when the local constable asks for their assistance with an apparent suicide which suspiciously looks like murder. Story first published in The Sketch on October 17, 1923 – Issue 1603. The story was later expanded into the novellaMurder in the Mewspublished in Woman’s Journal in December 1936 and then collected inMurder in the Mews(Hercule Poirot #16 1937).This short story collection counts against my Agatha Christie binge goal, so I now have 22 novels (if I include the 6 published under the pseudonymMary Westmacott), 6 short story collections, 16 theatrical plays and 1 autobiography left to go.Confusion for CompletistsAlthoughPoirot's Early Cases(1974) is listed as the 2nd last #43 in the GR Hercule Poirot series of novels & short stories, it collects short stories written in the 1920's and the concluding #44Curtain: Poirot's Last Case(published 1975) was actually written in the 1940s.Trivia and LinksAlmost all of the stories inPoirot's Early Caseswere adapted as 50-minute episodes for the TV seriesAgatha Christie's Poirot(1989-2013).
