"Pure Drivel" has assisted me in my current reading funny things adventure. If I had to put a name to these snippets, I'd categorize them as flash comedy, stealing the title from flash fiction. They are mini bursts of semi stand-up as should be expected from Steve Martin.The lower end rating of 2 stars is due to the hit or miss nature of these few page riffs.Nevertheless, there were some fall down laughing sections here. I highly recommend "Mars Probe Finds Kittens" both for the ridiculous premise AND the execution! When scientists studied these kittens in a test chamber, it was hilarious.P.23...6am: Kitten appears to sleep.7:02am: Kitten wakes, darts from one end of cage to another for no apparent reason.7:14am: Kitten runs up wall of cage, leaps onto another kitten for no apparent reason.7:22am: Kitten lies on back and punches another kitten for no apparent reason.7:30am: Kitten leaps, stops, days left, stops abruptly, climbs wall, clings for 2 seconds, falls on head, darts right for no apparent reason.7:51am: Kitten parses first sentence of lead editorial in daily newspaper, which is at the bottom of the chamber.Last behavior turns out to be an anomaly. YA THINK?As previously mentioned in my progress update, the following tidbit also grabbed my funny bone and wouldn't release it. "Times Roman Font Announces Shortage of Periods" It's the punctuation nerd's delight when Martin produces an entire piece with no periods til the end!First paragraph for your enjoyment... Then read the rest for yourself!P.30 "Representatives of the popular Times Roman font, who recently announced a shortage of periods, have offered other substitutes- inverted commas, exclamation marks, and semi-colons until the period crisis is able to be overcome by people such as yourself, who, through creative management of surplus punctuation, can perhaps allay the constant demand for periods, whose heavy usage in the last ten years, not only in English but in virtually every language in the world, is creating a burden on writers everywhere, thus generating a litany of comments such as: What the hell am I supposed to do without my periods? How am I going to write? Isn't this a terrible disaster? Are they crazy? Won't this just create misuse of other, less interesting punctuation???I'm harkening back to my recent noble attempt to enrich myself culturally by reading "Swann's Way" by Marcel Proust. Unfortunately, I didn't have the fortitude to continue spelunking for diamonds buried within page long passages no matter how beautiful and poetic the prose. Perhaps there was a shortage of periods back then as well??? Much admiration for those able to forge through Proust's entire "Remembrance of Time Past".I hear that Steve Martin has a new book out, so I'll try to get my hands on a copy!
