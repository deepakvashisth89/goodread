4.5 stars. An excellent collection of four short stories, Typhoon, Amy Foster, Falk and Tomorrow.

‘Typhoon’ is a story about a steamship and her crew experiencing a tempest in the China seas. Captain Tom MacWhirris a rigid, unimaginative leader who is inexperienced in dealing with typhoons. He is humane, ensuring the Chinese passengers small wages are fairly distributed after the storm.

‘Amy Foster’ is a tragic story of Yanko Goodall, a Polish immigrant, shipwrecked and sole survivor who finds himself in an isolated English village where he finds love with the simple, kind hearted Amy Foster.

‘Falk’ tells about Captain Falk, a tugboat captain who thinks he has a rival for a German’s young unmarried daughter. The rival is the narrator, and at first he does not know why Falk is treating the narrator as an enemy.

‘Tomorrow”, the shortest story, is a tragic tale of Captain Heghein, an old seaman living in a port town, consumed by the delusion that his son, Harry, will return home ‘tomorrow’, and marry Bessie, his carer.

I enjoyed all these realist stories probe the complexities of human nature and the human condition.

Here is a quote from the book:
“It was rather like a forced-on numbness of spirit. The long, long stress of a gale does it; the suspense of the interminably culminating catastrophe; and there is a bodily fatigue in the mere holding on to existence within the excessive tumult; a searching and insidious fatigue that penetrates deep into a man’s breast to cast down and sadden his heart, which is incorrigible, and of all the gifts of the earth - even before life itself - aspires to peace.”

This book was first published in 1903.
