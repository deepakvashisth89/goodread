I do not deny that the author has ideas to express, and perhaps even ideas worth expressing, but I deny that he has expressed them. Trow feels a sincere grief at the loss of American culture as something worth having, but once committed to paper, his ideas are irretrievable. His sentences tend toward comical vagueness, and together they are not an argument but a (visibly!) disorganized list of observations and laments. The spirit of these laments (clear from tone, diction and pace) I agree with, as things have gotten pretty bad. But his actual claims tend to be inscrutable: impossible to aver or deny.

That critics celebrate this book is proof that they shouldn't be counted on to spot even deliberate nonsense. They recognize the feelings that motivate the essay, but they validate those feelings by investing Trow's words with a set of claims, with contents, that they do not actually have.

I must also mention Trow's unabating italics - his way of making certain the reader pauses to the words. Pathetic.
