A couple of years ago I picked this boeok up at a used book store because I remember watching the movie as a kid. It is one that has a scene that has always stuck with me, so when I saw the book I grabbed it.
Sooner is a young girl who lives with a mean, moonshiner whom she calls Old Mam. Old Mam works the girl from dawn to dusk, switches her when she's displeased, barely feeds and clothes her, and doesn't send her to school. Sooner gets by because, honestly, she doesn't know any other life and she has a special relationship with a few woodland critters. Her pet bird is a redwinged blackbird who can't fly because of a broken wing. Sooner calls him simply Bird.
Mac is the local vet and him and his wife, Elizabeth, are going through a very rough time since finding out that Elizabeth can't have kids. When Mac shows up one day at Old Mam's place and witnesses her taking a switch to Sooner, he can't get the little girl off his mind. One thing leads to another until Sooner and her pets come to town to live with Mac and Elizabeth. Elizabeth is not happy about the arrangement, instead wanting to just wallow in her own unhappiness. When Sooner starts school, things are hard. She's a 9 year old country girl who can't read, write, or even spell her own name, and kids are mean when someone is different. One day the topic of Sooner's pet bird comes up and the kids want to see him. The scene ends in tragedy but also is the beginning of some eye-opening revelations on Elizabeth's part.

This book was all that I remembered the movie to be, though it's been a million years ago since I watched it. I loved little Sooner and despised Old Mam. There's abuse and death in this one. Not for the weak of heart. One thing that I was really surprised at is the fact that this is a young reader, but I am here to tell you that there are a couple of very adult scenes in this book that should not be in a young reader. Took me a little off guard. Keep that in mind if you hand this book to your pre-teens.
