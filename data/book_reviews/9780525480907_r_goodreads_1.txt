A very entertaining book, if you want to learn how Texans really talk -- or used to, back in the day. Maybe. One example, which I think I've heard used (once):
"If it harelips the governor" -- regardless of cost, full speed ahead! A genuine Texanism: (long "i" & accent on the genuine). Speaker was a Navy pal from North Texas, and I don't recall the context (early-70s). But I did learn recently that his first car was a 1928 Model A, that an Uncle helped him get running (around 1960?) -- and he just sold it! He'd had it in storage all these years in a disused garage, and a Dallas collector made him an offer he couldn't refuse.

And a classic, from memory, and likely grew in the telling. In the bar:
"BOY! You callin' me BOY? I got a yard of dick, a #2 washtub full of balls & enough hair on my ass to weave an Indian blanket. You're callin' me BOY?" (cut to fisticuffs)

I went to college in Houston and grew up in Oklahoma, which has a similar economy and culture. I recall hearing some of these when I lived there. Not the "hair on his ass" one!

Good luck on finding a copy. Out of print, and Amazon currently wants $50 & up for a used copy!
