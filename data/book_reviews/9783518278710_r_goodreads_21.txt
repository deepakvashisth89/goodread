Monumental, ground-breaking, confusing and conflicted at times, careful though ambitious. In short, a classic. John Rawls attempts the colossal undertaking of developing a theoretical framework to evaluate the justice of social institutions in a way that takes the autonomy of those involved as the first steps. There is a lot to be criticised. He is an American through and through, and his lack of political imagination shows at times. However, anyone who has an interest in the normative foundations of our societies, or even what grounds our duties to one another, shouldn't avoid this book. The received wisdom about A Theory of Justice is a pale shadow of this complex and earnest exploration of the "first virtue of social structures".
