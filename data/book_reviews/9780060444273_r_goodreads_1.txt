My one-star rating is based on the half dozen chapters of "The Art of Being Human" I had to read -- including the chapters on religion, morality, happiness and death -- and, based on those chapters, I can't tell you how relieved I am I wasn't assigned to read more. The book is simplistic, prone to cliches, has the occasional factual error, and uses terrible examples to make its points -- examples chosen, I suspect, not because they were the best but because they were the most recent, giving the authors an excuse to update their book and force students to buy a new edition instead of used copies.

As just one example, in a chapter on romantic love, authors Richard Janaro and Thelma Altshuler illustrated a point with the movie "No Reservations." Doesn't sound familiar? That's because saw it, and those who did don't remember it. It's a shame there have been only of other romantic comedies made over the history of film-making, many of which people have and , or the authors wouldn't have had to use some shitty movie that even Catherine Zeta-Jones and Aaron Eckhart have forgotten they were in.

If you're in a course that uses this textbook, consider dropping the class. Seriously.
