This buddy read was supposed to go quickly, but life had other plans. I know that whenever I see my copy of this book in my bookcase I will always associate it with the many events that happened after Mr. James and I started page one. Broken wings, new beginnings, unexpected endings, leaky roofs and more: life kept us both busy but we have finally managed to get ourselves, Omri and Little Bear through to the end of this first adventure in the series.

Since the book was not published until 1980 I never read it as a youngster, but I had vague memories of the movie, so I was slightly familiar with the basic plot.

The whole idea is captivating when you first think of it. Having plastic toys come to life is something kids were dreaming of even before the book was published. But of course without the cabinet and that mysterious key, we could only use our imaginations.

Omri was thrilled with his new 'toy' at first, but he soon grew up in many ways, learning to think of someone besides himself and beginning to understand the consequences of rash decisions. That is something I wish more people (especially politicians) could learn. Maybe the world wouldn't be in such a mess right now if.....well, never mind, I digress.

I enjoyed the story, except I did worry about Little Bear and Boone being carried around inside the pockets of Omri's and best friend Patrick's jeans. I still don't see how they would have kept from being completely squished or suffocated while in transit, whether they were plastic at the time or real.

Thanks to Mr. James for all the chats we've had about this book, squeezed in among all the chats we've had about life and the world in general since way back on page one. I couldn't have gotten to the end without you, buddy!
