We have a family friend who was a crewman aboard the USS Nautilus. He was one the very few (about 105 sailors and one civilian) who first crossed the North Pole under the Arctic Ocean ice. He knew a number of the men who were lost on the Thresher. So, when I came across this book, I had to buy it. I wasn't disappointed.The Silent Service is inherently dangerous. This book, despite the beliefs of many, demonstrates the truth of that. It is a technical and and human read, discussing the men and officers aboard, their families, and the submariner community. It also skims over other nuclear submarine disasters and accidents.The one great annoyance is the repeated comment that the Thresher is the worst submarine disaster in history. It is not. The French submarine Surcouf was lost with 130 souls, one more than the Thresher.Additional issues are the sporadically poor editing, such as referring to submariners and 'submarines.'Nevertheless, if you have an interest in submarines, naval disasters, or the US Navy, this will likely interest you.
