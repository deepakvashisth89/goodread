Years ago, I saw an absolutely stunning book cover. I have never been so drawn to a cover before, and I knew that I HAD to have it.

That was this book. A simply gorgeous 1898 edition.

For Harvard’s Masterpieces of World Literature, this book is covered. It is one of the original examples of a frame story. The Sultan discovers that his beloved wife is cheating on him. After he doles out the necessary punishment, The Sultan is in the market for a new wife. Every night he marries a new woman, only to end her the next morning. After this goes on for a while, Scheherazade volunteers to become the next wife. She plans to tell The Sultan a captivating story every night, so he has to keep her around to hear the rest of it.

So essentially this is a series of short stories.

And short stories are some of the most difficult to pull off, because the author only has an incredibly short period of time to hook the reader. As such, these stories were a bit lacking in character development.

And this translation…..it is very odd, because it never returns to the initial story. That’s right. We never return to The Sultan and Scheherazade.
