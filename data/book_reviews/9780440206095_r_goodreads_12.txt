I lingered between 4 & 5 stars because it's not that I don't have issues with this book. However, most of those issues are more cultural in nature than anything. You really have to try hard to put yourself into a world with an almost completely different set of moral standards and beliefs. And remember that RL, at the time, would have likely been even harsher than what Joyce shows us. Bottom line is, this is a page turner and I felt the love between Rolfe and Ciedre. A five* it will have to be (although if GR would let me, I'd remove a half star for Alice's sake).This book has been reviewed many, many times so I'm not going to give a synopsis. I will ramble some about my misconceptions though.I'm a fan of the bodice ripper, but had actually sworn off ever reading this classic of the genre due to one major plot point.CHEATING! I absolutely hate cheating.Yes, Rolfe marries Ciedre's legitimate sister to secure power. He doesn't want her though and he's not exactly bed-hopping the way I assumed he would be from the reviews. It was a while before he ever consummated the marriage and the few times he does sleep with Alice are all about Ciedre (he must try and forget her, he's horny for her, he's angry at her, etc).Poor Alice though, right? In a way, yes. She didn't love Rolfe, but still I'm sure it's humiliating to know he's screwing her illegitimate half-sister. However, she was not a nice person and also made many of her own troubles.She'd HATED Ciedre all her life out of jealousy (I think Daddy loved her more even though she was a bastard. Plus, Ciedre was incredibly beautiful). From the very beginning Alice sets off on the wrong foot with Rolfe by insulting Ciedre's birth and taking every opportunity to be cruel to her. If there was one thing Rolfe couldn't stand it was someone mistreating Ciedre... aside from himself :PAnyway, if Alice had concentrated on being less of a vindictive b, her story would have turned out differently and 'who knows' maybe Rolfe would have even kept his vow to leave Ciedre alone. Regardless, her eventual banishment to a convent where she committed suicide was sad and seemed very unfair. Yes, she'd defied Rolfe one too many times by trying to maim, hurt, kill Ciedre. And an unwanted wife's banishment to a convent was probably fairly common. Still, it was sad and I'd remove 1/2 a star for it if I could. I know it's an overused plot device, but I kinda wish they'd killed her off in a different way. However, that fate had to be saved for Ciedre's husband...Ciedre was married to Rolfe's best man, Guy. It was neither's choice. Rolfe ordered it to put Ciedre in a position where she'd be watched and wouldn't get herself into trouble (she was always spying for her brothers). Plus he knew Guy was the type of man who would be good to her. Rolfe worried A LOT about her doing something that she'd have to be punished harshly/killed for and he'd have no way to prevent it. Anyway, Ciedre and Guy never consummated their marriage. He was very good to her, but he thought she was a witch (every once in a while she had a lazy eye that made its appearance and freaked out the townfolk). Anyway, Guy was more than happy to take the land Rolfe gave him as dowry and continue sleeping with all the kitchen maids. He did get killed in the final battle with Ciedre's brothers, however, which was obviously for convenience.Anyway, if you want a book that contains many of the same plot points but doesn't "go there" when it comes to the more unpleasant aspects of medieval life, check outMy Lord Conquerorby Samantha James. I actually liked the book, but the author definitely did pull her punches. The H is a Norman Conqueror, the h is the beautiful outcast by-blow of the dead Saxon Lord who everyone believes to be a witch. Her legit sister is even a vindictive, plotting b. However, the H never even considers marrying the legit sister. He just allows her to retain her position and live in the keep like she always has. Now that I think about it, that doesn't make a whole lot of political sense. BUT that and the fact he performs more of a forced seduction and less of a violent rape on the h is less likely to piss off/disturb readers. Some people just don't want such ugly things in their romance and I can dig it. To each his own.
