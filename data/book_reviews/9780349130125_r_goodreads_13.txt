Another tale about obsession and compulsion, in this case a loner millionaire's crazed sleuthing on the trail of a woman he believes has been murdered by a man he witnesses die in a Post Office. A cache of letters fuels his investigations, with the case leading Luke Wakefield into relationships that he might otherwise have shunned.Rubens has barbed humour but it's another shabby view on the human condition. These novels read like a grottier version of the cool chromium wipe-clean surfaces of Muriel Spark. In Rubens world, we may be living in a vale of tears, but most of the time have deserved it.The ending adds more surprise. It's certainly inventive and perhaps a more surprising place to find Peter Capaldi, who starred alongside 'Last of the Summer Wine's' Pam Ferris in a TV adaptation in 1992. It's sufficiently outside the run of the usual to explain the TV treatment, with the book itself worth a go so long as you're not looking for a heartwarming escape.
