An absolutely fascinating read. Truly and utterly fascinating. I wondered why there wasn't any better description of this book anywhere, and now I know that there simply is no better way to describe it. Wholly unique.EDIT: The best way I can describe this book is the following: regularly, I was able to imagine this work as a tv event series, but not just any event series. It has the precise flavor of the mid 2000s Sy-Fy network fairy tale adaptations (The Alice, Wizard of Oz, and Peter Pan ones of note). I mean this not insultingly, for while those shows (with the exception of the two part "Neverland") are not good by any means, they have the right je ne sais quoi to match Rumors of Spring in this perfectly, especially with the blend of fantastical and sci-fi eco-futurism elements present in the novel.
