A deeply, deeply problematic book, "Love's Wicked Ways" was written under a female pseudonym by the late science fiction and erotica writer George H. Smith, and the author's experience shows. Clearly Smith was cashing in on the exploding market for historical romance. The novel tells the alleged love story of Lorinda and Kirk, a Mississippi belle and a Maine (by way of Texas) riverboat gambler, whose destinies collide in the 1850s Gulf South. Fate conspires to bring Lorinda low and thus begins a lurid bodice ripper that tries to figure out just how many ways it can degrade and abuse its heroine. If it can be done to Lorinda, it's going to be done to her. Fortunately, the author leaves the worst of it to the imagination. The racial politics are unbelievable, even by 1970s standards--and yet, sadly, despite its excesses, the terrible things described in this novel did happen to enslaved people and happened often. Consensual sex, pseudo-consensual sex, perverse sex, rape, voodoo, violence: this is one big, albeit readable, pulpy mess.
