On Liberty is the first philosophy book I ever read, and made a huge impact on my ~18 year old self. What seems like a simple argument became more nuanced and problematic after each reading, though, and it's only after reading it in conjunction with Utilitarianism that I was able to come to terms and resolve (to my own satisfaction, anyway), its ambiguities. The Subjection of Women is also a very fine text. If you're familiar with On Liberty and Utilitarianism, the arguments therein shouldn't come as much of a surprise, but it's absolutely worth reading as it is the most empirical of the three texts. Utilitarianism is straight-up abstraction. On Liberty is mostly abstract, with some examples. Subjection of Women is Mill (and almost certainly Harriet Taylor Mill) holding you by the hand and trying to show how the actual condition of women in Victorian England is tantamount to slavery, etc.Finally, this edition of the book contains very valuable commentary in the endnotes. Miller's contribution really isn't trivial, and it helped me appreciate the text even more.
