I read this book not long ago. The title was so evocative and calming that I had to give it a go, but I remember reading it too quickly and not doing it justice.I found an edition with illustrations by Arthur Rackham, and I thought I could give it another chance. It seems this was one of Rackham's final illustrated books. I find it amusing that people could make a living illustrating children's books. I guess that's something quite difficult nowadays, although it probably wasn't easy back then either.I read it soon after The Hobbit, and even though they are almost thirty years apart, I found in both a similar feeling of timeless literature.The story is quite simple, but the characters are well characterised, each with a distinct personality that contrasts nicely with the others. The feeling evoked by the title continues throughout the book, with its descriptions of a countryside that feels charming and comfortable. More than the plot itself, what stayed with me was the atmosphere.
