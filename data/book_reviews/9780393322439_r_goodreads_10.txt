Something was constantly off about Highsmith’s description of the daily life of an 18 year and his family in what I take to be Missouri in 1983. She was getting old by then so probably was a bit out of touch with what kids were like. Something was also off about her depiction of a few of the other families. And there was a lot of anachronistic smoking and drinking. But what she got dead to rights were the people who knock on the door, creeping around with their “literature”, their innuendo, and their holier-than-thou attitude. Boy, is it horrible what that movement would eventually morph into: our paranoid state of hatred and lies. Our Orange Jesus.The book was more fantasy than realistic. It was suspenseful though. On that Highsmith delivered as usual.
