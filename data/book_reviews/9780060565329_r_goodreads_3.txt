I started this somewhat reluctantly, being interested in the notorious Mitford family and thinking that Diana was going to be the most boring of them all, and assumed De Courcy was going to write a lightweight, gossipy book. It turned out to be a heavyweight book about the history of the British fascist movement, the intellectual life and society of the period, and of course the Mitfords. It's a Pulitzer Prize-level work in its scope and thoroughness. It's also plain to me that De Courcy has been squandering her talents by writing about debutantes and husband-hunting when she had the ability to be a more profound writer.

De Courcy portrays Diana as something of a hollow vessel who was Mosley's doormat, and other reviewers have complained that they don't get a sense of what Diana was like from this work. Diana was very willful, but there didn't seem to be much else there. She was not a complex personality. Oswald Mosley eventually admitted that he had squandered his life, but Diana never faced up to that realization. It's a very good work about a woman who ruined her life by being attracted to political extremism and who became mentally imprisoned by it. The book is a fascinating examination about the UK during this time period.
