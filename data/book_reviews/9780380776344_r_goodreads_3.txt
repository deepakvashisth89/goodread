I worried a bit that this one would feel dated, since it was first published about 20 years ago, but I didn't miss cell phones or GPSs at all when reading it.
Eagle does an excellent job of balancing the present day (20 years ago) story with the story of the past. I love reading books where there's a past mystery embedded in the present story, but so often one story overshadows the other, and the reader is left frustrated when bouncing between the two. In this one, I enjoyed each story equally and was never sorry when it was time to move from one time period to the other.
I was a bit nervous that this story would be depressing, since it seems obvious that the story from the past - a romance between an asylum patient and nurse - was unlikely to have a happy ending. However, I didn't find it so - both stories are covered with a layer of sadness that must be part of most American Indian stories, considering the past & present treatment of the people, but I was satisfied with the ending in both stories. This is one of my favorite reads of the year.
