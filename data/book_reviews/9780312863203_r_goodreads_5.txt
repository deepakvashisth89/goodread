Maybe a 3.7 really, but I went with 4 because it was a smooth, enjoyable read.I don't know whether Dedman studied Japanese martial mythology and then decided to write a book, or the other way around. Either way, he stays only just on the good side of Too Much Information in that regard. Occasionally the use of the supernatural things seemed forced.It's your basic "protagonist discovers he/she has some powers" story, and, as with too many of them, it becomes clear quite early thatour hero is going to end up going 1-on-1 with the main bad guy, and winning.But hey, there are only so many plots out there on the bookverse.Credit Dedmon for the occasional revelation of another layer of the plot, in several "oh yeah, I should have seen that" pieces.I think the bakemono doesn't stand up to logical analysis, but that's not Dedman's problem. He didn't invent it.Will I read #2? I dunno. Probably if I come across it, but I won't look for it. With Mage having developed so far in this book, one wonders what's left for him. Maybe one has to read #2 to find out.
