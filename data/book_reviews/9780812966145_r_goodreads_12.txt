2.5 stars rounded to 3, just for its influence on the genre and perhaps changes in the Royal Navy pre-WWI. Written in 1903, the novel definitely points out vulnerabilities in the British naval defense that with changes that subsequently occurred helped the defense of England during WWI. This was not the espionage novel I was looking for, waaay too slow ( it didn't help that I have been sick and every time I picked up the book, my eyes began to close!), filled with lots of sailing jargon, and guesswork on the part of the two main characters that seemed to meander just as their sailing exploits did.

My edition had a wonderful introduction by the author's Great-grandson, that highlighted the amazing life and influence of Childers. It was the best part of the book for me. If you are a sailing enthusiast this novel may be right down your alley.
