When I first readThe Republicmany years ago I read this in parallel. And I did the same this time. And it is probably fair to say that my view on Plato was heavily influenced by Popper.For Popper Plato was the father of Totalitarianism and an enemy of what he called the Open Society. Actually it is not necessary to have Popper as an expert to see that the ideal state of Plato is much closer to the state of Orwell’s 1984 than to a society that really aims for the bliss of regular people. But he does give some perspective. Especially interesting are the quotes from Pericles who at least compared to Plato is an early proponent of the Open Society (even criticising to some extent the practice of slavery).I read the German translation by the greatPaul Feyerabend. Which is very good of course, but one can see starting with the title that translations are always problematic. The spell of Plato is rendered as theZauber Platons. The correct translation, but it seems to me that spell has negative connotations that are missing inZauber. Zauber is maybe closer to enchantment.Popper himself mentions that translating Πολιτεία as Republic is already misleading. You will automatically think of a positive form of government. Something you would not do with the more neutral (or even negative?) State.I was interested in the philosophy of science and that is why I started reading Popper. And I always felt he was the very model of a great philosopher (this side of the true geniuses like Wittgenstein). And I still do (ignoring his late philosophy).But maybe one should not judge a man by the books he writes. I once met a guy who was a student of Popper and he told me that the famous book was always calledThe open Society by one of its Enemiesby the people who knew him.
