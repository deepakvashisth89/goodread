Before reading, I was certain this book wouldn’t captivate me that much, mostly anticipating an average read. That suspicion proved largely accurate, though there were moments where the author’s cleverness and craft really piqued my interest.

It’s a classic murder mystery, of course. Though it is by no means a great book to me, I think it is impressively executed for a debut. For the most part, it was fairly entertaining and adopted an incisive, well-structured approach. The story, told through a restrained and unadorned prose, was easy to follow—an essential quality for a mystery. Despite these admirable qualities, they are often impaired by the story being told solely from Hastings’ perspective, the sidekick.

I get it, the author deliberately chose a different approach. And by doing so, we experience the story through the lens of someone less intelligent and less psychologically perceptive. We are kept in the dark while Poirot, the investigator, gathers information, creating tension and anticipation for the final revelation. However, it is precisely because we are the secondary figure, not privy to Poirot’s analytical skills and conclusions until the final chapters, that the narrative occasionally feels dull.

I’m unsure whether I was meant to deduce the murderer alongside Hastings and Poirot. Given Poirot’s constant admonitions that Hastings should use his mind to organise evidence logically, I feel I should have also been more engaged. But I read for enjoyment, not to play detective. And if the only means to enjoyment is playing detective, I’d have to be pedantic… which I’m not.

This is literally Hastings’ line, which I realised, would have worked just as well if it came from me.

Still, I found much to enjoy in this story. The final revelation owes itself entirely to Poirot’s keen alertness and incisive analytical mind. It is a testament to Agatha Christie’s intellect and narrative craft. Though this book may not be a groundbreaking read, I am aware that finer instalments await, and I look forward to discovering them.
