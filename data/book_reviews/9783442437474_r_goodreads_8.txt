An enjoyable book with a colorful cast of characters. It’s a satire of the publishing industry.

Frensic has long given up on his dream of writing the Great Novel. Now, he finds success as the literary agent of commercial authors.

Every book he handles emerges from the process of rewriting His partner, Sonia Futtle, handles the foreign publishers.

One fateful day, Frensic receives the manuscript for . It’s a dirty book.

The book is similar to Harold and Maude. It recounts the love affair between a 17-year old boy and an 80-something year old geriatric. Frensic and Futtle have been handed a gold mine.

The problem? The author insists on staying anonymous and the only way Hutchmeyer (the American publisher) will agree to a contract is if the author goes on a U.S. tour.

So, Frensic and Futtle pass off Peter Piper as the author of . Piper is a self-proclaimed genius-in-the-waiting who has slaved for years to get his memoir, , published. Frensic wines and dines Piper and humors him all these years. He has a soft spot for Piper because he also once aspired to literary fame. But, Frensic secretly thinks Piper’s book is derivative trash and will never see the light of day. By passing Piper off as the author of , Piper will finally get his recognition and will be published as part of ’s contract deal.

But there’s another hiccup, British publisher Cadwalladine and American publisher Hutchmeyer are made to believe that Piper is the genuine writer of . And…once Piper reads , he despises it. Piper is full of regret, but on the upside he’ll get published and he’s going on the U.S. tour with Futtle, whom he’s been harboring intense feelings for.

Will Piper keep up the act or will he spill the beans? So many hijinks ensue. It’s over the top and hilarious. We’re introduced to Baby Hutchmeyer, the wife of the American publisher, and she is my favorite. Her husband is verbally abusive and cheated on her for years and she’s finally had enough. Piper is the catalyst to finally taking life in her own hands. And, boy, does she.

I felt like the story went a little too hay-wire, but it’s a worthwhile read. This is my first time reading Tom Sharpe and I look forward to his other books, especially which is a satire of South Africa’s apartheid, the publication of which got him booted out of the country.
