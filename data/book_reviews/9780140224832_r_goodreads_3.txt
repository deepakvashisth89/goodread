I read the edition from the 70s, so it was of course radically outdated. I am curious the changes between that and the 90s edition. (Also curious how some people found such an old book, there are reviews here from the 2010s. Mine was owned by an elderly relative)The most useful thing I got from it was the idea of 'Social Techniques', breaking social interaction down into 'techniques' and using the ones that might be most appropriate for the given situation. However, the book doesn't really go into sufficient detail on this and when is best to use a given technique.Big sections are dedicated to specialist topics like interpersonal behaviour and mental conditions (outdated ideas of mental conditions), or interpersonal behaviour in different occupations. Not enough detail is given for those topics for someone interested, and they distract from the topic of interpersonal behaviour in daily life. In general the book is very vague and does not give much practical or useful information. Nor does it provide an over-arching theory or even a summation of knowledge on the topic. It is also very light on experiments. Sometimes they are referenced but far to often the author uses language like 'it is probably the case that...' or 'likely this is because...'Overall, it is not very useful but was more interesting than I thought it would be.
