Rostnikov would never be more than a chief inspector in the MVD, a position higher than might be expected of him considering his inability to control his tongue, his frequent impetuousness, and his politically hazardous Jewish wife—a wife who had no interest at all in either religion or politics. Fortunately, Rostnikov had no ambition; he was politically uninterested. His job was to catch criminals and occasionally punish them at the moment of capture."There is a lot of “police work” in this thriller and some time spent giving us a more complete picture of both Rostnikov and Karpo. Yet the narrative moves along in a satisfactory way and the twisting threads lead to some surprises before everything is pulled together.Very much of its era, Kaminsky obviously enjoys playing with those tropes.3.5*
