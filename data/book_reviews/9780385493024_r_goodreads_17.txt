Oof. More detailed review to come, probably? Probably a 4.5 but I’m rounding up because I learned so much and I thought the prose was so lovely. Also I just can’t with all the negative reviews, lol. The critique that it’s focused on a specific demographic of mothers feels fair—she acknowledges that her focus is on the experiences of middle class women, broadly defined, and the interviews seem to heavily draw on her social network of highly educated middle class women in heterosexual marriages. But I think she’s pretty upfront about who she’s talking about, and she acknowledges that the experiences of working-class mothers, lesbian and single mothers, etc. are complex enough to warrant their own books rather than a chapter shoehorned into her book. Like, I totally hear the criticism and as a lesbian who is considering single parenthood I would love to read a book specifically focused on that experience. But not every book has to be all things to all people? It’s okay for a book to look at a slice of the population, and you can go and read other books for other groups (I liked Choosing Single Parenthood: The Thinking Woman’s Guide, for one). I still really enjoyed this for what it was, and I also feel like it did give me greater empathy for the experiences of mothers in heterosexual relationships, where there are all these complicated and exhausting gender dynamics that have to be continually negotiated and renegotiated.
