There are 4 requirements every folktale needs to fulfill to be able to be called a folktale. The first aspect of a folktale is its author. Folktales never have a specific author; instead they are passed on from generation to generation. The People Could Fly was passed on until Virginia Hamilton decided to write it down, she says so on the background of page 63 “… these people nurtured a strong sense of tradition, passing stories from generation to generation. Virginia Hamilton (1934-2002) grew up listening to such stories. As an adult she put many of them into writing…” Also on the first line on page 63 it says, “They say the people could fly,” proving that there were many others. The next requirement is that a folktale should be focused, or based on a specific culture. The People Could Fly specifically refers to the African American culture and even more detailed, slaves in southern USA. On page 63 it directly tells us what we want to hear, “ Between the 1600s and 1800s, millions of Africans were taken forcibly to the Americas as enslaved people.”The third aspect of a folktale is that it includes a supernatural power or force. In The People Could Fly, as the title says, the free slaves rose up and flew away. As it says on page 65 “And he flew away." Last but not least, the last requirement for a folktale is that it has to teach a lesson. The People Could Fly has a very abstract theme, what I found after digging very deeply was the following. You have to be patient and have hope because there is a time for everything. I drew this conclusion because on page 64 it says, “I must go soon, she told him. Soon, he said.”

I totally recommend this folktale because it is interesting and it reflects a lot of its culture. I really liked how it narrates the story or what is happening.
