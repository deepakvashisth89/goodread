Two short Betty Neels novellas. They are very much, well, Betty Neels novels - in both cases a tall, beautiful, obliging young woman trapped in a dead-end job (or recently fired from same) ends up coming to the attention of a large, avuncular Dutch surgeon who makes it his personal mission to fix her life, in part by providing her with employment in which she can mother his ignored goddaughter (and serve as explicit contrast to the child's beautiful but selfish mother) and proposes to her at the end of the story. It was delightful and exactly what I wanted to be reading. Despite being written in the nineties, they both have a timeless quality - I think it helps that Neels wisely chose to make neither heroine a nurse in this one, meaning that her more ridiculous "this isn't how things worked in the nineties" moments less glaring.
