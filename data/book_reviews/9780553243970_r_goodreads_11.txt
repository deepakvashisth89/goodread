‘Anne of Windy Willows’ (or ‘Windy Poplars,’ as it’s often styled) was published in 1936, twenty-eight years after ‘Green Gables ‘(1908) and feels quite out of step with its companions. Apparently it was written to please her publishers and fans after the success of the original series, therefore our beloved Avonlea characters play little part in this crowd-pleaser.The novel’s uncanniness arises from a dreamlike quality in that it’s so completely removed from Avonlea in every way that it feels more of an exercise in filling in time. The principal character is - but isn’t - Anne, and the scenarios and individuals that she describes in her letters to a disembodied Gilbert often feel distractingly modern.Nevertheless, there’s a very beguiling maturity to Montgomery’s writing and characterisation here, alongside the warmth and descriptive beauty that her work has always displayed. An intriguing addition to the Anne canon.
