I enjoyed Fred Mustard Stewart's intercontinental saga, "Ellis Island", which takes place in New York City and Ellis Island in the early 1900s. The novel spans various genres such as romance, literary drama, historical fiction, and some scenes even fall into the slapstick humor category. The story follows five different immigrants (one from Russia, one from Italy, one from Bohemia, and two from Ireland) who fled their respective home countries to pursue the American dream.

They all arrive at the legendary immigration station at Ellis Island and later their lives veer off in different directions; one becomes a famous pianist and songwriter on Broadway, one works in the coal mines of Appalachia, and one goes into the trucking industry and later becomes a star politician. Through it all, we catch interesting glimpses of the popularity of the times: of vaudeville, of ragtime, the days of early cinema, the rising workers' unions, the Harlem renaissance, and World War I. We also learn about the hardships, struggles, and discrimination of being a new immigrant in America.

Two of the main male characters weren't likeable because of their sexist, womanizer/player personalities. I wish the main female characters were stronger and not so stereotypical. Unfortunately, part of the problem was that they were repressed/oppressed in the times they were living in and some of them were used and manipulated by their male counterparts.
