This was exceptionally well researched. I wanted a steady, almost methodical medieval read that actually felt realistic and like I might learn something. For this, Ms Gellis delivered. Lots of effort clearly went into depicting the actual political intrigue and violence in this period in England. I love how she captured how gritty and high stakes even ordinary days could be in this period, and yet had beautiful passages of angelic landscapes.It’s just a shame that I found the romance pretty dull. It started off as a great premise. Big strong MMC knight with much to prove protects FMC with 10th century version of a TikTok tarot girly (ie weaves prophetic tapestries). He fights off suitors, they deny their attraction because of circumstance, and then they engage in a secret affair that feels like it was directly inspired by Burton’s The Meeting on the Turret Stairs!!And yet, the whole time I was reading it I felt like I was watching the two quirky kids in high school get together. They were just both a bit…odd. And I didn’t totally care. It was like seeing a post on FB that Jane and Joe from high school finally got together and you’re like…Cool, I guess! Like I get the whole point is that they’re both social outcasts in their own way (FMC because she’s got witchy talents and is socially peculiar and MMC because he’s got no family/land and is ugly). But I didn’t find either of them particularly compelling people for a romance (tbf maybe it was because they simply weren’t enemies to lovers). It just wasn’t for me but maybe it’ll be for you!Also, Ms Gellis, PLEASE I beg of you, trim down your interior reflections. How do I say this? Ms Gellis has a habit of writing in text what should otherwise be a footnote. For example: “Audris came into the room smiling because she knew Hugh was there. Hugh saw Audris smiling and, because XYZ long ass reason, thought Audris was only smiling because it was just a pretty day.” All the “XYZ” long ass reasons would (in scholarly writing) be footnotes because they’re somewhat useful context, but not essential to the point. One or two of these side by side comparisons of two characters’ different interior reflection on an event/action is fine and even useful! But almost EVERY interaction between the two MMCs (and most other characters) has this. I never thought I’d ask an author for LESS character interiority. But it just bogs down any dialogue or action flow.
