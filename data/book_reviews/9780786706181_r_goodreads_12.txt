My second read of this book. It took a bit longer to read due to work and circumstances than it deserved.G M-F uses an unusual style to tell this tale, using multiple witnesses to the rise and fall of Tom Molineaux, freed black American slave. Like another black American boxer, Tom's ready mouth and boasting did not endear him to the British public in the way he felt was his due.Molineaux's natural talent as a boxer was cultivated and developed once he arrived in England, where bare knuckle fighting was all the rage, albeit actually illegal. However, women and drink were his downfall and he died young in sad circumstances.The witnesses speak in their own voices, and often with heavy accents, and use the cant of their class and their time. Also reflective of their time is their attitude towards the black American.The book is worth the read not just for the high quality of the writing and the entertaining story, but to grasp the time and feel of Regency England.
