I haven't yet decided what I think about this book. I enjoyed it, but it didn't blow me away. It also read very quickly. I finished it in 3 days, which is fast for me. The hook of the book is that 15yo Jack is told by his father that the reason his parents divorced is because his father is gay, and is now living with his boyfriend. This sends Jack's world into a tailspin. This subject matter was very edgy when this book was published in 1989, but doesn't seem to hit the same way today.Jack is a likeable character and he sprinkles his observations with some humor, although I never thought this book was laugh out loud funny. I think this is very much a book of its time. It feels like one of those "issue" movies that network TV used to run with an air of self importance. The difference is that this book does not come across as self important. In fact, it avoids saccharine sentimentality about found family. It easily could have gone that way, but Homes did not choose to do that.Overall, this book is fine. I would suggest it as a cultural artifact, but don't be surprised if it feels a bit dated.
