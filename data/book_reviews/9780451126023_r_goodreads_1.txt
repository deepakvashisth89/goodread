I’d classify this as a non-romantic bodice ripper. There’s plenty of violence, bloodspray, & torn clothes, but the love story—or what passes for a love story—is buried under a lengthy exploration of 1890s Chinese history & communication breakdowns between two very different cultures, all of which culminates in the Boxer rebellion. And herein lies the problem, because this historical period holds little interest for yours truly; I learned some things, yes, but the political discussions & lengthy asides made this a herky-jerky read. When things were happening, it was enjoyable—e.g. Constance’s two visits to Port Arthur were intense in very different ways—but whenever the action or interpersonal conflict slowed down it was a slog.…But perhaps that’s due to personal preference. If you’re a fan of novels about historical China the expositional sections might not be an issue. In technical terms this is well-written; the descriptions are vivid, the characters are dimensional, & there’s such tragic poignancy in Constance’s internal conflict between what she knows, what is expected of her, & what she’s drawn to at various points in her life. It’s an interesting story overall, but it does drag at times, & I really had to push myself through the duller portions. So…yes, a classic 3-star experience. I still want to read the sequel about Adela, though, so there’s that. *shrug*Be forewarned of some un-PC language, mostly due to the period setting.
