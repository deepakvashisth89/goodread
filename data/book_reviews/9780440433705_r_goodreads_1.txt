3.5 stars, rounding up. I found it caught me up very quickly into the story - Mrs. Palmer is a larger (tee hee!) than life psychic medium who resembles an elephant when wearing a grey mac, a widow with seven children and bells, feathers and pieces of wood hidden under capacious gowns of crushed velvet for her totally-legit-but-perhaps-with-a-little-embellishment seances
Take aways from this are: 1. Don't go into graveyards at night, calling up the dead. 2. If you absolutely insist on calling up the dead, you'd better have a good reason that suits both parties. And 3. If you call up a lonely ghost who calls you its friend, perhaps consider treating it like one. It was (is?) a person, too - kindness usually goes a long way in this life, who's to say it wouldn't also in another phase of reality?! 4. Not all men lurking about graveyards at night wearing capes are perverts and flashers (Cassie's brother Tom's pat explanation of Deverill was delightful!).

Also, it seems irresponsible, to say the least, to have seven children, just so you can have a 'seventh child of a seventh child'.
Mrs. Palmer is a terrible mother. But a hilarious caricature. I think anyone with a Tiger Mom who has the child's life planned out for them before they are even conceived would recognise her faults. You might even feel hurt, especially if you're one of those other useless children. ;) Cassie, of course, gets a double-helping of crap from the self-absorbed parent, whereas the other kids just get neglect. Cassie was already 'haunted' well before she called up Deverill - haunted by her mother's expectations, a lack of autonomy, and doomed to a life she doesn't want. Fun stuff.

That said, light-hearted stuff. Though as an adult, I felt sorry for Deverill the whole time, and the discovery of his past just made it worse. A bit depressing, but if I had read this at the intended 8 - 12 years of age, I probably woudn't have noticed a thing. ;)
