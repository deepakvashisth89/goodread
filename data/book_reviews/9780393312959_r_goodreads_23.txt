I started reading this book at the outset of a role change to "operations analyst". Mine is the task to think and present new ways of organisation - and I thank John Gardner for providing a timeless, compelling frame with which to attack the problem. Moving beyond my context though, the topic of innovation and renewal is relevant for any one aspiring to global citizenship, and essential reading for leadership. Some quotes that will spark reflection for a long time:The role of technology (worried your job will be automated?): "we must discover how to design organisations and technological systems in such a way that individual talents are used to the maximum and dignity preserved"... "Knowledge will be a safe weapon only if it is linked to a deeply rooted conviction that organisations are made for men and not men for organisations".The gap between processed and raw data: "every top executive should periodically emerge from his world of abstractions and take a long unflinching look at unprocessed reality; every general should spend time on the front lines; every research administrator should spend some time in the laboratory; every sales manager should call on customers; every politician should ring doorbells".I've only shared 2 of 5 bookmarked. Truly a mighty return from such a short book.
