In this 1986 novel, Highsmith portrays the 1980’s Manhattan artsy/yuppie cultural scene through the central characters of Manhattan artsy/yuppie couple Jack and Natalia Sutherland. Jack is an artist who makes drawings that accompany books. Natalia works in an art studio. Their young daughter attends a private school with door-to-door pick-up and delivery service.The dramatic catalyst occurs when Jack’s wallet is found in the street by Ralph Linderman, a 50+ year old male security guard. Ralph returns the wallet to Jack and his obsessive and twisted views on life have him inserting himself in Jack’s life. When Jack observes Ralph annoying a coffeehouse waitress named Elsie, an extremely beautiful and captivating young 19-year-old, Jack soon finds himself bonding with Elsie over their mutual annoyance with the pesty Ralph who insists on acting as her guardian angel to protect her from people who might lead her astray. Elsie then turns to both Jack and Natalia for general life guidance. They both become extremely fond of Elsie as they help shepherd her through making a living in the more glamorous side of Manhattan life.Those are the four main characters: Jack, Natalia, Ralph and Elsie. The first 3/4 of the novel shows the development of their interactions. Most of the chapters are third person through Jack’s eyes so I identified with him and viewed the story through how it affected him. Natalia is seen only through Jack. Other chapters are from the point of view of Ralph, and we view his thoughts, increasing creepiness and perception of a truth based more on his beliefs than on facts. A very timely portrayalThrough this part of the book, Highsmith’s writing and character development are as outstanding as usual. I especially enjoyed Highsmith’s portrait of artsy/yuppie 1980s Manhattan life as seen through Jack and Natalia’s non-conventional marital relationship. However, with the advent of the unexpected climactic ‘thriller’ events that come in the last quarter of the novel, things went awry for me. While I normally welcome unexpected events, these were either disappointing or did not ring true and were accompanied by character developments that also were either disappointing or did not ring true. Thus, while I appreciated the early characterization and setting depictions, I felt dissatisfied when I finished the novel.I was going to rate this as 3 stars as it is clearly a level below other Highsmith novels I ranked as 4 stars. Yet, I liked it more than This Sweet Sickness, which I rated as 3.6 stars rounded up to 4 stars. So, for now, I am rating this as 3.7 stars rounded up to 4 stars to be consistent with my other Highsmith ratings.My ranking/rating of the Highsmith novels I’ve read:The Tremor of Forgery - 4.5The Cry of the Owl - 4.4Deep Water - 4.3Ripley Under Ground – 4.3Ripley’s Game – 4.3The Price of Salt - 4.1The Boy Who Followed Ripley – 4.0Strangers on a Train - 4.0The Talented Mr. Ripley – 4.0Found in the Street – 3.7This Sweet Sickness - 3.6Little Tales of Misogyny - 3.4
