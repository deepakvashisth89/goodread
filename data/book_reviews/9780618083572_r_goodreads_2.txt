The History of Middle-earth is a massive “update” for all hardcore fans of J. R. R. Tolkien’s world. Many magical elements never made it into the final versions of his books, which is exactly why I strongly recommend reading The History of The Lord of the Rings.

It is endlessly compelling to read Tolkien, whether it’s a first draft or a fourth or fifth version of a text. Ideas shift, evolve, get discarded, and new ones come into focus—everything is subject to change, yet the quality remains. It’s fascinating to follow the flow of his thoughts: how he envisions future events or reflects on what he isn’t satisfied with. At times, he simply writes without a fixed destination, letting the story shape its own path forward. On the other hand, it is astonishing how certain he is about the existence and place of even the smallest details within the text. The development of The Lord of the Rings feels so natural, while its final form is ever-present—inevitable, like the rising of the Sun in the east.
