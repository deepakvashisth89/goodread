Deus Irae is a novel written collaboratively by Roger Zelazny and Philip K. Dick that was published in the bicentennial year of the U.S. Both writers worked collaboratively at various times, but I don't any of those novels were as good as most of their solo efforts. It's a post-apocalyptic novel about religion but doesn't have any of the rich imagery of many of the others, such as Miller's A Canticle for Leibowitz. It's sharply satiric but lacks subtlety, and I imagine most of the plot came from Dick and most of the lyrical passages are Zelazny. It's a quest novel featuring a limbless heretic named Tibor McMasters. I always thought that Zelazny and Dick would have been better off writing the same story on their own and then letting readers contrast and compare, rather than mashing their ideas together. It's one of those books that you feel let down because you expect a very high level when you see the names on the cover, not because the book is really poor.
