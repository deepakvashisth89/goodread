I thought I had already posted my review of this book, since I read it a while ago, as I was catching up after reading The Rules of Magic which is the prequel to this book. Review here: https://www.goodreads.com/review/show...

I absolutely loved the 1998 movie with Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman. If you haven’t seen it, I would recommend watching it – or if you have seen it, watch it again. I think it has risen to classic status.

By now, you probably know that the author has now also written, Magic Lessons, which is the prequel to The Rules of Magic. Review here:

Your best bet is to read in the following order: Magic Lessons, Practical Magic and then the Rules of Magic. Even though they weren’t written that way, it does probably flow better that way now.

On to the review:

The Owens sisters confront the challenges of life and love, but not exactly in a normal way. You see, for more than 200 years, the Owens women have been blamed for everything that has gone wrong in their Massachusetts town. Gillian and Sally, as children were forever outsiders, taunted and talked about. Their elderly aunts encouraged the whispers of witchery. Of course, it didn’t help that they lived in a musty house and enjoyed exotic concoctions and a crowd of black cats.

Even though Gillian and Sally may want to get out, as adults they still return.

The plot moved slowly, but it was still entertaining. The magic was enticing – but I wanted more.

I couldn’t help but find myself comparing the book to the movie, since I saw the movie first and kept visualizing Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman in the parts. And of course, Stockard Channing and Dianne Wiest were brilliant as the aunts in the movie, so they were easy to see in my mind, as I read the book, as well.

So, which did I like better, the book or the movie? The movie.

But the book had its enjoyable moments.

Still… Beware, there are trigger warnings here, too…

Domestic abuse, grief, emotional abuse, death, bullying and sexual violence.
