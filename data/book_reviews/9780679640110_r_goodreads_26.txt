So I put this in my "religion" category yet the author of this classic (1902) will state that is more about the psychology of religious experiences versus a treatise on religion itself. This book is a compilation of lectures the author gave which tries to empirically explore an individual's religious experience and the impact on one's life. It has extensive quotes, letters & diaries from people who have had a profound religious experience. His identification of a typology of religious experience is still utilized today, the two he identifies is healthy-mindedness and the sick soul; in the concept of the "divided self". Although it is primarily from the Christian perspective , he sprinkles a few examples of religious experience from Hinduism, Buddhism, & Islam. In his chapter on healthy-mindedness he discusses "mind-cure" and delves into the medical experiments along with Transcendentalism and the new community of Christian Scientists. All had a New Age feel to it and now is interesting from a historical perspective. I particularly liked the chapters on conversion and saintliness, even though apparently the chapter on mysticism is the most widely read.
