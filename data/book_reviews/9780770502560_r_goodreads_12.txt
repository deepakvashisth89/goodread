Unlike MacLennan's other books, I just couldn't get into Each Man's Son. After the oddly disconnected opening chapter, the book segues into an examination of life in Cape Breton and a ersatz father-son connection between the town's doctor and a child with no father.Given how morose the tone is, it might be better suited as a film adaptation. The flow of the book doesn't do much to engender the reader, and the whole plot and end result feels like a pointless waste of time. Likewise, the fact that it focuses on drawn-out descriptions of everyday life in Cape Breton and the odd lyrical style makes it much more difficult to get into.I'm not sure it has much to say in the same way The Watch That Ends The Night or Two Solitudes did about the human condition, which pains me because I love all of MacLennan's other works.
