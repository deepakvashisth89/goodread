Maigret and the Man on the Boulevard, also known as Maigret and the Man on the Bench, is an intriguing Maigret. More a character study than the others I have read so far. But then doesn’t Maigret always endeavour to get to the heart of things?“When a man is found stabbed to death in an alley off Boulevard Saint-Martin, his identity card shows a workplace that had gone out of business three years earlier. As far as his wife knew, he still worked there, and she insists that the shoes and a tie he was wearing when he was killed “couldn’t be his.”I really enjoyed this mystery of what the victim had been doing with himself for the last three years. And because of the circumstances of the crime Maigret is forced (in my mind anyway) to do a lot more personal digging than he usually does. Even the named chapters are intriguing:The Brown ShoesThe Pug Nosed VirginThe Boiled EggA Funeral in the RainThe Policeman’s WidowThe BeggarsThe Rainwear ShopMonique’s SecretJudge Comeliau Grows RestiveHere is Maigret pondering on the victim’s daughter and widow:“Why was it that he had a feeling she was not being altogether sincere? Her mother had expressed incredulity but being the sort of woman she was, that was only to be expected. Basically, as far as Madame Thouret was concerned, it was a disgraceful thing to get oneself murdered in an alleyway off the Boulevard Saint-Martin.” Three and a half stars.
