I enjoyed Gulliver's Travels so much! (But I have absolutely zero clue about what's going on in his "other writings.")First of all, I gotta give it to Gulliver for being so adventurous and fearless that his history of being captured by bizarre creatures does not stop him from being down for his next odyssey. He's probably the pioneer of YOLO.Next, the satires are profound and timeless. I love how Jonathan Swift organizes his satires so well. In Lilliput, he ridicules politics and governance, underscoring the pettiness of religious strife and warfare (the nations went to war over how they crack their eggs). In Brobdingnag, he mocks societal morality: Gulliver's consternation when the King repudiated the idea of using gunpowder, mankind's "greatest" invention, to frighten his subjects into subordination. In Laputa and the islands, he takes multiple jabs at modern science and philosophy (likely the Enlightenment and Industrial Revolution). Finally - and this is my favorite part - in Houyhnhnm, he exposed the ugly truths about human nature. It is only appropriate that in this final chapter, Gulliver ends his lifelong adventures after being utterly disillusioned and disgusted by humanity.I highly recommend these reads. They aren't particularly exciting, but I can see how they ended up being considered 'classic literature.'
