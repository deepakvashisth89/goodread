I found this book sitting in my professor’s bookshelf in her cubicle. Curiosity was what made the book to be immediately in the palm of my hands. As I skim through the pages, smell the book’s natural scent, and immersing myself with the book’s condition, imagining it being well-loved, I knew it was going to be my next read.Yesterday, I told my professor that I was close on finishing the book. We got to talk about it a little bit, helping her remember how the story goes, since she last read it almost 30 years ago, when she was still in high school. It’s amazing how literature can connect people and make their bond stronger. And it can also transfer you back into a specific time, and help you relive it all.The book’s storyline started slow, but nevertheless, I kept on turning the pages, because Mary Higgins Clark’s writing was utterly incredible. Her use of metaphor and idiomatic expressions were on point. The plot was very intriguing, as it deals with the psychological conditions of human beings, specifically the abused ones. Storylines like this is what makes me keep on reading whether I’m sitting on a bus, having my morning coffee, or even during listening to a lecture. I’m amazed on how I can simultaneously listen and read. Only few books can do that.This book is a special one to me, because it was the reason why I got to talk to a professor I didn’t know during one of my times reading in the food court in our campus. And it made me feel so so happy. We got to talk about reading a little bit, and about how not a lot of people reads these days, especially from where I’m from. He told me that one of the brilliant professors in the faculty of our college was his student back then. And he also told me to try to be like him, since I’ve mentioned that I’m very interested in teaching as well. The whole conversation made me feel in ecstasy. It’s a small thing to other people, but it’s big to me. I felt seen. I felt appreciated. I felt less alone.
