Yes, this is as bad as they say, perhaps even worse than Vulcan! There Ms. Sky went for the bigotry hard and fast and stuck with it. Here, she gives us a potentially interesting story at the beginning, but instead of making that the focus of the book, it serves merely as an entree and deus ex machina, a bookend of what could have been.At least in Vulcan! it's her new character that has all the problems. Here, Ms. Sky gives us an almost flawless new gal who can do it all yet is totally dependent on the men when it really counts, and our command crew has all the flaws (mostly nonstop sex drives). She is willing to assassinate ambassadors, blow up flagships, and destroy peace conferences, but she needs male guidance concerning men who treat her badly. She can pretend to be people's moms to forgive sexual assault, and she's willing and eager to be another notch on Kirk's bedpost. She's the highest ranking officer in the story, but the crew is fine with insulting her even for situations they know are beyond her control. It's not a good book, no. Everyone falls in love with her and touches her throughout the book. It's just bad.As others have said, the ambassadors from other planets are all just Earth animals made large, wholly uncreative and amateurish. The tone is inconsistent and frustrating: the book pretends to be a thrilling, scary murder mystery, but no one takes it seriously, especially Susie Perfect, who flirts, drinks, fondles, and coddles her way through a sort-of investigation, stumbling accidentally on the answer, which is an infuriating treatment of canonical characters.The only positive element occurs when Susie Perfect doesn't know 19th-century literature. Every other '70s ST author wants us to believe every single member of Starfleet is profoundly aware of every minute detail of human history, from 17th-century Scottish ballads to 12th-century Balkan intertribal conflicts, and everything before, during, and after. Refreshingly, Susie is unfamiliar with Sherlock Holmes. Irritatingly, a crocodile ambassador from another planet knows him and tries to imitate him, during the non-stop comic relief. It's embarrassing.Read it so you've read it, not because it's a good Star Trek book or a good book or, really, even a book. While it probably should be -3 stars for what I've mentioned and things I've ignored here, 2 stars because at least Ms. Sky wrote and worked at her craft. It's easy to criticize without having done it myself. Plus it's not pretending to be what it isn't (except for pretending to be a good ST book, I suppose), and I try to save 1-star reviews for bad theology, downright dishonest works, and to disabuse people who need such correction. No one misunderstands what this is; those who claim to like it may do so genuinely, but surely they are few and far between.
