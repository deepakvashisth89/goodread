The writing itself is very well done.I had problems with a few situational things, like if the author is going to be very good at trying to get the time period right for most things, why didn’t she do it for all? Like post-wedding MC shouldn’t have been receiving visitors since she’d be in her confinement period and you can’t tell me just bc she wasn’t living a noble’s life prior that she’d never heard of it. That’s just one example I picked off the top of my head but there were several. Oh yeah, she doesn’t know Prinny is the prince of England? She’d need to live under a rock to have never heard him referred to in that way in that time period. Also, a duke, one of the most important persons in England, only behind the royal family, just marrying anyone like its nbd and the most society apparently cares is limited to this post-marriage bit, then ignored. Lol obviously I remembered a few more. I guess I’m weird that I can totally accept time-period books that really only use it as a setting and are very inaccurate, but if you try to be accurate, you best be fully accurate or I get irritated.Anyways my main gripes are that too much of the book was like a hallmark movie with how amazingly saint-like Gideon was. Too perfect. Contrast that with Sabrina just constantly lying. You could argue that she’s been damaged and trust takes time. That’s another issue I had, the timeframe on this is so freaking short. So irl it makes sense she’s hesitant but somehow it feels like a lot more time has passed when you’re reading it.My last thing that took away from the whole story for me was how nothing happened for so long other than Gideon winning everyone over and being amazing and perfect and everything being so saccharine I got bored, wondering when the directly mentioned bad guy would finally show up, then he does, it’s over with quickly, both MC’s just jump to crazy assumptions about what the other was thinking… usual trope, then HEA.So while the author can write incredibly well, I just didn’t like the pacing of the plot, the perfection of the hero, or the not-trust of the heroine despite all evidence, even when it directly puts her family in danger (bc where would the plot end without that contrivance?).And midway through the book, I could’ve sworn I previously read the book about Damon or Raf, bc I didn’t understand the hero worship when they “met his parents” scene and they were almost like a McGuffin fix-it-all in that book, but now I can’t find the book to confirm….
