"The scholarly consensus on the total number of executions for witchcraft ranges from 40,000 to 60,000", not counting the ones who died in unofficial lynchings or because of the poor hygienic conditions in the prisons - 75% to 85% of these people were women.

Vassalli exposes in great detail an often overlooked part of European history linking it back to modern day, reminding us of everything there is to learn from our past. Antonia's story is a tragic one, my heart bled with every sentence as I imagined how afraid she would have been, suddenly accused of witchery, ostracised by everyone around her, tortured until she confessed, and eventually burnt at the stake. Vassalli did an incredible job writing her story and the narrator not only gives Antonia some dignity and autonomy back, but is also unapologetically by her side. I wish she could have read his account of her story.
