Continuing to read the unread books on my shelf, this one dating from 1971 or 1972, price tag of $1.45 reflective of the era. I made a few underlines in it back then, but don't recall finishing it. So now, 55 years later, and at 220 pages, I figure I averaged four pages a year, or one page every three months.It was published in France in 1939, and my English translation is copyrighted 1947. It was tough going at times, in part due to changes in the way we think nowadays, and my slim knowledge of the context.But finally, it confirmed some of my thoughts about the general trend of the Enlightenment, and the connections between all those characters we ill-advisedly refer to as our "founding fathers" and the French thinkers, Voltaire, Rousseau, Diderot, and Montaigne, among others. Now we are stuck with the worship of the "founders" and the misinterpretation and misuse of their ideas by the ultimate court, the orange man, and his henchmen with their philosophy of "originalism."Maybe it's time for another revolution of thought.
