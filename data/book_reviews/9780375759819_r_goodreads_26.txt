a really wonderful, page-turning, and fascinating glimpse into the cliquish world of science and the peer review process. ultimately, it's about a man who stumbles across a completely new theory of scent that makes more sense than the more commonly accepted one, only to be chastised by his fellow peers of science--for only God knows why. i already knew a fair bit about Luca Turin, having watched a presentation he did on scent on a TED talk. some of that info is in this book, but this obviously delves deeper into the subject. Turin is a brilliant and eccentric scientist whose first love is perfume, and virtually any science as his second. The writer is Chandler Burr, who was a journalist at the time and afterward eventually became a perfume critic in his own right for the New York Times. Burr is a great writer and brings the subject to life in an entertaining way, while still maintaining his journalistic professionalism. the subject of the book is truly fascinating, though it does get a little too science-heavy for my tastes. ultimately, i can't really ding it for that, because the science is the story, but there is so much in here that i dont understand because i dont have that type of scientific training. my biggest criticism is that the story kind of doesnt go very far. for a story like this that has you rooting for the underdog, you expect either a tragic end or a triumphant conclusion--otherwise why write it? neither happens in the end, only another mention of the rejection that Turin had had during this time. perhaps it was written too soon? who knows. i really enjoyed the book, but this deserves a sequel at some point to further show how Turin's research has advanced. though Burr didnt explicitly write "to be continued..." the ending felt like it. BUT, that aside, the journey itself was enjoyable. highly recommended!
