This reads like an '80s sitcom -- the same kind of humor as well as the level of inappropriate/offensive things that were considered acceptable then but not so much anymore. I have a second-hand copy of the version published in 1983, but New Directions reissued it in 2023 with a new intro by Danzy Senna & a note from the author. I'd suggest looking at a preview of the ebook version & reading Senna's intro for more info about the tenor of the book.

Apparently, this book was based on/inspired by Henry James' The Aspern Papers, (which, in turn, is based on letters Percy Bysshe Shelley wrote to Mary Shelley's stepsister, Claire Clairmont). Warren Harding did have extramarital lovers & had a daughter with one of them. (DNA testing in 2015 confirmed it.) Apparently, the lover/mother () of his daughter wrote a book, , that was considered a scandalous, tell-all account of their relationship, published in 1927. I appreciate the rabbit holes I've explored because of this book & now plan to read both the Henry James & Nan Britton books.

Back to the original book I was reviewing here: It's quick & easy to read, moves at a fast clip, but has plenty of offensive material, much of it used as "humor". Senna's intro (mentioned above) says that Larry David kept copies & had the writers for Seinfeld try to imitate the tone. As a GenXer, I'm familiar (very familiar) with that '80s style humor, some lines in here were indeed funny, but there is also a lot that just doesn't pass muster anymore. Caveat emptor.
