The title and book cover represent truth-in-advertising. It’s a continuous parody of religious devotion expressed by the activities of gay men. If anyone uses the term blasphemous anymore, they might apply it to this novel (just as they might apply it to a straight-sex parody).

This was a DNF for me. (So no rating.) I appreciate the humor, the wit, maybe even the brilliance of some of the parodies but it’s like a joke that goes on too long. I gave it 30 pages or so and realized every single one of its 170 pages has these parodies – a couple of examples below. Apparently the story takes a darker turn toward the end with HIV/AIDS but I did not get that far.

Our main character travels through time from medieval days to the present consorting with other notable gay men, especially authors, such as Jean Genet, and various real or invented religious figures. You also have to be up on your French and your Latin to appreciate all the puns. (I’m not.)

I looked up a couple of names to see if they were real people or not. So there are imaginary saints like Saint Juan of Barbes-Rochehouart, a pun on a Paris metro stop where there were gay gathering spots. “…a rendezvous for souls fond of the darkness that hid their earnest labors. Only God, all-hearing, all-seeing could list and describe their holy industry”!

A ‘saint’ [a partner he just met] “…immediately accepted the suggestion we should pray matins together….Sadly I abandoned the linen threshing ground that was chapel to our and . I never chanted the prayers in my Breviary with such a feeling of holy release”!

“…by night, beneath our windows, he did lay in my bed and bestow upon me prayers and blessings from his breviary.”

Besides AIDS, there’s another dark side to this writing. The author was born in 1931 and, although he published this book in 2000, I imagine that it largely reflects earlier times when gay men were forced to meet in bathhouses and restrooms.



The author was bisexual, married to a woman who apparently did not mind his affairs with men. He was a prolific author winning awards for many of his works, although he was banned from publishing in Spain during Franco’s time. More than a dozen of his books are translated into English and he’s best known in English for his novel, Marks of Identity. However, based on GR ratings, he is relatively little known to English readers.
