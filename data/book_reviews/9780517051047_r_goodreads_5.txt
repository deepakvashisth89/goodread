I must read these books in the correct order, otherwise I won't understand half the references in them, as the trilogy (Berlin Game, Mexico Set, and London Game) is followed by (Spy Hook, Spy Line, and Spy Sinker) and then comes (Faith, Hope, and Charity).

When providing a review of a book, you normally have to avoid providing spoilers for a single book, but with this book and all the others I'm hoping to read soon, I will have to avoid spoilers for any of the preceding books too.

As you can see, the book I'm reviewing is the fourth book in the Bernard Samson series, where he appears to have a settled life again, but he doesn't of course, as he's not aware of the full context regarding what happened in the Berlin Game book.

In Spy Hook, Bernard Samson is pursuing a personal investigation into a family matter, against the advice of his colleagues and employer, and receives some surprises regarding a large amount of money. People he thought were dead are only undercover and at the end of the book, Samson doesn't know who he can rely on any longer, as his own people appear to have turned against him. He has planned for this day but when he was planning, the expectation was that he would be pursued by the East Germans and Russians and not his own Department.
