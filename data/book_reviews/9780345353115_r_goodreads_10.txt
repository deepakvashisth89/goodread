I read this as a Buddy Read with members of the Hugo/Nebula: Best Novels group. I read lots of Heinlein in high school & college, but somehow never read the Juveniles. This was the second of the series in the Buddy Read.

It was SO much better than Galileo, very entertaining. Not only did he avoid the problems of RSG, but while it retains a little cheesiness, it's not nearly as cliche as many novels of the same era. It could have been an (original) Star Trek episode, just without a red shirt getting knocked off. Or a younger, milder prelude to .

Yes, apart from mothers of the main young male characters, there are no female characters, but this was written in 1948, in an era where women rarely if ever entered such professions. Ironically, it also precludes the inferior treatment of women that so often occurs when there ARE female characters in books of this era. It goes hand-in-hand with such magazines as "Boy's Life", the target audience being teenage or young adult males.

Apart from minor flaws, overall, a fun nostalgic read.
