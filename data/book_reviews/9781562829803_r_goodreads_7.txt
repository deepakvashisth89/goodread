The Sonniers may be a wealthy Louisiana oil family, but they are still white trash. Dave Robicheaux grew up with them, so he knows only too well how fucked up they are. Still, he has a soft spot for the Sonnier brothers, Weldon and Lyle, and their beautiful sister, Drew. He may even go so far as to call them friends. They may have been dealt a bad hand, but they weren't bad people.When Dave is called in one night to investigate a possible break-in at the Sonnier house, he finds a dead police officer and three bad dudes who have been hired to kill Weldon, the oldest Sonnier sibling. They elude Dave, but he has enough to go on to find them. The problem? These bad dudes have links to a local crime boss who has links to a prominent Louisiana politician who is gaining popularity in the polls despite his links to the Klu Klux Klan.The fifth Dave Robicheaux novel, James Lee Burke's "A Stained White Radiance" pits Dave up against Louisiana's rich upper-class, which---it turns out, not surprisingly---is just as seedy and immoral---if not moreso---than the criminal denizens of the lower class.Another riveting page-turner from one of my favorite authors, "ASWR" proves once again that Burke is one of the best crime-thriller writers out there. Burke shines a spotlight on the modern South, in all its charming beautyanddisgusting seediness.
