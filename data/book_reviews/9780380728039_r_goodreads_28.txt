Beverly Cleary is a great writer for children. She really understands them. She seems to understand dogs, too. In "Ribsy," she tells the story from the point of view of Ribsy, Henry Huggins' dog. This story was written in a time when dogs had a bit more freedom to wander about than they do today, so long as they weren't bothering anyone. These days, a dog has to stay on a leash or be picked up by Animal Control--where I live, at least. I thought the dialogue was realistic, and the characters were well-drawn. Ribsy gets separated from his family and has a series of adventures trying to find his way back home. Wouldn't you know it, Henry had just taken off his collar so he could scratch at a flea, so he wasn't wearing it when he escaped from the family car. This meant no one who found him knew whose dog he was. The same thing happened with our family dog once. I forget why, but my daughter had taking off our dog's collar with the dog tags attached for some good reason--just a few hours before our dog got out of our back yard without our even knowing it. We'd had no idea the people who came to read our electric meter had knocked down part of our fence to get to the meter. Sometimes these things just happen. I was reading the well-worn edition, with illustrations by Louis Darling, that had been my children's. I like this book a lot. I like everything I've ever read that Beverly Clearly wrote.
