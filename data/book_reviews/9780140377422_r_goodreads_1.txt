There are few things I find more exciting than rediscovering something I half-remember from my childhood. I went to a used book sale recently (and promised myself I would just look and not buy anything [I only got about ten books, so that's better than usual]) and found this book. I read it when I was probably about 9 years old. I remembered that it was about a boy who's terrified of sharks that befriends a boy who is blind. What I specifically remember was a moment in the book where they create a ball with jingle bells in it so that the blind boy can play soccer. I always thought that was impressively innovative.The weird part of the book, that the main character has freckles that talk, was utterly forgotten by me.I was having dinner with a friend recently and she said one of her favorite book moments was when a book caused her to realize she's bipolar. I wondered out loud if a book had ever made me realize something about myself. I think this silly little children's book made me realize that I rely on other people too much. The character who is blind talks about how he doesn't want help from others because wants to maintain independence. I realized that I need to stop waiting for other people to have the time to help me with stuff before I just do it. I live in China and I haven't learned much Mandarin, so I usually ask a friend to help me when I want to upgrade my data plan or even try a new restaurant. I want to stop thinking like I need my hand held through stuff like this.Thanks book!I wonder if a braille version of Shark Shock was ever published.
