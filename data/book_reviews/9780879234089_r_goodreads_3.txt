This is my first book in the series. I did not realize it was book three when I got it. However, it works as a standalone book. It is vey character-driven, and it its with certain aspects of Western and Central PA today, though much has also changed since the 1970s in which this book is set. The murder victim has a tragic story and it has led to isolation from most people, so her death makes her circumstances become more tragic. While this content was interesting, especially being from Philly and having gone to graduate school at Penn State, it felt like the victim got lost in the story at points, as she became the jumping off point for a philosophical and social commentary on some aspects of academia and society at the time. Nonetheless, I enjoyed it well enough that I would read the next book in the series at some point to see how I feel about that story. I am not in a rush to read the next book, but I likely will at some point in the future.
