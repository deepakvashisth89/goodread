I give this book 2 stars mainly for the few insights I found at the beginning. Overall, it didn’t resonate with me at all. I wasn’t expecting a highly psychological or scientific approach to healing, but I did expect something more professional. Instead, the book relies heavily on fairy tales, dreams, and myths, which made it difficult for me to continue reading after the first 40 pages.In the end, the author suggests healing by taking something from each archetype (like the puella or the armored Amazon), improving what’s missing, and embracing our inner and feminine creative power. But isn’t that quite basic? I didn’t need to finish the book to arrive at that conclusion. It feels like the book doesn’t really offer practical ways to heal.Overall, the writing is very broad, to the point where I don’t think you even need to have ‘daddy issues’ to relate to the stories.
