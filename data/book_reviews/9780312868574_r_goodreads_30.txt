This story supposes a world where the native life is not only toxic to Earth life forms, it is aggressively so on a cellular level. Zoe Fisher arrives on this world, genetically engineered to be resistant and technologically protected, but emotionally immature due to her own life history. Her interactions with the station personnel are interesting and well developed. The tension extends right to the last page, as the various stations encounter ever worsening difficulties.

I loved to hate a few of the authority figures. But I have to say that the portrayal of those characters as sniveling autocratic bad-guys, and the nature of Zoe's environment suit, make me wonder if Wilson has targeted a more adolescent audience with this book. This is not a sense I have picked up in his other books, such as Darwinia and The Chronoliths (consequently I liked them better). Nonetheless, this is an intriguing science fiction adventure story.
