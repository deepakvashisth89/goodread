Clifford D. Simak's tale about robots and a robot pope trying to find God is compelling. Set near the edge of the galaxy on a planet known as "The End of Nothing", it revolves around not only the robots, but also two human visitors who get caught up in events. I found it's large scale events and ideas very interesting.

Update: Initially read around 1994, revisited/re-read in 2024, some twenty years later. Amazing how much I still enjoyed this, and find it really underrated. I absolutely love the End of Nothing as the setting. It's haunting and humbling somehow. What's interesting to me is that it begins with Tenyson, the protagonist on the run on board a starship with a clearly bigoted captain, who loathes his alien passengers. Once he arrives though, Tennyson becomes embroiled in a robotic search for God, and this takes him via "storage cubes" on a grand tour of numerous alien races, and that doesn't even cover the locals on End of Nothing: the fascinating Old Ones. I find this wonderful novel both cozy and epic, with a huge canvas and a wonderful world as its home base.
