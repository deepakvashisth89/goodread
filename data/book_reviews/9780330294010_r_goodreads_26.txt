Joanne Greenberg drew from her own experience in a psychiatric institution and writes through Deborah Blau, a teenage girl who has retreated into this inner world she created of Yr. The novel moves between the two realities (Real life and Yr) and allows us to inhabit Deborah’s illness and her logic first hand.

The prose at times, can feel so dense, which I enjoyed at times and at others felt like walking through mud. Deborah has her own language, metaphors, symbols, and coded meaning. I will say this feels like an intentional challenge, something for you to not necessarily passively consume. Struggling with mental illness isn’t always linear, it’s confusing and doesn’t make sense to anyone other than yourself. So maybe Yr is just a metaphor (as I’ve seen others comment on) but this place gives us insight on how Deborah is doing and why her actions are the way they are. For me I had to pay extra attention when character from this place popped up or new words were used.

I enjoyed how Greenberg included Deborah’s parents, her doctors, and even 1940s America POV into the story. A culture that insists on dividing people into “well” and “unwell”. There was this constant issue the parents had of trying their hardest to understand why Deborah was the way she was. A box they needed her to fit in and mental illness just doesn’t work that way.

In the end, this isn’t a sensationalized account of psychiatric treatment, or even a simple story of recovery. It’s something a lot more intimate: a study of what it means to live between worlds, to long for connection while fearing it, and to slowly, unevenly, find a way back.

I’m not sure I would recommend this to everyone. But if you want something that dives in a little more than Girl, Interrupted or even dips their toes into horror (imo) I would recommend picking this up. The writing is beautiful, the story is fascinating and devastating. Very excited to watch the film now!

4.5 stars
