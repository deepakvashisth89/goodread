Evelina, or The History of a Young Lady’s Entrance Into the World was written and published during the eighteenth century by author Frances Burney. This opens with Fanny Burney addressing the authors of the monthly and critical reviews as she addresses the extensive plans of their critical observations.

“As magistrates of the press, and Censors for the public, to which you are bound by the sacred ties of integrity to exert the most spirited impartiality, and to which your suffrages should carry the marks of pure, dauntless, irrefragable truth to appeal to your MERCY, were to solicit your dishonor; and therefore, though ‘tis sweeter than frankincense, more grateful to the senses than all the odorous perfumes of Arabia, and though ‘it dropped like the gentle rain from heaven upon the place beneath,’ I court it not! To your justice alone I am entitled, and by that I must abide.”


It is also said that this epistolary novel was one of the favorite books and authors of Jane Austen as it is a cutting satire of eighteenth century society, and viewed as a significant precursor to later works by Jane Austen by many scholars. follows a naive and illegitimate young woman’s navigation of high society and awkward courtship. Evelina moves between rural life an fashionable London, maturing after several humorous faux pas and gaining the love of the respectful Lord Orville. All that being said, I found this a bit of a slog periodically, perhaps because of the satire. In the words of the novel — But the beautiful and fairy-tale ending made it all worthwhile.
