Quick RecommendationDemons is perfect for these who seek a political satire with philosophical themes. It is inspired by a political murder that took place in 1869 in Russia. Dostoevsky makes witty comments about radical movements. In the process, he mocks the radical people and expresses the strong opinion that atheism and other ideologies are a curse brought by ‘demons’ upon society.It might has a slow start, but it's thought-provoking long after one finished it.IntroductionFirst of all, this was the darkest Dostoevsky novel I read so far. A close second is Crime and Punishment which I initially didn't think would be surpassed. Second of all, its crucial to obtain historical knowledge before reading this one. Its based on a political murder that took place in 1869 in Russia. His own opinion about the matter is very clear and important to know, so that one can understand the meaning behind ‘Demons’. Lastly, it's very dark. A reason for that is the doom-like atmosphere presented to the reader. It shows the true horrors of political extremism and how violent and cruel revolution can be.Compared to Dostoevsky's other novels, this one doesn't offer any sense of hope or escape. Its atmosphere is grim, gloomy and hopeless. It's a lot about brutal acts such as physical attacks and murder. It requires emotional resilience, to not feel overwhelmed or triggered by it in some way as many dark aspects of life are included.And why are they fools? They’re not such fools; nowadays nobody’s mind is his own. Nowadays there are terribly few distinct minds.To put it together, it's psychologically intense, has a chaotic start, intense political intrigues, it has a sense of tension, dread and hopelessness and finally it require background knowledge to fully grasp the idea Dostoevsky had with this one.Despite all that its absolutely worth reading as his commentary on Russian history and society is very profound and the themes presented are still urgently relevant to this day. In addition, it offers deep insights into philosophical ideas and shows the human nature of suffering. (It's also spiritual).If you want to overcome the whole world, overcome yourself.PacingThe start is dragging a lot, in my opinion. I had a hard time to get into this one, but the middle part catches up and the ending has a great impact. I would encourage anyone who has the same problem at the start to at least read half of the second part before considering dropping this book.Writing StyleDostoevsky uses many satires to bring his point across which can only be understood by those who have historically knowledge. Especially about the political murder event that this book is based on.I didn't really get them even with a bit background knowledge, but it isn't crucial to understand them to get what the book is trying to tell. Though it can definitely enrich the reading experince.ThemesMain ThemesExplores political ideologies, chaos and society's breakdown by demons who cause chaos and destruction. The consequences are social and political decay.It's characterised by corruption, desperation, violence, despair, and moral decay.Other themesThe consequence of abandoning faith, of past actions and of political intrigues. Other ones are marriage (of convenience) and being possessed by ideologies.CharactersPyotr Stepanovich Verkhovensky[...]you have no right to betray the common cause on a word of honor! Only swine and people bought by the government act like that!Pyotr represents radical socialism and revolutionism. He's the most prominent character in this novel and it was fascinating to watch him having a dark nature and how it unravels through the story. Not knowing his next move or true intentions were really scary which made me curious about him even more. Pyotr doesn't immediately show what he is capable of which makes him charismatic and scary at the same time. Though he shows his true abilities when it's important, especially to lead his group. He achieves things most people could only dream of. What makes him so successful is his lack of guilt or remorse. No price is too high for him to achieve his goals. So ruthless. So power-hungry. I love it! He is also unpredictable, making his appearances always feel fresh. Even though I couldn't belive it at first, he also has insecurities. Thus making him a complex and disturbing character, not just a flat one.There is so much to analyse with this character, making him my favourite. After all, the most intriguing characters in Dostoevsky works are always flawed and I liked him for being flawed. He contains many layers, making him accurate to real people.(Without him I would have had a harder time to understand the true meaning behind the book's title. He made this read a bit easier to understand).Slaves must have rulers. Complete obedience, complete impersonality, but once every thirty years Shigalyov gets a convulsion going, and they all suddenly start devouring each other, up to a certain point, simply so as not to be bored. Boredom is an aristocratic sensation; in Shigalyovism there will be no desires. Desire and suffering are for us; and for the slaves—Shigalyovism.Nikolai Vsevolodovich StavroginI somewhat liked him. Seeing not only a character not believe in God but also the decisions and actions driven by a rejection of faith and morals was interesting. Even more interesting was to discover Dostoevsky's own message behind this character.Slaves must be equal: there has never yet been either freedom or equality without despotism, but within a herd there must be equality, and this is Shigalyovism! Ha, ha, ha, so you find it strange? I’m for Shigalyovism!He managed to influence many characters in this novel in a way that might sounded right at the start but turned sinister and evil soon enough. My first impression of Nikolai was actually that he is innocent and kind hearted and this impression lasted till the very end. Some might disagree, but compared to Pyotr, he was able to feel real emotions and reflected on his past mistakes. Though he was pretty empty from the inside, making him sometimes appear to be a side character. I wish he could have had a strong will like Pyotr. He is even more charismatic than Pyotr and with a bit more energy and will, he could have influenced other characters even more than Pyotr did.He felt very detached from everyone and everything around him, but I felt a bit attached to him. Nikolai's beliefs differ from the other characters, making him stand out from the rest.Stepan Trofimovich VerkhovenskyOh, my friend, marriage is the moral death of any proud soul, of any independence.I didn't like him that much, but it's still worth mentioning him. Pyotr, his son, was able to change society. Stepan, on the other hand, was only dreaming about change but was never able to put his ideas into reality. 'Cause of this and many other things, I wasn't able to like Stepan. He might represented (radical) liberalism, but wasn't quite active in the story to really say that he made an impact. Stepan thinks so highly of himself, but is actually contributing barely anything in this novel. The only interesting part about him was how he was financially dependent on others and his lecture at the end of the novel.You married out of a passion for torture, out of a passion for remorse, out of moral sensuality.OutroductionWell, it might was hard to understand this novel sometimes, but I also learnt many new things. It was really insightful. For instance, I now know what slavophilism is. Before reading, I didn't even know that something like this existed in the first place.Every nation has its own idea of evil and good, and its own evil and good. When many nations start having common ideas of evil and good, then the nations die out and the very distinction between evil and good begins to fade and disappear. Reason has never been able to define evil and good, or even to separate evil from good, if only approximately; on the contrary, it has always confused them, shamefully and pitifully; and science has offered the solution of the fist.I was also able to see Dostoevsky's own perspective and opinions about the matter. Although I didn't always agree to them, I still enjoyed to see his opinions be expressed in satirical comments and in characters that embodied different ideologies.StoryGraph review (analytical approach, with marked spoilers)Started: 03.
