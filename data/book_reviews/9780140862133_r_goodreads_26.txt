"Remember that hope is a good thing, Red, maybe the best of things, and no good thing ever dies."How do I even begin this review?I first heard of this story in movie format, called The Shawshank Redemption. Rather than Rita Hayworth and Shawshank Redemption, I suppose, for marketing reasons. This was many years ago; I think I was 13 or 14 at the time, and I loved the movie so much that I rewatched it every single day for the next week. To this day, I still watch the movie every summer, and every time, I cry at the end.I am not sure why I took so long to finally read the book. Granted, I actually found out that the movie was an adaptation of a Stephen King novel somewhat recently, but I think maybe I was just worried I wouldn't love it as much as I love the film.Thankfully, King proved me wrong, and I loved every single line, just like I love every single line in the film. Frank Darabont did such an amazing job adapting this novella to film format. There are some small differences, but I feel he was able to capture its true essence.At this point, this feels more like a review of the movie, but here's what I think of the novella. I think everyone should read it. I think the message is beautiful and emotional.We begin with Andy, a young and successful banker, being charged with the murder of his wife and her lover. He ends up stuck in Shawshank prison, where he meets Ellis Redding, called “Red” (maybe because he's Irish 🤭). Like Red says, "prison ain't no fairy-tale", and Shawshank is there to "institutionalise" its prisoners. So Shawshank Redemption is a story about the human spirit and about hope, and it's a damn good story.I think now it's time to rewatch the movie again, and cry again, and fall in love with this amazing cast of characters and their stories again ❤️
