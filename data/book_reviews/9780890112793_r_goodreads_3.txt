I discoveredThe Letters of J.R.R. Tolkienback in '96 when I moved to an LA suburb and was searching through the library for something interesting to read while I took advantage of their free A/C during the blistering summer heat.This was definitely something interesting, but only because I was a Tolkien fanboy. Who else would find joy in pouring over mostly mundane letters to friends, family and publishers? Me, I pored over over them, so happy to read even the most minuscule detail of the man's life.It's been a while, so my recollection of what's contained in the letters is not precise. I seem to recall a feeling of being let down that more personal information wasn't revealed. English reserve seemed to be at play here.The correspondence with his book publisher and seeing how that portion of his work came together held just as much interest, and there is a good deal of that herein, if I recall. I was already aware that Tolkien had some trouble convincing them to publishLord of the Rings, even after the success ofThe Hobbit. And after publishing went forward, there was still a struggle over issues that needed ironing out. None of which is terribly riveting readingunlessyou're a fan.
