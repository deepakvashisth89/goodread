David Carroll is around sixty, a troubled Bostonian whose knows the feds are waiting in the wings, about to pounce on his troubled bank. Frances his wife, suggests a cruise on a refurbished luxury ocean liner to break up the tension.

From the time they embark, David and Frances are forced to share their table with Burton Rutledge, an elegant retired New Yorker who talks endlessly of his late wife. For five days they must listen to this man’s stories. He is there to convince them that he has lots of money so they will never even consider that he is after theirs. At one point he subtly mentions a money exchange in one of his long monologues. From that point on, we know the con man has just begun to reel in his fish.

I found the characters in this tale rather flat. Even Melissa, Carroll’s former mistress who is also on the cruise, does not have any color. David and Frances seem to be quite simply shallow and boring.

Higgins has a distinctive style of writing with pages and pages of fast paced dialogue. There are quotes within quotes which makes the narrative thread convoluted and reading gets to be a long tedious process.

There is an interesting ending. But I did not find this an enjoyable read.
