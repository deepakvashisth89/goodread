I picked this book up out of curiosity, since the Jane Austen mysteries were the only ones I had previously read set in the same general era. Unfortunately (for my research at least), the novel was actually set towards the beginning of the Georgian era, when George III had just ascended to the throne and the Hanover dynasty was beginning to settle in to ruling England. This book is set largely in the seedy underside of England (and contains suicide triggers, just be aware), and deals with the murder of an actress named Lucy Dove, ostensibly by Robert Fairfax's student Matthew Hemsley. Fairfax believes Hemsley to be innocent and sets about trying to uncover the true murderer.I was intrigued by the author's use of non-forensic techniques to discover the villain. The time that the actress was murdered could possibly clear Matthew Hemsley's name, but there's no way the Georgians would have been able to tell that. This may be frustrating for some readers. I did not, however, connect with Fairfax quite the way I would have wanted to, and I was deeply disappointed by who the actual culprit turned out to be (although some parts of the resolution seemed quite satisfying, if slightly unrealistic in that the person in question would likely not have been punished). In general, it's a good book, but probably not for me. I'll still read the second in the series, but after that I'll probably let it go.
