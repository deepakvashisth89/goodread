When I saw that this book was prepared by the Detection Club, I was all for it. If you are not familiar with the Detection Club, it is a "by invitation only" group of British mystery writers founded in 1930 by Anthony Berkeley and had such original members as Agatha Christie, Dorothy Sayers, Francis Iles, and Freeman Wills Crofts. The club is still very active and is currently headed by Martin Edwards.

In 1936, the club members released this book in which they looked at seven famous murders (I only knew three of them) to decide if they had been officially solved, making use of the advances in investigative techniques and the psychology of those involved. Each case was taken on by a separate member: Helen Simpson, John Rhode, Margaret Cole, E.R. Punshon (I am not familiar with this author), Dorothy Sayers, Francis Iles, and Freeman Wills Crofts.

It sounds interesting, right? I have to say that parts of it were extremely engrossing while others were too long and had too many trial transcripts which made them rather boring. It is worth reading, however, as we get an insider's look at some fairly obvious errors committed in some of the trials which will make you shake your head in disbelief. The book was somewhat of a disappointment but still entertaining.
