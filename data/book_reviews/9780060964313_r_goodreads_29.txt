This was assigned reading for my Civil War & Reconstruction class. It is an abridged version of the author's "Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution", cutting the original more or less in half. I have not read the original so I can offer no comparison.

I have mixed feelings about this book. The information seemed mostly good, but often out of chronological order which made some of it seem hard to follow. The writing didn't seem terrible, but the enjoyability was low. That may be simply an issue of the subject material; Reconstruction is probably a depressing subject for everyone. Foner's perspective is at times interesting, but his political beliefs are painfully apparent at times due to his excessive use of the terms "capital", "labor", and "bourgeoisie". It concerns me how much he may be seeing history through a Marxist lens.

Overall, the summary of Reconstruction I got from this book: Most Southerners refused to change and eventually Northerners got distracted by other problems and simply stopped caring (and in most cases their sympathy had limits from the beginning anyway). Redistribution of land from plantation owners to ex-slaves might have improved conditions for blacks. Other half-measures and corruption contributed to Reconstruction's failure.
