The Bloody Chamber is a 40-year-old short story collection that reads like something that could be published today; dark, feminist, and piercing.

Each story is an exquisitely written retelling of a classic fairy or folktale, and while all were interesting, some resonated with me more than others.


The Bloody Chamber (Bluebeard): My favourite story of the entire collection. Exquisite prose, evocative imagery, and slowly building tension that morphs into horror in the most delicious way. Oh, and the women in this tale have far more agency than the original! (5 stars)

"And, ah! his castle. The faery solitude of the place; with its turrets of misty blue, its courtyard, its spiked gate, his castle that lay on the very bosom of the sea with seabirds mewing about its attics, the casements opening onto the green and purple, evanescent departures of the ocean, cut off by the tide from land for half a day ... that castle, at home neither on the land nor on the water, a mysterious, amphibious place, contravening the materiality of both earth and the waves, with the melancholy of a mermaiden who perches on her rock and waits, endlessly, for a lover who had drowned far away, long ago. That lovely, sad, sea-siren of a place!" (9)


The Courtship of Mr. Lyon (Beauty and the Beast): I enjoyed the role reversal here but felt the character development was a bit too uneven to pull it off. It didn't draw me in or leave any lasting impression. (2 stars)


The Tiger's Bride (Beauty and the Beast): So much potential but felt too short to fully express the ideas Carter was starting to develop. I loved the weirdness but didn't connect to the characters. (2 stars)


Puss-in-Boots (...this one's obvious, I think): I was very pleasantly surprised by this one! It was more lighthearted than the others, funny and irreverent, with lots of madcap misadventures. (4 stars)


The Erl-King (based on the ballad of the same name by Goethe): An incredibly eerie atmosphere with hauntingly lyrical prose, this story's structure mirrors the fate of a young maiden lured into the web of the mysterious lord of the wood. (5 stars)

"He strips me to my last nakedness, that underskin of mauve, pearlized satin, like a skinned rabbit; then dresses me again in an embrace so lucid and encompassing it might be made of water. And shakes over me dead leaves as if into the stream I have become." (111)


The Snow Child (Snow White): A slash of a story. A jarring window into internalized misogyny and male fantasies of women (and girls) as sexual objects with no other purpose. (4 stars)


The Lady of the House of Love (Sleeping Beauty/Dracula/Jack and the Beanstalk): A vampire is caged in a dilapidated castle, trapped by a curse passed down through the generations, ever longing for a future she'll never have. A tale of contrasts and shifting perspectives that give an otherwise haunting and sad story a more humourous tone. (4 stars)

"She herself is a haunted house. She does not possess herself; her ancestors sometimes come and peer out of the windows of her eyes and that is very frightening. She has the mysterious solitude of ambiguous states; she hovers in a no-man's land between life and death, sleeping and waking, behind the hedge of spiked flowers, Nosferatu's sanguinary rosebud. The beastly forebears on the walls condemn her to a perpetual repetition of their passions." (130)


The Werewolf (Red Riding Hood): Short and not so sweet. An exploration of mass projection of our collective fears into devils: whether witch, vampire, or werewolf. An externalization of our deepest darkest desires, an avenue for condemnation without self-destruction. (3 stars)


The Company of Wolves (also Red Riding Hood): My favourite of the three wolf stories, a little absurd and tongue-in-cheek. (4 stars)


Wolf-Alice (and once more, Red Riding Hood): A role reversal where Alice is our wolf slowly finding her humanity. Interesting, but not my favourite. (3 stars)

"She inhabits only the present tense, a fugue of the continuous, a world of sensual immediacy as without hope as it is without despair." (154)


All in all, The Bloody Chamber was an engaging and thought-provoking collection that has lingered on my mind for weeks afterward. I would wholeheartedly recommend it!



Trigger/Content Warnings: death, age gap relationship, murder, torture, blood, gore, body horror, sexual assault of a child
