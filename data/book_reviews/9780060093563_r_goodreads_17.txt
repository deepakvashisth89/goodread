I'm going with four stars on this, though perhaps I'd bump that up to five stars on a re-read. I loved the setting of this book in southern France in towns like Avignon, Nimes, and Marseilles. I always love when a book sends me scurrying to my phone to pull up Google Maps and Wikipedia. The two other things I particularly loved about this book are the pre-teen boy, David, and several plot twists that I did not see coming at all. There is usually an element in Mary Stewart's books about not knowing who to trust and that was particularly strong in this novel and added in extra drama that I particularly enjoyed. The heroine, Charity Selbourne, befriends David and soon finds that there is a plot afoot involving David and his family. Even before she learns these things, Charity has a kinship with David as they explore Avignon and Nimes with its ancient history in the white-hot days of summer. The post-WWII setting is interesting as well because it shows how those years still cast their shadow in both moving and providential ways.
