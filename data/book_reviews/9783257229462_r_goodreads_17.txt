I understand this novel is a satire. I also understand that this book was published posthumously and so right now, Jane Austen may very well be rolling in her grave saying "Oh God, I can't believe they published Northanger Abbey." I understand I am one of very few people who feel this way. However, I do feel this way.

You can't just write a book about incredibly irritating characters who are flustered by very trivial situations and then say "oh no, but it's a satire books that have incredibly irritating characters who are flustered by very trivial situations."

That's fantastic. But satire or no, I am still reading a book about incredibly irritating characters who are flustered by very trivial situations! It's similar to people liking things ironically. The irony is irrelevant. You still like the thing.

Look, it didn't escape me. I saw the satire. I particularly enjoyed Mrs. Allen and Isabella. I didn't even mind Catherine. She was quite sweet, really. But for me it was just all a little tedious. And I imagined Henry to be older than Catherine. He treated her like a little sister more than anything, but maybe that's what flirting was like in 1817.



However, I'm incredibly excited to read Pride and Prejudice again. when I was about 17 and I've been waiting almost ten years to revisit it. I have an idea that is actually much worse than P&P and so if I've given this two stars I might even give P&P three! How very thrilling.
