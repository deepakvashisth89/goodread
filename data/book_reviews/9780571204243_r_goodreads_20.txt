High 3. A humorous and poignant look at the decline of old-fashioned print journalism in the face of the challenge of television broadcasting. The reader is parachuted into the mundanity of John Dyson's work as sub-editor of the crosswords and nature notes department of an unspecified newspaper. In the middle of a mid-life crisis, he is deperate to escape this journalistic backwater and establish his credentials as a broadcaster. Frayn mercilessly pokes fun at Dyson and his office colleagues' failed hopes for professional advancement as they devote hours in their favourite watering-holes bemoaning the class structure of the time as well as the situation at the office.
