A classic Wilbur Smith oeuvre!Smith takes the violent, amoral psychopath Sean Courteney Deuce ofRagewho beats his women and wantonly mutilates enemy dead and makes him the protagonist of this novel, who weeps inconsolably when his Ndebele number two dies. In parallel, he turns the pampered princess Claudia Montero, who starts out a lot like Kathy Shower's petulant character inThe Further Adventures of Tennessee Buckand turns her into a realist.Also far more grounded in reality thatElephant Songin that Sean's guerillas, like the real life Executive Outcomes, haul mortars with them while Armstrong's guerillas inElephant Songdo not.The plot itself is smooth while being entirely unpredictable. Smith gives enough length to not only fully develop the characters, but also to convey over a long time the stark austerity of operating in the bush with only one's wits and, above all, luck as tools.Also, Sean, as anyone who has readRageknows, is certainly no "hero" or "good guy," but the coldly calculating and manipulating General China, who can suppress his lust for vengeance in exchange for tactical advantage, is worse. In this sense, Smith has done humanity a great service by promoting the world as it is, as opposed to churning out yet another Karl May/Zane Grey-style Scolaxis/Parsifal rendering.
