I'm really into perfume, so it made sense to pick this one up. There is, in fact, a lot about specific perfumes and what goes into making them, and that information is really interesting, as is the central figure (Luca Turin) himself. The anecdote about a woman whose sense of smell changes suddenly so that everything smells suddenly revolving is fascinating. However, the beginning of the book is bogged down with details about molecules that I and probably the average non-scientist reader would be tempted to skim through. Once we get into the sections where Turin is trying to get his work published, the book really becomes about academic in-fighting and the absolute nonsense that is scientific publishing, which will be both funny and very familiar for anyone who has worked in scientific research or academia. Though it can be dry at times, this book is a must-read for anyone who wants to understand the scent industry and how our sense of smell works (spoiler alert, this book can't give a definitive answer on that!)
