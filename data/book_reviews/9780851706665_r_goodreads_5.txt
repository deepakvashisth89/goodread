Kaplan’s choice to bookend this collection of essays discussing films of the ‘40s and ‘50s with Gledhill’s two-part essay about Klute (a film from 1971) highlights the continued relevance of the noir style in unpacking revisionist criticism.

The choice also challenges future film readers to utilize previously marginalized lenses (feminist and queer for example) when engaging with filmic texts which may appear superficial and shallow at a first glance.

Like any collection there’s bound to be some ideological discord from essay to essay (is film noir a style, a genre, both, or neither?) but aside from a few sentences provoking eye rolls, this brief anthology succeeds in expanding the scope of analysis for the subcategory of film noir.
