I had read several reviews prior to reading this book, so I knew it wasn't a typical mystery; nor was it typical of Georgette Heyer.The Penhallows are a very dysfunctional family, which is always fun to read about. The patriarch, Adam Penhallow, is a mean-spirited, cantankerous, selfish, improvident old man who delights to keep his many children under his thumb. It's no wonder he is murdered.As I said above, this is not a typical murder mystery. It's more psychological study, but it's a lot of fun for most of the book.I was fine with the long buildup to the murder. I was even fine with Ms. Heyer showing us outright who the murderer was. The one thing I cannot forgive isthat she had Raymond, my favorite of all the siblings, both legitimate and illegitimate, commit suicide.That just ruined the book for me. Still, it gets 4 stars because it's very well written and has such great characters.
