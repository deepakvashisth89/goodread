The illustrations in this book are just wonderful and it's worth it just for that.The story itself is so very simple that it practically tells itself. It concerns an imaginative boy barely missing meetings with a disappointed young prince and his increasingly large entourage. In fact, the story is so simple and predictable, that I could probably recite it from memory even though its been more than a year since I read it. But there is a certain power in that, especially for children of a simple age who will love the repitition precisely for its predictablity.As I said, the illustrations are incredible, and the incongruity of the gayly dressed royal entourage looking like a pack of living playing cards, ascending and descending the stairs of the dark, drab and confined tenement apartment I think will charm and amuse readers of all ages. Apparantly the New Times choose this as a notable children's book of the year at one point, but its sadly obscure now.
