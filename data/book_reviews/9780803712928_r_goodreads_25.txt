Seven Candles For Kwanzaa by Andrea Davis Pinkney is the story of an American holiday that is inspired by African traditions and came to be known as Kwanzaa. The book has a wonderful introduction where it explains to the reader what Kwanzaa means and from where it originated from. There are no specific characters, but the book illustrates an African American family celebrating and showing the reader what each day of Kwanzaa means and the traditions that go along with each day. The book uses many traditional words used on the Kwanzaa holiday, as well as translating it for the reader and the specific meaning to those words. For each day of Kwanzaa a candle is lighted to celebrate a special principle.The candles are placed on the kinara, which is a wooden candle holder, and holds seven candles, three red, one black, and three green. The black candle is the first to be lighted and this is to celebrate umoja, which means unity. From the there the next candle to be lighted is the red candle to the right and after that the green candle to the left. After lighting all the candles throughout the seven days, the family is seen celebrating with a big dinner and lots of dancing.Seven Candles For Kwanzaa is not just about a holiday but shows the reader what being a family means. It is also a very educational book because uses real facts about what Kwanzaa is. One comes across many traditional words that are used during the holiday of Kwanzaa, which gives readers the opportunity to also expand their vocabulary in another language. The illustrations included are bright and detailed and flows with the story very well. I would definitely recommend this book to children to show them how other cultures celebrate different holidays. I very much enjoyed it and learned something new about another culture while reading this book.
