One of my favorites of the 'Dear America' series so far. Despite some typos, it was well written and I related to the character. It helps that Molly was 15 and turned 16 during the book as a lot of the 'Dear America' books tend to gear a little younger. It means that there was more mature subjects (nothing too intense) which was realistic for the time period they were talking about. I will say it was fairly depressing reading the Historical Notes section though due to
the author mentioning that it seems possible soon that America will not discriminate and we'll hear about our victories AND failures, when it sadly hasn't been all that true. I look forward to reading her brother's book to get the more gritty side of things.
