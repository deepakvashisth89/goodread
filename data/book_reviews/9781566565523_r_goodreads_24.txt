“New Pearl Harbor” was a great read. Though I have watched several documentaries and read a lot online about the many controversies surrounding 9/11, this was the first book I had ever read on the topic. Griffin raises many legitimate questions and presents convincing evidence to support a full-scale, independent investigation of what happened that day. He also presents different gradations of the complicity theory, as he calls it. These gradations scale up from non-complicity by the White House and complicity of others in government to full-scale complicity and planning aid by the White House. I liked this approach, as it indicates that there can be a problem with the official story without it implying that the Bush administration helped to plan the attacks.Griffin focuses on the Bush administration’s reactions to the events and the many inconsistencies between the official story and the evidence that we have. On the surface, the Bush administration’s reactions were cause enough for concern. Why did Bush stay in the classroom reading to children when the country was under attack? Why wasn’t he rushed out of that room to a secure location? Bush and his staff showed no urgency that day, indicating that they believed themselves to be safe. The repeated breakdowns at every level of defense from the White House to the Defense Department to FAA were extremely troubling and simply unbelievable. I believe that the truly ludicrous theory is to believe that that many people all happened to fail miserably at once on the same day. With the billions upon billions of dollars this country spends on defense each year, I cannot believe it. Griffin also writes significantly about the U.S.’s ties with the ISI in Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and the bin Laden family. Taken as a whole, this speculation could very well provide the canvas on which complicity would be painted. There were plenty of links to enable coordination between different parties prior to and after the attack.The best evidence comes in the inconsistencies surrounding the “plane” that hit the Pentagon, the Building 7 collapse, and Flight 93. If you have ever read any 9/11 Truth literature, you would likely have seen most of this evidence already. However, it is still worth reading. All evidence seems to support that Flight 93 was shot down; Griffin’s theory that it was shot down because the passengers were regaining control of the plane is certainly plausible. According to all physical evidence, Flight 77 could not have hit the Pentagon. It simply does not add up. All that the FBI needs to do is to release one video of this event to clear up all of these questions, yet they still have not done so (the two released videos do not actually show the plane). Looked at with an open mind, there is little question that Building 7’s collapse was induced by controlled demolition. Why has there been so little discussion about Building 7? These questions do not even consider the highly problematic Twin Tower collapses. Why did the 9/11 Commission ignore all of the eyewitness claims of explosions? There is so much here to make one question the official story, and taken as an aggregate it is impossible for me to believe that we were told everything that day.If you ever have a chance, please watch the “September 11 – The New Pearl Harbor” documentary which, though not related to this book, presents much of the same evidence with more elaboration. It was also released in 2013, so it incorporated evidence that was not available to Griffin when he wrote this book. This is a fascinating topic and I believe that it is unjust to classify 9/11 Truthers as nutjobs. As the internet brings more evidence to a wider audience, I believe we will see more questions entering mainstream discourse. We cannot wait much longer to open this independent investigation; the witnesses will continue to fade away and evidence will become more difficult to obtain with age. At best, if the official story is true, I have no faith in the American government to do anything competently. If they cannot prevent a plane from hitting the Pentagon when it is well-known that the country is under attack, why should any money go toward defense? If it cannot perform this most elementary duty, what use is a standing army? I, of course, am realistic and I realize the value of a standing army (though I’d prefer it to be used for actual defense rather than war adventurism abroad); however, if the incompetence theory was actually true, how could we trust military officials and the Defense Department to competently administer a standing army? At the other end of the spectrum, at worst, the U.S. government helped to plan attacks that killed 3,000 Americans and countless more civilians in the Middle East. There really is no way for the Bush administration, military officials, NORAD officials, and the FAA to come out of this looking good, yet somehow no one lost his or her job or was disciplined for incompetence. We desperately need a deeper investigation to answer all of these questions and to help a large minority of people to have some semblance of trust in their government going forward.
