In Dreaming of the Bones, Deborah Crombie stretches a bit beyond her earlier mysteries, and I really liked the result. The premise reaches back decades and leans more literary and psychological, which made this installment feel like a step forward rather than more of the same.

I especially appreciated the continued, very organic growth between Duncan Kincaid and Gemma James — their relationship evolves at a natural pace, full of uncertainty in a way that feels earned rather than shoved in to slow it down. The mystery itself is layered and interesting, though it’s told through a few too many multiple points of view, including a written account from Lydia that I mostly enjoyed. Lydia is a compelling figure, though she felt a bit too closely modeled on Sylvia Plath for me — something I probably noticed more because I’d just finished .

The final reveal didn’t quite work for me. What may not have been overdone at the time this was first published felt a little tired now. Still, between the characters, the atmosphere, and Crombie’s steady, confident writing, this remains a solid entry in a series that continues to hold my interest.
