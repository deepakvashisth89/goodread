Ramage is like a fever dream. There is a long literary canon of adventurers who wander through a lifetime of excitement by making nearly every correct choice and winning nearly every gamble. With Ramage, you can remove the adverb “nearly” and everything will just turn out. Not only will it come out right, but do so in the best possible way. A bit of a spoiler here, a named character is lost in this one, but over the last eight novels, it may be the first.
It’s so unnecessary and so uninteresting. Pope has done a good job building some intricate scenarios. By pulling through with the best possible outcome and the occasional scar (which in turn seems to help with the ladies) there is no growth. The books still tell a compelling story, but the endings are always, “he bested them all, made a ton of prize money, everyone got promoted, and his lady love is none the wiser to his most recent dalliance.”
