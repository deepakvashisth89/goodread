This is an expert level analytical presentation of the USS Thresher disaster. Direct quotes and 1st person information from the board of inquiry display the obvious personal connections of the author to the US Navy and the incident specifically. The parts of the book published in 1964 are insightful and well written and offer a good base knowledge for an understanding of all that we really can possibly know about the Thresher disaster. Unfortunately the updated and modern information (which you can tell for the most part by its insertion into the original work as well as most of the last few chapters speaking of events that occurred post-1964) are where things become less impressive. There are numerous typos and unfortunate errors that present the reader with misinformation that may be dangerous even to readers with less first hand or literary experience with submarines and undersea history. Most glaring: the author refers to the Russian Navy as the Soviet Navy throughout his entire description of the 2000 Kursk disaster. He also claims at one point (I’m sure simply a careless mistake and not a deliberate error) that the massive secondary explosion onboard the Kursk included detonation of nuclear torpedo warheads. Patently false and frankly impossible. Sadly, the new additions to this book suffer from errors and an editor who obviously lacked any technical or historical knowledge of submarines and their unfortunate disasters over the years. 4 stars for the fantastic contemporary description and insight into the Thresher disaster. Recommend 1964 first edition without any modern information added post hoc.
