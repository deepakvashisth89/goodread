The first two times I read this, I was lost. The multidimensional storytelling angles left me confused, the sudden changes in perspective left me frustrated, and the stream-of-consciousness thought-patterns made me give up. But reading this again knowing these obstacles, I was prepared.

I would describe this as I have mentioned above, but this time it felt dark, haunting, and gothic. The story basically is two college roomates: one telling a story (Quentin Compson) to the other (Shreve) while he attempts to reflect, internalize, & clarify what he's hearing. The setting for the two college roomates was in their dorm room at Harvard University. The storytelling started on a cold, winter evening and moved late into the early morning hours. Within this overarching dialogue between the two roomates, there were other narrators to create the "story within a story" effect.

The story was complex and hard to follow as it delved into southern history, culture, and social complexities. The story was about an individual named Henry Supten that came to town and built a plantation. As the different narratives progress, the story expands into glimpses of Antebellum Mississippi, the Civil War, post-Civil War, & family relations while focuing on the rise and fall of Henry Supten. Underlying this even further was a sense of darkness: this was characterized by the ghost & haunted house motif, themes of hereditary evil, and the personification of Henry Supten as .

There was a shifting narrator pattern that was unpredictable yet somehow it created mystery to the story. Intertwined in all the narrators were inconsistencies in what actually happened. For me as the reader, I had to piece everything together and attempt to find cohesion. Because of this, I will probably read this again in a few years.

Reading this again gave me a sense of accomplishment. I felt William Faulkner's writing style is an adjustment, requires patience & a distraction-free reading environment. Overall I would recommend this anyone who wants a challenge. Thanks!
