I can sure dig the intense conflicting emotions which Forster brings to the forefront of this anxious novel, read for my Modern Novel course at Queen's U. in 1971.

Forster was an ideal foil for my devastated psyche that year.

In 1970 I had tried to uphold the high ideals of my ego, first threatened, then defeated by the easy-going will of the uni majority - just as the ladies' affable and harmless Indian acquaintances are here crushed by the falling curtain of British imperialism.

There was in both instances a political price to be paid for being yourself.

How was I, in my innocence, to know ordinary ethics were noxious?

How was Dr. Aziz to know that he was not quite playing cricket?

For both of us heaven and earth fell asunder because of our native naivety...

And the world would never, ever be the same again.
