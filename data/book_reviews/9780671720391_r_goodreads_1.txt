For what it's worth, I read this without having read any of the previous books in the shared world series so I may not have the full appreciation of the characters featured here. In a general sense the book delivered the light-hearted adventure romp that I was expecting, but in a poorly executed unimaginative way. Many characters were one dimensional, plot was rather predictable.
The world building had some strange inconsistencies and seemed sloppy which is strange for the 8th book in a shared world series. Attempts were made to titillate the reader with scenes of topless women and mixed gender bathing but these tended to fall flat and lacked creativity. The word "buxom" was used to describe so many females, you have to wonder if there is a flat-chested woman to be found anywhere on the planet!
The basic premise of traveling about and mixing with the natives on a world in a medieval stage of development has potential for fish out of water problems and some swashbuckling fun but it is not to be found in this book. L. Sprague de Camp has a well-earned place among the founding fathers of sci-fi and fantasy, but this one misses the mark.
