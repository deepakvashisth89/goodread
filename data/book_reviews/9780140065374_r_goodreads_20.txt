Having lived in Australia for a number of years, Robert Barnard was well qualified to write a send-up of the state of academia there and of the nature of police work. The old goat, Professor Belville-Smith, is a doddering lecturer who has come to Drummondale University on a tour, and is brutally murdered in his motel room. Relationships among the professors, instructors, and teaching assistants are complicated, due in no small part to the machinations of Lucy Wickham, the wife of the English Department Chair. Police Inspector Royle has never conducted a murder investigation, and has little inclination or the skills to do so in this case, so Bill Bascomb, a lecturer newly arrived from England himself, begins to put things together. Most of the story is quite droll, but I did wonder what the Aussies thought of it.
