A skillfully written book, from dialogue to imagery to pace. Also the funniest of Highsmith’s books that I have read so far. The violence in the book takes more time to play out than her other novels, with the main event not taking place until most of the way through the book. It’s more experimental, slice of life, dare I say uplifting than her other books.Yet there is still suspense and a good deal of the uneasiness and tension is built up starting early on. I appreciated the depiction of conflicting views on abortion at the time, because history is always more complicated than we remember (because people are complicated).I enjoyed the themes of religious deconstruction, obsession, family, personal growth, sex, love, heartbreak, hope, estrangement, acceptance and friendship. There was some fatphobia but overall it was a strong defense of women’s right to choose and a strong criticism of religious moral fundamentalism.
