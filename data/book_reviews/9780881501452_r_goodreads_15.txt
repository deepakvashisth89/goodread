This 1932 novel is the second in the Asey Mayo series. Mayo is an old fashioned Cape Codder who gets involved in investigating murders on the Cape. The books are narrated by Prudence Whitsby, a Boston matron from an old Boston family, who keeps stumbling into the middle of mysterious murders.In early March, off season for the Cape, Prudence gets convinced to go to Wellfleet with a friend for a week. When they get there, they are invited to visit Adelbert Stires' fancy big Wellfleet house . He is an industrialist who they both know from Boston.Stires has several house guests as well as a mysterious niece from France at the home. Stires is not home but is expected to return shortly. A freak March snowstorm snows them in. The phones and electricity are out. The roads are closed. Stires arrives in the middle of the storm. He goes off to bed. The next morning, he is dead. It is quickly determined that he has been poisoned.Taylor delivers on this classic murder plot of a murder committed by someone in an isolated group. She develops three or four convincing suspects. Red herrings abound. There is a very clever murder technique. The ending is fair.The 1930s Cape setting is well done. she shows how remote Wellfleet was in those days. It was exotic. Candles were still used widely for lighting and people had their favorite candle makers, which becomes a plot point in the book. Old fashioned electric cars were still around, and older woman remembered their days as suffragettes.This is a very good old-fashioned murder mystery in an unusual setting.
