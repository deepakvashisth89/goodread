The writing here is very strong and often funny. The early sections of the book dealing with Alex’s childhood are, I think, the most engaging. My problem was, as Alex grows up (or doesn’t), he becomes such an unlikable character. Alex is not only self-centered and callow, he made me feel worried for the women he entered into relationships with. Does he even like women? He is unable to engage them with any depth or empathy. His fantasies and the sex itself is often tinged with violence. (The 2000 novel actually seems to predict the misogynistic manosphere of today). I was concerned that the blurbs on the back, as well as the reviews I read, made no mention of his real
borderline behavior with women (and the obsession with breasts; I thought I might count how often the women of the book were introduced with a description of their breasts). There is no growth by the end of the book, which ends with a cryptic comment by his mother about resisting change - while I was hoping that summer kind of radical change would redeem him.
