#4 in the Josefina main series in my opinion. It's a tricky one to rate, because this one felt less slow than the first few Josefina books (or perhaps I'm just used to the pace of these books by now), but I also wasn't a huge fan of the plot. I saw the Tia Dolores and Papa romance plot line coming from several books back and I know it's historically accurate to the time period, but it just didn't thrill me. I did like the growth and self-reflection Josefina has that having Tia Dolores around has eased some of the grief in her heart from Mama's death and her prediction that Tia Dolores leaving will feel like losing a maternal figure all over again; that all makes a lot of sense to me.And it was frustrating to observe how social norms keep the girls so constrained and in practice work against everyone's interests. Cultural norms say that Josefina isn't allowed to bring ideas to her parents or intervene in adult affairs, even though in this case Josefina had knowledge that other people in the situation did not have, and it would have been to their detriment if Josefina hadn't spoken up. I do love Josefina trying to find a solution that respects her culture's norms while also doing what's best relationally.Josefina continues to be a lovely, sweet little girl who just wants to make the people around her happy. Josefina blaming herself for Tia Dolores not feeling needed hurt my heart. I understand that her culture teaches children (and girls in particular) to be cautious and maybe submissive, but her world seems kind of small and I wish she could want more for herself and get all the love and care she deserves. She is so focused on other people; I wish someone in her life would take a bit more notice of her and give her some things that are just for her.I do think her depiction in this book is a very realistic one of a nine-year-old girl growing up in the time and place she was growing up in, and I liked her growth throughout the series (initially she is still grieving so hard over Mama, but then Tia Dolores comes into her life and helps her build confidence and discover new ways of relating to her grief; it doesn't go away, but the rest of her life grows around it). So overall even though the pace of the series might not have been the most to my taste, I still enjoyed these books and I'm glad I read them and got to know Josefina!
