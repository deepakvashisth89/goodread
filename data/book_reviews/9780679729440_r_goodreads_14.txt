There is nothing that Brookner could give to the world that I would not love. She is one of those writers I trust. It is a slightly peculiar thing to say that but that is exactly what I have come to see my relationship with Brookner's writing develop into. A relationship of trust where I know the kind of story I'm getting into and she expects that belief and works to write it in way that fulfils it. This 1989 novel follows the story of a young man named Lewis Percy. He is on his way to complete a doctorate in Literature where he's studying the representation of heroes in the 19th century French novels. He is a quiet, reticent man. His father is long dead. His mother lives in her drab old flat in London. But Lewis has to leave Paris and settle in London when his mother passes away suddenly. From there we follow the English hero get swept in the throes of life; choices made and unmade, promises kept and forgotten. He is living a life where desolation grows every passing day, where the home he lives in begins to taunt him of his sequestration. As his doctorate ends, he takes the job as a librarian, and marries, the life is anything but about the heroes he has studied. Here, we have the portrait of an unhappy hero, a hero that never was, a character who remained a mere protagonist without ever really being the hero of his own life, forget someone else's. I was heartbroken by the book. Following Lewis was unbearable. I almost wished for Brookner to not make his life so sad, so lonely, and so quiet. She brings about the quietness in his life with a loud sigh and a ghostliness that made me swallow the discomfort that rose in my throat. As much as I love her writing, in this she repeated herself quiet a lot, I feel that some tightening and editing would have made the read more smoother and similarly impactful. By the end, I almost wept as I do with most of her novels. You just follow this character and as the tale comes to a close, you almost don't want to leave them alone, or you want for them to be happy, to see them happy even for a second. What a character! She knows how to write about men as stunningly as she does with her women!
