I discovered this book through a Google search looking for mysteries set in Paris. The book opens with a "message from the editor" who says this manuscript was among the boxes his grandfather wanted salvaged before moving into the nursing home. Silly me! I took this literally instead of "literary."

So I read this "mystery" as if it were a discovered first time novel. I didn't realize that Engel is well thought of in his native Canada and has published many books. His other books may be great but after reading this one, I'm not motivated at all to check his work out.

This book is a bastardization of Ernest Hemingway's "Moveable Feast." I forced myself to keep reading, waiting and hoping for that little twist, that new take or unique spin on where this story was going. I didn't like any of the characters. Their lives seemed to revolve around drinking from one cafe/bar to another.

The very premise that a serial killer is loose in Paris and the French are comparing him to London's Jack the Ripper so they call him Jack de Paris??? At the very least, make it Jacques de Paris!!

Because this book focuses so much time and effort on regurgitating "Moveable Feast," it is not written like a typical mystery where clues are scattered here and there and you try to figure out who done it.

Instead, the narrator suddenly transforms from naive Canadian who just wants to get laid ("find me a girl" -- he sleeps with a woman one time and is in love with her???) into this Mister Detective who explains everything when all the cast is corralled into a bar room.

Sorry, but this book was overall extremely disappointing. The only thing I got out of it that was food for thought was the issue of just how did Hemingway's "friends" feel about being exposed in his best sellers?
