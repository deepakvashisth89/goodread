Enough time has passed—even Ned Rorem is dead now—that the casual cruelty of Capote's characterizations & disclosures can be enjoyed simply on the level of juicy gossip, tuned to a specific key of old school gay bitchiness that Capote managed to turn into a type of art across the last stages of his life & career. Capote kills his darlings here; I often just wished we got a better sense of what it meant to make such sacrifices in this way.

To read Answered Prayers is to strap into a roller coaster ride that veers precariously between the insightful & the downright tawdry. Which is, of course, the great fun of it all, & P.B. Jones, our raucous, idealized narrator—he's both impossibly sexy & excessively literate—is a great hang. He is primarily why Capote's posthumous text manages to work so well on a sentence-to-sentence level, even if it kind of evaporates on contact (I'm having to keep thumbing through my copy as I write this to remind myself what these pages exactly contain).

I'm glad this exists at all, but it can't be described as anything but one of literature's great ruins.
