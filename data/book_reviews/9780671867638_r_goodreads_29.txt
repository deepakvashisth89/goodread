If you’re deep into true crime, The Riverman is an interesting but very dense read. Written by Robert Keppel, one of the detectives who worked the Ted Bundy case, this book dives into Bundy’s "consultation" on the Green River Killer investigation. Bundy, ever the manipulative narcissist, basically tries to play Hannibal Lecter, offering insights into a killer’s mind while also trying to stay in control of the conversation. Some parts are genuinely chilling—he clearly enjoys talking about murder—but also frustrating, because he’s constantly playing games.The biggest downside? This book is long and technical. Some sections drag, especially if you’re not super into law enforcement procedures or Bundy’s rambling attempts at relevance. The writing can also feel a bit repetitive at times. If you’re fascinated by criminal profiling and don’t mind a very detailed, slow-moving true crime book, you might get something out of this. But if you’re looking for a fast-paced, gripping read, this one might be a little too dry.
