I give this 5 stars not so much for having enjoyed reading it, but because of the historical account it provides. The plight of the Nez Perce people on their journey to escape to Canada is one of sorrow and often times anger inducing. A tragedy, really."That is the way a white man remembers a battle. So many soldiers here, so many there. Such a captain here. Such a lieutenant there. This colonel in one place. That major in another. But not an Indian. An Indian remembers where his mother fell bayoneted, or his little brother has his skull smashed, or his big sister cried for mercy and was shot in the mouth."This novel, from the perspective of an aging Nez Perce who survived the journey is a great read.
