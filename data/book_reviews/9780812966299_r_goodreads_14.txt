I got my copy of this book on holiday in Devon as a child, probably on a Wednesday afternoon. The bookshop was shut, but there was a shelf of books outside with sign asking you to put the money under the door if you wanted something and for twenty pence I had myself a copy.It is a Vikings meet Zulus story, noble savages and fearless adventurerswith false teethcrossed with the mythical wealth of King Solomon from the old testament with a hidden heir and a treasure map book. It comes of course with the best advice for any traveller determined to bring about a coup de etat in a hidden kingdom populated by noble savages - always know when the next eclipse is due.Of particular interest in the nineteenth century racial thinking of the author is that when the White men are armoured in antique battle gear and armed with battle axes and spears they are revealed to be intrinsically skilled in their use. Which I suppose just goes to show why racists have such high self-esteem.Naturally not all the savages are noble - some of them (dramatic pause) are women, or as they are also known in the story: witches. Ah, there is nothing quite like Victorian values.
