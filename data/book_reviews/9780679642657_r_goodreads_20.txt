How much I loveSuttree— its density, with a bitter touch of humor and unrelenting bleakness. I used to think thatBlood Meridianwas the pinnacle of Cormac McCarthy’s work, but now I see thatSuttreeis something truly special, and, in some ways,Meridiangrew out of it. There’s this sense of otherworldly, Old Testament loneliness that permeates the novel from start to finish.McCarthy explores themes of death, guilt, family ties, and the futility of existence, all through rich symbolism and philosophical musings about life on society's fringes. The novel evokes an existential dread in an atmosphere of meditative reflection, where time seems to stretch, and the river becomes a symbol of the relentless flow of life.This is an epic yet intimate work about the search for meaning in a world devoid of simple answers. Its dark beauty and complexity are impossible to ignore.Suttreeisn’t just a novel; it’s a profound dive into the human soul, full of contradictions, pain, and glimpses of humor that make the bleakness even more palpable.
