Constitutional lawyer Robert Bork pins the decline of American morality squarely on the shoulders of the liberal left and their radical agenda.

Bork wrote this work in the mid 1990s and sifts through the moral milieu of his day. Crime, welfare, abortion, radical feminism, affirmative action, the U.S. Supreme Court, and more come under the fire of his pen. Much of his assessment is correct in that the political left have has taken over major pillars of society and are now pushing a radical agenda that is sending America into a moral tailspin.

I will not break down each topic here, but overall Bork's analysis of the liberal left is sound. He gives answers to the please for many of their initiatives. Think of this as a (1990s) conservative's guide to the left.

The sad part is that Bork attempts to face all of these issues from a secular (though politically conservative) angle. In fact, the chapter on crime ends with a lament that he doesn't know there is any answer for rampant crime or any way to get it under control. Bork claimed to be a protestant before being baptized into the Catholic church later in life, but no religious worldview seems to play into this work (and that may be by design).

In closing, Bork does repine, "Perhaps the most promising development in our time is the rise of an energetic, optimistic, and politically sophisticated religious conservatism. It may prove more powerful than merely political or economic conservatism because religious conservatism's objectives are cultural and moral as well."

One thing that does come to the front in his analysis of the liberal mind is the idea of "liberty". Bork helped me understand why our Founding Fathers believed America's government would only work for a moral people. He makes the point that "liberty" is always pushing against constraints. Even proper barriers are pushed against by "liberty" and only a moral people know where to make liberty stop. When morals have been dashed, there is nowhere for "liberty" to stop and it turns into licentiousness and debauchery. I think this is really what Bork is trying to say.

It is interesting to hear how Bork deals with the political issues of the mid 90s, and I don't think he would recognize the social landscape that we are in today. Worth a consideration.
