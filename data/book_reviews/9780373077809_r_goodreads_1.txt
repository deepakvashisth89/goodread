3.5 stars
This was a nice passionate read and made my blood boil seeing how the town bullies/criminals seemed to get away with stuff without consequences; I guess it wanted to show how small-town justice for the rich is different. The hero is a divorced single mom (daughter is 13 and fantastic) who has been alone ever since her disastrous marriage with a rodeo start at 18(hubby didn't know the meaning of fidelity). When the town bully is harassing her and about to hit her, the hero a stranger is the only one who comes to her rescue. The heroine recognises he is the drifter type despite the crackling chemistry between them and intends to stay away since he plans on leaving the next day but when he is injured she helps him recover. The hero is an ex-rodeo star who was injured something that has left him with mental scars while also trying to grapple with his abusive childhood which recently left his family dead (he is beating himself up because he couldn't get his mom and sister out despite asking them to leave so many times). The hero was filled with so much angst about his art (something his abusive dad was good at) and doubts over whether he would also hurt the people he loved. I liked how great he was the heroine's daughter and how much he tried to fight what was happening between himself and the heroine. The heroine was awesome; she recognised what the hero had been through and actually ordered a book to learn more about adult kids from abused families. I loved the ending when all four of them (H's sis is alive) become a nice family unit.
