FascinatingFor anyone interested in the history of the Romanov Dynasty in Russia or who loves a mystery, this is a book you will enjoy. I'm one of those people, so I've done a good amount of reading of Russian history, yet I had never heard about this "legend" of Tsar Alexander I 's mysterious death and its possible connection to a wandering holy man in Siberia. Troubetzkoy presents a good case for the theory that the two men were one and the same. The author is a living member of one of Imperial Russia's most prominent families; thus he has had first-person access to members of the post-revolutionary emigre community in Paris, Canada and elsewhere. In addition to his connections, he has obviously done his homework in libraries across the world locating what documentation exists to prove or disprove this as yet unsolved mystery.I withheld one star because of the excessive repetition of details in this admittedly complicated story. The author is so passionate about this story, I guess he wanted to make sure we didn't miss even one detail. That is my only criticism, however, and his writing is so readable, not plodding or obtuse, you may forgive him this one fault. Like Troubetzkoy, and many others, I hope that the bodies (if bodies there be) of both individuals can be exhumed and tested for DNA so that finally after over 175 years, this question can be answered once and for all.
