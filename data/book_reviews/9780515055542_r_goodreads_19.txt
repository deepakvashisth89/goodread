Aside from another terrific mystery with a fascinating cast, here Marsh allows Troy, Alleyn's wife, to use an appropriate expression, take center stage. Marsh had her appear briefly in earlier books, but here Alleyn and Troy experience something akin to what Lord Peter Wimsey and Harriet Vane go through inBusman's Honeymoon, resolving the conflict between Alleyn's work, and Troy's feelings about it. For most of the series Alleyn's personal life has been kept from us, even when his relationship with Troy began, but here, as they reunite after a 3 year wartime separation, they must both face up to their feelings. It is not a chief part of the plot, but nonetheless,it's an important sub-plot.
