A s one of the earliest and most famous space opera sagas, E. E. “Doc” Smith’s Lensman series should be absolutely my thing.It is not.It is written in a kind of hyper-dated WW2 pilot banter/hard-boiled detective stream-of-consciousness style that most of the time seems to consist of 50% telling the reader what an upright paragon the current protagonist is and 50% waxing lyrical about his honest machismo.All the women are depicted as ornamental in some way, and the author goes out of his way to say that women are physiologically incapable of becoming Lensmen. Imagine if George Lucas had decreed that women couldn’t be Jedi or wield lightsabres—we would have been denied all the awesome and varied characters such as Ahsoka Tano, Mara Jade, Jaina Solo, Bastila Shan, Asajj Ventress, Aayla Secura, Vergere, Etain Tur-Mukan, Luminara Unduli, or even Yaddle.The absolute best thing about the book is Smith’s creatively conceived planets—we are mercifully not subjected to yet another desert/jungle/ice planet. Instead we are treated to inhospitable worlds with insane climates and weird biomes, bizarre cities populated entirely by maniac drivers, and cold dark worlds inhabited by multidimensional sociopaths. It is a pity that the author doesn’t delve any deeper into these cultures and habitats.This is alas the only strong point to this story. The plot is risible, it is impossible to tell heroes from villains without the author pointing it out, and the climax of the book is an election where the ostensibly noble characters win by deploying mass voter intimidation. This is a story where one of the “heroes” advocates for declaring martial law and beginning a campaign of mass executions if they start looking like losing power.And these are heroes who are given special superhuman powers to dispense summary justice by a species of inscrutable aliens engaged in a multi-billion year eugenics program to breed an ultimate weapon to defeat their enemies. It’s like Cthulu giving rings of power to the Knights of the Round Table so that one day their descendent will be Judge Dredd.Definitely influential, but not particularly enjoyable.
