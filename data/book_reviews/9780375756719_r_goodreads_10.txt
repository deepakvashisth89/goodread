“In the life of each of us there is a place remote and islanded, and given to endless regret or secret happiness.”
― Sarah Orne Jewett, The Country of the Pointed Firs and Other Stories

I love Maine.

My first experience in the state of Maine was when I was a small child. My parents would take me and my brother on vacations there.

If you’ve never been to Maine, it would be impossible to understand it’s breathtaking enchantment. Suffice to say there is no place that I’ve ever seen in my country maybe not in the world that is anything like it.

To enter coastal Maine, is to enter paradise. You will see rocky coast, magnificent Rocky, sweeping coasts with ocean as vibrant and as blue as shimmering sapphires and the sea will be crashing all around you.

You will see cottages by the sea. You will see quaint little shops, gift shops, arts, and crafts shops, antique shops.

You will see wooded enclaves.

You will catch your breath in awe at the Rocky vistas welcoming you with arms wide open. Pulling you into the fragrant wildflower that the state of Maine is.

You will see little seafood restaurants with fresh lobster and whole belly, clams, and crab legs. You’ll see adorable and quaint inns to stay at.

And then, as you move inland, you will see woods, and creeks and rivers and lakes and nature

Maine – the way life be . That is their motto.

This book follows the people of Maine, some people of Maine as a writer has come to observe them to see how they live ,to see what kind of people they are.

This book is widely considered a classic so I was a bit surprised that I hadn’t heard of it. I found it only by doing a search for books that take place in Maine. I was hungering for one.

Now , here’s the thing. After my dramatic, build up here, you might think I fell in love with the book and that is not the case. In fact, I hate to admit it, but I really didn’t like it that much.

It doesn’t really have much of a plot. It’s just a little slice of life. I’ve noticed a lot of books that take place in New England are like this.

There is a book that I read a long time ago. That was also a mini series called Empire Falls. That’s a bit more modern than this one ,actually a lot more modern.

It had more of a plot and Empire Falls also was a slice of life, but I enjoyed that one much more.

I had a hard time I will admit with the dialect. And this surprises me too because I’ve read every kind of dialect there is to read, but I did have a very difficult time following this book and I started to get bored very quickly .

I feel almost apologetic, because I love Maine so much. I want to love every single book that takes place in her beautiful valleys.

Unfortunately, this really wasn’t for me. Oh well, what can you do? You can’t love them all can you?

I’ve always had this yearning to live in a glass house on the ocean, in Maine, maybe in Kennebunkport or Bar Harbor or Boothbay Harbor. I doubt that’s going to happen, but the best part of this book was the lovely prose describing Maine to a tee.

I really enjoyed reading about the little white cottages and the Meadows and fields.

But if I’m being honest, I could take or leave the story . I neither liked it nor disliked it, which is why I’m giving it a Three.
