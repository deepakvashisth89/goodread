I seem to find that not a lot of people seem to like Mary Anne, but I do.

At thirteen when I was reading these books originally, I could relate to being a shy brunette that could cry at the drop of a hat. Now in my early forties, guess what?

I still can.

One thing the Mary Anne books prove is that she can be a responsible person and stand up for herself when she needs to. She was finally able to prove herself to her father that he stopped treating her like a little kid and her friends know that at times it is not a good thing to mess with her. Another thing is that Mary Anne has a great rapport with young children as some can be just as shy.

When the BSC gets a new job for a new family, Mary Anne is the first one to babysit for the Martinez family. Their regular babysitter has quit and even though the babysitters will have to take turns, it is no problem. Oldest boy Luke doesn't seem to trust babysitters even though he sticks to Mary Anne like jelly on his baby sister's peanut butter sandwiches.

The club is told that there was a small fire in their garage recently and Luke was able to go to the neighbors and call the fire department but since then he has been very quiet. It isn't until one day that the words DON'T TELL are written on the window that Mary Anne realizes Luke is hiding a secret.

Mary Anne read in her father's paper that local developer Reginald Fowler is looking to put up a mall or something at the edge of the park near a sawmill that has a history with the town. That would mean tearing it down and having families move away...like the Martinez family. Being high school teachers, the couple are perfectly fine where they are despite the fact that others will take the money Fowler offers.

Having solved many mysteries at this time, Mary Anne can't help but think...yet it couldn't be possible? Could a grown man like Fowler be responsible for trying to set fire to the house and intimidate a young child into being silent because he just happened to witness this?

Of course, the other members have their own theories that perhaps even another neighbor could have been responsible. An older man is one of the neighbors and a middle school prankster with younger brothers who like to set off bottle rockets also live nearby.

Another reason the BSC gets involved is that they have been writing letters to the editor to try and save the park and sawmill from demolition. Other Stoneybrook residents have been writing too but the club are constantly persistent in signing their names every time Fowler gives his two cents about "kids" trying to handle big world problems. This guy is also being snide with younger kids from the elementary schools too...what a slime.

The only time it gets really sticky is when Mary Anne goes off to look for Luke when he disappears with his neighbor best friend. In searching, Mary Anne hears a noise and sees someone run off as she comes upon some vandalism done with green bricks being thrown through windows. Right as she innocently picks one up, a police car comes as someone has reported the vandalism.

Reported from a telephone booth where Kristy got the bright idea to *69 a threatening call that came to the Martinez house.

Is Fowler now trying to get the BSC in trouble to stop the protests?

Finding out Luke's big secret does tie-in with all of the stuff concerning Fowler in a very thin way, but the reveals to both storylines are slightly predictable. This isn't a heavy-handed mystery and despite being told by Mary Anne, all of the club is involved. The ending is light-hearted and triumphant, so it is at least enjoyable.

If you happen to like Mary Anne, this mystery is a good one.
