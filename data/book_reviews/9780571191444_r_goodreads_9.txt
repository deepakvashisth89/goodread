Read this thirty years ago; it is the book for which Golding won the Nobel, and in my view he earned it. It is so much better than Lord of the Flies. The other two books in the series are good, but this one is the best. I love the early 19th Century dialogue, and the description of the burial at sea is particularly entertaining, as the Captain and First Mate engage in a sotto voce conversation about maintenance of the rudder pintles, to the point that the Captain loses the thread of the burial service.Golding at his best.
