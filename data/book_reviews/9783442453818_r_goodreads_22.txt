2.5 stars. It took me a month and a half to finish this book, by the end of which I felt like one of Bluebear's exhausted audience members on the 90th round of a congladiator battle - a sort of duel-by-storytelling. I generally enjoy Walter Moers's absurd and madcap imagination, but at just over 700 pages, The 13 1/2 Lives of Captain Bluebear is too much of a good thing.

Bluebear, literally an azure-furred talking bear, begins his adventures as a cub of unknown origin adrift in the sea, minutes away from death by massive sinkhole by a ship of minipirates, harmless tiny beings born with peglegs and hooks. It only gets better from there: he's stranded on mysterious islands where everything is edible and delicious, he's schooled by a famed scholar with multiple brains that are kept outside his head, he goes on a long journey across the Demerara Desert (a sugar desert, of course) and later through a giant's brain, he invents a new type of pizza (the pizza sandwich - recipe included, but no baking time?!).

And so on, for 700 pages. I never felt compelled to keep reading, but enjoyed it enough to not abandon it, and since I can only read one fiction book at a time, I didn't really read anything else in the mean time, which is to say that Bluebear, quite a remarkable bear in many ways, also caused a month and a half long book slump. The word play is fun and Moers's imagination is never in question, but it's not enough to carry a book whose main character and narrator is just okay and comes across as a bit of a Gary Stu.

I finished reading with a mingled feeling of relief and impatience (and pain, having received all of my fall vaccines the day prior). I'll be ready for another Moers in the dead of winter, I think.
