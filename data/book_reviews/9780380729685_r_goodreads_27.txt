This book was given to me to read whilst on a relaxing holiday in Thailand, and it turned out to be anything but light entertainment.Reportage, edited by John Carey, sweeps you from 430 BCE to 1986 CE, stitching together some of the most turbulent moments in human history through the words of the people who actually lived them.What struck me immediately was the rawness: these aren’t polished accounts by historians or journalists with an agenda, but eyewitness voices—startled, frightened, awed, sometimes even darkly humorous.The collection moves through war, plague, disaster, and upheaval, yet every so often a small human detail breaks through: a gesture of kindness, a moment of clarity, a glimpse of ordinary life surviving extraordinary times. Those flashes make the brutality more bearable and the history more vivid.It’s an epic read—dense, thought‑provoking, and not something you can skim. I found myself pausing often, letting the weight of a single paragraph settle.By the end, I felt I’d gained not just a better understanding of history, but a deeper sense of how people endure it. It’s a book I’ll keep as a reference, but also as a reminder of how powerful first‑hand testimony can be.
