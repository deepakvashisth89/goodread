First of all, I love this title. My favorite childhood memories involve late afternoons/ early evenings spent swimming at the beach, after most people have gone home. Second, I also live on the wrong side of the bridge. Near Cape Cod but not actually on the cape. Wasserman has written a series of essays describing her life in a mainly blue collar town, both the positives and negatives of small town living. The home she lives in originally belonged to her grandmother. I'm assuming Wasserman grew up here, because she definitely sees herself as a local as opposed to a transplant from the city (like me).

Naively, I assumed anyone who is a commentator on NPR would have plenty of money, or at the very least, enough money. Instead, Wasserman tells a few tales including a reliance on food banks and food stamps, working overnight shifts unloading merchandise for the Christmas Tree shops, scraping together this and that for Christmas presents. The stories are well written, frequently poignant, occasionally funny.
