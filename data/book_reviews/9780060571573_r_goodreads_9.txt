I’ve read this before, long ago, and it left an indelible mark on me. In this reread, I identified characters and plot lines that have snaked their way into the fantasy worlds I’ve imagined over the years and the dreams I’ve had since.
The fast-paced narrative is, I believe, Fisher’s strength. Her world borrows from Egyptian, Greek, and Roman elements, that I can identify. The mythos reminds me of a Welsh legend that has inspired me too. It was a charming surprise to me to learn that Wales is Fisher’s home.
I like the characters’ names and for the most part I like the god system and the dichotomy of believers and nonbelievers and the mix of expected and unexpected places in society each occupies. I like that it is definitive in its purveyance that the mythos is legitimate and the political plot is unquestionably in the wrong. I find that refreshing in the current landscape of storytelling. (I know this book is not current, but I make my point all the same.)
I think the dialogue is a point that could be strengthened, but with that in mind, most of the characters are (for their own reasons) either socially awkward or timid. That is a smart choice because it meant most of their odd verbal interactions weren’t too questionable to me, the reader.
I think the book is generally well written, but overall, I can definitely say after 20 years, it has made a lasting impact on me. I am eager to return to the next books in the series. So much time has passed that I don’t remember what comes next.
The ending of this one is an odd note, but a good hook to pique interest in what comes next.
