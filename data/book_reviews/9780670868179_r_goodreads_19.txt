The Girl in the Picture by Denise Chong was an educational and interesting read and I learned a lot about the Vietnam war from the perspective of the civilians caught up in the fighting.

I have always been drawn to photographs that have changed the world and the "Terror of War" (AKA Napalm Girl) photo depicting 9 year old Kim Phuc running, naked and screaming after being caught in a napalm attack is a massively important one. It is credited as initiating, in part, the embracing of the anti-war movement around the world which ultimately led to the eventual ending of the Vietnam war after America pulled out in 1973. One quote especially stood out for me regarding this shot and photos like it and it's attributed to Horst Faas, the AP editor that encouraged the use of the Napalm Girl photograph in international publication despite Phuc's nudity: "Photographs are the seeds that prepare the ground for truth."

I enjoyed learning about Phuc and her family before and after the tragedy that changed the course of her life irrevocably. I didn't know much about her except that she lives in Canada now. Knowing her story adds a certain depth to the famous photo for me. The book is outdated in that it credits Nick Ut with taking the iconic photo but the information about it's ambiguous origin is brand new so of course it would be.

The writing is detailed but not super expressive or compelling. I wanted it to be more engaging—a book I couldn't put down. Still, it was educational and I appreciated that aspect. Reading about the Vietnam war has created an interest in me to learn more about it. For that reason, I would recommend this book.
