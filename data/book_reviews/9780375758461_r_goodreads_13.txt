Francis Bacon was a huge nerd, but not as much as the Scholastics and humanists he criticizes. Baconian empiricism actually requires touching grass! He criticizes how people in his day seem to worship the authority of ancient authors instead of conducting scientific experiments and learning from the world by observation. His issue does not seem to be with Aristotle himself, but certainly with Aristotle's fanbase, who he calls the "schoolmen." This refers to the Aristotelians or Scholastics, who are in competition with the humanists. But Bacon criticizes them both. He calls Aristotle the "dictator" of Scholastics, and says that their narrow reading of him causes them to write books like spider webs. They are pretty but lack substance. In one passage, he roasts the schoolmen and calls their efforts "degenerate learning" lmao. He says they summarize and simplify the writings of church fathers, but then add explanations to their summaries that make their work longer than the original books they were supposed to be explaining. He does not exactly hate rhetoric, and his own education is humanist, but he still thinks that humanists focus on rhetoric over substance. But Bacon is not doompilled. He thinks the invention of the printing press and other scientific and social advancements have made learning more accessible and that there is hope for the future of learning. It is worth noting that he is very excited about the discovery of the new world, because he believes it will advance science if people study the new plant and animal species there. He also does not see any contradiction between religion and science, and believes that studying the creatures of God is a way of glorifying God. Also, he dropped a lot of random quotable bangers in this book. I will list a few of them at the end of my review. Over all, I thought this was interesting and a very influential book.“For why should a few received authors stand up like Hercules’ columns, beyond which there should be no sailing or discovering, since we have so bright and benign a star as your Majesty to conduct and prosper us?”"knowledge may not be as a courtesan, for pleasure and vanity only, or as a bond-woman, to acquire and gain to her master’s use; but as a spouse, for generation, fruit, and comfort.”“Antiquity deserveth that reverence, that men should make a stand thereupon and discover what is the best way; but when the discovery is well taken, then to make progression.”
