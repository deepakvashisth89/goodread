Charlie the shepherd needs a new cloak in this amusing picture book from author/illustrator Tomie dePaola, and he sets out to make one. First he shears his sheep, then he cards and spins the wool, going on to weave and cut the cloth, and finally to sew his beautiful new red cloak together. After all that work, Charlie has a new cloak just in time for winter...Originally published in 1973 and then a number of times subsequently,Charlie Needs a Cloakis a story that put me in mind of a number of similar tales I have encountered, from Harriet Ziefert and Anita Lobel'sA New Coat for Anna, in which the steps necessary to create the garment are done by different people in the community, to Elsa Beskow'sPelle's New Suit, which also involved garments made through a series of exchanges, as the titular hero trades for the different steps necessary to make his suit. In any case, I found dePaol's tale enjoyable, appreciating both the depiction of the many steps which go into making an article of clothing, and the humor in the illustrations, as Charlie must contend with his feisty sheep at each stage of the process. Recommended to Tomie dePaola fans, and as a bedtime or story-time read, particularly in winter, when children are wearing cloaks or coats of their own.Note:If half stars were available, I would give this a 3.5-star rating, but as they are not, I have rounded up.
