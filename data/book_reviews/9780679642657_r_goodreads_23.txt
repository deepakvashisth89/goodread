Whilst I wasn't blown away as much by McCarthy's prose here like I was in Blood Meridian, Suttree is still one heck of a novel. The novel opens on the Tennessee River in the early 50s, and McCarthy goes on to paint a tough and vivid portrait where he shows little mercy to his characters; from prostitutes and gamblers, to junkmen and drunkards, as poverty rains down like hellfire. Cornelius Suttree's misadventures, all the wretchedness they are soaked in, and the vocabulary that took some getting used to, resulted in a powerhouse of a narrative, that still managed to infuse moments of illuminating beauty in all that unabated squalor.It's like a Doom-mongering Huckleberry Finn; a monstrous flood that bruises and batters its readers. It's a bleak book yes; a hard-hitting Southern piece of fiction that makes you want to take a shower, and that has a story that likes to meander, but it did produce a few laughs; most prominently and memorably being when the 'moonlight melonmounter' is mentioned - that being Suttree's friend, Gene, who got caught in a farmer's field shagging a pulpy fruit and sent to the work farm. I much preferred this to anything by the likes of Faulkner or O'Connor as it simply got its hooks into me and wouldn't let go. 4.5/5
