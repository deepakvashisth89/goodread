2.5 Stars!

I’ve read much about Laing, read a biography on him, seen documentaries and understand what he’s about, but I’ve never actually read him before, so I was intrigued to see what this much lauded book would reveal. Although this is a fairly heavy subject we have to remember it was written for a mass audience and published through a major mainstream publisher in Pelican, so the least you would expect is the book to be tailored towards its audience.

What becomes apparent early on is that Laing cannot write for a mass audience, he lacks the clarity, focus and precision of someone like an Oliver Sacks or Philippa Perry who understand that in writing such a book, it’s not just about showing off what you think you know, but being able to articulate it clearly to a wider audience in order for them to be able to understand and enjoy it too.

This isn't a terrible read, but its just a little too rough in a little too many places for it to be truly absorbing or great. The likes of Freud and Jung managed to write clearer for their audience and that was many decades before this, so you can't say it's too do with the era. I'd say this was interesting in places and revealed an occasionally interesting idea, but if just felt too raw and unrefined to be match the hype and praise it has received by so many.
