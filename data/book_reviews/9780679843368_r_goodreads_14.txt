I thoroughly enjoyed the book of American Black Folktales told by Viginia Hamilton. While many of the stories were new to me, a few of them were better versions of stories that I heard as a child. The books is broken into four section: Animal Tales, Tales of the Real, Extravagant, and Fanciful, Tales of the Supernatural, and Slave Tales of Freedom. In addition to enjoying the stories, I also love that each story is followed by a brief explanation and bit of history to give context to the story.

I think these stories would be great to teach to young children in conjunction with a unit on slavery, as they give a voice to the people of this time period. The tales are filled with sorrow, joy, silliness, anger, and hope and humanize the experience of each person who turned to these tales to escape, make sense, and cope with this horrific time. They are a true testament of the power of storytelling. I can see how reading these stories aloud would be impactful in a classroom (or any) setting as the dialect and cadence of the stories makes the listener feel like they’re getting a front row seat to listen to the storyteller. I would definitely recommend this as a classroom read aloud or a book to be read at home to your children.
