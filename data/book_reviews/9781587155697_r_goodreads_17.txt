Daniel Wallace’s “Big Fish” melded with “The Hitchhiker ‘s Guide to the Galaxy,” with a major helping of “Don Quixote,” (who shows up on the book), amped up to the chaotic nth degree. What a bizarre book: reads almost like hyperbolic, episodic fairytales, which includes a Sphinx, Roman gods, David’s slingshot, meat fruit, and person eating whales. I had heard of Terry Gilliam’s film, saw this book in an antique mall, and thought, why not give it a shot? After the first twenty pages, I did a bit of research: the backstory to the writing of this is fascinating: an anonymously written text (well, until the author was posthumously identified) that was written to mock an actual living person who was a purported braggart. As I read, I actually felt bad for the real person (who supposedly was irate at the publishing of this text). So creative, so weird, and so unique. I can see a lot of modern readers being turned off by the ridiculousness of what happens, and the episodic nature of the text, but armed with the context of why the book was written, and a twisted sense of humor, this is quite a hoot.
