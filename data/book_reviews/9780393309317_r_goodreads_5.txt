Update, 8/11/26: The Second Elegy continues the spirit of elegy #1 with a kind of tribute to eternal spirits that are the poet’s truest inspiration. It begins by repeating a line from the first elegy, “Every angel’s terrifying.” But it’s, again, because angels and birds of the soul and Beauty are such a striking contrast to all the bad news in the world that distract us and create so much anxiety for us that they initially seem almost frightening. This is a poem Rilke writes for Rilke, to encourage himself to get in touch with what inspires him to write, but it is also a call to every day people to do the same, find your inspiration, your touchstones to hel you overcome or speak to trauma. Not that it was easy for Rilke, as it took him ten years to be able to finish this book! But he compares this effort to engage with Beauty as like risking the (initially somewhat frightening) touch of a lover, since the payoff for that risk may be“the promise of eternity”Second post: The first elegy is melancholy, full of anguish about the state of the world, and the onset of WWI didn't help his disposition improve. The elegy introduces us to Angels as terrifyingly perfect in contrast to human frailty, and the mystical tone and lyrical mode continue. Rilke encourages poets and all of us who care about beauty to rise above the limitation of what civilization is becoming or has become, to live at one with the Angels, in that realm of artistic and/or imaginative existence.First post: A friend, CH, asked me to reread with him these ten elegies. There's a kind of fitting, wildly romantic tale of how it all began, as Rainer Maria Rilke, staying at Duino Castle, on a rocky headland of the Adriatic Sea near Trieste, went to the cliffs and claimed he heard, in a moment of despair, "Wer, wenn ich schriee, hörte mich denn aus der Engel Ordnungen?" ("Who, if I cried out, would hear me among the angelic orders?"). He wrote this in a his notebook and it became the opening of the first elegy. This was 1912, and it was only after more than ten years that he was able to finish it.
