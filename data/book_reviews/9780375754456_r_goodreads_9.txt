At Hand of Persons Unknown is not for the faint-at-heart, or for the casual African American history buff. This book is grisly, ghastly, and tragic. Phillip Dray delves into a subject that many cannot comprehend, one where people were sadistically murdered for questioning the status quo or for something as innocent as an adolescent catcall. Such lynchings occurred for one purpose: to maintain a system of dominance for those considered second class citizens. Lynching allowed bigots and racists to partake in extreme violence, as ordinary citizens became judges, jurors, and executioners because they held power in their statuses.Dray notes that Lynching was also meted out to whites, such as that of Leo Frank, the Jewish-American businessman falsely accused of rape. The Frank case illustrates that any minority group could be scapegoated and lynched if they threatened the "order of things" (Frank was more successful than many southern Protestants). This was a difficult read that gave me nightmares because it was real, and in most instances legal.
