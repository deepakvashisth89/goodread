To my great disappointment, Ghost Rider is far less about travel and healing than the title and synopsis will have you believe. Neil Peart takes great pride in clocking as many miles across Canada, The United States and Mexico without really spending the time to discover those places. This book is tantamount to a time-poor tourist rushing through all the tourist destinations taking as many pictures on their camera rather than actually experiencing any of it.Additionally, halfway through, the book becomes more of a collection of his letters to his friends where he tells the same details over and over and complains about how much pain he's in. At times, you get the sense that he's more interested talking 'at' people than actually getting to know them. The vast majority of his letters are to a friend, Brutus, whom at the time is being prosecuted for transporting a truck full of weed across the United States. The book might as well have been re-titled "Letters to Brutus" which Neil even states as a joke towards the end.In the end though, Neil's healing comes in meeting his second wife which felt very flat to me. For someone that points out that healing usually takes years, this ending felt too rushed and inauthentic to me. I would have been happier to experience healing that comes more in the form of acceptance of the past and an ambiguous future, but that never happens. Hell, we barely even get to know his wife and daughter or even his relationship with them in this book.It's unfortunate - the book started off well enough and I had high hopes, but unless you want to slog through 360 pages of Neil whining about his pain rather than experiencing something transformative, then I would recommend better travel memoirs to you.
