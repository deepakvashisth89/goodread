Angela Carter knew the cancer was terminal. This is her goodbye love letter to life. The living often find it fascinating what happens inside the being of a dying person. So much of everyday life in inexplicable. How much more must be death.The text is filled* with dichotomies, such as life and death & humor and tragedy.* with pairs, such as husbands and wives & parents and children.* with types, such as the high and working classes & high and flashy fashion.Apparently Angela Carter was making an inventory of the significant aspects of life. This is not her inventory. This is a fantastical story version of her inventory. The business, the theatricality, the carnality of life seems to have occupied the imagination of this dying writer.Having read this book, I feel as though I know have a better idea how to approach her other works. Now I feel more confident about sometime rereadingThe Bloody Chamber and Other Storiesand then finally writing a review.
