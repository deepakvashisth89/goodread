The problem is not: to be or not to be. But rather: to be and not to be. What it comes down to is that man lives while dying, that he represents death to the living, and that's where tragedy begins.God, what a disappointment.After loving Night and Dawn, I expected I will love Day as well. But whereas the previous novels had very strong messages, this one didn't really tell me anything rather than 'after a hellish life of Holocaust and torture, one can't simply love again and be happy.'Honestly? The whole book was a hundred pages of depressive thoughts with vague descriptions that made you feel bored and left you unattached to the story in a way that you simply could not care.I was expecting much of a better ending for this trilogy.
