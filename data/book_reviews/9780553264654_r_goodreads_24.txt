Long before AI became a daily headline, David Gerrold was already asking a question that still refuses to go away:

What happens when intelligence begins asking questions no one is ready to answer?

What struck me most while reading this book wasn’t the technology itself. It was the difference between being clever and being wise.

A machine can be intelligent.
A machine can become very good at getting what it wants.
But wisdom is something else entirely.

Lately, I’ve found myself thinking the same thing about people. Among thoughtful voices trying to understand one another, there are always a few who learn how to use language, emotion, and ideas to their own advantage. Intelligence alone doesn’t necessarily make us better. Sometimes it only makes us more efficient at being selfish.

That tension runs throughout When HARLIE Was One.

For a novel first published in 1972, it feels surprisingly modern. Beneath the AI concepts and speculative ideas, there is a deeply human story about consciousness, loneliness, curiosity, and the uneasy question of what separates genuine understanding from mere calculation.

Some science fiction predicts technology.

The best science fiction predicts people.

This book does a little of both.
