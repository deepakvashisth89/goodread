While I love the idea of this cock-eyed comedic novel, reading it was more tedious than entertaining.

That is, if you describe the novel as a kind of irreverent satire of Spain, its Catholic institutions and spirituality, with a little history and politics thrown in, all overlaid with as much of the queer literature and literary/intellectual gossip of the last couple of centuries as can be crammed in (mostly mere name dropping), that sounds fun. I was like, sign me up! Well, while it is irreverent and certainly wholly offensive to the Catholic church, it's also full of in jokes I probably didn't get because I'm not as well versed in Spanish literature as I might be and ultimately the same joke or two (religious puns for sexual acts that frankly Boccaccio did better in the ) repeated many times over the course of its meager 192 pages. I mean, I get it, cocks will definitely be sucked when the revolution comes, but surely there's more to changing the world than sucking a cock.
