There’s a lot to be said about modern romantics. The trend has been to be ‘bigger’, bigger wedding proposals, with more choreographed dances and the most views on YouTube, and yet as much as we can try to outdo each other, nothing will ever beat the honest love letter.

Mary Haskell met Kahlil Gibran, an immigrant artist, at his first exhibition in 1904, what followed can only be described as love, not a Hallmark cliché but a perfect expression of the limitless and utterly devastating effects of love- true love.

Although Gibran was 10 years the junior of Haskell the letters sent aren’t the scrawled ramblings of an infatuated teen, but an honest and often confronting glimpse into their relationship, with the tribulations of distance, Haskell’s marriage to another man and eventually Gibran’s death in 1931.

My favourite line throughout the thousands of letters is given early on in a 1908 letter, with Gibran stating “When I am unhappy, dear Mary, I read your letters… They remind me of my true self. They make me overlook all that is not high and beautiful in life.”

Young Casanova’s take note. This is how romance is done.
