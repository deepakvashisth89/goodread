A re-read of the second book in a series I loved as a teenager. This one convinced me not to continue the re-read, even out of curiosity. If anything, the protagonist gets more tiresome and boring and shrill over time, while more interesting stories seem to be going on all around him. Kudos to Foster for a very much ahead-of-his-time plot where the hot lady that the callow hero has a crush on and a secret connection to gently but firmly lets him know that his feelings for her don't obligate her in any way, and that him finding her sexy from a distance for years doesn't mean he has any idea whatsoever who she is or what she wants. There are some remarkably mature and vital messages in here about the difference between a fantasy relationship and a real one, and about how even in a fairly traditional "fighting the big evil" fantasy story, the protagonist doesn't automatically deserve to get everything (and everyone) he wants for a big happy ending. But even so, I found a lot of this novel pretty tedious in the lead-up to the big war at the end.
