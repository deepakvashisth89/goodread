Mr. Shaitana has invited four people he claims have gotten away with murder and four specialists in detection to a dinner party. A murder occurs while everyone is busy playing bridge. With the murder happening right in front of so many talented sleuths, finding the murderer should be a piece of a cake in theory. But will the sleuths be able to put all their cards on the table and work together to crack the case?


This story is all about Poirot’s ego and vanity. Like when he sees Mr. Shaitana’s moustache. He notices it’s a very fine moustache, perhaps the only one in London that could rival his own, although it’s clearly not as luxuriant as his. And his ego is really put on display when he has to work together with his rival detectives.


While the list of suspects is quite short, this is compensated by the addition of the detectives who work together and compete with Poirot, making this a rather unique and memorable Poirot mystery. Though the most interesting character is Ariadne Oliver, who is clearly a fictional version of Agatha Christie herself.
