This autobiography, which takes us from O'Toole's childhood through his first television starring role is quite disappointing. It is not told chronologically, and most of the stories are told in a very casual way, so casual as to make it difficult to figure out what the author is trying to communicate. It's also clear that the author is, at many points, full of baloney, to put it mildly.Throughout the book O'Toole rants about Hitler and his bind to the man, who must have died when O'Toole was 12 or 13. Throughout the book, O'Toole returns to Adolf, but writes almost not a word about his own 2+ years in the British Navy. Somehow there's also a chapter about WWI stuck in here.We do get just a scant few pages about his first appearances on stage, how he got into drama school (I had no money, but somehow I paid; I never studied, but I had deep knowledge of every topic they brought up and I was well-prepared....so lucky). He wants want people to see he's a very talented Joe, but a Joe of Myth! It becomes quite maddening.Somehow he gets to writing about a television show he did in 1976, Rogue Male, without mentioning his towering stage and film success following his stint in the Korean War.He should hired an editor with a spine.
