Less amusing than I expected it to be.

To be clear - this is not a full-blown chronicle of British Empire that goes through every tiniest fact related to it becoming, being and ceasing to be an empire. In fact, I didn't have a feeling there's too many facts here - quite the contrary. This book is an attempt to answer the following questions:
1. what made it possible?
2. why Britain (and not someone else)?

Ferguson is a decent storyteller, so I can't say I'm bored. But I'd really appreciate more detail on subjugating such vast territories with so little. How did they managed to remain in control. What was the monetary balance (how much they benefited for real, who benefited for real, etc.). A lot of that is PARTIALLY covered, but in a way that has left me non-satiated. Maybe it was because I was listening to the audiobook and the printed version has a lot of diagrams/tables I haven't seen.

Aaaanyway - in the end, I'm not sure I have my key questions answered sufficiently.

P.S. I didn't think the book is heavily influence by the epidemic of political correctness. That's a pro, for sure.
