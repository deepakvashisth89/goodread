Anne de Courcy did a hell of a job writing this book. Detailed and very well written, I was most impressed with the fact that she did not try and justify some of Diana (Mitford) Mosley's more ridiculous and short-sighted political ideas and life choices. I have been fascinated with the Mitford's for a long time - especially the stories about their childhood, their strange made-up language and the funny nicknames they gave each other - so I was eager to read this biography to understand more about Diana's fascism and her infamous friendship with Hitler. Usually when I read a biography, I finish the book with some kind of sympathy or affection for the subject in question. Not so here. Instead, I came away with the conclusion that the Mitford's - while still fascinating - were collectively disfunctional and Diana played a large part in that disfunction. As de Courcy frequently points out in the book, it is disturbing that the intelligent and by all other accounts independent Diana was so blindly devoted to the British fascist leader Oswald Mosley that she sacrificed family relations and friendships and ended up exiling herself from her home in Britain all in the name of love for him. While I believe in true love and I respect people who have strong spousal relationships I thought it a little pathetic that Diana - who survived her husband by 23 years - spent the last quarter century of her life lamenting the fact that he had died and also wishing for her own death. I agree with de Courcy's summation of Diana's life, which she describes in the final paragraph of her book: "it was the end of a long life that had dazzled, outraged, horrified, charmed, shocked, beguiled and appalled." I experienced this same mix of emotions while reading the book and applaud de Courcy's immense effort of researching and writing it.
