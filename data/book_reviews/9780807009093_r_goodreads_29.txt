3.5 stars. I had never heard of Jessie Redmon Fauset until someone told me she was like Jane Austen, and of course I immediately had to pick up one of her books. This is a novel of passing - passing for white, that is, which was common among light-skinned Black people of this period. Angela is light-skinned enough to do this, but her sister Jinny is not, and Angela's choice to pass pushes the sisters farther apart as they both move to New York for a new start.

I thoroughly enjoyed the novel's depiction of 1920s New York, both the world of the Harlem Renaissance, which Jinny inhabits, and the bohemian artists that Angela hangs out with while passing as white. As someone who went to grad school and still has a lot of friends who are students, I thought the way the characters relate to each other, the relationships they get in and out of, the lectures they attend and discuss, etc. were extremely relatable. The authorial voice has a wry tone that relies on understatement, similar to Jane Austen (but not quite so funny), and this and the way the book tackles issues of both race and class reminded me of her.

The one thing I didn't really like was how the romance was handled. The main couple don't have enough real scenes together for me to buy their relationship, and there's some drama that quickly becomes very corny and could have been resolved much more quickly than it was. That's the main reason this isn't getting a higher rating from me - I still look forward to reading more of Fauset's work!
