They tell me you are a man with True Grit..

I loved this book! It’s a revenge story. It’s about character, moral strength, stubborn moral resolve, and the ability to keep going when things are harsh and unfair.

Mattie Ross our FMC is looking for the man that killed and shot her father in cold blood. Her determination to avenge her father shows a kind of unbending will, but it also raises questions about whether that kind of resolve comes at a cost.

Rooster Cogburn is a US Marshall and a man with flawed justice. He’s a lawman, but he drinks, bends rules, and uses violence freely. He shows that justice in the real world often comes from imperfect people making hard choices— not from clean ideal systems.

LeBouef is a Texas ranger trained and disciplined, but he’s also driven by pride and reward money. He’s not purely noble, just more polished than Rooster.

•─────⊰⋅☽⊱✦⊰☽⋅⊰─────•

In a way, Little Blackie the horse (my favorite) is “true grit.” He doesn’t act out of pride, revenge, or ego like the humans do. He simply endures and keeps going until he physically can’t anymore. That makes his version of grit more selfless and uncorrupted. Little Blackie just does what’s needed. His role highlights how complicated and flawed human intentions are compared to simple, honorable action. I’m not a fan of spoilers, but Blackie did make me shed a tear— as I have more of a soft spot for animals.

•─────⊰⋅☽⊱✦⊰☽⋅⊰─────•

It does feel antiquated at times, and there is some derogatory racial slurs, but it reflects the period in which it was written, and that context makes it easier to understand. The writing is old and plain, but sometimes simplicity really is the ultimate sophistication. The prose pulls you in and keeps you hooked, making you eager to follow every step of Mattie’s story. There is a lot to love about this story and a lot of heart in it.
