I found the style annoying at times - some of the "discoveries" made by the writer sounded pretty common sense to me, but perhaps that's the time and space making a difference. The discussion of abortion did not impress me at all, and I thought her speaking about her privilege - as if it was a burden - felt a bit fake at times. But I did enjoy the focus given to the repercussions of the pregnancy and parenthood on straight couples. I still think she was a bit too apologetic in her description of the men regressing into pre-feminist roles ("they're good husbands! good guys! just can't imagine that they could sacrifice their career and not their wives... the friendship between the partners couldn't survive but they're a family now" made me raise my eyebrows a bit), but the stories she related about the seismic shifts and the danger of women losing their equality (or at least illusion thereof) in a relationship were convincing, especially coupled with the description of the way women may react to the shift in power.I had expected it to be much worse.
