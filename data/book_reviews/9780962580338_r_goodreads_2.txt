As a fan of the Pagan Robin Hood TV series Robin of Sherwood (ROS), I really wanted to like this Pagan Robin Hood book. The best thing about it is the characterization of Robin. He's definitely a Robin Goodfellow type full of mischief and snappy comebacks. That aspect of the book is delightful.I've seen other reviews on Amazon that complained about anachronisms,and those certainly do exist, but I consider them a minor problem.The main flaw of the book as I see it is that the Pagan Gods and Goddesses are shadows. They aren't really present. On ROS Herne actually appears. He speaks to Robin and mystifies Robin with riddles. There is also a man who shamanically carries the presence of Herne. Showing us that Herne is real gave the show tremendous power. The absence of divine presence in this novel caused events to fall flat that should have felt significant to me. The Paganism portrayed in this novel lacked vitality. It's really a shame.
