I have had a copy of this book for many years but never read it. It's the story of the 1980 Phillies world championship team, told by a local newspaper sports reporter who covered them. I was 4 years old, just about to turn 5, when the Phils won the series, and it is a formative memory for me. This book has a few different aspects, some of which age better than others. On the one hand, I didn't realize how close the whole thing was, with the Phillies only winning the division on the next-to-last day, and barely making it past Houston to win the playoffs. It's good to read about how they pulled together. And I did know something about the tension in the clubhouse, with Dallas Green's manage-by-yelling apporach. In the book it's made out to be a deliberate choice because that's what the team needed, not an inherent character trait. It's good to read about some of the old favorite players, but in hindsight we know some of them turn out to not be very good people (eg Pete Rose). Since the author is a newspaper man, there's a lot (really, a lot) written about the interaction of the team with newspaper reporters. In a way it's quaint to read this, remembering all the papers that have gone out of business over the years! But also it feels unnecessary, and is there mainly because it's the author's interest, and not because it's going to be interesting to the reader to dwell on the newspaper reporters so much.In all I'm glad I finally read the book.
