“Caddy smelled like trees.”Apparently, The Sound And The Fury is one of the most frequently abandoned books, put down after only a few pages, never to be read again.Admittedly, it took not one, not two, not three, not four, but five lectures for me to fully appreciate The Sound and The Fury, but Faulkner is attempting to convey some complex emotions by showing readers rather than telling them.Yale Lecture 1Yale Lecture 2Yale Lecture 3Yale Lecture 4How to Read The Sound and the Fury by Benjamin McEvoyThe first section is told from the perspective of Benjy who is intellectually disabled. All he wants in the world is his sister, Caddy.“Caddy smelled like trees.”That smell, the scent of trees, transports Benjy home, to a safe place. Forever, Benjy will be looking for Caddy.In the second section, Quentin is struggling with the scandal of his sister.“someday it will no longer hurt you like this”Quentin is so profoundly affected by this loss that he can’t even finish his sentences. He wants to freeze time. He doesn’t want tomorrow to arrive because he doesn’t want his memories of Caddy to fade.The third section is from Jason’s perspective, and he is a self-centered jerk.The last section is from the perspective of an omniscient narrator. The Yale Professor, Professor Wai Chee Dimock, asserted that Faulkner had some sympathy for Jason, and she was suggesting that Jason and Luster are redeemed; however, that is not my take.“Jason,” he said. “What were you doing with three thousand dollars hid in the house?”In my opinion, this is a Biblical allusion to Matthew 25:14-30. This is the parable where three servants were given money. Two of the servants doubled the money. One buried the money entrusted to him. The servant who buried the money said, “I was afraid I would lose your money, so I hid it in the earth.” The master replied, “You wicked and lazy servant! […] Why didn’t you deposit my money in the bank? At least I could have gotten some interest on it. […] From those who do nothing, even what little they have will be taken away.”Jason had an opportunity to invest in his family, in his community.
