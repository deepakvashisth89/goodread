This book offers a helpful peek into real conversations adoptive parents have had with their children. But, if you're not interested in psychoanalytic theories, go ahead and skip chapter 2. Favorite lines:1. "Adoptive parents must confront their own conceptions and misconceptions about what makes a family good and come to understand--as it turns out very young children do naturally--that it is love that defines family relationships" (14).2. "Adoption is a repair of trauma, not a trauma in itself (except for unsuitable placements); it is not a losing or a taking away of what never was, but a mutual giving and gaining of affirmative family relationship" (Viola Bernard, 21).3. "Adopting entails doing all sorts of things that seem beyond one's endurance. One simply does them" (111).4. "Parents do not adopt in order to carry banners against social ills. We adopt to have the joy of loving a child" (217).5. "Adoption, actual and imagined, 'is a metaphor for the human condition, sending us forth on that mythic quest that will prove that we are connected to each other and to all the creatures of this world--and in the process, reveal to us who we really are'" (Lifton, 219)
