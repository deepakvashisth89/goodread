A collection that shifts between the strange and the quietly thoughtful, The Wonderful Story of Henry Sugar and Six More brings together seven very different tales. The standout follows Henry Sugar, a wealthy man who becomes obsessed with learning to see without using his eyes after discovering a report about a man named Imhrat Khan, while other stories like “The Boy Who Talked with Animals” and “The Swan” explore darker or more reflective moments. There aren’t recurring characters across the book, but each story introduces its own distinct voice and mood. Some pieces feel more impactful than others, especially the longer ones, but the variety keeps things interesting. It’s an uneven yet engaging collection that shows a different, sometimes more serious side of Dahl.RATING: 4 stars ⭐⭐⭐⭐
