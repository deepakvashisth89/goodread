Messy and interesting with an obvious sensationalist bias. I agree with the common criticisms of a bloated roster of larger than life figures and a desperate need of editing, especially given that a shorter, more focused look at the rivalry between Drew and Ralph would have certainly done the job; the fact that one chapter was dedicated to a psychic's vision of a missing persons related to another of the Company's members is an example of Denton wanted this to feel far-reaching and outlandish.I do think it captures the Sin City aspects of Lexington's 70s and 80s' hidden crimes, but it erases the normal day-to-day life by doing so with only the briefest mentions of alternatives to people like Anita Madden and Henry Vance. I also understand the need to explain Drew's status as an unwilling underling of the various cartels, but the details become weights put onto a story that buckles under said pressure. My main takeaway is that I would really want two separate books: one heavily streamlined version of Drew Thornton and the Company and a separate deep dive into John Y.'s administration and the death of his career.
