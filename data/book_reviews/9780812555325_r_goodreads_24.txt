This is the sixth novel ofthe Black Companygrimdark military fantasy series, the first ofthe Glittering Stonearc. As with the previous volumes, each book tries a new sub-genre, from mil-fantasy to horror mystery to multiple interlaced intrigues to almost computer game grand strategy… this one isn’t different.This time, we once again get a new narrator/writer of the Company’s annals. It is Murgen, the relatively young (compared with millennia-old Lady, century-old One Eye and Goblin, and even Croaker, the original Company’s annalist, who is a septuagenarian) member of the Black Company and its standard bearer, even if he lost a standard two books ago, when Croaker and Lady supposedly perished. Readers know from the previous volume that they survived, but this book is roughly parallel in time withDreams of Steel; it supplies the view from the besieged city.There is a conflict between the besieged and Shadowlords and within the city between the ‘old guard’ of the Black Company, who came from the North with Croaker and local descendants of the Company before they moved North, headed by Mogaba. Each group has its own ideas on how to proceed, including who should head the Company.As if it isn’t enough, Murgen has visions. These visions are both mythic, like with a figure on a throne, and ‘real’ of the days and weeks past, for he has a way to travel as an invisible spirit. These visions make the book quite hard to follow (and make re-reading helpful).I have to admit that I like the author’s experiments and trying new genres in each book, they became even more interlaced and harder to follow (say Murgen in the previous 5 volumes appeared but only marginally). However, I like the initial trilogy most, where each book could have been the last and sowing of hints for future stories was less plentiful.
