Okay, since this is quite an old book(92?), pre-cell phones&all other common devices, I don't feel bad about making the whole review full of spoilers. Not that its age in itself is a bad thing, but this one hardly stands the test of time. The final plot twist wouldn't really work in these times, because why wouldn't Faith just take pictures of her masterpiece with a cell phone. (True, she might go back with the intent of using fancier cameras for some magazine-quality shots, but the impact of her work being lost forever by Mrs Griffin's insane vandalism would hardly be the same.)If you've read countless suspense/psych-thrillers, having the main character's life be in present danger to prevent the disclosure of past events might start to seem like a cliché. Until you take that threat out of the equation. Then you're left with what, imaging that someone HAS to want her dead? That'll carry the suspense for a while but ultimately that's pretty unsatisfying. Along the way I kept imagining all kinds of possibilities: that given the oft-mention resemblance between Faith and Mrs Griffin's daughter Cassandra (and how she favored her father), perhaps Holt Griffin will turn out to be Faith's runaway father of yore?
