I saw the film “Practical Magic” when it first came out decades ago and, after reading Alice Hoffman’s book, rewatched it. Right away, I could quickly see a huge disconnect between the novel and the film. I believe that disconnect occurred because of how this book was written.

Hoffman is absolutely brilliant; she’s a tremendously gifted writer and extremely descriptive, poetic even. The problem, from what I can see, is not just in her lack of chapters (the book was comprised of only a few sparse but very long so-called chapters) and the low amount of dialogue, but rather with how her book was arranged. Hoffman took deep dives into the lives of even Sally’s children. And, although these parts were extremely interesting, they almost detracted from the story.

The time periods for this book took place over several lifetimes, from Maria to Sally’s children. While Hoffman buried Jimmy under a lilac bush, for some strange reason, the filmmakers chose to have him buried beneath a rose bush. But it wasn’t just the lilac bush that changed. Nearly everything found in the novel is so different from the movie.

Even with Hoffman’s hyper-reliance on narration rather than dialogue, she’s such an engaging writer that I found it difficult to put the book down. The writer’s skill kept me engaged from the time the lilac bush was cut down to when the frog burped up the ring, but at times I felt like the story was dragging along.
