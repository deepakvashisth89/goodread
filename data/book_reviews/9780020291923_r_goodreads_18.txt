“For human nature is such that grief and pain—even simultaneously suffered—do not add up as a whole in our consciousness, but hide, the lesser behind the greater, according to a definite law of perspective. It is providential and is our means of surviving"A gorgeously, spare memoir of survival and resilience amidst terror and hatred. Primo Levi's memoir stays with you, right at the very core of your bones. This is an essential memoir for anyone who wants to be an ally to make sure fascism and racism is a very real, and evil intent- and hell bent on destroying anyone who's deemed the other.This was recommended to me by a dear writer friend who told me about how this memoir is one that should be read and studied.From Italy to Auschwitz, to Buna- Levi's trip into hell is as affecting and personal as any good memoir should be. Like the other canonical Holocaust memoirs such as "Night" and "Maus", this is just as essential- and out of those three books, this one moved me the most.
