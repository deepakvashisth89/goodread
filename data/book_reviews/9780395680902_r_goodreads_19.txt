As a big fan of their first book, I was excited to return to the journeys of Mark and Delia Owen. Cry of the Kalahari was a major piece of conservation literature and rekindled my love of wildlife. Though the couple had their issues and struggles in the dessert, none of that quite compares to this book. While still getting a good chunk of daily life and animal observations as the first, Eye of the Elephant introduces a new issue into the equation. True conflict. I now understand why this is called an epic adventure, because from the moment that our conversationalists step foot in Africa they are set upon by all sides. From the government, from the environment, and most surprisingly, from each other.I loved this book as much, if not at times more, than Cry of The Kalahari!
