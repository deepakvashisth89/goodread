I don't see any point recapping the story of this novel - what I will say is that I was again (this is my second reading) overwhelmed and impressed by the utter truth and honesty of this tale of, mostly, schoolboys growing up in Danzig in the years of Nazism and WWII. For readers of my generation we didn't need to have read Grass's first novel The Tin Drum to know that whatever was going to happen to the novels protagonists they were bound, in some way, to be doomed because they were only a few years away from their whole world being consumed in a gotterdammerung of horrific completeness (for anyone for whom this meaningless look up expulsion of Germans after WWII).

I have read quite a few in depth analysis of this novel, and of all Grass's works, some have made sense, almost none have contributed to my enjoyment of this or any of Grass's other novels. If you want in depth literary analysis there are loads of sites which provide it. I think Cat and Mouse is a brilliant and moving novel of growing up and doing so under a regimen of unutterable frightfulness which, although it tried to be all pervasive, was in the end only superficially able to penetrate or control the thoughts and lives of its citizens.

How life goes on in all circumstances consumed by ordinary things is one of the most powerful ideas this novel brought home to me. I loved it and it has forced me to again find room soon for Grass's other novels.
