It’s 1944. Germany is losing the war. The day after his 17th birthday, Sebastian Westland enlists in the Waffen-SS, and leaves his small German town. He endures the humiliation of basic training. After a short home leave, he ships out to fight a war he’s woefully unprepared for.

A rare World War II story from a German soldier's perspective. You might go into this book expecting a cautionary tale about the evils of nazism. How Hitler indoctrinated an entire generation of teens to fight and die for the cause. Well, you’d be wrong. For a book about an SS soldier, it’s…strangely apolitical.

One of the most striking things about “Night Over Day Over Night” is how it goes out of its way to NOT talk about nazi war crimes or nazi ideology. There’s really only one scene that touches on nazi ideology in depth. While at a training center, Sebastian and some friends steal wax molds of German, Jewish, and Anglo-Saxon noses and melt them down. And even then, it’s framed as a harmless teenage prank rather than an act of rebellion.

The book is written from Sebastian's first person point of view, but he has no inner life. Who is he, really? What does he believe in? I have no idea, Watkins barely develops his character. Even during moments that SHOULD get a strong emotional reaction- killing his commanding officer, his friend dying, shooting another soldier and stealing his motorcycle- he doesn’t react at all and keeps trudging on.

Sebastian doesn’t really feel like a nazi character. He’s more of a generic soldier who kills and follows orders without question. The only things he cares about are beer and sex. The other soldiers in his squad are just war movie archetypes: The cruel NCO, the officer wannabe, the bully, the pessimist, etc.

A few errors/goofs I noticed:

-Sebastian’s older lover drives a car everywhere. In 1944. During a fuel shortage.

-Sebastian, a German character, uses a few British slang words like ‘soused’ (drunk) and ‘bloody’.

-Sebastian’s disabled friend Benjamin, who’s in a wheelchair. I found it hard to believe Benjamin wasn’t either dead or in an institution. (the nazis had an extermination program targeting disabled people)

Aside from those mistakes, it's VERY well researched. The battle scenes are gruesome and harrowing, and vividly described. Unfortunately, I didn’t care about Sebastian or his platoon of stock characters.
