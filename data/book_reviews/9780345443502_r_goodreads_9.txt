This short novel (a two-evening read) tells the story of Jesus from the cynical first-person perspective of Pontius Pilate, writing 30 years later. The author weaves a lot of historical background into the small book that creates a background for the familiar events readers will recognize from the Gospels. (Some places even include a footnote with a reference for further reading.)

I was interested that the author allowed Pilate to maintain all of the cynicism that his character shows historically throughout the book. I recently heard a radio pastor point out that none of the characters we read about in the Bible knew they were living in "Bible times," and they were not waiting for their scripted lines. Pilate tells his story from the point of view of a "reasonable" person--and in his character we can see the reflection of many modern politicians, businessfolks, and--perhaps--someone we know. (The author's own time serving as a politician surely have him plenty of material to work with.)

I picked up this book for a quick read, but found it full of historical information to consider, and possible modern parallels to mull over.
