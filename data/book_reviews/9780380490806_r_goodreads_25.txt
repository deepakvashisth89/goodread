The best summation I have for my feelings on The Family Vault is: it was okay. There was a foreword in my copy that sold Charlotte MacLeod as one of the best mystery writers to ever practice the craft, and that The Family Vault was so good, the foreword author lost confidence in her own writing ability in the face of MacLeod’s godlike prose. Yeah, it wasn’t any of that. Maybe I’d have liked it better if I hadn’t read that silly foreword, but probably not.

I like Max, but find Sarah Kelling to be very unrealistic. The mystery was predicable, and the mystery’s premise was drawn out far too long. Once the reader had the crime laid out, the book was over. There’s also a lot of unsubstantiated speculation done by the characters, like MacLeod had a great, twisty-turny plot in mind, but had to over explain to make it work.

The worst part, though, is the language. I felt like I was studying for the SATs. MacLeod throws in words like, “punctilious” and “superannuated”. Maybe she did have an incredible vocabulary but it reads like she had a Thesaurus next to her instead.

Safe to say I won’t pick the Bittersohm-Kelling series up.
