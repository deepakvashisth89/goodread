Travis McGee's friend, Sam Taggart, is back in town after spending four long years away after screwing up a relationship with a woman. Now, he's back with thoughts of apologies and rekindling a relationship with the girl. He's got a strange story to tell, too, about Aztec gold figurines which could be worth thousands. Unfortunately, Taggart isn't in town for a full 24 hours before McGee finds his friend brutally murdered in a hotel room.

The girl asks McGee to find Taggart's killers and get revenge on her behalf. His investigation takes him to a seedy Mexican village where exiled Cubans and rich American assholes find ways to get rich on the backs of innocents like Taggart.



"A Deadly Shade of Gold" is the fifth book in John D. McDonald's series featuring his beloved detective/fixer/hero-for-hire. It is, so far, the best book in the series and vintage noir at its finest. It is also the bloodiest book, so far, in the series, with an extremely high body count. So, be forewarned.




Published in 1965, "A Deadly Shade of Gold" was MacDonald's not-so-subtle criticism of the 1961 Bay of Pigs invasion of Cuba that resulted in an abysmal military failure for the U.S. government. While statistics vary, roughly 1000 casualties---mostly Cuban---resulted in the debacle. It is more than implied in the novel that McGee had been somehow involved in the military invasion, suggesting that he possibly had a CIA background. It is never confirmd in the novel, although it helps to explain McGee's life choice of living quietly on a houseboat in Florida and his disdain for politics.
