"'My name is Flint, and when I see either of you again, I'm going to start shooting . . . no matter where it is. And you have had all the warning you are going to get.'"-- the title character, taking no guff, page 149Serviceable 'frontier story' from the prolific L'Amour, what was most memorable - or at least was a very good 'hook' for the narrative - was the situation of protagonist James 'Flint' Kettleman. An orphan rescued from a wagon train massacre, Flint is raised and mentored by a tough gambler / gunman to become a savvy and strong adult in the 1870's. Although very successful in business in the New York City area, an ailing Flint believes he is slowly dying of stomach cancer - similar to John Wayne's character in his excellent final filmThe Shootist- and promptly lights out to Alamitos in the burgeoning New Mexico territory to live his final months in peaceful anonymity. Of course, life (and the plot) has other plans for the terse Flint after he throws in his lot with an attractive young female rancher whose valuable land is desired by the usual duplicitous and insatiable local power broker. The two-fisted, sharp-shooting, stallion-riding Flint then rises to the occasion to handily deal with a revolving door of hired goons sent by said villain. Not a bad book at all, but I did not appreciate the health-related plot twist / U-turn late in the story, which killed some of the established momentum.
