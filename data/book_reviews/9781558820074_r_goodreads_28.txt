They say you learn something new every third day. "Craig Rice" was actually born Georgiana Ann Randolph Craig (1908-57) and as a child was adopted by an aunt whose surname was Rice. Now largely forgotten, the author was famous in her day and the first mystery writer featured on the cover ofTimemagazine. Which made me wonder how many other mid-list writers I've falsely pigeon-holed because of a pseudonym.Eight Faces at Threewas Rice's first novel and the first of a total of 14 mysteries featuring hard-drinking attorney John J. Malone. In this first episode he's just one of three male leads mixed in with two female leads. Too many characters cause the story to be a wee bit clunky and there's not enough space for them all to be developed. Two characters will be having a lengthy conversation when a third voice surprisingly pipes up -- the reader forgot anyone else was there. Set in Chicago, the murder mystery is light-hearted and humorous in a Nick and Nora slash Thin Man vein featuring lots (and lots and lots) of heavy drinking and witty banter with the traditional big dramatic reveal at the end. The mystery takes a back seat to the repartee, which is only mildly annoying as the leads' detection abilities are minimal, with the characters often going over the same ground for pages at a time, not solving much but finding new confusions. The mystery itself is fairly pedestrian depending a lot on coincidence and the far-fetched, while the reader will be generally one step ahead of the "detectives." Determinedly average and mildly entertaining, Rice may have intended this for Hollywood. Not a necessary read but I'm curious about where the underdeveloped Attorney Malone went from here, so I'll be seeking out the next installment,The Corpse Steps Out(1940).
