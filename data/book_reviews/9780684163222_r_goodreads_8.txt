A fantastically well written book about a subject I love, wolves.

Wolves are to me magical, revered, skillful, misunderstood, beautiful creatures. Which makes it all the more shameful that we, our shitty human species, has captured, ensnared, maimed, tortured, poisoned, slaughtered and almost completely decimated the wolf populations of the world over. What is the matter with us??????? Why are we so intent on grounding animal species into submission and degradation???????

Barry Lopez, though he presents a very comprehensive study of the wolf, does not seem to have the answer to my question. In fact in some ways, he seems strangely impassive at times to the havoc men have wreaked upon the wolf over the many centuries. In fact he goes so far as to feel some compassion for the men who used to hunt them by the thousands (because they're lonely old men now with no purpose to life and "didn't really know what they were doing back then"; hmm where have we heard this before-just following orders!!!)

Nevertheless he does present a good argument for the preservation and increased understanding of the wolf in modern times, and provides an expert level of knowledge about these elusive creatures. The photographs even though they're printed in black and white in my copy of the book, are spectacular too; more heartbreaking are the ones showing wolf abuse and murder. Shameful, just shameful.

I just hope that attitudes change towards wolves and people learn to leave them be, to survive, to c0-exist.
