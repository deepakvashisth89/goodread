This book is exactly what it needs to be if you take it as it is intended to be taken. It's a series of aphorisms, like those written by Pascal, which revolve around a central theme of the distance between experience and the observation of experience, the intermediation that lies between modern man and the world he occupies thanks to layers of technology, culture, and media. We are unable to have unmediated experience and this leads to a sense of disconnection and permanent disassociation. And it's all very stylish and the aphorisms feel/are deep, well-expressed. The thing is, it's not a persuasive essay and it's not intended to provide compelling proof of its claims. It's not an academic monograph. You're free to say that it's therefore not worth taking seriously, but I don't see the utility of attacking it for not achieving what it doesn't intend to achieve. This book should be seen as a piece of complementary commentary to work by people like Lefebvre, McLuhan, and Marcuse, and as an object of poetry. And at that I find it profoundly readable and deeply inspiring. And sad. Very very sad.
