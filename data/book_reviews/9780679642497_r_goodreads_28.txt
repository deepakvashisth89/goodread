Great little book recommended by Chris Dixon on a podcast. Comes in at 180 pages or so and weaves a compelling narrative for the history of the company through political, social and national lenses. Makes an interesting case for example that the UK lost its hegemony to the world due to the South Sea Bubble and its consequent Bubble Act which made corporate formation difficult for many years as well as the fact that politicians had been capricious and complicit in other behavior towards the company. Add to that the landed classes view that business was somehow below them and you have a recipe for the UK's comparative decline.Book was published in 2003, it would be interesting to understand the authors' perspective on the last 20 years.
