I always love to find new mysteries set in the southwest of the U.S. With this first in a series that is new to me, I found more than I expected. Texana Jones, lead character here, has lived in this rural area on the border between Mexico and the U.S. in southwestern Texas all her life, and is bilingual, as are most who live there. Texana's husband Clay is the local veterinarian, while she runs a trading post that is the main supply for daily food and household items for those living on both sides of the border. Written in 1996, this story depicts a very porous border, with people from both sides passing easily back and forth, only needing to cross a part of the Rio Grande that is more like a ditch, one they can easily drive or walk across. Recently Texana's life-long friends Maria and William Deed were murdered, and law enforcement is finding few leads in solving the crime. Soon another murder follows, and Texana is very much in the midst of the investigation.While the story and characters here are very well drawn, what I enjoyed the most was the author's portrayal of this multicultural community, and the blending of most all aspects of life, from language, food, art, religious belief and practices, tradition, and identity. In her description of life on the border she states: "The border with Mexico is a boundary only in the minds [of some people] but to fronterizos it is a country in itself, a country of the mind and soul, a place where two cultures grate and bleed and blend into a hybrid country, ambiguous, harsh, and full of extremes."As an example she sites spoken communication, which switches back and forth between Spanish and English, and is labeled "Tejano" by the locals.I look forward to the next book in this series.
