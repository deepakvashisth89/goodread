Our first essay argues that art reproduction in prints, postcards, magnets, digital media generates a translational gap. Much like how words lose meaning across language (yes I’m still reading ), or how meaning loses integrity across people. Do you get what I mean? Visuals are different than perception… how someone may perceive ___ may be different than how someone else does. While I wish Berger further explored the last sentiment through an art lens, he instead posits that reproductions flatten their original work through every photo, print, screenshot. And so hey what’s the big deal about all that? Well friend, he’d respond, “A people or a clan which is cut off from its own past is far less free to choose and act as a people or clan than one that has been able to situate itself in history.” Nowadays art has a kind of an access code, where those that indulge are either students or wealthy. But if we were able to connect ourselves with humans of our past, through their art of course, we may understand ourselves better. We may feel less alone. Who’s to say.

Placement of pictorial essays between wordial (yes) was great. I don’t know, they seemed to be in conversation with one another! Not until now did I realize the power of a certain curation of images…yes Berger….I’m picking up what you’re putting down. Nude women in paintings…represent idyllic concepts…or give the viewer a sense of ownership…or even generate dissatisfaction in one’s own body…oh GOD why are women in paintings sooo object. Was that a good enough segue to the next essay? Of course there are many artists who don’t paint a woman’s body for such purpose, but what a trend it’s been. In the following wordial essay, Berger notes something that’s become banal now: every woman has a surveyor within herself. Maybe it is because of this art that women always watch themselves? Art is a reflection of society, is it not? And if something as banal as a woman’s body is on display, does that not give us a sense that our existence is something to be observed? Life imitates art imitates life. How tiresome. It’s true though, and to prove it, I’ll recall an imaginative exercise in comic book spaces that suggests you replace a female superhero with Hawkeye… notice the pose he’s in, his expression, the clothes he wears. Does that not feel like violence?

On the last essay. Art has infiltrated publicity because ads are everywhere and we’ll look if they’re purty. Ads must always be up to date, but “they never speak of the present. Often times they refer to the past and always the speak of the future.” Oh EAT. John Berger more like John burgers! He ate. And of course, they’re in conversation with each other… reading this now, it’s hard not to think of the American Eagle v. Gap jeans ads. Both promise different futures, different ideas of who should be idealized in jeans. Is it the diversity suggested by Katseye’s feature in Gap? Or is it whiteness? Reading this essay reminded me how intentional ads are and also how I need to block Sydney Sweeney.

But the juicy cherry on top is this: ads like these suggest a freedom of choice. Do we want diversity or do we not? Unfortunately the decision is not one easily made in government, it’s one we are instead encouraged to make with our dollar. Support diversity, buy Gap. Support whiteness, buy American Eagle. But how childish they think of us to cast our money as ballots, like tip jars at coffee shops asking us to vote whether or not pineapple belongs on pizza. No choice is being made except purchase. Or to put it succinctly, “publicity turns consumption into a substitute for democracy.” While there’s much to be said about purchasing power, has purchasing power become all the power we have?
