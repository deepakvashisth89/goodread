(Audiobook) This work really surprised me, in a good way. The Confederacy is a controversial topic, and deservedly so. Yet, it can be hard to get an objective analysis of the inner workings of this unsuccessful rebellion, especially how it managed to bring itself together and what happened as the Civil War turned against it, resulting in one of the biggest defeats in military history. Davis does a masterful job of highlighting the key figures behind the founding of the Confederacy as well as the political maneuvering that got this rebellion up and running between the 1860 US Presidential Election and the start of firing on Fort Sumner. A big point of Davis' thesis is that this was a rebellion from the Southern plantation owners and the extremists who felt that the Federal government was not only stifling their way of life, but most importantly, their position of power. All of the arguments for the founding of the Confederacy, as abhorrent as they are today, can all tie back to the desire for the plantation class to hold on to their power.

Davis does not shy away from the ills of the Confederacy, and he does not come close to buying into the "Lost Cause" narrative. Yet, he is fairly objective with the facts and noting the various issues with the Confederacy as the war progressed. A lot of those that fought for the South did not stand to profit by the separation from the Union, and a lot of those that lived in the wartime South were not exactly gung-ho about the Cause. Jefferson Davis may have been the only man who could have led that group of states, but even he was flawed, and in particular, his inability to accept the facts when the South was truly beaten is both fascinating and infuriating.

I can see why this book garnered a lot of accolades, and if there is a work that can come as close to objectively showing the true story of the founding and evolution of the Confederate government, this is the one. The rating is the same regardless of format. Admittedly, the narrators lame attempt at Southern accents (think Brian Kelly's horrid attempts at a Southern accent when he was the coach at LSU) is a bit annoying, but the content is beyond solid and can withstand that flaw. A must read for any wanting to understand the Confederate States of America.
