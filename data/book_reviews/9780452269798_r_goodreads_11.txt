This book is indeed an INTIMATE biography of Queen Victoria! Weintraub focuses relentlessly and obsessively on Queen Victoria the person, but gives short shrift to the times and events surrounding her. He frequently mentions a law or crisis, but never provides any detail or context; he assumes that the reader knows the history of that period (1817-1901). Many times I went to other sources to find out what he was talking about. He also does something I absolutely HATE: he often uses a German or French word or expression, but never translates.
The first half of the book deals mainly with Victoria and her relationship with Prince Albert, the love of her life, and ends with his death (1840-1861). I read the first half carefully and enjoyed it. (I guess I am just a romantic.) The second half is devoted to the rest of her life, without Albert,(1861-1901), and deals with her relationships with her children, grandchildren, Disraeli, Gladstone, and John Brown. Weintraub toggles between the private Victoria and the public Victoria, and the narrative is choppy and flat. I did not find the second half as interesting and skimmed much of it.
If you want to know EVERYTHING about Queen Victoria the Woman, read this book. If not, look elsewhere.
