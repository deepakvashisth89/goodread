4.5 Stars.In theThe Summoning, Little has traded his usual corporate-satire horror for a sprawling, shockingly visceral vampire plague. Set against the sun-baked, dusty backdrop of Rio Verde, Arizona, the story fires on all cylinders right from the opening pages.We follow a fractured ensemble cast —including a local medical examiner performing a bloodless autopsy, a reporter forced to confront the impossible, and a young woman named Sue Wing, who identifies the unfolding terror not through Western lore, but through the terrifyingCup-hu-girngsi(the hopping, blood-drinking corpses of Cantonese myth).The multi-cultural lens on vampire folklore is one of the books sharpest, most original elements, providing a refreshing break from typical Dracula tropes.Overall, I found theThe Summoningto be a terrifyingly addictive read which kept me glued to the pages well into the darkest hours of the night. For fans of unrelenting, old-school horror, this is a desert trip worth taking.
