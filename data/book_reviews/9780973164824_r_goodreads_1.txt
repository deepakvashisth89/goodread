I wanted to like this book, however, the serious issue is that in no way has the author made a compelling argument for her case. Indeed a lot of her sources are from folktales and mythology. She has a very select few authors that she uses to agree with her statements, but fail to advance her argument. Due to the fact that for the most part there are no photographs, that is to use to strength her argument, instead there is the odd small drawing, much of this is conjecture. I looked up one of the relics she infers is a goddess and a snake at the back and I'm not convinced at this representation. Furthermore, this book is too short to be convincing and it doesn't read as well researched. If anything the argument against this book is that it has been carefully cherry-picked to advocate a weak argument. She doesn't provide enough evidence about the so-called "Celts" in regards to a serpent belief system and primarily using folktales (Grimm Brothers) and myths doesn't make a case in favour of her views. The reader doesn't feel persuaded to agree but mostly disagree because of a lack of evidence, strong or otherwise to her views. She hasn't used any sources from any archaeologists or historians. In my opinion a convincing argument includes dissenting views to further strength the argument. While it is certainly the case that in the past women in media have been compared to dragons and snakes, however, Foubister doesn't utilise that to even put forth her argument. I had a university class that discussed the ramifications of dragons and monsters portrayed with human female sex attributes, breasts, etc. The best case scenario is that other students use this book as a way to improve the argument of a great snake goddess or what not to do in order to advocate their argument of a great snake goddess.
