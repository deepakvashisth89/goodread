Walden is a somewhat atypical and indefinable book, as it straddles the boundaries between autobiography, philosophy, and poetry. Thoreau explores varied themes in his account of his original “experience” of life. Indeed, for two years, he lived in isolation in a cabin near Walden Pond to detach himself from material goods and have only the essentials for human life - namely, food, shelter, clothing, and heat. The story combines very concrete descriptions of his life in Walden (construction of his hut, planting of his field of beans), poetic descriptions of Nature (the pond, the snow, animals of the different seasons), and philosophical reflections ( on the economy, social relations, the need to refocus on Nature amid the Industrial Revolution).I liked this reading, which I found accessible for a philosophy book. I was sensitive to many ideas developed by Thoreau, particularly those on Nature, which remain very relevant today: it is not for nothing that Thoreau is considered a pioneer of ecology.
