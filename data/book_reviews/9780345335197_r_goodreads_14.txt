In the afterward, Klein tells us that he is more intersted in Guthrie's life than his music, which explains my initial disappointment with the book. But, with such a life it is a great read.Born Oklahoma 1912 and named for Woodrow Wilson. Father a democrat who held some local political positions but always thwarted from statewide office by political machine. Dad was well-read, ambitious, and made money in real-estate but when oil boom came he declined considerably financially. He was fiercely anti-socialist and took part in the lynching of a Black family. KKK member.Mother was mentally ill. Daughter died from burns from her? Or was daughter covering for her when she said she lit herself on fire? Set Woody’s father on fire and was committed while he recovered. She DIed of Huntington chorea.After mother’s commitment, Woody lived alone and with various families as a teen and traveled some. Observed hobo culture. Rejoined his father in the panhandle but often stayed with others or rented motel rooms. Father eventually on skid row, but always wore a tie as a symbol of dignity.Never achieved at school but was at library reading all the time when a late teen and into early 20’s. He was interested in a great many things. He would plunge into a topic learn everything and then move on. Got into a fundamentalist church and knew and loved bible, but then moved on. Read “The Prophet by Kahil Gilbran and loved the idea that ther is a unity to all things and every living thing had value. This led him briefly to be a spiritualist. No charge, just a donation if you wish.From early age an outcast who performed jigs and played harmonica for others. Took up guitar as a teen.Country music was looked down upon and since poor listened to it, was no market. But, then the Carter Family and Jimmie Rogers and Grand Ol Oprey came in the 1920’s. Old time mountain standards and gospel. Rogers borrowed from blues and was less traditional. He formed a trio with a guitarist and fiddle player. They played house parties, dances, on radio. He also did lots of comedy on stage mostly physical.Money bothered Woody. People acted like animals to get it and losing it drove them crazy. Thus, squandered any windfalls and did not care to think about it beyond what was needed for immediate use.Money was not a problem for him in Depression. He was a great artist and had perfect penmanship. Made money making signs for businesses. Also sent in art and slogans to companies, not asking for money. Sometimes they sent it and offered him a job but he would say no. Was a soda jerk who sold booze under the table. Many ways to make money and lots of opportunities but turned them all down which frustrated his friends.A wanderer - He would unexpectedly and inexplicably vanish for months at a time traveling the country as a hobo, although he feared the trains as dangerous. Would abandon his wife and kids. One time finished playing a song with a buddy and got up and announced he was going to California again and left.In LA he established himself on radio with a woman he called lefty lou. They sang old songs that were familiar to all the migrants in California, often changing the words. He was a master at word games and coming up with new lyrics on the spot. While not a good guitar player or singer his harmonies with lefty lou worked and people loved them. Published own song book.Not political until he came to see the treatment of migrant farm workers in California. Saw communists as the only ones willing to fight for them. “I’m not a red, but I’ve been in the red my whole life.” Perhaps never joined party but a strong supporter and friend of many. If he did join he probably quite after the first or second discipline. Wrote a column for the Daily worker and raised money for labor causes. Followed party line including supporting FDR in 1944 and then Wallace in 1948.1940 was his breakthrough in NYC. Benefit concert got him noticed. Invited to DC where he recorded for National Archives (not released until 1965); recorded Dust Bowl Ballads album which sold only 500-1000 copies but one of most important recordings in history. CBS radio hired him in NYC. They paid him well and he moved family to nice apartment. ANd then, he threw it all away. Felt guilty he was a sell-out. Would have to tone down politics. Quit and abruptly moved family to California.It was at this time that he became close with Leadbelly. Had been recorded in prison along with many other Blacks. The man who recorded him got the governor to pardon him for an assault charge. He had previously been pardoned for murder! Woody also became friends with Burl Ives although he did not like his music, and met and befriended Pete Segar while being recorded in DC for National Archives. Seegar just learning the banjo at the time.Guthrie was legit on race. One time was staying with relatives in Louisiana. Told them he had eaten at Black restaurants on way there from New York. The enraged man of the house asked what woody would think if a Black married one of his daughters. He said, I wouldn’t mind that a bit. The relative threw him out of the house. When he was told he could not sit with two Blacks he had just performed with for dinner, he told them he would meet them at the Train station and then turned the buffet table on its side and walked away.Guthrie a part of the democratization of the arts and culture during the 1930’s. An appreciation for America. Having survived depression without resorting to extremism meant that we were an extraordinary people. Those who had suffered and survived were special and should be learned from. Thus, the oral history project, the interest in folk music, Thomas Hart Betnton, Dorthea Lange. The common man was celebrated for his resilience. Very few of the photos taken by Farm Security Administration showed people choking with hunger or groveling. Pete Seegar and Woody were a reaction against tin pan alley music production and saw music as political.“This Land is Your Land” - Written in reaction to God Bless America which Woody hated, because it suggested we should do nothing. God will save us. An atheist by now. Wrote it originally as “God Blessed America for Me” as the chorus. Put it away for 5 years. In 1944 a folklorist with a recording studio recorded Guthrie and others in marathon sessions and this included the This Land we know today. Based on an old church tune that Carters had also borrowed.“Union Maid” written in response to criticism that his labor songs did not sing about women. Included the iconic line “I’m stickin with the union.”Hired for a month by power company in northwest mostly because they pitied him when he showed up with his family. Wrote a bunch of songs including the Grand Coulee.Almanac Singers - Pete Segar, Woody and others did peace songs when Soviets were neutral then switched to union songs. Members were strong communists. Travelled from one strike area to the next, spoke to strikers and then wrote songs right on the spot and performed them to promote the strike. Once again, they were only writing lyrics. Borrowed old tunes. Once Pearl Harbor came they ditched everything for pro-war songs. This is when Woody put “This Machine Kills Fascists” on guitar. Group would get noticed but then chances for big success would be ended once it came to light they were communists. Broke apart when most went into service.Lived in New York away from family during war. A publisher had him rewrite the draft of his autobiography - Bound For Glory - an old song he sang for a group of hobos on a train. At times it was more a novel than a fact based autobiography. Got good reviews and Woody wrote fiction from time to time. Many projects that did not seem to get finished. Became a celebrity in NYC with book published. Lots of radio appearances as well as the usual performances.Second wife, Marjorie, was a dancer for a show that Woody was a part of. She got pregnant while they were both married to others. This would eventually be Arlo’s mom. Had 4 kids together. They lived together on Coney Island but Woody continued to have flings.WWII Service - He was in his 30’s. When the draft board came for him in 43 he joined merchant marines. Did it again when the army wanted him. The third time the merchant marine rejected him for his connections to communism that he did not deny. Trained in army but not deployed. In Merchant Marines he was torpedoed and also on a ship that was hit with a remote mine.NYC recordings - in between deployments in 1944 he was recorded along with other folk singers. Some albums came out of this which sold a few copies to nYC leftists.Post WWII - Could not decide if he wanted to be a writer or singer primarily. Finally finished a novel only to have it rejected. He Seegar and others formed the People’s Songs group in Greenwich Village. Song writers from the left and also arranged gigs. But, much had changed. Organized labor not interested in folk singers with ties to communists any longer even though Communists had helped them greatly in the past. Workers had found prosperity. Also, took to writing long ballads and when an audience was not receptive he became hostile to them. Continued to write songs constantly. Children's songs were good but Deportee in 1949 was his last great work. Woody’s drinking more of a problem now. Many stories of how he loved to provoke others in social situations. Went to prison for writing pornographic letters to a woman - something he did often. Really quite a jerk at times!Tragedy - His daughter Cathy died from an electrical fire as a little girl - the second person in his life lost to fire. Unlike his other children they had been very close.The Weavers - when People’s Songs collapsed, Seegar formed this and found commercial success first as a novelty act. Blacklisted. Came back in 60’s without Seegar.Publishing - If you change a folk song significantly you can claim royalties so a publisher had Woody record hundreds of songs looking for songs to publish - “This Land is Your Land” was one of them. Eventually, in the 60’s this brought in 50,000 a year. At this point Guthrie in hospital and a trust fund set up for children of his second marriage.Behavior became increasingly erratic in early 1950’s. He became dangerous to kids and wife so she kicked him out. He drifted out to California where he took another man’s wife, aged 20. Both got divorced. They lived in a swamp in Florida. Had a kid. Again, his behavior became erratic - was it drinking? Finally at age 40 diagnosed with Huntington chorea, a nervous disease that made you shake uncontrollably and slowly rotted the brain. At first he was able to check himself out of hospital because he was there voluntarily. Took one last hobo trip in which he got arrested many times. Eventually, he was committed to a hospital in New Jersey. Dylan who had mimicked him met him there and was taken in by the Gleasons who took Woody out of the hospital every Sunday to their place where old friends and up and coming folk singers came to pay homage. The folk craze was hot and they knew Guthrie. Music industry had embraced it as an alternative to Rock. First were clean and safe like Kingston Trio. But then Phil Ochs and Dylan came along. Dylan was a disappointment to Seegar. To seegar music was to be political and pure. Dylan got into Folk because it seemed to offer more freedom than formulaic rock and his music became more personal. Making popular music not a problem to him.Religion - found Jesus at the end. Prior to this he believed in god but did not want to play favorites.Eventually his second wife took charge of his care and sidelined the Gleasons taking Woody to Brooklyn hospital and bringing him to Coney island on Sundays. Died at age of 55 in 1967.A 1953 benefit concert for his care is what got him noticed. In addition, This Land had become a standard in schools and recorded by many. Whitewashed of its radicalism some thought it a better national anthem.Americana - singer songwriters of 90’s who would not call themselves folksingers - less political, but outside of mainstream.
