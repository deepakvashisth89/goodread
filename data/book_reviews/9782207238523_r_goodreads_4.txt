I've skimmed through a bunch of reviews of Ira Levin'sSliver, and the word "trash" or "trashy" seems to populate many of them, and I agree, yes, that's an apt description.This was really bad, even by trash standards.Let me say, I wasn't expecting this book to be some revelation, or award winning literary fiction. I'm not judging it against the likes of great writers. I'm judging it against 1) other Ira Levin novels I've read and 2) decently written "trashy" novels.I read and truly enjoyed his debutA Kiss Before Dying. I didn't love, but appreciated the iconic ideas behindThe Stepford Wives.I really don't want to believe Ira Levin wrote this book. I have to, because his name is on the cover, but holy hell is this bad. Not only "trashy", but often unreadable trash, as in, terrible writing. Sometimes you can't tell whose point of view we are in. Are we in the pervert's head? Or the victim's? Who knows. Sometimes the overly snappy writing with important words missing and innumerable ellipses make scenes SO confusing (and trash should NEVER be confusing, the great thing about trash is that it moves along fast and easy and doesn't challenge the reader). Thankfully for me, I had accepted that it didn't even much matter if I understood, so I stopped re-reading these problematic scenes. Once, a character was suddenly introduced by name by the head-hopping narrator, but none of the heads knew the character, so how could the name be known? Several times, the name and age of murder victims was repeated only a few paragraphs apart.It was as if Levin carelessly handed a sloppy first draft to his editor, and the publishing house just printed it, as is.And what a laughable ending that was! Utterly laughable. I laughed out loud. I can't say I wasn't entertained. :)Too bad, I was on board for a somewhat trashy story about voyeurism. Instead I was agog at how absolutely bad it was, and oh how the mighty have fallen!I bought my copy at a little used bookstore I frequent every few months. The woman who owns it is a real character, and had never commented on my purchases (vintage Updike, Thomas Mann, Anita Brookner, and others) untilSliver. She lit up. She and her coworker heartily recommended it. Go figure!
