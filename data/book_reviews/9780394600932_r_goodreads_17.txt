Hester Prynne wears an "A" embroidered on her clothes, a scarlet letter so that everyone can see the nature of her sin: she has had an affair with a man other than her husband, from which little Pearl was born. Two distinct characters complete this stunning picture of Puritan America: Arthur Dimmesdale, the young pastor, and Chillingworth, the doctor and husband of Hester.This novel intrigued me for a long time, and after reading it, I found it challenging to form a definite opinion. After a difficult start and a period of adapting to the author's style, we discover a Hester who accepts her fate and does not rebel against the Church's decision. She thinks that her daughter sees only this letter from her and becomes increasingly intrigued by her mother's evasive answers. Chillingworth's character is demonizing: he persecutes the young pastor to drive him mad. All the scenes in the story borrow from a religious symbol. The ending reveals sovereign puritanism: the lovers have not managed to escape far from this insane world. One question obsesses me: Is it love between Hester and the pastor? Because she protects him from her husband, apart from their last conversation, they are never together, and their desire to flee is not a passion for freedom. It was a reading that allowed me to understand this era of America and how difficult it is to find one's place when we deviate from the path laid out.
