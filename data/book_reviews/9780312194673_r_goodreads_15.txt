The promise suggested in the foreword was not kept.

I like this story, and I enjoyed reading it, but it didn't ultimately move me. Tbh I was more intrigued by the questions presented and the context given in the foreword by Huxley's wife than I was by the story itself.

Contemplating the concept of healing and healers, how healing works, why some treatments are effective on some people and harmful to others are very interesting questions. The notion that some people have a "healing" presence that is so nebulous it cannot be defined but is undeniably present. The idea that healing the body and healing the soul are different, and how can one know what should be prioritized? The idea that some treatments work but we do not know why is something I find fascinating and I've love to read books that really explore these questions.

This book just didn't do that. Perhaps in part due to the brevity I found this fable to be a little uninspired. Especially the side plot between Earl and Sharon just felt so predictable. Jacob also read as flat to me, just an ultra simple guy who is naive but virtuous. The takeaways as well felt very basic and didn't leave me with anything to chew on, which I was very let down by.

2.5/5
