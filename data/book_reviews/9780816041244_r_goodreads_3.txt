Outstanding! Pierre Berton's legendary "Arctic Grail" has always been a favorite of mine and kicked off my earlier readings years ago about the Northwest Passage(s) and polar explorations at each end of the globe. But Delgado's book is no simple coffee table book (although it is of those physical dimensions). The author is an experienced arctic hand and starts the reader way back before Berton's book with the voyages of Hudson and Frobisher. Then he goes all the way forward into the 1990s discussing more modern attempts at the passage by the Canada government, world militaries and commercial ships. Of course Berton's book also dealt with the North Pole, but Delgado has a focus just on the Northwest Passage, a truly amazing story in itself. Too bad, it was published before the "Erebus" and "Terror" were discovered in the last ten years. But, then again, there may never be an end to the discovery of new artifacts from the myriad of missions sent to look for John Franklin's lost expedition. This story will never get old.
