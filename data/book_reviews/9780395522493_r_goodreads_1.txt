This book states that ground covers are plants with a purpose, just like a pet dog who herds squeaky toys. And the book is correct: Ground covers work hard, covering slopes and other un-lovely garden areas. In hot areas, it takes special plants to maintain coverage when the weather amps up the thermometer. If you live in such areas, and with climate change we all will at some point, this is a handy publication to tell you what works and what doesn’t.The guide begins by explaining the need for ground cover and then explains the soil, light, and caring that’s needed for the plants. It also comes with a handy zone map for the United States so you can see which plants match your home garden zone. Then, each highlighted plant receives one page with a color photo on top and an explanation with growing tips right beneath the picture.I like to garden, and I found this quite useful, even though most plants wouldn’t survive my zone (hot desert). But it was educational to learn that I had been calling some ground covers the wrong names and assuming some of the plants could only reside in certain soils, when that isn’t true. The book fits the hand, so you can carry it while shopping at the local nursery. Basic and does the trick.Book Season = Spring (time for planting)
