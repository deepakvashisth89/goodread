This is a great book, though it falters somewhat in the final chapters. What would happen, White posits, if a rationalist/atheist (clearly modelled on White himself, even down to being named Mr. White) and a dyed in the wool Irish Catholic woman experienced a visitation from what might be the Archangel Michael, who enjoins them to build a new ark? The exchanges between the absurdly intellectual White and the equally absurdly anti-intellectual MRs O'Callaghan are consistently hilarious. Those bothered by vicious satire might not go for this book, especially given the gleeful harshness with which White calumniates the Irish (who apparently look more askance at a dog digging up a recently-buried baby than they do at the murderous mother who was too lazy to bury it properly--as just one example). The humour here is black, almost obsidian, and consistently laugh out loud funny. White's (the character's) attempts to rationalize and plan are meticulously and amusingly detailed. The Irish are mercilessly lampooned. The ending is typically modernist in its studied ambiguity. We are left doubting the meaning of everything that has occurred.
