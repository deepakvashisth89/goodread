I always want to like the Viagens Interplanetarias series and being that the Swords of Zinjaban is the last novel having to do with planet Krishna, it’s stands to reason that I should love it. But here reason fails. The reality for me is that L. Sprague de Camp became more and more preoccupied with the sexual antics of his characters on a planet where the inhabitants—the Krishians are humanoid and on more than one occasion prone to the same sexual antics, and so the stories became more of an interplanetary space “soap opera.”

Now, I enjoyed the return of other characters from previous Krishna books: Percy Mjipa from The Prisoner of Zhamanak and The Tower of Zanid with Anthony Fallon and a half-dozen more. Revisiting old and favorite characters is a warm and fuzzy experience. But the Swords of Zinjaban, while supposed to be about the first movie made on another planet, is merely the backdrop for the two protagonists,  Fergus Reith  and Alicia Dyckman, who spend most of their time “trying to get back together again,” while reliving old and disastrous memories of their relationship which are recorded in three earlier installment in the series.

To mix it up, there is a kidnapping thrown in, an unexpected imprisonment, and even a rumor of war that becomes a war, but is reduced to a half-dozen pages as does the process of making the first film on planet Krishna.

In the writing there is that lightness which should bring about some light humor. Well, I couldn’t find it. Maybe other readers did—or will. Once I began the series back with volume one, The Queen of Zamba. I wanted to get to the end, which I did, but I wish the story was stronger with less sexual sparring.
