This is a very insightful account into military and executive decision-making during the events surrounding the invasion of Panama and the Gulf War. A lot of insight is provided into structure of military decision making in the White House, and the personal involvement of Powell and Cheney, among others.Personally, I found the writing to be quite interesting, both in terms of the personal aspects involved in tough decision making, as well as the background suspense around the political events, especially when it came to the tense escalation in the Gulf War.Perhaps the only negative point I'd give to the book is that it seems a bit doubtful to what extent these accounts are accurate, although it seems that the book is well sourced.
