The Book of Merlyn is a sort of epilogue or coda for T.H. White's epic The Once and Future King. As King Arthur comes to the end of his life, he wonders whether everything he has striven for was really worth it. He finds Merlyn in a sort of pocket universe, outside of time, and for one last time the two discuss war, peace and how to live a worthy life. It was an interesting way to provide closure for Arthur's life and the saga itself. However, I do have a couple of complaints. First, it rehashes a lot of what has already been covered in earlier parts of . Secondly, in finding the best way for man to live, Merlyn uses the example of ants and geese to discuss capitalism and communism, fascism and anarchy. Needless to say, I didn't think assigning ideologies to animals worked. While this didn't decrease the enjoyment of reading , this coda didn't add much to the experience. 3.25 stars
