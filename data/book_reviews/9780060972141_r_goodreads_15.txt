Well, this was my first foray into Biblical criticism. I thought I would be more impressed, but I wasn't.My primary thought after reading this was "*This* is the big bad boogeyman that religious people are afraid of? These are the emperor's new clothes."Having realized that the Torah does indeed have some question-raising elements like internal contradictions on facts, I was curious to see what Biblical critics would raise.I was not impressed.Kudos to REF for writing engagingly and clearly. The book is not a hard read and he explains himself simply.The content, however, is just unconvincing.A. He provided far less specificity than I expected. There were only a few cases when he lays the text out side by side to show supposed repetitions.I went to another book where he lays out all the "damning evidence" in the Torah and I was still not impressed.B. Much of the reasoning was circular. I really do not understand how this passes in academia. "Source X uses this word ergo all the the times where this word appears is Source X." Really??"Source X, which we designated because it lacks a certain word, also doesn't ever mention X and X. See, this also bolsters our claim that these are separate sources!" Uh, you're the one who split them up to suit yourself. Also, maybe they don't mention X and X because it's a very large book and X and X can't appear everywhere, all the time.C. A lot of the other assignation of source was so ridiculously speculative and creative that I cannot believe this is taken, or was taken, as "science" and not just creative interpretation. There is no other external source to make you think this and you're saying that something was written by a supposed pro-Aharonic faction because it praises Aharon here and then when it praises Moses it must have been written by a supposed pro-Moses faction?Has it never occurred to you that the same author can write both good and bad about the same people? I cannot understand this wholesale invention of factions to explain something that's much more simply explained by "people are complicated and the author recognized that."And suggestions such as Yom Kippur being invented when "Judah felt guilty in the wake of the destruction and exile."... come the heck on? That's how you're going to argue something was invented???D. The author points out a lot of very interesting parallel word choices between later prophets and the Pentatuech. He then concludes that this must mean that those sections of the Pentateuch were written at the time of those later prophets.It's much more reasonable to assume that the later prophets were specifically referencing their national text. All you did was show me how literate and well-read the prophets were.E. The author claims that the Northern text and Judean texts were separate but then when the Northern Kingdom was exiled, scribes coming to Judea combined the 2 traditions and this was then somehow accepted. Aside from the obvious underlying question about documentary hypothesis that any version we find of the Torah is our current one and not a split up "source" text, this sounds ridiculous. People don't just accept texts as divine from one day to the other.I know the starting premise of this book is that no divine revelation could have occurred and therefore it's all made up, but at the very least go back as early as Moses rather than make up this totally irrational way of holy text acceptance.F. Everyone in the Jewish tradition is a liar, apparently, and their intended audience complete dummies. But don't worry, it's a pious fraud, it's not as bad as it sounds. Gee, thanks.G. The "redactor" is the most contradictory and weirdest editor that there ever was. In some cases he cuts up sentences and splices things around word by word, and in others he leaves the text alone because it's so holy. Which is it? It can't be both. And also, who the heck edits a text like that? No one. And this redactor is a real moron for leaving so many "contradictions" in if he really was already editing it to suit his taste.A lot of the "contradictions" are not contradictions. Stuff like Moses being righteous and then Moses also losing his temper are not contradictions. I don't know how this is not obvious. And some of the other real contradictions are still not solved using documentary hypothesis.H. Another assumption of this book is that prophecies are not possible ergo any time an earlier book prophesies something that happens later, it must have been written in later. I understand that you're operating with certain axioms but... the premise of the book internally is that prophecies are real.I. An appalling ommission in this book: any external archeology or linguistic or historic studies. One cannot simply study a text and make things up based only on internal texts and completely ignore any external context that can either bolster or disprove your ideas. It's absurd that an academic book did this.J. At the same time, a major criticism of source theory is that it fails to look at the text as a literary whole and extract plausible reasons that an intelligent, good writer would choose to frame things the way they did.Things I did like:A. Political analysis of the two kingdoms. It was enlightening to revisit this history from a more general view and I have no problem saying that the Books of Kings etc are politically motivated. Duh.B. Showing the dimensions of the Tabernacle were aligned with those of the Temple and fit beneath the cherubs. I liked that. One more point for the continuity of Jewish practice. You're welcome.C. Showing the cool parallels between Jeroboam ben Nevat's Golden Calf and the desert generation's Golden Calf. Yes, the authors of the Book of Kings text were clearly making a rhetorically loaded association in *that* direction rather than the other way around.D. The book's general legibility to a popular audience.Yes, there are some very good questions to investigate within the Torah, such as the aforementioned contradictions and insertions such as "to this very day" that contradict a very fundamentalist view of the Torah that is popular today in religious circles.Nevertheless, much more reasonable answers exist than this creative and inventing documentary hypothesis theory.
