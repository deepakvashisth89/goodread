As complete a biography as you could hope for, with an impressive amount of research required to compile hundreds of letters to tell of Brown from his own words and those of the people who knew him best. And as one of the best historical analysts ever of Black America, Du Bois provides the level of insight and analysis you would expect.It's a dry going, but naturally picks up as Du Bois gets to the Bleeding Kansas years and then of course The Raid. And though I've read a lot about John Brown and am quite familiar with the Harper's Ferry events, this book taught me more about the prevarication of his allies in the run-up to The Raid, and the unfortunate circumstances that led to his mission's delay and subsequent failure.I'm familiar with Brown's speeches from the last months of his life but I never tire of reading them again, they're that righteous and inspirational. And I'm happy to have them all compiled now in one source that I can tab for future reading.Not Bad Reviews
