5 stars for sheer enjoyment, this one is my favorite of the series so far, I think, it drew me right in and wouldn’t let go!It’s summer of 1942, Henry Gamadge is overseas doing hush-hush war work, and his young wife Clara is at a charming cottage in rural Connecticut. Friends will be joining her soon, as well as her husband, hopefully. Meanwhile, she and her cook have been seeing an odd character lurking on the edge of the woods about the place - a woman in a faded purple dress and a large sun bonnet that obscures her features. She’s appeared a couple times at sunset, never approaching within hailing distance or waving, just watching.Clara explores her temporary home, finding a similar purple gown and bonnet in the attic; she learns from a near neighbor that elderly sisters used to live there, and one of them (the miserly, sickly one) died in the house the previous summer. The other sister moved to the family farm nearby upon inheriting her sister’s small fortune, remodeled it for herself, and the cottage for renters - but seems reluctant to enter it now that Clara is in residence. Rumors abounded about the sister’s death - is the apparition lurking on the edge of the property seeking revenge?After the lurking apparition comes close enough to cause the sister’s horse to spook and cause an accident, Clara and her cook have no choice but to bring the injured, unconscious woman inside. Clara and her neighbors take turns keeping watch over the woman, but during Clara’s shift the apparition appears through an outside door, Clara is paralyzed by fear but manages to run to the do9r and scream, but too late - the woman is dead.This was really a fun read - the creepy tension was palpable and well done. I suspected I knew whodunnit and how, and was eventually proved right, but I didn’t mind, there were so many satisfying twists and turns and red herrings - and a double twist at the very end! I can’t wait to read on, I see why Daly was considered Agatha Christie’s favorite American mystery writer, she’s getting better with every book. I’m so glad her books are available again.
