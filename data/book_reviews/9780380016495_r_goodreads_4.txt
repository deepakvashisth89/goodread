This novella about the morning hours in the life of a man who is falling apart is authentic New York narrative AND somber urban fable.Written in '56, it is still supremely relevant and I bet there are dozens, perhaps thousands, of Tommy Wilhems out there in the world, and they are all MODERN MEN; Wilhem is a man confused and, in the same vein as lame-o Holden C., one very unhappy with his placement in society, while also questioning his duties as a "man." The episode between Wilhem and his father is an example of what is at the center of most of these "The Modern Man Undone" novels (this one reminded me instantly of Nathaniel West's "The Day of the Locust"... in its authentic representation of the ambiance of an American city [Los Angeles]),which is Freudian, deep & serious, & it is this discussion between son & elder that is served so ruthlessly here. & you know that with a title like "Seize the Day" the plot will be pretty much an ironic one.
