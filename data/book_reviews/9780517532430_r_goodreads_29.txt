This book is amazing.As far as I can see, the main criticism of this book goes something like this: "This is a book about a master assassin-philosopher-caver-lover-wizard, and that's silly". Well, yes. It's very silly, and intentionally so. I think most of the people who criticize it have simply missed the point.The book's handling of its silly premise is what makes it brilliant: it knows it's silly, and yet takes itself seriously. The author asks: imagine there was such a man; in that case, who would he be? What kind of character will he have? What kind of training? What kind of history? What kind of life philosophy?It's a very clever book. Some of it is wisdom, and some mere cleverness. But it works, and it's great.The protagonist isn't merely a self-insert power fantasy (though he is also that, of course): he isn't some childish edgy guy who is somehow hyper-competent, but rather his character fits his role in the story. And honestly, that's rare to find in this kind of book.I would categorize this book as belonging to a wide meta-genre: intelligently written, well researched, a half-parody that expertly toes the line between a true parody/comedy and a serious book. Another example in this meta-genre would beThe Princess Bride(fantasy); and to a lesser degree perhaps alsoThe Moon Is A Harsh Mistress(revolutionary). While all these books aren't as deep as they could have been, I still find them most enjoyable. As far as reading-for-fun goes, this is perhaps my favorite genre.
