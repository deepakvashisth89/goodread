This was a surprise. After not being impressed with several of the last winners, I am impressed in some ways with this book. However, here is the thing: I am impressed as an adult with this book. I still think this is a book adults think children should read. I think getting a child to agree about this book might be pretty hard. For one thing, the cover, although pertinent to the story (one of Dicey sitting with her arm over the rowboat) is not one that will make many kids interested in finding out what happens next. While we drift through much of a school year, this is most emphatically not a school book, although it is at the same time. We do not get resolution in this book, except on one item and that is huge. There is a character in this book that hangs over the family but never reaches into their daily lives: Momma. Dicey still feels responsible for her younger siblings but is relieved to let more of that responsibility fall on Gram. Eventually though, Gram tells Dicey she needs to assume some of that responsibility again because she knows the kids in a way Gram simply cannot after such a short time. Dicey does well in English. Perhaps too well. I rather have a suspicion this might be an incident from Voigt's own life.

If you feel like my review is wandering around, you just experienced much of what I felt as a reader of the book itself. While I was content to watch the story unfold, kids are busy creatures these days and I suspect will not have the patience to sit through this title. This is not your typical book where things wrap up at the end. There is some of that in the one big resolution. Much of this book, maybe around 90% really happens in Dicey's head while she is observing her siblings' life as well as her own life. But we do not know if James's idea for teaching Maybelle how to read is ultimately successful. We do not know if James works out his issues with a "friend" or not. We don't know what happens with, pretty much everything. Because life is like that, you don't often know when life has finished a chapter. That part is going to always make this title a hard sell. Beautiful story and writing, never will be extremely popular. 1983 Newbery Award.
