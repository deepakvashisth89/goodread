A nice collection of science fiction stories dating as far back as the 1890s. The collection features works from the science fiction "greats", including Wells, Clarke, Bradbury, Le Guin, and Asimov. While entertaining, I did not find much of any real substance. Bradbury'sAll Summer in a Dayis probably the only exception- there's a reason why we all read it in English class. My main gripe with the book is that almost half of the collection are excerpts from larger works. Despite giving you a short synopsis before the story begins, I felt that that alone was not enough to get me up to speed and invested in the story. A lot of them felt awkwardly ended and unresolved. That's not the authors' fault though; their works weren't meant to be chopped up like that. I suppose it could entice people to seek out the rest of the book from an excerpt, but for me it did not. My other weird gripe with the book is the inclusion of an excerpt from Ursula K. Le Guin'sThe Wizard of Earthsea. Having read some of her books already, I was thrilled to see her in the roster. While the story itself was good (though again suffered from being clipped from a larger book), it was thoroughly and completely fantasy. Literally wizards and dragons. I have no clue what it was doing in a sci-fi anthology, and found it incredibly jarring. None of the other stories even hovered close to a sci-fi and fantasy blend, which isolated it even more. Just a really odd move on the curator's part.Overall, I would most recommend the book to those looking to sample a lot of different voices in classic science fiction. It's pleasant, doesn't make you think too hard, and highlights some of the fun and fantastical ideas people were pushing the genre in during its mid-20th century heyday. Would also be good for younger/ teen readers.
