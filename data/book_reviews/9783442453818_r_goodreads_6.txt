The first life the bluebear remembers is that time he was seemingly abandoned. He was drifting in a walnut shell in the middle of the ocean. That’s when the mini pirates discovered him. He grew up with them. Until he grew so big their ship was about to sink. So they marooned him on an island.


This is a huge book. It’s quite heavy and has 700 big pages. 700. Seven hundred! That is a lot, considering this feels aimed at children as the target audience. I didn’t think it was going to be a problem in the first 300 pages, but after like 500 pages I did start to feel it to be honest. But, you do get a lot in return.


In terms of plot, there’s not much to say. It actually reminds me of Beyond the Deepwoods from the The Edge Chronicles series in that regard. It’s a very episodic story. There is sometimes a bit of plot in these mini adventures, but it’s just not the core of the story. It’s all about exploring the world building. And each mini adventure gives us a chance to explore another interesting setting and creatures in this fantasy world.


The main character is a bluebear who we don’t really know anything about. Not even he knows who he is or where he came from. What we do know is that he’s a bear, so he’s big and strong. But he never abuses his power. He is also not a brave and fearless adventurer. He adapts and perseveres in every new setting because he just wants to find his own place in this world. And he slowly grows as a character in every adventure.


Let’s just say it like it is. This book has too many pages. But, I do think it’s a fun adventure with a lot of cool world building. And because it has so many pages, the world building is phenomenal. There is a lot to explore and discover. It’s also fun and quirky, and pretty imaginative. Despite its many pages, it also never feels like the story is too slow. It’s an amusing and enjoyable read that moves along at a steady pace. And I definitely chuckled a few times. But it’s so long.
