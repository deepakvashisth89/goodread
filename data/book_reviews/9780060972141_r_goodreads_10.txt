A splendid book. In a better world than this one, a dozen of books like this would come out every month, to great admiration and acclaim. It explains a complex and controversial subject in a clear way. A lay-person with very little knowledge of the subject can come to this book and emerge at the end significantly better informed. The author's opinions are clearly labeled. The author invites you to disagree with him and look into the matter further for yourself. The author corrects, clarifies, and improves earlier editions of the same book.Still, I had to consult reference works fairly often, see below.Since the author has done a fine thing, of course, the result is that he is subject to ridicule by certain other experts who feel he is dumbing down the complex topic. Meanwhile, religious nuts and narrow-minded bigots tear him down because he dares to suggest that the holy text, while inspired by an intelligence greater than our own, has been filtered through mere human beings so, therefore, may be liable to shortcomings, often a result of human bias and political maneuvering.As many others here on Goodreads and elsewhere have said, even though the book’s title suggests that the entire Bible will be explored, it is largely about the first five books of the Old Testament (a.k.a., “Pentateuch”, “Torah”), with some talk about later books of the Old Testament.That’s the end of the review.I studied this book in preparation for entering a graduate school program that will treat, in part, the Old Testament.
