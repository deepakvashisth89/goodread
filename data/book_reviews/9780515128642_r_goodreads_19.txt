I liked the general story, which was what kept me reading through the many, many unnecessary paragraphs. Multiple times I had thought,why did I yet again read what Bethia thinks of Rory; I understood her sentiments the first twenty times. In short, it was, consequently, unnecessarily long.Also, Bethia in the ending was annoying."'I will go with you,' she said stubbornly." "She did not want to leave [Dougal, her brother]. But she would.For the Black Knave." I don't think it was for the Black Knave at all. Rory needs to go back to Braemoor to pretend that he is outraged by Bethia's absence. Instead of one person going to Braemoor and then travelling back to the coast, he has to take Bethia just because she wants to come (like a petulant child). Instead of having to find one fresh horse, Rory has to find two. And Bethia is weak and very likely slowing Rory down. To quote her thoughts, "she would be happy to never to see a horse again, much less a saddle" and "only Rory's presence kept her going." Rory mentioned, "'I should no' have brought you, lass.'" She replies, "indignantly," "'I can keep up with you." Sure.Rory (and Bethia) does avoid capture by the English by pretending that Bethia is the escaped Dougal, but I believe that Rory could have skirted the English soldiers some other way. Therefore, her coming along was just an excuse to put the two main characters together, which I guess serves the book's purpose, but, nonetheless, I thought it was annoying.During the ride to the beach: "It was a miracle he had not ridden her to death. She had gone through hell because of him, because he'd not been able to contain his lust." First, again, she wanted to come and threatened if he left her behind. Second, they are both experiencing the same kind of hell. If she cannot ride for that hard and for that long, well ... sucks for her. She could have very easily not gone with him in the first place. Third, their moment together was consensual; it was her fault as much as his.I detest characters who think that by just "being there" that they are being helpful.
