McDermid's protagonist Kate Brannigan seems to have potential as an appealing female private eye, but this third in her six-book series seemed a little ho-hum. The first part about she and boyfriend Richard posing as car buyers is great fun, but then Richard gets framed in one of their spiffy short-term auto capers and lands in jail on a drug charge. Predictably, the police do little to consider alternatives, so it’s up to Kate to investigate the who and why on her own. Most of that involves rather dull car chasing and surveillance scenes, in which clues to explaining the whole mess appeared almost too easily. The bit about babysitting Richard’s son was a mere distraction.We notice other readers feel this set is a bit lackluster compared to the Tony Hill & Carol Jordan series. We’ve also read two of those and perhaps might find better entertainment there. That said, it’s almost like McDermid needs a better editor for the Brannigan set – it seems it could be way better without Kate appearing on every page – she’s just not that interesting after a while.
