i first read this book in high school. i bought a mass market paperback copy of it at my local library for 35 cents, put in my backpack, and would read it a paragraph or page at a time when a class finished early or i wrapped up an assignment.

it is a small book, but it took me 5 months to finish.

on my second read, it took me three days.

for one thing, i highly recommend rereading a classic you read closely in high school. i could not make heads or tails of what i was thinking with these highlights. but what i know is i thought it was perfect – i gave it five stars.

i'm surprised i loved this so much then — it's an uneven book, with pages of curmudgeonly old-man-yelling-at-the-sky resistance to change suddenly interrupted by a brilliant observation or breathtaking turn of phrase.

i don't know either if i picked up on its gift of self-awareness, steinbeck's knowledge that the america he's looking for is either dying or never existed, and that its rebirth is not for him.

there's also a shyness i glimpsed, an earnestness and curiosity.

in the time i spent with this book — two widely ranging periods a decade apart — it meant something different to me. but meant something it did.

bottom line: a strange book i'm glad we have.
