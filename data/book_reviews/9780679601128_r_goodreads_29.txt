This is a quick and fairly easy read, drawn from his magisterial 3-volume history of the American Civil War. The prose is vivid and more than a dry history. He provides us with close-ups of what the participants -- mostly higher-ranking, mostly-Confederate in his telling -- are thinking and saying. If anything, the dialogue and characterizations are familiar to me, echoing Michael Shaara'sThe Killer Angelsand the subsequent film "Gettysburg." This edition does provide maps at key points during the battle and Lee's subsequent retreat, which helps. An order of battle might have been helpful, as the Confederate habit of naming brigades and divisions after their commander (rather than numbering them) can be hard to follow, especially as those commanders tended to be wounded or killed in combat and the unit becomes someone else's. The narrative mostly follows the Confederates, although there are a few mentions of Union participants, notably the evening when General George Meade, the new Union army commander, arrives on the battlefield.(If you want the Union side of events, Bruce Catton'sGlory Roadis a good account, drawn from his 3-volume history of the Army of the Potomac.)
