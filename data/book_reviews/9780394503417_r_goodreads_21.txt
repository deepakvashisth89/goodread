Vreeland was an editor at both Vogue and Harper’s Bazaar, neither of which I’ve ever read. I’d also never heard of Vreeland but love memoirs so I picked this book up at a used bookstore. After the first several pages I thought - wow, this lady is a snob, and a weird one. But I stuck with the book and within a couple of chapters I was hooked - on the book and the author.

Her adventures are fascinating. Her humor is engrossing. Her style is adorable. Examples: She refuses to read Gone With the Wind in manuscript form because it “made the telephone directory look like a pocket handkerchief” and she preferred to sleep some at night. She glues on 3-inch fake eyelashes to get her depressed friend to talk. So many people come with style from Indiana, she says, though don’t ask for a list. She tells you lots of stories and then admits at the end some aren’t true - but “you believed me, didn’t you?”

She has chauffeurs and 3 fittings for a single nightgown and thinks lettuce is divine but isn’t sure it qualifies as a food and she wears enough perfume to save you from having to sniff her like a hound. She’s crazy and mad about pretty much everything and everyone. She has the soles of her shoes shined because if you lift your foot at dinner you need impeccable soles.

I think Vreeland and her book are simply divine and dawling, I highly recommend this book.
