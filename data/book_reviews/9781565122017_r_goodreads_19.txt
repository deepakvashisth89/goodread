Wade Brackenbury used his journals and his memory to compose this book, published in 1998. Its strength isn't his writing style, but the thrill of risky ventures in remote parts of China and Tibet. Once I let go of criticizing his writing and simply settled into the narrative, the book held my attention. Brackenbury portrays himself as headstrong. However, he reaches into his upbringing to help the reader sympathize with his stubbornness. The journey into forbidden and foreboding territory, where he is an unexpected alien, results in personal growth. If you like hiking, mountain climbing, or inter-cultural encounters, you'll enjoy this book.
