"Don't quit five minutes before the miracle" is advice often given and heard around 12-step recovery programs, and it is the advice I would give to readers of this short but demanding novel. The protagonist, an artist confined to a wheelchair by an auto accident suffered many years prior to the novel's events, is surrounded and besieged (or is it befriended?) by a coterie of needy individuals as emotionally disabled as his body is physically, and he brings his own supply of need and negation to include in that dark brew. After 150 pages or so of this, the reader longs for some kind of breakthrough, and less determined readers will have set it aside well before then. I can only say that there is one, that the wait is worth it, and it occurs in two unforgettable events that transpire when Maximilian finally ventures alone outside his apartment into the streets of Montreal. In one incident in particular Maximilian finds himself "felled by the stray bullet of...kindness," and I was blown away by the section as well. It redeemed and justified the whole preceding morass, or as the great Canadian songwriter Bruce Cockburn puts it in one of his songs, "that was the straw that broke me open." If you pick up this book, don't quit reading before you reach that part--it is miraculous
