89th book read in 2025My Big Fat Reading Project, in which I attempt to read as many books as I can that have been published in my lifetime, sometimes develops holes, like moths getting at a wool garment, and years after I think I have read all I want from a certain year, I discover books I missed.Cheaper By the Dozen is one of those, published in 1948! I began my reading for the project in 2005, starting with books from 1940, figuring I would get to know the decade into which I was born in 1947. Cheaper By the Dozen came out when I was one year old.It is a memoir by a brother and sister who were born into a family of twelve children. It was a bit hard to follow the birth order but essentially Frank and Lillian Gilbreth brought them into the world from 1902 to 1922. Twelve pregnancies and births in 20 years! Their mother was a professional through it all, teaching and writing books.Such large families are unusual in the 21st century and I think are sometimes considered a sign of poverty, irresponsibility and even abuse of women. The Gilbreths did not see it that way. They decided from the start of their marriage that they wanted a big family, and they got one!Aside from all the hilarity and hair-raising events in this book, I came to the end full of respect for the Gilbreth family. I am sure that was the intent of the authors.I once knew a family who had 12 children. They were Catholic (another stereotype, I know, but still) and they were a jolly bunch living a successful middle class American life in the 1970s. Their mother seemed healthy and content.My sisters and I begged our mother to have one more baby because we wanted a brother. She said she was done having kids. My sisters have four children each.In any case, if you have never read the book, I recommend it.
