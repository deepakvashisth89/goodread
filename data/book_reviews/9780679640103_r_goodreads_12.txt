My, how I loved every word of this book. For anyone interested in the history of the United States from the mid-1850s through 1905, it is must reading. In order to enjoy it fully, one must have a good knowledge of the history of that time or be willing to stop and look up the references.

Adams, much like his best friend John Hay, lived a charmed life. As the great-grandson of the second president of the United States, the grandson of the eighth, and son of the ambassador to Great Britain during the Civil War, for whom he served as personal secretary, inherited wealth made him just as home in the best addresses in Europe as in his native Quincy, Massachusetts and his putative home of Washington, DC.

Adams rubbed shoulders with many of the great men of his age. He wrote histories of the U.S. during the and administrations that, more than 150 years later, are still recognized as being among the most authoritative of those eras.

A pure joy. Essential reading for anyone who strives to understand the scope of American history. Probably more so today than ever.
