I've been reading all the Forgotten Realms novels as nostalgia for the ones I loved as a child. This isn't one of the ones I read back then, so there's no nostalgia for this one in particular.Character-wise, Midnight has slightly more personality than she did in Shadowdale (though it's still rather bland). She is also not a jackass to Adon. I still hate Kelemvor (I'll expand on why but it's spoilery), Cyric's heel turn is worse than I'd hoped for but better done than it could have been, and Adon continues to be the most interesting of the main 4.Some spoilery parts:Kelemvorcontinues to use his curse as an excuse to never do anything helpful despite never showing any inability on his part to control who he kills or anything like that as a result of it. He also manages to convince himself his friends aren't innocent of Elminster's murder, decides to go after them, when he confronts Bane tries to convince himself Midnight's repeatedly betrayed him, holds Midnight hostage and threatens her as part of convincing her she's on their side (and for some reason both Adon and Midnight buy it) and despite being so set on being a hero now that he's gotten the curse removed (seriously, do not make the god of Strife trust a random fighter to keep his word) complains about having to go after the second tablet. Also, he's totally a jackass to Adon.Early Cyricis actually rather interesting, as he has a cynical take on the likely result of the trials, notes how the dalemen are happy to coupe de grace downed Zhentish soldiers instead of taking them prisoner (which everyone else dismisses as the realities of war, and Cyric isn't saying not to do it exactly, but he is saying... Hey, you guys aren't really all that different from the Zhents. Which with the Dalesmen with Kel fantasizing about how they'll torture the party members if they get them, really rather rings true.) It's somewhat unclear why he rescues Midnight (and begrudgingly Adon). Based on the relationship in the previous novel and a little bit at the very beginning of this one, it's because she's his one friend (even Kel is incredibly fast to disavow him as his friend). But a couple chapters later it pivots to full utilitarianism. He is a jackass to Adon, but unlike with Kel, it actually does slot reasonably well into his previous characterization (though previously he wasn't downright murderous and it was more a vibe of 'oh how cute Adon wants to play fight with the big boys'). We repeatedly see that Cyric's life has taught him that power and ruthlessness ensures your survival, and Adon 'willingly' in his eyes giving into passiveness is anathema to him. I don't like it, but it doesn't actually feel horribly out of character, and in a better novel, it could have been an examination of ableism and toxic masculinity. His power thing is also why I said his slide into evil could have been worse. I can understand why, with how the world shaped him, he focused on the tablets and the power it could grant him. I can also understand some of his performative evil later on with his knowledge of Zhentish culture. I do wish, however, that his slide into actual evil had been less abrupt. Some thoughts about how this is what he has to do when he tortures the dalesman, less asshole behavior to Adon and Midnight on the Ashaba, regret at how his actions have pitted him against his friends - this could have been done more elegantly, since in the last book and the first couple chapters here you'll find yourself going 'he kinda has a point though...' Maintain that for a while while he's on the wrong side, why don't you?Adonis basically catatonic in the beginning. It's not 'just' the scar as much as some reviewers think, though something he says later in the novel is that he was nothing before he joined the Sunite order, then, he lost something that's very important in his order, his goddess can't grant his magic either, everyone tells him he's a useless cleric and 'how is this man a Sunite', he's seen goddesses die and is having a general crisis of faith and on top of everything else he feels guilty that he couldn't save Elminster. *takes a deep breath* Point is he's honestly the only character having a semi-realistic emotional reaction to the gods walking among the people of Faerun, and because of it, boy needs soooooo much therapy. Anyway, for the sake of helping his friends survive he eventually manages to get first back to the somewhat bitter but capable point of the end of Shadowdale, then after a chat with Torm manages to find a bit more meaning, though he's still struggling with his faith. Notably, despite all this he manages to remain a genuinely kind-hearted and well-intentioned character, very much a 'help others if I can't help myself' situation. One of the funniest moments to me is mid-book Adon who's still struggling to care going 'I won't steal from the dead!' and Midnight replying that they weren't using it anymore. Dude is still a cleric at heart.Plot wise, the start of the novel is reliant on the awfully done cliffhanger of the previous book, so of course it's terribly done. (It's also slowed AF down by the need to recap literally everything that happened in Shadowdale). The text tells us Cyric is under suspicion due to association with Midnight and Adon. Note, he and Kel still have freedom of movement, and Kel apparently hasn't been mentally linked with his party at all, because that makes sense. The trial goes exactly as cynical Cyric predicted, because they put a catatonic Adon and a gagged Midnight against angry villagers, and called Kel up to testify but he couldn't say anything helpful because of the curse and his own cowardice (seriously bro just reveal the damn curse), and as you might expect, a daring rescue is staged.As you might not expect, the only rescuers are Cyric and Lhaeo the scribe... because, you know, Kel couldn't be bothered because of his curse. Then there's drama because the rescue isn't clean and pretty. Shock and horror, Cyric killed some guards in breaking his friends out to prevent them from being executed. Yeah, it'd be nice and all if he knocked them out, but it's also not like he went on a serial killer rampage and killed a bunch of people who, again, were involved in keeping prisoners for execution. Apparently this is shocking to literally everyone in the novel but him, including the other heroes, and is enough to convince Kelemvor they must all be evil.Anyway, the party travels, and continues their streak of running into random monsters that don't contribute much to the story. Past this point there's a convergence and split and anything I can say will be spoilery, but I will say Scarsdale and Tantras did actually have enjoyable if predictable plot. Also, kaiju battles are fun enough.The bell was stupid convenient though. Also, lol at 'we'll just kill off all the assassins to justify not having any assassins in 2E' instead of justifying it by saying they're specialized thieves or something.
