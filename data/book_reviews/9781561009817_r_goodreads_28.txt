Alice Hoffman has written a new version ofWuthering Heights, this time set in a remote, modern day New England village. Hollis is the dark-eyed orphan heartthrob brought into the Murray household. March is the spitfire who falls insanely in love with Hollis (and he returns the favor). Alan is March's slimy brother who persecutes Hollis. By and large, Hoffman follows the original plot faithfully, but it is her own lyrical prose which puts a pleasant spin on Brontë's dark tale of obsessive love. Many readers have observed thatWuthering Heights'sstory of mad love is a bit over the top. Yet in Hoffman's version, Hollis and March's intense, life-long romance is perfectly credible.
