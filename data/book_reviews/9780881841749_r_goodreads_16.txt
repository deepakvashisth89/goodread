“It would have taken so little to make us happy.”

An odd, depressing novel about a family that moves to an old, overgrown house on mortgaged land and begins farming, but can never get out of debt or stop living in fear. There is tragedy all throughout this story. There are a few inexplicable scenes that make no sense and/or that seem out of place (e.g., one involving a crazy knife accident and another in a school with a neglectful teacher). And there is unfulfilled love (i.e., the family has three daughters, its hired help is a young man, he likes the girl who doesn’t love him, and he overlooks the plainer girl who loves him). Mostly, however, the novel is about farming life and how hard, unhappy, and hopeless it can be.



I am a little surprised that this novel won the Pulitzer Prize for fiction (in 1935), although I guess it makes sense given the time period and what was going on in the world, and especially in the United States. I can also see the Pulitzer judges being swayed by the prose, which is sometimes beautiful. The plot, however, falls short, in my view.
