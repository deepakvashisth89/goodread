This is my second novel by Leon, and it was her second on theCommisarioBrunetti series.I am finding these novels are excellent when traveling and as breaks between readings of history or more engaged literature.The plot in this one is ambitious. We are not faced with just a “Whodunit?” but with a wider and more complex crime network that involves nations, secret service and the health of our planet. This is a dangerous area for a thriller, since it can easily degenerate into conspiratorial and populist theories. But Leon manages to pull it through convincingly.What was less convincing were two elements. One is that if Brunetti’s boss, Vice-Questore Patta, is such a snob, that he would not pay more attention to the way he treats Brunetti given that the Commisario’s father-in-law, Count Orazio-Falier, is such a big fish in Venice (and by extension, the rest of Italy). And the second is that it ought not to surprise to the police that a victim of theft would remember well the paintings that had been stolen (of the calibre of Monet, Guardi and Gauguin) but not the face of the thief.Anyway, highly entertaining and suitable for reading in certain circumstances. And then, it is always such a pleasure to read about Venice, its water, its light, and its windingcalli.
