Such trivia may not arrest the attention of most readers, but I cross-checked my public library's Duke Classics public domain copy against the 2001 Penguin, as John Bryant's buoyant preface alerted me to the American rev. ed., where Melville, half-willingly to ensure any American audience for his 1846 debut did not diminish, gave into censorship of risque passages, and/or those mocking missionaries...A Northwestern-Newberry scholarly version, copied by Bryant, tried to adjudicate between an English original publication, and U.S. compromise. Whereas common renderings now use the Yankee on the cheap. This matters because in Melville, a determination to preen about and rile up begins very early.That cleared, what's on deck? As critics seem to agree, this obviously semi-autobiographical yarn is both a tribute to and a send-up of noble savage vs. Polynesian man-eater tropes. Melville and his pal spent only a few months in the Marquesas, but brevity never stopped a storytelling endeavor from a young talent eager for acclaim. However, as Bryant notes, he did, after spinning out his invention, see what his predecessors had composed about the setting, and he incorporated their views into his effort.Melville dives in enthusiastically. His narrator and Toby jump ship, reckoning fun on the shore, up in the mountains and down into inland valleys, beats maritime discipline. As long as they can rush back before their vessel sets sail again. He conveys their scampering vividly, clearly in his fluid, "native" element, although barely in his twenties. But however robust, his injury and lameness cautions him.For he reasons, the hospitality shown by those he'd feared may have a short shelf-life, lest they see him as a burden, and thus a likely repast rather than a novelty to be gazed upon, a guest outwearing their welcome. Meeting Marnoo, who as a boy was taken to Sydney for three years, thus acquiring at least broken English, only increases his doubts...nobody's willing to level with him why he's "kept."The account, relying on enforced lassitide, drifts into diet, dogs, dance, and damsels. I didn't find these sections as engrossing, but my taste tends towards social critique rather than ethnographic customs. Yet, allowing for the elevated rhetoric with which he castigates those Christianizing the Sandwich Islands, he tempers his tantrum with acknowledging that, as ports, tropical enclaves inevitably succumb to vices beyond control of moral scolds, or do-gooders with sincere intentions to "save." You can tell Melville's staying in the good graces of the godly, in spite of his pantheist tilt.Speaking of salvation, heads do roll, tattooing is a constant threat to the still-ailing guest, and fears of his fate grow as conflicts invade tranquility. Their vaunted harmony, he decides, cloaks motivations which remain opaque to passing observers who won't be inducted as intimate participants in strategy.Of course, he escapes. An afterward brings us up to date on Tommo's partner in crime Toby. And (in the Penguin, but excluded from Duke) what happened in Hawai'i as the fickle, imperious, indigenous law enforcement met with sailors swarming to Honolulu for R+R. Which is a bustling entrepot that I presume its Tahitian "sequel," Omoo, reaches, as he's just getting warmed to South Sea shore leave.P.S. Now, I've read Moby Dick three times, only the first by assignment (Norton Critical Ed. footnotes did come in handy), and like my favorite long-haul go-to, Joyce's Ulysses, I'm aware of both the nabobs of negativism naysaying, and big resentment that practically any challenging classic raises, having taught four decades lit-crit.But lackluster reactions on Goodreads unfurls red flags ahoy...Therefore, I aim anew to hoist up MD, as it's been a generation since I dove in. Ordered a U of Cal reprint of Barry Moser's 1981 craft, as now, online fora offer alternatives to Norton's stern compression, and I've found under Patrick O'Brian's immersive command an easier voyage letting adventure lift me away, gaining sea-legs, instead of niggling over nautical arcana at every moment along the tour. But strangely, which volume to sign on to surfaces little on this exchange. A concern which hasn't stirred up GR's still waters from running deep. But one, obviously, I take seriously in my own quests. Meanwhile, I'll try on HM's White-Jacket.
