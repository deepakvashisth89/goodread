I first read a condensed version of this book in the Reader's Digest. I am always interested in journeys into remote places on Earth, but I have mixed feelings about this book.The Taklimakan Desert in western China is sometimes called the worst desert on Earth. I do not know the basis for this claim and how this particular desert is more dangerous than other deserts, like the Sahara in north Africa. Maybe it's because of the name itself. The name "Taklimakan" is supposed to mean in the local Uighur language as, "Go in and you won't come out". So there. Whoever dares to venture to trek this inhospitable place certainly cannot say, "I was not informed".The author and the predominantly British expedition he led succeeded in defying the curse of the place. They went in and they came out. But, they had a supporting logistics convoy that travelled parallel to their route OUTSIDE the desert. It may or may not have been a form of cheating, but they might not have survived the trip without it. And since the support convoy used motor vehicles, the group that went into the desert could have spared the camels they used from misery if they had used motor vehicles all the way. It certainly could have spared all of them from all the threats of dehydration and heatstroke - and from their nosy Chinese government minders who were anxious not to let the foreigners stumble into parts of the desert that the Chinese government uses for nuclear testing.
