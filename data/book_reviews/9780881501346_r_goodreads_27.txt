A most unsympathetic Inspector, a small English village where a ton of fairly sordid improprieties are going on, Dover One reads mostly as a satire of golden age of British crime fiction, turning on its head many of its tropes, while still functioning as a competently developed whodunnit.Dover One needs a certain frame of mind to be appreciated. This was no doubt at the time of publication an hilariously improper and even edgy or mildly offensive read, In the light of 2024, it alas feels a lot like it's trying too a little too hard to shock and awe and be funny, and as a result the humour is sadly very dated and a bit flat, even in the way it attempts to satirize misogyny, homophobia, the upper class, addictions and so on. It should still please readers who love "vintage humour" oddballs from the 60s.
