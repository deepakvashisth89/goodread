I occasionally read authors who belong to the Golden Age of mystery murders, most of whom are unknown to me. And this was the case with Nicholas Blake, or rather Cecil Day-Lewis. Thou Shell of Death is Book 2 with Nigel Strangeways, a man of means and lots of time on his hands, who becomes an amateur sleuth, and a good one, too! Thanks to the family connections in Scotland Yard, he is allowed to investigate when a complicated problem appears. In Book 2, Nigel is asked by a famous WW1 flying ace, Fergus O'Brien, to look after him as he's received some threats to his life. Unfortunately, he fails to fulfill this task, but the he's determined to find the culprit. The novel is charming in its own way since it's more about the solving the mystery and logical thinking, which is good old school,rather than vivid descriptions of murders as such. Language of the novel was an extra bonus for me, as some phrases are not used in modern English any more and it was fun for me to read some dialogues or idioms.*Many thanks to Agora Books and Netgally for providing me with ARC in exchange for my honest review.*
