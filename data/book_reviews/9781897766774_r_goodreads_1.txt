Decided to read another one of these books, and this is decent, though basic. Good enough number of recipes, but no photos. Foods are grouped, with introductions to each. At the beginning are some store cupboard ingredients. The author also tells in the introduction about what the usual meal order in this area is.

Some recipes I liked:
Red lentil dip, Mushroom olive and yoghurt dip, Rice and potato patties, Fried garlic tomatoes, Cauliflower and beans in tahini sauce, Vegetable and lentil soup, Sun-dried tomato and vegetable soup, Chickpea and vegetable stew, Pasta with green lentil and courgette sauce, Baked spaghetti in tomato and vegetable sauce, Garlic rice with potato topping, Tomato rice with mushrooms and broad beans, Minted rice with noodles and peas, Saffron potatoes, Mushroom and bean salad, Courgette and green lentil salad, Turkish cornbread, Sun-dried tomato and olive bread, Mango milk pudding, Sesame biscuits.

If you're allergic to nuts, the number of recipes one can use does go down, though in some cases one might omit or replace them. The author favors soya milk and yoghurt as dairy replacements in this book, but the reader might use something else (at least in bready recipes something like oat milk might work IMO). Coffee is also mentioned as common drink, but tea gets less attention – I feel that tea might be also used almost as much, but I'm just guessing.

Anyway, this book is pretty basic, and not all recipes work for all, but even when being picky, I still found some interesting recipes, as I list above. Not necessarily essential – there are other vegetarian/vegan books on Middle East food – but in the end, not a bad place to start with.
