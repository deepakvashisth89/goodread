Oh look, it’s Barbie and Ken on the cover.The title of this book brought Darcy’sThe Unpredictable Manto mind, and there are some other echoes too -- which is kind of unfortunate, because that book was so much more interesting. Unlike the unpredictable man, this heroine is “impossible” to the hero primarily because she’s conventional.Landscape gardener Judy and architect Mal are immediately taken with each other when they meet at the house he’s designing, and only a visitor stopping by keeps Judy from falling into bed with him that afternoon. Although wary of rich, important men, she agrees to keep seeing him -- but Malcolm refuses to consider marriage and Judy refuses to accept anything less.There were many enjoyable aspects of this story. Judy is a strong and forthright character, with a career that matters to her and a warm, loving family. It was a pleasant change that she and Mal liked each other right away. The plot is somewhat dated, obviously, and things move awfully fast -- most of the events take place during two days -- but Judy’s point of view is so well drawn, the situation seemed plausible. Unfortunately, the abrupt happy ending didn’t. Mal says some very nasty things about marriage and what women want out of it, and his abrupt turnaround fails to convince. A pleasant story, though.
