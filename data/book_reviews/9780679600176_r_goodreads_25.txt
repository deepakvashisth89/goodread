It's just my first novel by Faulkner (unfortunately) and I am under a tremendous impression - I cannot stop thinking about the book because it evoked strong emotions in me.It is a story of a Southern aristocratic family and its inevitable decline. Why is it inevitable? Because the family members fall into two groups - according to their degree of vulnerability, values and the world outlook. The ones belonging to the first group are empathic, vulnerable, kind-hearted and probably weak-willed. They are the real victims of the ones belonging to the second group who are unable to sympathise and love, cruel (even sadistic) and selfish, always taking advantage of others. The family members belonging to the first group suffer, degrade, often die in this loveless atmosphere. The family members belonging to the second group, paradoxically, consider themselves victims. However, the most unfortunate victim here is Benjy and it breaks my heart to think about him.Faulkner's brilliant writing and the structure of the novel convey the atmosphere of the things constantly falling apart in the family. Some semblance of normality is maintained by the only one person, an elderly Black servant Dilsey who already feels the approaching death.'I give it to you not that you may remember time, but that you might forget it now and then for a moment and not spend all your breath trying to conquer it.
