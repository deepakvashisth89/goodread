James Blish was hired to write the prose adaptations of the original Star Trek episodes. These books (with five or six episode adaptations per volume) meant a lot to me growing up. I still remember being disappointed when I learned that Blish disliked the series and considered the adaptations "hack work" that he did because the pay was nice. Apparently, starting with about Star Trek 6, he started letting his wife J.A. Lawrence and her mother handle the bulk of the Star Trek work. This workload shifted even more when his health began to go. Lawrence, though, did not get an author credit until Star Trek 12, the last in the series.

Oh, well, I still love this series, even if Blish was a little snobbish about it.

There were two episodes--both featuring con artist Harry Mudd--that were not adapted for those books. So, in 1978 (three years after Blish's death), J.A. Lawrence wrote this book. It starts with adapations of the two Mudd episodes--"Mudd's Women{" and "I, Mudd." Both are faithful adaptations of the original episodes, though the prose version of "I, Mudd" doesn't quite capture the absurdist, slapstick humor that makes the TV episode so much fun.

The largest portion of the book is a new Mudd-centric adventure. It starts with a sudden galaxy-wide shortage of dilithium crystals, then spirals into an adventure involving a chase through the energy barrier that surrounds the galaxy; teleportation to another galaxy entirely; Chekov hallucinating that he’s the 14th Century Mongol ruler Tamerlane; some short term time travel which briefly convinces Star Fleet Command that Kirk and Spock have gone nuts; and Harry being put on trial by androids. There’s also a scene in which Kirk has to decide whether the android population of a planet can be considered sentient beings--with arguments similar to those that would be made 11 years later in the TNG episode “The Measure of a Man.”

The story is fun, effectively mixing together action and humor. I'm not sure the plot line of the new Mudd adventure stands up to logical scrutiny, but its an entertaining enough romp to make up for this.
