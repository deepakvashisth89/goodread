this short work is a quintessential example of using concise language to explain deeply human emotions, memories, thoughts, and experiences, without even a splinter of a shred of frill or arrogance. from absurdity to tragedy to hilarity to the mundane, they all exist in “The Pains of April”. what Hollon accomplished in under 100 pages is something i imagine some writers work their entire lives to accomplish in hundreds of thousands of typed attempts to achieve unabashed clarity. a true diamond in the rough; this is a novel i will read every year until i die, and i have no reserve in believing i will discover something new with each revisitation.
