This was a strange landscaping book. It was written in a narrative style, which I wouldn't have minded if the author had told his stories with efficiency or with style. Instead it was like talking to a long-winded neighbor who is droning on and on about projects from 10 years ago. Please know that none of my neighbors have done this to me, but, no thanks to the author, I now can imagine it all too well.

The other thing that was strange about it is that while the author states that he will give recommendations, etc. for all regions of the country, it is limited to an index at the end of the book. There is really not a lot of content for regions outside of the author's southeastern US.

He had a few great ideas, such as stealing (my word, not his) plants from the side of the road, using some compatible non-native plants, using evergreens as backdrops, and using successive plants to have all-season appeal.

If you live and garden in the southeastern US, I think this book may have a lot to offer. For me, in the Pacific NW, it was tough gleaning.
