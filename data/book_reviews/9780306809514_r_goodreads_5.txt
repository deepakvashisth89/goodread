Mae West was a liberated woman long before it was the trend. Born in 1893, and actively working from the early 1900's until the 1940's, she had an unshakable, almost male-like, sexuality and a brilliant comedic sense, using both of these qualities to entertain others in racy plays and movies. Every production she worked on was assailed by various persons or groups acting as the "moral police", yet she never voluntarily budged from her positions staying true to her self until the end, even if it meant the end of her career.

No detail seems to be left out of this biography making it read a bit like a textbook. However, by the end, you are well educated in the persona of Mae West and the trials (literally) and tribulations she faced throughout her time acting on the vaudeville, Broadway, and Hollywood stages. The author covers her early start in Brooklyn to the beginning of the end of her acting career in the 1940's. The epilogue ties together her last 40 years in several pages.

Mae West was so much more than a "vamp"...she was an extremely intelligent, savvy, quick witted, hard-working champion for the liberated woman and "average joe". What a world this would be be if all women could have such a strong sense of self and be unwilling to back down in the face of critics! Can't wait to read one of Mae's own books...
