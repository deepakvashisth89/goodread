Well, I've lost track of all the new setting elements Zelazny is adding to the series. There's only so much magic you can fit into a world and still have it follow any comprehensible rules; not that the author seems interested in writing a rules-based story, but at this point he couldn't if he tried.And when the world has no rules it can only draw us in by emotional attachment, either to the strong characters or the engaging plot.Characterwise, we're stuck with Merlin. In the original series he was dull and unwanted, tacked on to the story but adding nothing to it. Somehow, paradoxically, he became worse. Whatever interesting features his upbringing in the Courts of Chaos could have added to his personality, it has been wiped and replace with his life as an upper middle class American student. Whereas Corwin's memories centered around surviving the plague and fleeing the revolution, Merlin keeps reminiscing about sailing a boat and sporting matches. He has no clear ambitions other than to be an arrogant jackass, which means a lot of boredom when he does nothing, and confusion when he suddenly decides to do something, but without explaining his goals to the reader. And the characters from the old book have been shunted into the background to be replaced with a cast as fascinating as the lead.And the plot? I couldn't for the life of me remember what happened in this book, though I've just read it, so I looked it up on Wikipedia.Merlin escapes from the crystal cave, and decides to gain leverage over Luke by rescuing his mother from the Keep of the Four Worlds. He spars with the sorcerer who now controls the keep, and who seems to know him. He escapes with the petrified Jasra, and returns to Amber where an unusual Trump summoning imprisons him in the Mad Hatter's tea party.Yeah, that pretty much says it all.
