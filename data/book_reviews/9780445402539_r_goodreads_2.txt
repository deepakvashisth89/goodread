This is well worth the price of admission and I enjoyed it. It is well plotted (though not "sinewy" or "hyper-complex" as the blurbistas have it) and atmospheric in describing interesting and diverse settings and people. His personal interest in history also makes these rather different in tone from many thrillers. The two things that stop him being John Le Carre (say) are, first, that there is too much _telling_ us that lead character David Audley is clever and wily (and not enough _showing_ us as with Smiley say) and, secondly, that in order to do the grand reveals he likes, the start of the book is really quite unrevealed (like a landscape under fog frankly). While this may get some readers salivating with anticipation, non fans may simply find things rather puzzling until some of the reveals are actually revealed: Since the author presumably knows it all already, it may be hard for him to see how it might look to the reader perhaps.
