This is the last book in the main Thoroughbred series written by the original author, Joanna Campbell (real name Jo Ann Simon). She agreed to do four books a year for HarperCollins and SURPRISE she got burned out quickly.I wasn't sorry to see the back of her. She didn't ask any tough questions about racing. She barely knew anything about it. In a 2014 online interview, she admitted that she knew next to nothing about racing when she agreed to pen the initial four book run. The concept was actually HarperCollins' idea. She said she rewrote their outline, then subscribed to the most popular racing publications of the day.And apparently didn't read them. She never bothered to use the professional jargon of Thoroughbred breeders. She describes a colt at auction as being out of Seattle Slew (Slew would be one of the very few real horses ever mentioned in the series.) Seattle Slew was a stallion. The colt at auction would've been by Seattle Slew, and out of a mare. See the difference?She also still refers to Shining as Wonder's half-sister. They had the same sire. Again, that's not how breeders really talk. Shining would only be considered a half-sister to Wonder if they shared the same dam, NOT the same sire. Since top sires can have up to 200 foals A YEAR, it's just impractical to call every other horse a half-sister or half-brother.It's also feared that Glory may cost $10, 000 to buy. Again, this shows how much of a dimwit Simon/Campbell was. She was writing in an age when any horse that cost less than $100,000 was sniffed at. Million dollar yearlings were common. HELLO!Well, the Cindy books were, for the most part, completely forgettable, and this one was no exception. I had to flip through the whole damn thing before reviewing it, even though I'd only read it about two years ago.Other horses that appear briefly are Fleet Goddess, Fleeting Moment, Precocious, Mr. Wonderful, the twin foals fromShining's Orphan, Wonder's Pride, Rocky Heights, Bo Jangles, Butterball the pony, and Cisco. Cindy is only 11 here, and starts taking jumping lessons.Campbell/Simon also didn't know much about Child Protective Services. She has them as interfering busy bodies ... which is so far from the truth, it's more pathetic than laughable. Even back in the 1990s, CPS was overworked and underpaid, resulting in very high turnovers. It was rare that foster children, or even kids suspected of being abused, were ever checked up on. I don't know how many times I've read about kids dying because CPS did nothing for years.So anyway, glad to see the back of Campbell/Simon. She would come back to the series in a roundabout way, creating the inexplicable Ashleigh series. She wrote the first three and a half books, then left that floundering.She set a pattern for the new ghostwriters to follow. It was also for HarperCollins to follow. They had a winning formula on their greedy little mitts ... so they'd totally blow it.
