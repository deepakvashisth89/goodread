Translating the Bible into contemporary language is hard. Many of my Christian friends may be familiar withThe Message, which was Eugene Peterson's attempt at just that. While I knew lots of people who loved that paraphrase, others didn't feel that he put the emphasis where he should have. Other translators have tried, and with mixed results; while the NLT, which calls itself a "thought for thought" translation of the world, has been around for quite a while, it has yet to surpass the KJV or even the NIV in popularity. Even the various NIVs, which are still closer to everyday speech than many English translations, still use difficult words or phrases at times.That brings us to this book,The Book of God. I had this on my shelf for a while, and had forgotten about it until I was recently looking for something different to read from my own collection. For the most part, Mr. Wangerin succeeds at bringing the Word into contemporary language; though he does use some of the original Bible's catchphrases--i.e., "In those days"--it definitely is more accessible to today's reader than the original text, especially the KJV.However, there is one glaring issue: Many events and people are left out. The story of Deborah omits the details of Sisera being killed; some beloved stories, such as that of Esther or most of the events from the book of Daniel, are nowhere to be found; and, the book ends with the apostles being anointed with the Holy Spirit, not even mentioning the later events of Acts or anything from Revelation. Those expecting the Bible in its entirety are likely to be disappointed.If you want to read God's Word, but have trouble with the language of translations such as the KJV, I would suggest trying one such as the NLT, the ERV, the ICB, the CEB, or even either the old or new NIV. People often associate the Bible with words such as "thee" or "thou," and think that every version of the Word has them; the truth is, most don't. Even the NKJV (the New King James Version) lacks such archaic wording. To really get into the Bible, I would also suggest listening to an audio version alongside reading it.The Word of PromiseandThe Bible Experienceare two very well-done dramatizations; they even feature Hollywood talent. Even if you don't have a CD player or any way to get MP3 files onto your device, many Bible apps contain audio versions that you can stream. I would recommend any of those over this.
