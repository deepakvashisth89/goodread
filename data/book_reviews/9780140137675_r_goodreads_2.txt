This book is unfortunately a product of its time, being first published in 1950. It can be a difficult read for stretches at a time, filled with stilted language and obtuse words. There are constant references to WW2, and event clearly still looming large in the author's mind.
However, there is a clear argument and narrative within each chapter and some of the facts are still interesting for someone familiar with Tudor history. I think it would have been a fantastic resource in the 50s and 60s. For contemporary readers, I'd recommend a more modern book on this subject
