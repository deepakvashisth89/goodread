catherine morland, a young woman who loves gothic novels, often interprets reality as if it were fiction, just like the books she reads every day. she grew up in a loving family, is an idealist and dreamer. however, this innocent perspective leads to some misunderstandings, as her imagination sometimes runs wild. jane austen introduces gothic elements into this novel, but in a humorous way, almost like a parody without criticising gothic novels but offering a very clever social critique, defending novels at a time when they were criticised.

‌‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌‌ ‌ ‌‌‌ ‌ ‌‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌ ‌

on a visit to the stylish city of bath, catherine finds adventure, new friends and love. this city offers balls, parties, theatre and romance. her understanding of the society around her is coloured by her limited experience of the world outside the village where she has always lived, as well as her trust in the goodness of people. catherine believes that everyone is honest and kind. when the possibility of love presents itself, her unwavering trust in people and her active imagination lead her to new experiences where she learns from her mistakes, about the world and especially about herself. catherine begins to appreciate reality more than the exciting and frightening stories in her favourite books, realising that society can be even more thrilling and dangerous than a gothic novel.
