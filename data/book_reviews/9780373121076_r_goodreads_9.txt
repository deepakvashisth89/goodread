Lee Wilkinson writes the same story again and again: the heroine meets/reunites with the hero, who is rich, irresistible, and devious. Hero is out for revenge because [heroine held responsible for something that she is not responsible for]. The hero is vaguely menacing and seems to have closed a net around the heroine, threatening every aspect of her life: work stability, relationships, sexual and emotional independence; financial solvency. Hero forces the heroine to (a) have a sexual relationship with him (seduction, not force) and (b) marry him so he can take over her life completely.

My problem with her books is that the reasons for revenge are usually pretty weak for the amount of obsession they engender in the hero. I think her heroes just get off on revenge-sex, to be honest (which is fine with me because I love a good revenge/own you body and soul trope with strong D/s overtones), but the heroine's willingness to go along with these life destroying plans always seems OTT.

Wilkinson is skillful in writing tension and ramping it up throughout her better stories, and in writing hot sex scenes that play with power exchange, but it's only in her stories where the need for vengeance seems justified --and the heroine accepts her culpability--that it really works. It worked best for me so far in Blind Obsession and A Husband's Revenge because while the heroines weren't truly responsible for the heroes' traumas in those books, it still made sense that they would feel a lot of guilt and sorrow about their past actions, which definitely played into the betrayals the heroes suffered. (Wilkinson's heroes always seem to have a crappy past full of rejection and betrayal--even before the heroine's supposed crimes--that do, at a minimum, provide some backstory for their insanity.) In this story, the cause of the hero's need for revenge seemed flimsy. Also, most of Wilkinson's heroes threaten more than they deliver, and the actual punishment (we will tour the Southwest and enjoy Vegas! I, an uber-wealthy man, will marry you without a prenup and have the best sex of our lives as punishment!) seems kind of enjoyable.

Some things work: Wilkinson is very good at doling out backstory slowly, which intensifies the suspense and air of mystery that she also does well. But overall, another near-miss for me from LW--fun while it lasted, but not memorable and left me looking for the real highs and lows and thrilling drama of Blind Obsession.
