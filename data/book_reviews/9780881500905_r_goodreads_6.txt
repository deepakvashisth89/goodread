This is a 1938 Asey Mayo mystery. It has all the things I like in these books.

Phineas Branbury is a rich man who moves back to Wissett, a small Cape Cod town. He dedicates himself to helping the town. He supports charities. He helps businesses flourish. He sponsors civic activities. He is a model citizen. Someone starts spreading rumors about him. A bunch of people get sick when he donates tarts from his bakery to a town event. Doctors find arsenic in the tarts.

Asey Mayo gets called in to find out who is attacking Banbury. A local resident is killed, and Banbury is arrested and charged with the murder. Mayo sets out to find the real killer and clear Banbury.

Taylor knows small New England towns. She captures the petty disputes that go on for generations and the lightning speed of gossip and the basic decency at the core of the most irascible citizens. As usual, she features someone who rents rooms to the tourists. The local police are always jumping to conclusions and the local GP, Doctor Cummings, is a commonsense ally for Mayo. We also get a few good fights and chases.

This is a first-rate example of a great classic local color mystery series.
