Read this knowing I am not reviewing the book but rather leaving notes and asides to remind me of my readings year to year. Much of my prolific reading was lost from childhood on, but perhaps today’s children can use the tools now available and be more vigorous in their record keeping.I didn't even know about this author until I saw the movie starring Colin Farrell (miscast, I think), and Keira Knightley (miscast without a doubt.) I was interested to read the book. It varies in a number of ways from the film and I can see why. Numerous plot points are identical to those in the Billy Wilder filmSunset Boulevard. The makers of the Farrell film must have found the parallels all too blatant. I'd cite chapter and verse, but that would spoil it for you. I have to think that author Ken Bruen's rip-off of plot points is intentional, given the similarity of the titles, "Sunset Boulevard" and "London Boulevard." Despite that odd authorial choice, it's still not a bad read. I guess I should add that there is no London Boulevard in London--and it's a city known for it's very quirky street names. There are even web pages to direct you to them: Bleeding Heart Lane, Tweezer's Alley, Man in the Moon Passage...Postscript: There was a ton of author-book name dropping going on, as well as popular music references. Sign of the times, I guess. Robert B. Parker and Elin Hilderbrandt both come to immediate mind when thinking of this behavior. The problem? Restaurants and shops come and go, especially restaurants.
