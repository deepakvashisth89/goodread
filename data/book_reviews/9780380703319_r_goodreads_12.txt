Including the novels written under her pseudonym, Alisa Craig, Charlotte MacLeod created four mystery series. To a greater or lesser extent, they were all cozy, madcap and screwball comedy mysteries. Of all the series, The Grub-And-Stakers series is the most madcap and screwball of them all. The Grub-And-Stakers Move a Mountain was first published in 1981, but in many ways it reads like a story set in the 1950s. The activities of the energetic women who comprise the Grub-And-Stake Gardening and Roving Club are often viewed with disfavor by the menfolk, who think they should be tending their homes. (Not all the menfolk, though, some are quite supportive.)

Dittany Henbit, professional typist and one of the younger members of the Lobelia Falls Grub-And-Stake Gardening and Roving Club is having an extremely upsetting morning. First of all, she discovered a large man with a backhoe on the Enchanted Mountain, a rather fanciful name of a community owned piece of land that housed any number of rare wildflowers. Then, while Dittany is forcefully explaining to this man (Ben Frankland, as it turns our) that he cannot dig up the Spotted Pipsissewa, nor any other vegetation on that land, a lethal arrow whizzes by the pair of them, close enough to feel the breeze, if that wasn’t shocking enough, investigating the arrow leads them to the body of John Architrave, the irritatingly inept head of the Water Board, skewered by another arrow.

Before she knows it, Dittany and the rest of the Grub-And-Stakers are wrapped up in an investigation into Architrave’s death, organizing a write in campaign for the election to the Development Committee and planning a 50th Wedding Anniversary. All while someone seems intent on planting the next arrow in Dittany.
