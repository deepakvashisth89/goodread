The book consists of 8 stories. The story that gives the work its title, *The Country of the Blind*, was written in 1904 by H.G. Wells, one of the important representatives of science fiction. *The Country of the Blind* is a long dystopian story. A mountaineer named Nunez falls from a cliff in an accident. In the canyon where he falls, there is the *Country of the Blind*, where people lost their sight generations ago. Nunez immediately realizes that the place he has arrived at is the legendary *Country of the Blind*. The famous saying comes to mind: "In the Country of the Blind, the one-eyed man is king." With the confidence given by this saying, he arrogantly tries to explain to them what it means to see. I have read many dystopian novels, but this one is very different. Our hero thinks he is smarter than them, he doesn't speak up, but they hear his every move. He is very surprised that they can hear the sounds of his own movements while he cannot. However, a seeing person only hears with their ears, while a blind person makes sense of the world with their ears. "Intonation had long ago replaced facial expressions, and touches had replaced gestures." Reading the story made me wonder, is what we call "normal" simply what the majority accepts, or is it what's right? I guess everyone's abilities are valuable in their own world. Living in a place that doesn't suit you isn't better than experiencing a suitable absence.
