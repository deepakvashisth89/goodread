An interesting book to be sure, but choices made by the author throughout the novel made it so it didn't stick it's landing.
Given its placement in continuity, I imagine that the story was meant to be more reminiscent of the Original Series, but the more fantastical elements of the planet and the aliens made it more like the Animated Series making me feel like I should be expecting something more lighthearted.
Being that the book was also penned by Joe Haldeman, with no input from Gene Roddenberry (that I could tell at least), the choices made by Haldeman classed with Roddenberry's vision of "utopia". The three of the biggest examples of this that stand out to me are the multiple mentions of sex, or sexuality, and Bone's wife leaving him for another man because she was unhappy in their marriage, and the revelation that a female member of the crew had an abortion when she was younger because she thought a child would ruin her career. There were several other dialog choices that, while grounded and real, were not something that necessary fits into the framework of Star Trek in this era.
The ending of the book was an interesting idea, focusing on the limits of technology and of willpower vs. violence when fighting off a superior enemy. Those concepts tie in directly with Haldeman being a Vietnam veteran and are the bred and butter of his other works, but they were not given enough time to breath here and were relatively one dimensional.
With all of that being said, I am not saying that this is a bad book or that Haldemen did a bad job at writing it, I feel like he did a wonderful job based on the constraints that he must have been under with a project such as this. I am just saying that if this was an original novel free from the structural framework of Star Trek and if these ideas were given more time to breathe, I think it could have been one of the greats.
As it sits, it is a little bit clunky and awkward at times, has a rather jaring tonal shift in the second half of the book, and somewhat of a rushed ending.
