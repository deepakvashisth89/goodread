I started reading this book for the chapter on Alexander - one of Plutarch's lengthier lives. It was of course engrossing and enlightening, particularly reading Mary Beard's chapter on him after in her book Confronting the Classics. However, it was the chapter on Dion that I found most interesting. An extraordinary story and slice of history. I was already familiar with parts of the story from reading Mary Renault's Mask of Apollo, and Plutarch's impassioned account is an excellent version, rendered in vivid prose by Ian Scott-Kilvert's translation.I'm excited now to move on to Plutarch's other lives in Fall of the Roman Republic - Crassus, Pompey, Caesar, Cicero.
