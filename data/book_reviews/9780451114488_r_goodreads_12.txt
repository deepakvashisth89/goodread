I haven't been able to recall when it was I read this for the first time, but the pub date means I was at least in my mid-20's. There were characters, events, even key lines that were engraved somewhere in my soul that first time, and this second time through I understood why it resonated so strongly. The way the story navigates class and race in the South in the 1950's, played out in a small town high school ecosystem, explained the world I grew up in to an almost uncanny degree. This time through it felt like there were good ideas that got tediously long, like the ironies of small-town Southerners moving to the Big City to join the civil rights movement, hearing wealthy Yankees describe the people they loved in ways they didn't recognize (and ultimately rejected).Is it by accident that I re-read this on the eve of my 50th high school reunion, having lived (as the natives say) "up North" for most of those 50 years? Did I need to remind myself about the world I was going back to visit, even knowing that it isn't - can't possibly be - the world I left half a century ago?I wouldn't say this is the best book I ever read, but it is clearly one of the ones that had the most impact on me.
