Bad behaviour. Sad human beings.

At its peak Gaitskill's now-iconic debut collection is a mishmash of erratic cruelty, poignant urban prose and a metallic tang, quite hard to swallow. At its worst, it just drags. Drags on and on in a bland loop of doom, trapped within the structural confines of trying too hard. The bookish equivalent of dry winter skin that welcomes scratches in all its grey, flaky ease. Be it sex, romance or simple friendship(s), everything gets skewered in the catacombs of youth.

Further props, however, to the author for her ability to leech off the muddy heart of metropolitan cities. New York, New Jersey, etc. Embellishing her stories with fatigue. All in an attempt to paint a greyscale portrait of heavy nothingness. It works to some degrees. Despite the odd, disjointed way of description that often rains all over her literal parade, she makes it out alive. And that's the least you can get out of this infamous bunch of stories.

Although I do pity anyone who picks this up for the smut. I know it's more of a recent social media trend. But there's none here. No stylised sex whatsoever. But sexuality as a weapon to hurt, manipulate and control, however? That's more in line with the pretension wrapped within. Although two of my favourites of the bunch, 'Connection' and 'Other Factors', both focus on disjointed female friendships and steer towards a completely different sort of intimacy. Something that (almost) made me think of a gloomier 'Frances Ha' at points.

Ahoy, sexy! 

No?

Okay, before I fuck off, here are three of my personal favourites from the nine presented in the collection. 'A Romantic Weekend', 'Connection' and 'Trying to Be'. (Sorry, 'Secretary'. There's already too many Pinterest souls rooting for you. You'll do just fine without me.)
