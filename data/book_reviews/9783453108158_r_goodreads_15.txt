An early Dean Koontz novel that was later re-published after he became a successful novelist. The novel's title, as you can see, is Chase. It references the name of Ben Chase, our main character, and also reflects that there are various chases in the book. For Koontz, this is basically ten-dimensional chess.The good: it's a very thin book, clocking in at under 150 pages. The bad: it is our stock Koontz characters, featuring the protagonist who is a Good Man with a Troubled Past who gets paired with an Angelic Woman Who Heals Him. No dog in this one, but there is the bad guy who kills for some absurd reason, in this case he is a religious nut who is passing judgment on his victims.Ben Chase is a Vietnam vet who is suffering from PTSD and alcoholism. He intervenes when he sees an attempted murder, and the rest of the book is about the cat-and-mouse scenario between Chase and the murderer who now decides that Chase will be his victim.It is extremely paint-by-numbers, but the story at least is very lean and not filled with some of Koontz's attempts to inject societal commentary which absolutely sinks some of his books. So, far from his worst outing, and not actively offensive (especially allowing for the time period in which was written), but also not exactly much to recommend it either.
