Meh. Yawn.

This is one of those paperbacks that likes to put on airs & pretend it's some uber-literati feast of academia because it questions Historical Roles For Women, ie., the main characters Explore Their Independence whilst they Engage in Sexual Congress with various archetypal males in a series of vignette scenes that Reveal Their Depth of Personality, even as they conform & yet Stretch Their Proscribed Boundaries.

(Capitalized phrases are an indication of importance, natch.)

It's also one of those novels that keeps a deliberate layer of frosted glass between the reader & the subjects -- nothing too oogy or emotional or crass or, y'know, will break the tedium, so read without fear. Or something. I know there are people who love this sort of fiction, but I'm not one of them.

Standard 2-star DNF. It's competently written, but not a style I like. YMMV, as they say.
