This collection from Le Guin contains both stories and poems themed by all being about animals or non-human consciousness.

However, the poems that constitute half the collection are on a wavelength with which I was not able to connect, so this brief review can recommend only the stories.

Le Guin’s storytelling makes demands on her readers: we have to spend time allowing ourselves to be drawn into her narrative worlds until we twig what’s going on – that a girl is running with coyotes, that an entity is conscious of being played with in a maze, that a she-wolf discovers her husband is human; trees move, grasslands are fearful, horses that see ‘horse camp’ from their own point of view, and there’s an intriguing exploration of ‘therolinguistics’. And the last two stories I specially liked: one about a woman’s relationship with ‘a lion’ and the other about Eve quietly rebelling against Adam – not that he is aware enough of her to notice!

Subversive, challenging, good-natured and good-humoured – Le Guin continues to push my imagination into new regions of understanding.
