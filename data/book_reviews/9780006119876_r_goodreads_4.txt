I used to love Victoria Holt's books, so when I foundThe King of the Castleburied in the back of one of my bookshelves, I had to reread it to see if I would love it still. In many ways, it did hold up for me, but I suspect that this time I was more aware of the author's formula than I used to be. Many of the clues the heroine missed about what was really going on in the castle seemed obvious to me, although I never felt like she was stupid because she didn't see them too. I liked that the heroine of the story is a woman of education and skills and I like how she finds her way to the chateau and demands that the Comte respect her abilities as an art restorer, just as he would have her father. I think the Comte must have been one of Holt's less sinister heroes. He is certainly mysterious, and there is plenty of gossip about him, but the reader never sees him doing anything too suspect. In fact, he's notable more for his absence than his presence in the book, and the contact between hero and heroine is so scant it's a little hard to understand why they fall in love. But of course they do, because that's the formula, and it works. All in all, I enjoyed the story and may browse around for more of Ms. Holt's gothic novels to reread.
