I chose to read this book because I had accepted to read the second in the series (The Last of the Mohicans) as part of a challenge, but I didn't want to jump in at the second book. This book also conveniently met the requirements for yet another challenge I'm completing; so, a win both ways!

I really wanted to like this story, but it was so excruciatingly slow. I could seriously have let the audiobook play for thirty minutes without listening and then pick right back up with the story without really missing anything. I'm not sure if it was the time period during which this book was published (1840s) or if it is simply the writing style of the author.

Three things I learned in this story:

1. Hetty Hutter lacked intelligence. Every time the poor girl's name was mentioned, which was a lot, readers were reminded of how she was.

2. Judith Hutter was beautiful. For how many times we were reminded that Hetty was dimwitted, we were reminded an equal number of times that Judith had the looks the brains out of the two.

3. Racism has existed in many forms for a long time. I'm not really sure the author intended for all of Deerslayer's remarks about gifts and gifts to be viewed as racism, but when you insinuate that someone simply cannot help the way they act because of the color of their skin, that's racism. A major plot point in this book was to lay out all the differences between white gifts and red gifts. Toward the end of the book did elaborate a bit and say that all human nature is the same but we are different based on how we are raised. I'm not sure if that was the main theme he was trying to drive home, but I think he fell a little flat.

I was really looking forward to reading , but I'm not so sure I want to put the time into it now. We shall see.
