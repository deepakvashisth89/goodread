Travis McGee is lounging on his houseboat in the Florida Keys, passing time with some beach bums and lovely ladies, when he receives a call from an old friend of his named Sam Taggart. Taggart had left three years ago after breaking off a relationship with a local woman named Nora, not to be heard from nor seen again. His return, both to repent to Nora and to ask for Trav's help, is a surprising one.

Before they can get things settled, however, Sam is brutally murdered, perhaps because of the items he needs Trav's help recovering, which Trav only got a quick passing description of. Old Incan gold totems.

Now Travis and Nora are investigating the circumstances leading to Sam's demise, so that 1) they can pass vengeance upon those who killed their friend, and 2) they can split the bounty of these ancient gold artifacts Sam had been in possession of. Their investigation leads them to a remote getaway resort in Mexico.

The Travis McGee stories all have a weird mix of free love and righteous street knighthood that doesn't always work, some to more damning degrees than others. "A Deadly Shade of Gold" (1965) suffers on those terms and more, with MacDonald getting more free with philosophical ramblings about overpopulation, environmental abuse, the stifling of independent thought, the attitudes of social elites corrupting third world places, and corruption everywhere using and abusing the common man.

Trav sleeps with six different women in this story, so I wonder if, maybe in his next mystery he's going to try to figure out how to cure his many STDs, or maybe he can sail his houseboat to a new location to avoid all those child support payments. But I digress.

Verdict: "A Deadly Shade of Gold" manages to keep a tense and puzzling edge as we follow Trav's attempt to solve the whodunit while also participating in a thrilling action/adventure attempting to retrieve the stolen goods and do justice for his murdered friend.

Jeff's Rating: 3 / 5 (Good)
