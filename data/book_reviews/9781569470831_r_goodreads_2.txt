Confusingly this novel is listed as the seventh book in the St-Cyr & Kohler series yet it takes place before the last few I read, in June 1942. Not sure what's up with that.I have come to the conclusion the way the books take place consecutively (mostly) over such a short time period has limited Janes' ability to develop his characters. Or maybe that is why he has chosen this approach, so he doesn't have to change his set-up? Anyway, when compared with, for e.g. another crime series set during the Second World War, Ben Pastor'sMartin Borabooks, which take the lead character through different locations and important points in the war, there is a sameness about Janes' books that is a definite weakness in his writing. At least this time, with a case in the Dordogne region involving pre-historic caves and Himmler's desire to further the Aryan mythology of the Third Reich for propaganda purposes, St-Cyr and Kohler work mostly together. Their relationship is, yet again, what keeps me reading despite being confused by the other characters and convoluted details of the murder investigation. The way Janes mixes character POVs, and interior with spoken dialogues, makes reading the books a muddling experience some readers could find off-putting. By the end I just didn't really care enough to be much interested in the resolution of the crime.After several St-Cyr & Kohler outings to the provinces, I would like to see the two detectives return to Paris for a while, though I appreciate why they were hastily sent away after stirring up trouble in the delicate circumstances of the Occupation. Or just a few scenes with them sitting down together for chat that has nothing to do with murder victims. As it is, the books have become too plot driven for my liking, at the expense of character depth and reader's engagement. Janes does, however, have a wonderful way of creating atmosphere.
