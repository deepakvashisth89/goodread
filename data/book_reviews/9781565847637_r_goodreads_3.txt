Hoberman puts an interesting spin on American history, showing how the movies influenced politics and politics influenced the movies over the course of the thirteen years he considers the sixties to have been. Hoberman's film criticism was always something I liked to read in the Village Voice's classic period, and it's great to have found a book by him, even if it's some weird amalgamation of cultural criticism and history.Running from the opposition of Spartacus and the Alamo in 1960 to what Hoberman calls the last 60s movie, Blow-Out in 1981, he describes the differences in spirit between the Kennedy, Johnson, and Nixon presidencies not just through their actions, but through the movies of their time. Kennedy loved James Bond, Johnson epitomized the changing face of westerns, and Nixon worshipped Patton. Hoberman's prose is playful, albeit ready to slip into the darkness that haunts so much of the story he tells.
