From a start in rented rooms as a child of an immigrant mother and a father who refused to acknowledge her, to university professor, author, and college administrator, this book takes us through Faderman's life and tells about the times she lived through along the way.

Really embodied for me the idea that who you are now is not necessarily who you will be, never limit yourself.

I heard Faderman talking about this book around the time it came out (2003) but just had a chance to read it now. I think a lot of the times she talks about, especially the rougher side of gay life, has been lost to younger folks (and was always probably hidden from straight people at the time). People have some idea about 'cruising' but the amount of sex work even women did in order to get money is not widely known. This is what it took to live outside of expectations. Throw in an unstable upbringing and immigrant family devastated by WWII and you get this fascinating autobiography.

I really enjoyed this and got a lot of insight into the generations before me. Read this in 3 days.

Lastly, could be a bus book, but there would be parts that would be hard to put down once you reached your destination.
