It's natural to be suspicious about novelizations. What could be so different about them that makes them worth the read?The Wicker Manis a great answer to that: overall, the story feels more fleshed out and detailed. On the other hand, I found myself being irritated by Howie, the policeman who travels to a Scottish island to investigate a case of a missing girl. He's got a pile of religious hang-ups that results in endless patronising rants about the religious beliefs of the island. There is a girl missing and he's worried about the luscious lips of the innkeeper's daughter. Come on now. Get a grip.That being said,The Wicker Manis fairly effective as horror, just more quiet than what you'd expect. A bit too quiet at times, but it does have a very present eerie mood. You just know the people have something to hide, and then it all culminates in one of the greatest endings of horror cinema. It's a bit different in the book and the movie, but they're both good in their own way. The former leaves a lingering mood and the latter has more desperation. Obviously, the drawback of the book is that you won't be able to hear the music or seeChristopher Leeprancing around in a wig and a dress.
