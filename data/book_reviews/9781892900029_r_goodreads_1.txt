With a click and a clack from the player of noise,I learned of a creature of infinite poise,and a fair haired young knight who while quite true and bravewas a bit of a air-head, and an awful young knave -His plans were quite foolish, and he needed much helpto defeat the wild Grumpuss, (he was only a whelp.)But the afternoon passed with such grand old hilarity -and I learned not to give a wild Grumpuss some celery.This was a fun-filled audiobook (though at eighty-odd minutes, a short one) that made me laugh out loud. First, the reader put such wonderful character into performing it that at times I completely forgot that the lyrical quality was a function of the completely rhyming nature of the poetic story.Here's the deal: Brave (and a bit ego-centric, book-tour-planning, maid-desiring) knight told by King to go defeat the Wild Grumpuss. Knight tries, and learns a lot about wild creatures, listening prior to acting, and, of course, that any cat creature is inherently superior. (That last part is a given, naturally).Splendid, fun for adults (or maybe I should qualify that with 'vaguely adolescent adults') and children both.
