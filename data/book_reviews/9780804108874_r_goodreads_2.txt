“Now, when you sit, your blood sort of sits along with you. It don’t go rushing around your brain no more. Consequently, it takes that much more time to get rid of some sad idea in your mind. The process is slowed considerable. Whereas if you hurry your blood up some … There is a sizable amount of people could benefit from what I know.”
This is my first experience of reading of Anne Tyler and it’s one of her early ones. I picked it up when I bought a job lot of virago books (about twenty of them) some years ago. It’s set in small town America, in a town called Larksville. I presume it’s probably somewhere in the South as the main industry nearby revolves around Tobacco.
At the centre of the novel is the Pike family. The novels begins just after the death of their six year old daughter Janie Rose. There is Mr and Mrs Pike and their ten year old son, Simon. There is a small community of people immediately around them. Brothers, Ansel and James live next door and have their own issues. Joan cares for the Pike family and appears to be somehow attached to James. There are other minor characters as well.
It is a study of grief and its effects on individuals and communities. In that respect it is interesting. However I did have a niggle. The novel starts after the death so there is no sense of how the characters were relating before the bereavement, which makes getting a sense of the effects of grief and its impact on relationships more difficult.
The plot sort of meanders along and doesn’t really end: not a problem generally for me, but getting a grip on the characters was more of an issue, apart from the boy, Simon.
I think I’m left feeling a little indifferent, but I will at some point read more (especially as I have two others on my shelves).
