I first gave this book 3 stars for the beauty of the setting--Venice--and a somewhat competently handled mystery. But the more I thought about it, the more certain aspects of this book bothered me: the young Moroccan man Habib, the everyone calls "the boy," who is treated as the exotic other in a rather racist way. The ambiguous relationship of the main character MacIntyre to this young man--are they lovers? They don't act like it, but what exactly IS their relationship? It's very vague and rather strange. Another character hints that he's gay, but that's it. The ambiguous relationship of MacIntyre and the Contessa, who are supposed to be close friends but don't really act like it. Perhaps if I had read the books leading up to this one, I'd understand, but I shouldn't have to have done that. Also, here's another mystery that's solved as if the police are doing nothing, with the final act taking place in an apartment that should have been closed off as an active crime scene. Finally, most of the characters we meet are not that likable and it's difficult to understand why they're hanging around together. They say not-so-nice things to each other without recrimination, which is not believable. And they all seem to be hung up on various objets d'art. So yeah, downgraded this to a 2 and probably won't read another one in this series unless someone tells me that this was a low point and there's one in the series that's a lot better.
