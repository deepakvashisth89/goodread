It's such a pity to see that this novel is unfinished. Although I'm not an orphan or had an overly traumatic childhood, something in this novel, that I can't quite grasp, deeply resonates with me. I don't know what it is, but I feel drawn to it like a magnet. Just like Netochka was drawn to her stepfather like a magnet. It read like a story I always wanted to read in life, something I always wanted to see on paper. If this book would have been 600+ pages long like some of his other works, I would have read it and possibly even re-read it. It has such great potential, it's sad to see that it wasn't fully used. While I write this, my mind is rapidly making up its own continuation of the story. It shows how connected I felt while I read this one.It starts of with a girl named Netochka Nezvanova which translates to ‘nameless nobody’. We see from her point of view how traumatic her childhood is and how it later impacts her (daily) life. I felt pity for her, I felt sorry for all the bad things that happened to her. I just wanted to embrace her like a true loving and caring parent would do. Never once did it cross my mind that what happened to her was deserving. It felt so utterly unfair. Every time she wept, when she was lost in her sorrow, I was feeling so strongly for her. I wanted that Netochka would be strong, but at the same time I just wanted her to let her emotions run free without being ashamed.So far as the novel goes, it explores her life. From the moment she gained consciousness (when she was eight and a half years old) to her teenage years (up to where she turns sixteen years old). Her biological family is manipulative and isn't able to provide her with a healthy and comfortable environment. There she was already oftentimes distressed from her poverty life, characterised by a tense, miserable and depressed atmosphere. Her new family is quite the opposite wealthy and noble. While this environment is more healthy and comfortable, she was still in great distress and experienced many obstacles. Some of them were overcome, some others were never resolved. Although she is accompanied with a kid named Katya she was still tormented with isolation and felt unwelcome most of the time. Early on, Netochka was psychologically conflicted. At first with her stepfather Efimov, later on with Katya. Seeing her not only growing physically but also emotionally and how her emotional states impacted her life was sometimes hard to read. I deeply emphasised with this young girl. It both read like his more popular works but also at the same time like a coming-of-age story that I deeply wanted to see completed.Although it is an unfinished novel it is by no means bad. I was able to analyse quite a lot in so little pages. Being able to observe how traumatic experiences (especially in childhood) shapes a human being. Netochka's life circumstances (poverty, neglectful and abusive parenting) dominated her emotional state and later influenced her relationships with other characters (Katya). Her environmental change and also social status change was hard for her to get used to. At first her new family seems to be more caring and loving, providing her with a safer environment than the one she grew up in. It introduces new (internal) struggles that Netochka had to overcome. Such as social class prejudice and the struggle to find her place in a world she didn't belong to. Her name is a metaphor for feeling alienated, feeling like a nobody in a world full with people who have done remarkable things in their life to be remembered. Netochka never really had anyone that she could bond with deeply. Every time she did, they abandoned her. As a result, she started to feel like a nobody. As if she was insignificant.This was Dostoevsky's first attempt at writing about the human nature and I think if he would have finished it he would have been very successful. I already mentioned it so often in this review but this story had great potential. Seeing how not only the poor but also the rich suffered gave me such an unique viewpoint that I never had before. Seems like social roles and expectations are always highly-demanding in society, no matter the social status. We all suffer from the same thing, but do nothing to fix that. Quite a tragedy, indeed. It also explores how childhood trauma affects children, especially their development. The character who resembles that is no other than little Nyetochka.I wanted to give this novel 5 stars but it's unfinished and I'm not certain what Dostoyevsky's true intentions behind this were. It's in general hard to rate this one 'cause it definitely has potential, but I don't know where the story would have lead after it abruptly ended. Perhaps I wouldn't have enjoyed the next chapter that much?
