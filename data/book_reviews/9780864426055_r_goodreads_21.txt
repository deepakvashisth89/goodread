Mostly a memoir but with a bit of travel writing blended in for good measure. This is the story of the Newby family’s purchase in 1967 of a dilapidated farm house in northern Tuscany and their 25 year relationship with the house, their neighbours and the local rural culture. The first half of the book recounts their search for a dwelling in an ideal location, its purchase and the numerous renovations required to make it habitable. The narrative then shifts to their neighbours, the rural lifestyle and the activities in which the Newbys are invited to participate i.e. grape and olive harvesting, mushroom collection, festivals and family gatherings. There are also visits to other towns and villages in the region, all with their own distinct features and characteristics. The book concludes with reflections on some of the personal, social and economic changes experienced by the Newbys and their immediate neighbours over the course of their time in this small corner of Italy.

I found the first half of the book a bit dull, evidently padded and in need of some serious editing. And yet, somewhere in the middle, it transformed into quite an enjoyable read. From that point on, it was the old familiar Eric Newby - at his best. 3+ stars
