The Second World War was truly terrible. Over 50 million lost their lives in this conflict. This book exhaustively charted the main events, day by day, from 1939 to the final Japanese surrender on 2 September 1945.The account was clear and factual, with just enough authorial perspective on the events to help readers appreciate their significance. Sometimes it was too factual: do we need a list of the exact number and type of ships used on a given day in a particular battle? I enjoyed the relief provided by contextual tidbits shown in textboxes, such as certain major films released during the war or the deaths of notable public figures not related to the war.Unfortunately, this hefty tome published in the '70s misses out one or two events which are now considered historical milestones, such as the liberation of Auschwitz-Birkenau.It has taken six years to read this (2,194 days to be precise), as we followed the passage of war in real time, 76 years to the day after each event. It has familiarised me with the major players on both sides in the war, but more than that it impressed upon me the sheer length of the struggle and the weight of the sacrifices made by people on all sides of the world.
