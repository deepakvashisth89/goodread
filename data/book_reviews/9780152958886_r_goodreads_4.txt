I read this a while back, and I'm happy to find that:

1. clearly, according to the other reviews, I was not the only one fascinated by it,

2. it more than holds up.

This is much tighter than some of Hamilton's books, while retaining most of her characteristic weirdness, and that weirdness cohering better than usual thematically. (I can see why it's out of print. Its cultural references are dated, and it remains overall... odd.)

This novel does so much well. Everybody's interesting (to varying degrees). For example, the father, a minor character, has so much going on. Our villain is great, and long after the rather obvious reveal is apparent we continue to see new sides of him. I remembered him being very menacing, but Hamilton also has real empathy for him. And the book works at least as well as an unhealthy romance as it does an Examination of Social Problems.

Hwe probings of high school racial dynamics are always tied to character, and no one is a stock type. She has a shining fondness for the loser white drug addict. Her young Black protagonist is not an Everywoman, but a loner with her own worries. The Judas Priest concert is vivid and so particular; the writer is fascinated by people. We aren't dealing with whiteness as a monolithic experience, but a specific subculture. And while the rich folk feel a little vague, Hamilton always keeps an eye out for class and the way class changes interracial interactions.

The whole thing is just the opposite of a heavy-handed YA book about an interracial relationship.
