"That is because we are all queer fish, queerer behind our faces and voices than we want any one to know or than we know ourselves."I've always felt like I need to get into more F. Scott Fitzgerald, since (like most people) I've still only bothered to readThe Great Gatsby. I don't know why I chose this collection — maybe because it's not too big; maybe because Betty was once reading it onMad Men— but it feels in a lot of ways like as basic but potent a primer on Fitzgerald's short fiction work as you could really want.Only ten stories but very well curated, including ones that feel like companion pieces or development drafts ofGatsby(such as the masterful "Winter Dreams" and "The Rich Boy") and others that give a bird's eye perspective on the life, heydey, death, and post-mortem of the Jazz Age as a whole — "May Day", "The Ice Palace", "The Freshest Boy", and "Babylon Revisted" are other standouts.Plenty of unrequited love, plenty of heartbreak, plenty of drinking, and plenty of deeply unhappy rich people; what more could you want?
