A lighthearted, charming accounting of an English country vet’s experiences after WWII. He often mocks his own decisions, mentions his insecurities and questions his treatment choices, thus endearing the reader to his humble, meek manner. I appreciated how he always seemed to view his fellow humans (owners of his patients) with objective positivity, even when such humans have annoyed him or caused him harm or inconvenience. He truly aims to see the good and humanity in everyone, especially when the object makes that challenging.I would not call this a “quick” or “compelling” read, but it was charming to pick up and read a chapter now and then. The chapters are “self contained” stories and made it easy to pick up and put down in multiple intervals.
