I don't know a lot about the other volumes of the Pelican History of England series, but this is an interesting little book. If you want to know the circumstances of Mary Tudor's death, or the date Henry VIII got the crown, this isn't the book for you. This is not the stuff of a straight, factual, chronological history.

Instead, this is series of essays covering the economics of sheep raising and cloth making, the making of the English navy, the devaluation of coin, the rise of Puritanism, the lives of ordinary Englishmen and other topics. I'm somewhat familiar with this timeframe (thank you Will) so I could follow it. But if you didn't know the basic chronology of events, this book could leave you baffled.

This book is also very old and the references to "present day" are amusing. The author talks as if health care and services for the poor were a universally accepted good. (Oh, those wild and crazy 1950s - they were so naive and foolish!)

If you have some knowledge of this time period and you enjoy someone with a good amount of knowledge and wit giving you his opinion, this is the book for you.
