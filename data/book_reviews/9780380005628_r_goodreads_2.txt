I figured a bodice-ripper-looking book entitled "Dawn of Desire" published by Avon in the late 1970's would feature some rollicking action...but mostly I found this book very ho-hum and passionless. The first 200 pages are dull, particularly the opening chapter where everybody lays around in the heat, sleeping or waking up. Fortunately, the last half of the book was compelling enough to make me finish it.Queen Nefrytataten of Tamehu and Prince Amenemhet of Tashemau are betrothed to marry and unite the two kingdoms of Egypt. It's love at first sight for the blue-eyed Queen and golden-eyed prince: a very boring, bland love. Before they can enjoy their honeymoon, the queen's ship is attacked, she is kidnapped by a handsome desert bandit and gets amnesia. As evil priests plots to control the kingdoms, the newly crowned king searches for his wife by disguising himself as a commoner. A set up like this should have lead to a riveting tale, but the author's voice was so bland and and the dominoes fall predictably. There was no tension: villains are introduced and quickly dispatched. Lovers reunite. End Part One.Part Two is actually interesting: Our heroes travel to Atlantis and discover Egypt's heritage, ghosts appear and magical spells are cast. A priestess plots to seduce the king and kill the queen. A silver cat, a cobra, a hawk and some more ghosts save the day.My summary was a little less exciting than the actual book. The great thing about older romances were the elaborate plots that were always page-turners. This book...not so much. Since I own one of the sequels, I'll read it...one day. Maybe I'll be pleasantly surprised. I enjoyed the ancient Egyptian setting and some of the paranormal events, but the romance was blah. As a romance Dawn of Desire is a two star read, as history-fantasy-epic, it's a three-star.2 1/2 stars/C- rating.
