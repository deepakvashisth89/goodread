the south of France is warm and lulling, pleasantly sleepy in the heat; its little villages, picturesque ruins, and various architectural and natural wonders make for leisurely day trips, in-between light breakfasts, naps, cocktail hours. a pleasing place to escape to, for a young widow or for a little boy or for those looking to set a trap. the sun shines down brightly on all such visitors, perhaps too brightly: features and motivations get blurred in the brightness; it is easy to leap to conclusions. it is easy to visit the south of France as well, the options are many, the highways and backroads provide opportunities for those who travel, those who flee, those who hunt. our heroine drives well, an inheritance from her husband, killed in battle. she speeds and races and hurtles from town to town, stop to stop, her sturdy little car her steed, her hunter a madman. or is he? a father searching for his son can appear to be many things. perhaps the young widow should slow down, take in the sights, rather than racing by and speeding away. but it is hard to fault her. the author understands this need to rush forward, the newness of it, moving away from the past, into what comes next, into an adventure. into a trap, perhaps, but also into a new life...
