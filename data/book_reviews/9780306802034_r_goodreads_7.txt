4.5 stars rounded up to 5For anyone interested in film history, this book is not just a study of a particular place and time in Hollywood—the mid 1940’s and 50’s, but it’s a study on the way that the specific genre of film noir came about. It’s a uniquely American style of film making, and as all things American, it blended styles from other countries with an American flavor yo create this interesting film niche.My absolute favorite part of the book was how the author broke down both the literary and the visual styles that would influence film noir. He goes back to the OG detective story by Edgar Allan Poe, all the way to the colloquial writing style of Ernest Hemingway. He also details how important the German expressionist film makers were to this genre—a message we should remember about how immigrants (forced out of Germany by fascism) are truly what has allowed America to have her great moments in time.The one downfall of the book is it is a bit of an academic read, so may not appeal to a wide variety of readers. Readers who enjoy film and Hollywood history, however, will eat this up.
