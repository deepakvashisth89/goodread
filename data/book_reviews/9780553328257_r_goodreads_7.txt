I was recently asked by my GR friend Colin, “what novel had the most impact on you in your teens/early twenties?”I had to sit back for a few minutes and ponder that question. I read voraciously as a kid. A lot of times it was silly romances, because, well I was always looking for my Prince Charming.But I was also growing into a modern girl, one who had big ideas about becoming someone, and doing something big in this world.I was also fascinated by people. And mostly people who did bad things to other people. I never understood why humanity was so cruel to each other. I wanted to learn. I wanted to fix them, I guess.My Dad gave me this book as a gift, and I remember devouring it the minute I got it.I was fascinated with Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson. And I have been a fan of the two in all the many versions, since.But the true genius is right between these pages.When Conan Doyle started writing about the private detective, Holmes was a self-proclaimed anomaly, something that didn’t really exist in our world or at any point in history.Although the detective novel may have been in the beginning stages.One of the most interesting facts, is that Conan Doyle didn’t think Sherlock Holmes was a worthwhile investment of his time. It is the reason he tried to end the series at different times. Remember when he killed him off the waterfall?(Oh no - did I just give away a spoiler?! - forgive me my dear GR friends! You know me, I don't typically do this...but...it was part of the point I was making about the author - so I apologize for this!)He was more interested in writing historical pieces, and about the after-life. (We won’t go there!)But people wanted Holmes, and he complied. Thank goodness!Another interesting fact is that those in academics considered Sherlock as a historical figure, as if he truly existed. It is done in jest, on some level, but it is a common convention which began quite early, because Dr. Watson, was publishing the exploits in periodicals as case notes. And that made people clamor for more. (Of course, we know that Dr. Watson was Conan Doyle's character, too.)About this book...It has 4 full length novels and 56 short stories about the colorful adventures of Sherlock Holmes and his side-kick Dr. Watson.The author, John Le Carre wrote in November 14, 2006, as he reviewed the 150th printing of this edition, “Dr. Watson doesn’t write to you, he talks to you, with Edwardian courtesy, across a glowing fire. His voice has no barriers or affections. It is clear, energetic and decent, the voice of a tweedy, no-nonsense colonial Britisher at ease with himself.”Ahhh.... you can feel that, right?For me the stories and novels are well-written. They appealed to my younger self. I was fascinated with Holmes ability to read people. And I wanted that skill-set. I decided early on that was going to be an important “ability” for me to have as a life skill. Focus on the small details. Look directly at people. Listen to them. Hear what they are not saying.And to be honest those skills have served me well. (Thank you Sherlock!)There are passages of beauty, moments of suspense, and many, many quotables. It is addicting. We as readers, are drawn in and become the detective, too.Holmes was a towering genius in so many ways. Dr. Watson gives the stability, where Holmes in his creativity is sometimes off kilter, but we love him, and we are always mesmerized by how he can solve cases.And…This was probably the only time I was not intimidated by 1,122 pages.I still have my copy of the book my Dad gave me. The cover may be weathered, and the pages are well-read and worn, but, this book, is also, well-loved.Thank you Colin for the question.
