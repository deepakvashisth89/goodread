Queen Zixi of Ix was an interesting fairy tale from Baum, but doesn't quite capture the magic of his most famous series, Oz.

The setting and tone are very different than the two Baum series I've read, as he tries to give this book a more classical feel. He sets it in a vaguely European country instead of an American one, and avoiding modern technology, which are two of the things that make his writing really unique. While not poor, the settings and the characters both lack a certain flair and distinction, as his normal ironic social commentary is almost completely gone, his characters lack personality, and the narrative lack direction. His normal authorial tone is different here too.

The most interesting aspect of this novel is the character Zixi herself. While the other plots drag at times, when Witch-Queen Zixi enters the novel halfway through, the story gets radically more interesting. As the most engaging character she dominates the book. Bud and Fluff are almost completely passive and agenda less, and it is Zixi who pushes the story forward, and also she who is the only character who exhibits any kind of growth.

Baum's feminist agenda in Queen Zixi of Ix is intriguing as a reader because of the continued trend in his work where he depicts women political leaders as strong, competent, and best for a country. Men, on the other hand, like the childish Bud and his advisors, quickly lead their country to chaos. But even Bud has an older sister Fluff, who reels him in and provides guidance when needed. Both Zixi and Aunt Rivette act as critiques of vanity and vacuousness, and while Aunt Rivette is a cruel older woman, her foil Zixi is a good ruler and person despite a lapse in her better judgement, which she grows from. The important detail of her person as not fairy, but a human Witch-Queen seems to be an idea taken directly from Matilda Gage's political writings. Overall, this book was mildly entertaining to those who are passionate about Baum's work but probably would not be so to others. It's a fun read if you are interested in Zixi or the feminist influences on Baum, but otherwise I would not recommend it.
