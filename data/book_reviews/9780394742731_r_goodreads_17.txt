Setting: Sweden; 1971.This is the seventh book in the iconic decalogue 'The Story of a Crime' featuring police inspector Martin Beck.When a terminally-ill senior police officer is brutally murdered in his hospital room, all the efforts of the police force swing into action to capture the culprit. But, as Beck and his team investigate the victim (Inspector Nyman), they come to the conclusion that he has so many enemies it will be hard to determine which one actually killed him! Indeed, the 'Abominable Man' of the title turns out to be the victim rather than the perpetrator.....This is another great read from this series. Reading around the book itself, with the introduction and the commentaries about the series and the authors, it is clear that the couple (Sjowall and Wahloo) were trying to expose the degeneracy of Sweden in the 1970's, when most of the world probably thought of Sweden as a country of peace, tranquility and beautiful women. Yet these books expose corruption at the highest level, an almost militaristic approach to policing. high suicide rates and the oppression of the poor at the expense of the rich. Indeed, the police are portrayed in a pretty poor light (although Beck and most of his team are exempted from this), in a situation where it is no point an ordinary citizen making a complaint against the police (and there are several involving the victim and his cronies) as they will not be actioned. Enjoying this series for its 'early' setting (1960s-1970s), the characters and the political background - 9/10.
