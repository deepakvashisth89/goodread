Ginny looks like a normal teen on the outside, but inside she fears that there is nothing left to live for and no way to save herself. She is facing a crumbling family and a host of peer pressures, with only her best friend Beth to pull her back from the brink of despair.

Bringing Norse mythology to the modern world, Sargent combines fantasy with reality to expose the troubles that teens face and their confusion and anxiety over them. It is a beautiful blend of a fictional story and a very real set of problems, heartbreaking surely but also reaffirmation that with some guidance there is hope out there.

I first read this as a pre-teen, and found it mesmerizing at the time. Rereading this book as an adult, I can see just how important it is for teens to read this story and feel the subtle message it sends to them about hope and a promise of a better future.
