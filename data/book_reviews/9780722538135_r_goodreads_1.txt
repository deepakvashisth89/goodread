A VERY USEFUL MODERN INTRODUCTION TO SPIRITUALISM AS A RELIGION

Lyn G. de Swarte is a Spiritualist medium and healer, as well as lecturer, and editor of "Psychic News," the world's oldest weekly Spiritualist newspaper. She has written other books such as 'The Spiritualists' Handbook: A concise and extensive guide to Spiritualism and all its practices,' etc.

She wrote in the Introduction to this 1999 book, "Spiritualism is a religion, a science, a philosophy and a way of life; not only is life eternal, but this can be proved... So how does Spiritualism prove that every human being, as well as every other, creature lives for ever? This is established through mediumship." (Pg. vii)

She adds, "The main difference between Spiritualism and other similarly revelatory religions is that in Spiritualism the revelation demonstrates that all sentient beings continue consciously to exist after the physical transition we call death, unconditionally. Spiritualism has no Messiah..." (Pg. viii)

About reincarnation, she admits, "Here again there are various groups within the framework of Spiritualism who have differing views and opinions about this possibility... National Spiritualism as propounded by the Spiritualists' National Union does not express a belief in reincarnation. The SNU has a policy of supporting only the proven and provable fact or survival after physical death. As far as the SNU is concerned, reincarnation remains a hypothetical phenomenon. Having said that, there are many of its members, and people among its committees and officials, as well as ministers, who must believe that reincarnation is a fact." (Pg. 19)

She points out, "As Spiritualism has no one day earmarked as 'holy' or labelled the 'Sabbath' or specific day of rest, services are held on any day or days throughout the week, whenever has proved convenient to the members. Because Spiritualism believes in nothing that can't be proved, and is founded on the belief in unconditional life after death, which is made manifest through mediumship, mediumship is evidence at every meeting." (Pg. 59)

She asserts, "There have been many attempts throughout the ages... to curtail and even assassinate those who have been in touch with these messages. This is why there have been exhortations against talking to the dead in the Bible, and why even common rumour has it that it's wrong to communicate with those who have passed over. Yet all the great prophets recorded in the Bible were mediums. Jesus himself is reported as having been seen speaking to both Elijah and Moses... Knowing what I know now, I feel even more deceived personally, and angry too on behalf of the many thousands... who may be as convinced as I was that the way of Spiritualism was wrong, and who may be lost to the truth of Spiritualism." (Pg. 63-64)

Of the afterlife, she explains, "For those who have led distinctly unspiritual lives on earth, unpleasant conditions beckon... Many adjustments have to be made and drastic changes undertaken, supervised by more highly evolved beings, before any spiritual progress can be substantiated... there are crystal caves and halls of learning, and places where those more highly evolved in the spirit realms visit, in a guiding and instructing capacity... There are libraries---with all the books there ever were, and entertainment centres which ... never close. Sleep, eating and drinking, are naturally unnecessary." (Pg. 70-71)

She concludes, "Spiritualism is a down-to-earth religion. It has nothing whatever to do with superstitious practices, beliefs or rituals. Instead of 'faith,' Spiritualism offers demonstrable knowledge." (Pg. 81)

This is an engaging, accessible introduction to the religion of Spiritualism, that will be of great value to anyone wanting such an overview.
