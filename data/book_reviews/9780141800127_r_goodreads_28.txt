The Body was a surprisingly emotional read for me, bittersweet and heavier than I expected. It stirred something personal. As I followed these boys on their journey, I couldn’t help but think about my own friendships at twelve, and how just one small change could’ve sent my life in a completely different direction.

This isn’t just a story about finding a body, it’s about what it means to be young, brave, and a little bit stupid all at once. It’s about the kind of friendships that feel like everything when you’re a kid, the kind that stay with you even when the people don’t.

I found myself smiling at their dumb jokes and reckless decisions, especially Teddy and Vern who were hilarious, only to feel a tight ache in my chest a few pages later. It’s the kind of story that makes you want to look out a car window and quietly fall apart, not because the story is tragic, but because it reminds you of something you’ve lost without realizing it.

“I never had any friends later on like the ones I had when I was twelve. Jesus, does anyone?”

That line hit me hard, and it’s the perfect way to sum up what this story really is: a love letter to the fleeting, powerful magic of childhood friendship.
