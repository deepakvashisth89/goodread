The storyline is interesting enough: a deadly airborne virus, concocted in a US military laboratory, mistakenly gets exposed to an individual. He escapes with his family, and when they show up dying in a small town, the men who come out to help are exposed, and so on. The virus has a 99.7% kill rate. The beginning of the book is dedicated to the spread of the plague and the mass deaths. Some of this is very interesting and may parallel how people would really react. King presents the mass deaths in such a deadpan way, it was actually a nice complement to the seriousness of the chain of events.

The world's population is decimated. The novel then begins to show the few survivors, finding one another and their dreams seem to propel them into one of two directions—and two leaders. Randall Flagg is the leader of darkness, located in the west. Mother Abagail is a 108-yr old black woman in Colorado who is empowered by God to see the intent of the evil Flagg and she represents the good.

The characters develop, some taking on unexpected roles in this newly formed society, and the events bring out both the best and worst in the individuals. Another fear for the remaining individuals is whether their offspring will be susceptible to the virus. One of the central characters is a pregnant young woman (in the good camp) who meets and falls for a guy who is one of the leaders of the good camp. They become a couple and another source of tension is the awaiting of the baby’s birth.

Added tension arises as some characters in the good camp are actually evil and are drawn to Flagg’s camp, but are plotting against the good camp for their destruction.

An eventual battle ensues, good against evil.
