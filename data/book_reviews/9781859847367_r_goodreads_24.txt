4,5/5 starsThis book is pure dynamite and I was absolutely unable to put it down. Full with fury, sarcasm, irony and facts Hitchens dismantles both the man and the Presidency of Bill Clinton. As a prosecutor he goes through all the different facets of Clinton's carreer and the result is damning. Clinton is shown as a morally corrupt individual , a sexual abuser, an abuser of power but also as important as a political hollow figure whose 'populism' talked a lot to the left but always delivered to the right. And I think the central point Hitchens makes is that there is a connection between his moral corruption and his political one.But not only Bill Clinton is dealt with here but also the whole Democratic Party and liberal intellectuals who decided to choose party before country , democracy and integrity. Hitchens his book is very much a critical attack on Clinton and his legacy from the left and there is often a sense of disappointment that people who should have known better ( and sometimes were friends of him) , acted the way they did.This book is not easy though and in some ways it's a bit dated. It was written right in 1999 and it absolutely expects that the reader knows about American politics and more especially about the politics of the Clinton era. I can imagine that some people picking it up today might struggle with that. I also think that his writing is very beautiful and powerful but not easy. Sometimes I had to reread certain sections because they were beautiful but also because sometimes I didn't understand it immediately. So be prepared for that. On the other hand I think this text is still so relevant. All the sections about Clinton's connections with wealthy backers and sexual misconduct hit harder now we know the Epstein files. The criticisms of the ideology of the 'clintonism' with it's desire to appear 'bipartisan' could have been written today. Similary the ways democratic lawmakers and pundits came to their rescue of their president and aren't willing to take any sane criticism of that president reminds certainly of more recent episodes of US political history. Last but not least Hitchens warns that the acquital of Clinton and the putting of his abuses under the rug has put future crooked presidents in a stronger position. Something that seems in 2026 very much to be a reality.
