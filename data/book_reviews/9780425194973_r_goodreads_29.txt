I first read Francis in the late 80’s. I was planning for a semester abroad in London and went to my local library in search of books set in England. I loved mysteries and was drawn to those bright covers of Bolt and Longshot. I don’t think the racecourse-centered books really prepared me for my time in Earl’s Court, but I was soon hooked on Dick Francis.

I’m not sure when I stopped reading his books, but I’m glad a horse prompt in this year’s reading challenge enticed me back. Despite aspects of the plot probably falling apart in an age of cell phones, Dead Cert, Francis’s debut mystery first published in 1962, holds up pretty well, and provides ample evidence of why his mystery writing career took off so quickly with its publication.

His books have a formula —good guy jockey/ex-jockey/other horsey job, finds something nefarious happening on the racecourse or racecourse adjacent and has to — to avenge/clear/save a friend — investigate. He will be beat up at least once, likely more, and will find instalove with a pretty young woman (my least favorite part by far). Despite knowing the formula, his books are always engaging and often a bit informative. In the past I liked best the ones that brought in another business (I remember wine, gems, films, and weather forecasting) along with the horse world, but bring the first, this was understandably focused. It definitely put Francis back on my TRL.
