I've scanned through several reviews here of this book and note that it tends to be rated very highly or quite poorly, with few in the middle. I think it was a terrific story in its time, a visionary look at AI with some insight into the human condition, too. It's a fix-up novel that appeared in 1972, comprised of novelettes collected from Galaxy magazine where they appeared beginning in 1969. It's the story of David Auberson, a robot psychologist who guides H.A.R.L.I.E. (Human Analog Replication, Lethetic Intelligence Engine) from "childhood" to "adulthood." It's a very slow-paced story, mostly discussion and philosophy, but his handling of the concept in those days when machine intelligence was solely in the minds of sf writers is brilliant. (Spoiler: HARLIE goes on the figure prominently in many of Gerrold's other, later novels.) Gerrold revised the novel significantly and changed the ending for a re-release in 1988 which I have not read. The technology is now dated, but it's still a good, character-driven study.
