After reading and enjoying several of Hillerman's great Leaphorn/Chee novels in no particular order, I decided to check out from the library an online copy of the first book in the series, The Blessing Way and to hopefully read the rest of the series in chronological order at some point. This novel was originally published in 1970 and although it was definitely dated, it was still an exciting and thrilling page-turner that I enjoyed a lot. This is the first outing for Leaphorn who in the novel is a young Navajo Tribal policeman working for the "Law and Order" as the police were called by the Navajo. When a young Navajo man is found dead by the side of a road, Leaphorn is hard-pressed to find clues or a motive for his murder. The man had been suffocated like he was buried in sand. But why leave him out in the open to be found when he could have been buried in the vast wilderness of the reservation? The locals feel that a wolf-witch is to blame. An entity had been sighted by some that appeared to be a wolf but then turned into a man. Why are sheep being wantonly slaughtered and is the supernatural to blame? Into the mix comes Bergen McKee, an ethnologist who is trying to put together a case for the existence of the wolf witch by interviewing members of the tribe. But then when he meets up with an archaeologist in a canyon with many Anasazi ruins, another murder takes place. So what is really happening here and how does the mysterious wolf/witch come into play? Is the supernatural to blame?

This one was a really fast-paced story that kept me guessing up till the end. As usual, Hillerman did an excellent job of describing the landscape of the desert southwest to make you feel like you are there as you read the story. He also provided a good amount of Navajo legend and ritual that made the book all the more interesting. Jim Chee does not show up in this novel; I guess I'll need to read more to see when his character is introduced. And although Leaphorn is the main protagonist of the novel, McKee actually has a somewhat bigger role in the story. He was a great character and maybe he'll show up again in future novels? Anyway, glad I finally read this first in the series and I'll look forward to more.
