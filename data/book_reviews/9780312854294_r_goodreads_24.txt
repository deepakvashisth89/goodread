A family of foxes live under a long abandoned house. Not long after we’re introduced to Kitsune, brother, mother and grandfather, the master returns. Yoshifuji, Shikujo, their son and servants show up. There’s a lot of tidying up, the foxes discovered and driven from their den.

The reason they're back is that Yoshifuji didn’t get a posting in the city. He decided to come back to this rural property to perhaps recapture the joy he felt years ago when he and his wife lived there. It’s harder for me to explain exactly what Shikujo wants, whether it’s to be a dutiful wife or whatever. Following her husband to the country was the only conceivable option. There is no longing to go back to the city. Meanwhile Kitsune is unnaturally intrigued by the humans.

There is a lot of upper class ritual. Women being proper, poems being exchanged. It doesn’t extend to fidelity, Yoshifuji has no compunction about extramarital sex. There seems to be servants for whatever manual labor needs to be done and no shortage of food or other necessities. All part of the setting. When the magic happens and Kitsune is a woman, she goes directly to being noble.

What we end up with is three people (well, one’s a fox) with longings or dissatisfaction struggling to come to grips with their life. For something of this nature (no definite conflict to be resolved) it surprisingly held my interest all the way through. 4.0 stars.
