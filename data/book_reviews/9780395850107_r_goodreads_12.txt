Wow. I was blown away by the sheer amount of research that went into investigating the ways that lesbians have contributed to women's rights as we know them today. As a lesbian myself, I wish I had known about this sooner. It would have saved me from the shame I had and the feeling I needed to hide my identity. Famous women like Susan B. Anthony and Jane Addams were mentioned in this work for their contributions as well as their unmistakable love lives with other women. Every lesbian should read this. Seriously. This book is proof that there is a vast amount of documentation proving the lesbian identity of numerous female politicians, activists, doctors, lawyers, professors, and countless other women who worked to improve living conditions all across America.I also appreciate the mention of lesbophobia that grew during the 1950's. In an attempt to thwart the progress women's rights activists made, many "experts" and "sexologists" labeled lesbians as sick to discredit the work they did towards advancing rights for women in the workplace. I still see this type of discrimination to this day in many workplaces. It is important to know the connection between lesbophobia and people who were resistant to women's rights.Like I said before, every lesbian should read this book. Knowing how much our group has contributed to advances in civil rights and living conditions in general would help dispel negative myths about lesbians. It would also help shed light on just how vital our existence was to American life from the 1800's to now.
