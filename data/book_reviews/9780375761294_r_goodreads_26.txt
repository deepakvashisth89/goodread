McTeague is a study of greed in America.
Almost all of the characters succumb to greed in the end, meeting their fates in misery. Norris leaves one story, where greed has taken a loved activity away from one character, with a hopeful ending where love may conquer. It isn’t much to go on, but this is all the reader is left with. This is a profoundly negative story, but well told. It is probably the only story I’ve run across where the main character, McTeague, is continually described by the author as stupid. Strangely, he is a dentist, and although his speech showed his lack of intelligence, he was quite professional in his work. McTeague usually repeated everything he said, and the reader may tire of reading "You can't make small of me. You can't make small of me." as McTeague thrashes back at folks that were usually making small of him. It was disappointing to see McTeague


There was also this running bit in the beginning involving a flying squirrel. I didn’t catch any resolution of that bit. Probably could have made another book all its own about that.

I listened to this on audio narrated by Wolfram Kandinsky. I’ve heard Kandinsky narrate before, on some of Updike’s Rabbit books, and he has an old-timey voice that I didn’t like with Updike. His voice works well in this turn of the century book, and his characters are well acted and distinctly voiced. I had a hard time trying to figure out who McTeague sounded like. The best I can come up with is Mr. Ed, a bit less horsey, a bit lower IQ. But it fit the part to a T - McTeague is something like an old workhorse. Overall, a take on greed in the world that will leave you feeling worse than when you started.
