Jherek Carnelian's adventures in pursuit of his love for Amelia Underwood continue in this beautifully told tale of time travel, infatuation, and a science fiction in which the fiction takes charge. Having returned from his brush with execution in 1896, but without Amelia, Jherek continues plotting for a way to win her back. Behind that Moorcock carries on building his characters and their dream world of half remembered, mainly distorted, historical facts. The Duke of Queens has constructed, if constructed in the right word, magicked up perhaps, a version of early twentieth century New York."Very true to the original, I think,"he assured Jherek and goes on to explain the tallest building was known as the Empire State Apartments, which was the home of New York's greatest king, Kong the Mighty."What are those tiny flashes of light?"asks the Iron Orchid and the Duke explains:The muggers... At that time New York attracted a good many artists, primarily photographers (called popularly 'shooters', 'muggers', or sometimes 'mug-shotters') and what you see are their cameras in action.""You have a talent for thorough research," said Jherek.As Jherek contemplates the nature of time travel and the hopeful imperative of the Morphail effect - if a traveller interferes with the past, time will shunt the person back where and when they came from to avoid upsetting the continuum - he and his friends are confronted by Captain Mubbers and his gang of hilarious Lats, space marauders with a taste for rape and pillage. The Lats take a role similar to the Keystone Kops inMack Sennett's silent comedy movies, always ready to do a comic turn when time travel philosophy becomes a little over-heavy. Through them Jherek falls in with the robot Nurse and through her a new time machine and a return to 1896.What follows pushes an already splendid story even further as Jherek meetsH.G. Wells, finds his way to Bromley and Amelia and runs away with her, being chased by the police and Amelia's husband. The chase sequence is a triumph of story-telling in itself, culminating in chaos at London's Cafe Royal and some revelations concerning Lord Jagged of Canaria. Back at the End of Time Jherek is made to understand his love affair will remain as turbulent as ever.As a story it is the best ofThe Dancers at the End of Timetrilogy. Completely entertaining from beginning to end, farcical at times, witty when it wants to be, and with a hint of the profundity of time that will come inThe End of All Songs.
