My father passed of complications of Alzheimer's several years ago, so I started and stopped a couple of times before I was able to get all the way through the book. It's very powerful, not just in the story about Charlie's father and the work to identify the source of the disease, but in the impact on those around the victim and their different ways of dealing with it - denial, withdrawal, or engagement. There is a poignancy to the telling of the story, too, as his father and all his father's brothers were taken by the disease. As Charlie likes to put it, he himself has an ugly dog in this particular fight.Highly recommended, if you can find it.
