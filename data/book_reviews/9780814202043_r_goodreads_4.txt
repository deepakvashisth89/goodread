Like Mosses From an Old Manse, it mixes slight and trivial (though not unenjoyable) stories, with a fair amount of decent stories, and a handful of tremendous ones. His best stuff (In this collection: "My Kinsman, Major Molineux," "Ethan Brand," "Sylph Etherege," "The Wives of the Dead") crackles with obscuring shadows and veils, sly fireside devilishness, sharp irony and sometimes a disquieting ambiguity. I probably overuse the term "disquiet" or "disquieting" when praising a work, but I think the best works often have a strangeness and especially something troubling about them. They are an incantation to reveal.
