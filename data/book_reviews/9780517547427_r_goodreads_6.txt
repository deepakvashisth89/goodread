"The Shattered Eye" is the 3rd in the November Man series by Bill Granger, and there's no letdown in pace, plot, or craftmanship in this one. Main character Devereaux, the November Man, is just as deadly efficient as ever.The story begins in the early 1980s as computers are making their way into the analog spy world. A Section R (an American government spy shop that is a competitor of the CIA) computer analyst identifies an anomaly in the data she's reviewing, and thus cascades a series of events that nearly results in a world war. It seems that the Russians devised a plan to sew division between America and its NATO Allies by infecting CIA systems with bad data and arranging for the assassination of the French president while pinning it on the US. Underlying it all is a series of computer war games run by the Russians that predicts what American response will be to the anticipated series of events. Devereaux, currently on the outs at the Section, is brought back in to figure out what's going on as a Section spy who'd been investigating is murdered. With his deadly logical approach, Devereaux finally succeeds but not before facing his own lethal challenges.For the life of me, I don't know why Bill Granger isn't considered to be near the top of the list of American spy novel authors. I've bounced around in the November Man series and each novel is outstanding, with fine writing, great tradecraft, and excitingly plausible stories. I realize much of his work is set a few decades ago, but the plots haven't aged all that much and seem timeless. The Shattered Eye is one of my favorites in the genre and a fine example of the quality of Granger's output.
