Fairy tales are usually the first stories kids get into, right? They're magical, fun, and full of adventure - but they also quietly shape how kids see the world, think about themselves, and build their values.The problem is, a lot of these stories sneak in outdated gender stereotypes. And because kids absorb this stuff so early, it sticks. Over time, it can really influence how they see themselves and others - especially when they’re making big life decisions later on.Honestly, the more fairy tales a girl reads, the more likely she is to grow up thinking she needs saving instead of being strong and independent. And boys - they start believing they’re supposed to be the heroes all the time, which I think, feeds into a cycle of inequality that just keeps going.Take Snow White, for example. She’s bullied, cries a lot, and ends up eating a poisoned apple from a stranger - 0 survival instincts there. Her whole story revolves around waiting for a prince to rescue her. Even the 7 dwarfs seem like a weird message: flawed men are still somehow the 1 who get to protect and save women. And that whole “true love’s kiss” thing? Really? Poisoned apple meets magical lips and she’s alive again? That’s some wild logic, don’t you think?And the Grimm brothers didn’t stop there. They had a princess kiss a frog, locked Rapunzel in a tower thanks to her unstable dad, and then had a random prince climb her hair to “rescue” her. These stories have been around for centuries, passed down like treasured classics, but they keep pushing the same tired idea: women are weak, passive, and obsessed with love, while men are brave, capable, and always in control.So it’s no surprise that girls who grow up on these tales might start valuing looks and romance over independence, while boys get the message that they’re supposed to be dominant and that women are just side characters in their story.Yes, these are just fairy tales. But they still matter. That’s why I think it’s so important to be thoughtful about the stories we share with our kids. Read with them, talk about what’s happening, and help them understand the real message behind the magic. Because teaching the right values early on - that’s the real happily ever after.
