Fun fact: I almost DNF’d this book thirty pages from the end. No real reason, I was just that bored. Bored out of my friggin mind, matter of fact.Look, maybe I went intoThe Quiet Gamewith high expectations. It has been reviewed quite favorably by many friends of mine — friends whose opinions I trust. And that isn’t to saytheyare wrong, orIam wrong; it simply means we all digest art differently.This book commits arguably the largest sin possible for a book to commit: I cared not one bit for any of the characters. The narrator (what is his name? I already can’t remember — oh, Penn Cage, that’s right . . .) is bland stale bread; his “romantic interests” are forced, the villains are laughably cliché, the whole narrative feels ripped straight from John Grisham, only done much worse . . .Oh, and Mr Iles, black people do not smell “funny” or “different”, despite what your protagonist says. WTF?Giving this two stars for the occasional “thrilling” twist, the minute hints at literary depth, and the town of Natchez itself . . . which is, I must admit, given more life than any of the humans who populate it.
