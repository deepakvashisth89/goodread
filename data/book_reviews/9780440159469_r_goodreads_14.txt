It is difficult to rate this book. I wanted to do more justice to Agatha Christie and her brilliant mind, but my conscience nags at me; it tells me that, in honesty, I was not drawn into the story as I normally do when I read her novels. So here I'm divided, trying to do justice to both of us.

It was overall a good plot, though not a novel one. The setting is a small village in England where life is slow and stagnant. In such a small village, it isn't surprising that everybody knows everybody's business. Gossip mongers and story creators are all too common in such a closed community. In this setting, Agatha Christie brings in some interesting characters - old spinsters, widows, eccentrics, spirited, and the manipulative. As always, Agatha Christie invests well in her characters.

is the first in the Miss Marple series, where we meet her celebrated lady detective. Miss Jane Marple, who has made it her hobby to observe human nature, rarely misses out on any of the important happenings in St. Mary Mead. She is sharp-witted and shrewd, and although she is no professional, nobody can fool her.

Coming from reading the Poirot series, it was quite an adjustment for me to connect with a strikingly different detective (if I may use that word for Miss Marple). Whereas Poirot was brilliant and unreachable, Miss Maple was ordinary and reachable. There was a huge contrast between the strict professionalism of Poirot and the ordinary powers of observation and a decent deductive mind of an old lady who helps solve a murder mystery, which the local police find baffling. Agatha Christie doesn't portray Miss Marple as a sleuth. She doesn't do active investigation; she only acts upon the evidence that was gathered by others and thrown her way.

The story engaged me for a good measure. The events and characters are presented in a tightly woven plotline. There was a fair amount of suspense from the time the murder took place. There were a fair number of red herrings, so it was hard to accurately guess the criminal. It was all good. But after many twists and turns, Christie takes us back to the initial suspect. I was quite confident in my guess of the Criminal before Christie deliberately created confusion. I call that cheating. :) Also, Miss Marple's brilliant summing up felt a little too contrived.

Having said all that, I cannot claim I didn't enjoy the story because I did. An Agatha Christie murder mystery is something hard not to enjoy. But to speak the truth, this didn't live up to my expectations. I wasn't totally drawn in. After all the deliberation, the ending felt like a slap in my face. I'm half satisfied, half disappointed.
