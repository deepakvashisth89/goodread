After reading the first few pages, I regretted to have picked this abridged, easy-to-read version of the famous Robinson Crusoe's story. I realized right then and there that I should have picked the full version instead.In fact, it's not so much an easy-to-read type. The language used for this paperback sounds strange. I didn't know if the language represented the original narration of the story, but I found it very odd and therefore, made this book an uneasy one to read.Many times, I had to re-read the paragraph two or even three times to fully grasp the meaning of what was going on. The story used linear plot with first person POV. It felt like Robinson's is telling the story of himself through some kind of a journal, or a diary, where he cherrypicked what he wanted to share to the readers, instead of telling the whole truth.It was, after all, a great adventure story. The part when he had to adjust to the new life all by himself in a desolate island, without any tool to live by, or even anyone to speak to, probably for the the rest of his life, was so inspirational.Imagine that don't have any table to put things on to, what would you gonna do? Robinson had to chop down a big large tree, set it on an edge, hew it flat on either side with an axe until it was thin as a plank, and smooth it with adze. It took him months to build a single table.Now imagine that you have to build your own house (which Robinson calls "a fortress"), that of course should have many furnitures to help you live by, with nothing but a few simple tools you found on a wreckaged ship. It would take years to do so.But instead of weeping endlessly, which I might do if I were ever in his position, Robinson kept doing what he needed to do to survive. He hunted animals to feed himself, made clothes out of animals' skins, raised livestocks, and even grew plantation. All of those things he did out of his strong willing to survive, to live.And in the moment of distress, he even had this brilliant point of view, that eventhough life was difficult, he was, after all, the king and lord of this island. No one to serve to, no one to work for. Freedom!Now every time I find myself in a very bad moment, that I think I won't make it through, that I won't survive, I will think of Robinson Crusoe, and his journey, and his bad luck that helps him to be a very strong person, who chooses nothing other than to keep going on and live.
