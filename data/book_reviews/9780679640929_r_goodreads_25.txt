Küng doesn't just provide a short history of the Catholic Church, he's also relentless in criticizing his own church. As a protestant, I'm sometimes jealous of Roman Catholic's authority with regards to what is right doctrine. But Küng shows most definitely that they have been all but right and that it has failed to operate in the right way - with the 19th century as the worst example. Küng deromanticizes any notion of this church's infallibility for sure.

In the end, one wonders who or what Küngs final authority was. Certainly not his own church, certainly not the Fathers. Perhaps Scripture? (I figure that he has been jealous of Protestantism).
If I had to guess, based on his vision for the church on the last pages, I'd say his final authority became progressivism.
