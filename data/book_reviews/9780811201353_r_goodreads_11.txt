This was largely a disappointment. The autobiography, _Safe Conduct_ was interesting and engaging. Having studied in Marburg myself, where I remember a marker where Pasternak lived, I found that part of the narrative quite interesting, and of course the later sections dealing with Majakovsky and Ostap Mandelstam were as well, even if the part about Mandelstam doesn't show Pasternak in the best light. Yet the stories left me cold. To me they lacked a conflict and were more portraits. "Letters from Tula" confused me in particular, it seemed to describe a movie set, but was listed as written in 1919, when such thing would have quite unusual. I grew into the poetry. While I found the first poems difficult to engage with some to the latter poems moved me, but not in a way that leaves me thinking I'll be reading anything more by Pasternak in a while.
