So. Many. Characters. Sweet baby kittens on a cracker, I spent most of this novel trying to keep the characters straight (and failing miserably). Otherwise, though, I enjoyed this one quite a bit. It's more humorous than most (all?) of the previous Hercule Poirot novels I've read, and the unexpected humor is really quite a pleasant surprise.The mystery itself is solid and I never saw the big reveal coming, although that's pretty par for the course for me when it comes to Dame Agatha's novels. What I most enjoyed about this one, however, is Ariadne Oliver. She's well-known for being a fictional version of Agatha Christie herself, and Oliver spends some time in this one lamenting the creation of her most famous character “Sven Hjerson.” She complains of his “idiotic mannerisms” and wonders why she made him a Finn when she knows “nothing of Finland.” She obviously isn't thrilled to have such a character “tied to her for life,” and mentions that if she met him in real life she'd “do a better murder than any [she's] ever invented.” Now, I could be wrong here, but it seems like perhaps Christie might've been a little sick of Hercule Poirot while writing this one? There definitely seems to be some uncanny similarities between Sven and Poirot, at any rate. I don't know why, but it completely cracked me up for Christie to be (likely) complaining about Poirotin a Hercule Poirot novel ...But, anyway, this one was fun. I've called half of the Poirot novels “one of my favorites” at this point so it's a fairly meaningless distinction, butMrs. McGinty's Deadis almost certainly in my top ten.4.4 stars, rounded down.
