Cup of Gold is Steinbeck's first novel and his only true work of historical fiction. Told in five parts, it follows the famous British buccaneer Henry Morgan, through important parts of his life. While the writing is pretty solid (but not up to peak Steinbeck prose) and the story flowed easily from one part to the next, Steinbeck's skill at characterization isn't present here like in his later works, so I didn't really connect with this like I have with virtually everything else I've read by him. (At this point 14 works). But it's still a good book, but when measured against Steinbeck himself it falls short.
