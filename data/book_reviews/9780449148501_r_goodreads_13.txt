I've read a few Ann Cleeves' books, from the Shetland and Vera series, and I've found them an enjoyable read. They do tend to be a little on the slow side but I've always assumed that's intentional, given that they're based in rural or semi-rural locations where the pace of life is slower than in cities.This book is, however, exceptionally slow. It focuses on the death of an elderly woman who lives alone and is in dispute with the local landowner. The death doesn't occur until 18% through the book so there is a lot of background and scene-setting before then.The death is at first thought to be an accident or suicide but eventually turns out to be murder. It's investigated by an elderly couple who seem to operate independently rather than as a team and aren't very convincing in their actions. They also have a relationship with the police that I'm not sure would be possible in real life.They do eventually discover the killer and the reason for the murder. However, their findings seem to be based on a number of assumptions, some of which turn out be wrong. The story does come to rather an abrupt and unconvincing end so it all seemed a little unsatisfactory.I found the dialogue a little stilted in parts and the writing a bit clumsy. I realise that Ann Cleeves is an award-winning author but I suspect this is one of her earlier works before she adopted a more polished style.I will probably read more books by this author but will avoid those in the George & Molly Palmer-Jones series.
