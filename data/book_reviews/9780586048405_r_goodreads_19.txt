Eurasian barbarian chieftain Alp is plucked from his time to land in the future. The inhabitants there (then?) run a massive, star-spanning cross between an MMORPG and a reality TV show set in the Dark Ages, and a couple of them hoped to snatch Alp as a point of reference to get a leg up in the game. Undeterred, Alp breaks free, and enters the game himself. There, he uses his knowledge and experiences to carve out his own personal virtual empire.I'm on the fence about this one. The action is great, the developments are interesting, and the game concept is amazing. Kind of a pre-cyberpunk way of jacking in to a virtual experience. On the other hand, Anthony often stops the narrative as Alp watches cartoon effigies duke it out on TV, representations of in-game standings. No, really. Those sections are a dull history lesson, too much exposition to catch my interest, and they begin to drag after a while. Anthony also has confusing terminology to represent in-game and real-world increments of time.Unexceptional and kind of forgettable, but it has enough good moments to entertain you for a few nights. Very light and fluffy kind of reading.(Full review found here.)
