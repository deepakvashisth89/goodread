read - 6 October 2005 - This is a minor work by major sf writer Frank Herbert. It's about the pretentious Chem society, who have manipulated humanity throughout its history in order to make vicarious recordings of its emotional traumas. When Inspector Kelexel shows up, the famous director Fraffin distracts him with the personal tragedies of some individual humans he's been preparing for just such an occasion. Psychologist Andy Thurlow's girlfriend Ruth and her whole family is dragged through a murder, and subsequent criminal proceedings. Ruth is kidnapped in a plot to snare Kelexel, and the trap is sprung on him.The Chem are very much like humans except they are immortal, and that very immortality has brought them to the brink of insanity with their own death-wish. Clearly Herbert has meant the Chem to represent the jaded and voyeuristic elements of our own society, as seen in our mass entertainments. It is a somewhat trite observation, I think. Fortunately, the novel is much shorter than a typical Frank Herbert work, and comes to a conclusion before it becomes tiresome.
