I went into The Book of Nothing expecting an interesting science read, but it turned out to be much more than that. John D. Barrow uses the idea of “nothing” as a thread to explore mathematics, philosophy, theology, and modern physics, and somehow makes it all feel connected and surprisingly readable.
From why the ancient Greeks struggled with zero, to St. Augustine’s fear of nothingness, to the bizarre early experiments to create a vacuum, the book is full of moments that made me pause and rethink ideas I’d always taken for granted. The sections on quantum physics where the void turns out not to be empty at all were especially fascinating.
It’s thoughtful, wide-ranging, and occasionally challenging, but never dull. If you enjoy books that stretch your thinking and wander freely across disciplines, this is a rewarding read.
