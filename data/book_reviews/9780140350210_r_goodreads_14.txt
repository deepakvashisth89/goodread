“At once I thought of Lorna Doone, the little maid of six years back, and how my fancy went with her. Could Lorna ever think of me? Was I not a lout gone by only fit for louch-sticking?”

Long before the Lorna Doone shortbread cookies we buy at the grocery store, there was this guy named R.D. Blackmore who wrote this classic and epic Victorian romance novel named Lorna Doone in the 1800s. I know that this book has been in print since its inception, but I’m surprised this book is not as well known in Victorian literature circles or classics.

One thing we must speak of, and that is the difficult dialect and 1700s West Country vernacular used throughout, most notably in the initial stages of the novel (luckily, there is a glossary of Dorset words used in the back of the novel to help a little with this). That, and this a novel that does not take a very quick route. Our narrator tends to have some asides that drift our focus, as he is quite the storyteller. So, this is a novel one must have some patience with. (I found that a nice audiobook will make the difficult dialect go very smoothly).

Why? Because if a reader can get past this there is a pretty good romance here with quite a bit of charm and entertainment value.

Our protagonist John Ridd narrates, looking back on the timeline of his life and younger days, and telling the story of his family, the history of the region and the infamous clash with the Doones, and most notably, how he met and fell in love with Lorna Doone.

There are actually two focuses in the novel. One obviously is the forbidden love between John Ridd and Lorna Doone and the other being the battles and historical perspective of clashes between the Doone and their enemies. I found the novel to be a rollicking good adventure and romance.

John Ridd makes secret trips to Glenn Doone, the forbidden property of the Doones, to see Lorna Doone after being captivated by her as a youth. However, he must try to figure out how to continue the romance given that Lorna is of the Doone household. The Doones are like the community bullies and have had a long tradition of lawlessness, so there is quite a bit of danger for John as he continues his love for Lorna.

Given it is a Victorian, sure there is quite a bit of melodrama, characters swooning at the drop of a hat, etc. Still, this was just a fun novel, and I like how through John’s voice, there is such passion and enthusiasm at looking back at his former years.

This edition has some nice little tidbits in the introduction about how Blackmore, after many struggles, was finally able to get this novel published, and it went on to become a classic.

This is a classic that is not talked about with some of the other Victorian novels, but it was a fun, entertaining novel with a lot of charm.
