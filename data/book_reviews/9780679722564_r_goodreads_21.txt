What I loved most was the complete lack of internalized angst about being gay — Will is arrogant and vain, but unapologetically himself, and I enjoyed following his story. The book offers a vivid snapshot of a particular London subculture. However, I was uncomfortable with the fetishizing of anyone not English, especially Africans, and wished there had been some counterpoint. I also would have liked a bit more resolution — not tidy answers, but a sense of change — yet the characters do still linger in my mind.
