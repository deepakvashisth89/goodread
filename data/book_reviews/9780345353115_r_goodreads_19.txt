For me, many of Heinlein's books fall somewhere in the range from decent to downright unpleasant and uncomfortable. Even his most known and revered works, which I can appreciate in some respects, have difficulty finding their way into my science fiction "favorites" or "best of" lists. I tend to find his earlier works, and especially his short stories, of most interest. That said, I still find myself coming back every couple of years to read something else from his bibliography.

So how does Space Cadet rank? I would have to put it somewhere in the "decent" range. This book is one of Heinlein's juveniles, what would be labeled as young adult now. However, the young adult target audience in the 1940s was vastly different from that of today. As such, it is not going to resonate very well with many readers today, not only for this reason, but also due to a lot of outdated social assumptions, world views, and scientific details.

The story follows a young man, likely college aged, who signs on to a space patrol, which is basically an international military branch that specializes in outer space environments. The first half of this very short book is taken up by the obligatory training sequence. The latter part gets more into a mission with moral and geopolitical issues, where most of the thematic heft lies. At the same time, the narrative does feel rushed and goes a little off the rails here. It makes me wonder if Heinlein was trying to finish out the story within a small page count.

There are also some things in the story that are just off-putting. Not quite as bad as what I have encountered in some of his later books, but it is still present to a degree. Some of these things are small and subtle, but one prominent example is that later in the book, the characters always refer to one person by the nickname "Stinky". It is especially strange to me in a military setting. Is this normal? Was this normal at the time the book was written? This is the sort of thing present in many of Heinlein's books, and it tends to tarnish my opinion of many of them by at least a small degree.

Space Cadet is, in my humble opinion, just an okay book. It has some elements of what would come later in and Heinlein's own . However, it is not as good, nor is it as memorable, as either of those. The book is quite short and can therefore be read in just a few hours, but I would recommend it only to readers interested in doing a deep dive into Heinlein's works.
