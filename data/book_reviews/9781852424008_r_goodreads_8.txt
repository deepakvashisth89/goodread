Haunted houses is about three middle class, American girls in what I’m guessing is this late 60s mid 70s(?— I’m basing this on the needle drops) in the suburbs of New York. The characters never meet and the only thing that really connects them is the circumstances of their upbringing and the fact that they’re girls. Lynne Tillman’s writing captures the inner lives of girls and what it feels like to be a girl growing up in such an understated but successful way. I found myself remembering things that I felt when I was a teenager that I had completely forgotten I ever felt. Her writing is that good.I do agree with a lot of reviews that say that there is something off about the structure of the book. Jumping between stories was somewhat jarring, and keeping track of who was who was kind of a chore. Maybe if the chapters were lengthened and the book was only three parts instead of five? All that aside, I’m pretty obsessed with this book.Also, as a no-plot-just-vibes girl, this line really spoke to me: “Emily comforted herself with the idea that plots were like skirts, you either did or you didn’t use things like that. Why do people want stories to go somewhere, she asked herself, and retired to bed.”
