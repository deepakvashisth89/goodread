Why do I keep reading James when she annoys me? Well, because when she’s good, she’s very good. Isolated scenes in this book are terrific: the Dalgliesh interviews with Fredrica Saxon, Louise Fenton, and Nagle in his studio; the tension (and the cat) during the climactic scenes; even the Committee meeting, which reads like a short novel by C. P. Snow. She obviously knows this milieu very well.Still, the first half is rather ramshackle, the detection seems miraculous, and the murderer unconvincing. It’s missing another rewrite. And I am too hard on her. But she’s very good. When she’s good.On second thought, I will round this one up to four stars.
