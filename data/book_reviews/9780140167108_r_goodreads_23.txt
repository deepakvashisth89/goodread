Yevgeny Zamyatin'sWewas an enthralling read from beginning to end. His imagination and creativity in crafting this novel, at a time when space travel was barely a concept, is impressive. The dystopian post-apocalyptic world that he paints is eerily reminiscent of George Orwell's 1984 (which was inspired by We), yet somehow gave me a less bleak and pessimistic feeling. The world in We is regimented and totalitarian, however, lacks the cold-hearted violence and spirit-crushing cruelty of 1984.I particularly enjoyed Zamyatin's ingenuity in weaving mathematics and hyperrational thought into the story. This provides a stark warning about the dangers and limitations of embracing extreme rational thinking. It has a tendency to be totalitarian (assuming that it has considered all possibilities) and even worse, to fall in love with its creations. I also found the description of the vices of humans of the past like smoking, alcohol consumption, lust, love, imagination etc. was very believable and enriched the story. In many ways, I could easily see a prequel or sequel to We, being possible (although Zamyatin never did write either of these!).My only very minor critique of We is that a few times the thread of the narrative got 'tangled' or 'blurry' - it was unclear to me what exactly was happening. This left me slightly confused for a few pages until I figured it out later. Nevertheless, We is undoubtedly a masterpiece of dystopian and science fiction literature, which I highly recommend to everyone without hesitation. I will certainly be reading this novel again in the future!
