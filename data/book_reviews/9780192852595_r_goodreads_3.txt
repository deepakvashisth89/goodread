This book is very much 'hit and miss'. Some chapters are very good and interesting, others are written in a very dry way or from an apologist's point of view. Chapter about Islam is a little irritating, because it keeps asking a question why Christians in the Bizantine regions were assimilated into the Islamic society and Muslims weren't assimilated into Christian Europe. The answer seems so obvious, but somehow it eluded Oxford scholars. Muslims were invaders in Europe and hence the strained relationship with European Christiandom.That particular chapter also claims that after the Medieval period no Islamic societies survived in Europe - a Muslim minority, that came in 14th/15th c, still survives in Poland.Overall it is worth a read just to get how complicated and colourful history of Christianity is, but beware of the biases.
