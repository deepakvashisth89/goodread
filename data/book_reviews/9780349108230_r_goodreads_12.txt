Faith Fox goes from being newborn to about three months old at the end of the novel, so she really doesn’t do much. We judge all the other characters by how they act around her.I hated the first part of this book, found it irritatingly cheeky, manipulative, and sarcastic. Gardam told us what we were supposed to think of the characters instead of letting us decide for ourselves, and I didn’t like ANY of them. Only two things kept me reading: my husband liked it, and I rated Gardam’sThe Flight of the Maidensfour stars. It had to get better. I hoped.It did. About 100 pages in, it climbed from 1 star to 2. After page 200, I really did want to see what would happen. I was starting to like some of the quirkier characters, the supporting cast rather than the stars, and the action sped up. It was touching at times and hilarious at others. She even pulled off some great slapstick humor. This is one of those rare books that might be better on screen. It could have been a four-star book for me if the front end hadn't weighted it down.
