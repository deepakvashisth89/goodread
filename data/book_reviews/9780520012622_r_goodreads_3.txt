Divided into three sections, Genesis, Exodus and Leviticus the book described how Japanese came to America and their reasons. Detailed description of their experiences follow up to the bombing of Pearl Harbor. Exodus is devoted to detailing how the idea of internment camps evolved and the reasoning behind them. Excerpts from war documents detail the attitudes of prejudice against the Japanese while those of Italian or German ancestry were not targeted to the same degree. The book places most of the blame for the camps on General DeWitt. Leviticus is the law and examines how the courts reached incorrect decisions all the way up to the Supreme Court contradicting standing doctrine and laws mostly related to citizenship. Overall the book was boring, repeating statements and points already made. The book did convey much information on the behind scenes formation of an effort that is part of America's tainted past, which we hope to never revisit, though the freedoms being taken now in the name of another war are crystal clear.
