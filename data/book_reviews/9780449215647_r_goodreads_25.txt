The Austrian placements here are splendid- the visuals and the actions both would make an excellent film. Very expensive film!

It's closer to a 3.5 star but I rounded it up for the character nuance developed early between Timothy and Vanessa. You do not fine a dynamic duo often that quite equates to that mature married woman/ 17 year old boy turned man on his "own". Not as a traveling or work or any type of companions' escapade- it's an odd and rather interesting pairing. I loved it- they both brought out the best in each other and NOT in a sexual liaison way either. Not a jot of that. But in all the action and social radar arenas that needed improving for both of their rather odd and uncomfortable situations.

It was overlong- especially in the first 200 pages. But those parts would also be good in a movie too, and probably better than all the explaining for the missing husband and alternative identities portions. After page 200 it would be a superb action film (different pacing than all that former circus surrounds) with chase and elevator scenes perfect for the visuals. Not to mention the train, mountain, Mercedes and phenomenal horse dancing sequences. Especially that one with the "piebald" in the alp's wood.

Mary Steward did the descriptive hotels, venues, events, natural habitats and the styles of fashion extremely well. Yet I never read her in this genre type before and was surprised at the quality of the "case". It wasn't shabby or trite. Ones I had read before tended toward the Gothic or Romance genre that I knew was her forte and huge success.

Very entertaining and of a time/era when sharp characterizations in a chronological progression form were tops. And so appreciated by this reader as opposed to the flip flopping narrator, time and questionable source for this genre presently.
