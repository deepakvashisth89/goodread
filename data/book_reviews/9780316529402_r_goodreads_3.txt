William Manchester, the eminent historian, needs little introduction as the author of the award winning biographies of Churchill and MacArthur. This remarkable work on the Krupp family was penned more than fifty years ago. I’ve read a good deal of history and did not know the Krupp story so all of the content in this book was new for me.

The best portion of the book - the latter half - focuses on Alfried Krupp during WWII and the immediate years after the Third Reich. The post war trial and the section on the Allies willingness to look the other way during the Cold War gave me the greatest insights of how the Krupp family got away with murder.

There are few historians who can write a book this riveting about a family of arms manufacturers. Manchester’s success is in part because he focused on the story of the family and less about the business details. And there is a flare to his writing that transforms what might be dry reading if it had come from an average historian.

4.5 stars. This took me a while to read at over 800 pages. It was worth it.
