“By July 1863 the Civil War had been fought over the farmlands and sea coasts of the South for better than two years, and is already one of the bloodiest wars in human history.”


And that is the introduction by author Jeff Shaara of The Last Full Measure, the third book in the trilogy following the key characters of the Battle of Gettysburg, through the surrender at Appomattox, and beyond. This story primarily follows three men: Robert E. Lee, Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain, and Ulysses S. Grant. And this gripping and powerful story is told from the points of view of the characters themselves, through their experiences as they stand apart, each in his own way, rising to a higher level as not only a war hero, but as a man of character, dignity and honor.

This book is divided into four parts, each opening with a beautiful epigraph of key portions of the Gettysburg address given by Abraham Lincoln in November 1863, at the dedication of the Soldiers National Cemetery in Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, four-and one-half months after the pivotal Civil War battle. It is still revered today because Lincoln connected the soldiers’ sacrifice to the nation’s founding ideals from the Declaration of Independence, arguing that their deaths were not in vain and that the living must continue the fight to preserve the Union and its democratic principles.



This was such an involved conclusion to the Civil War Trilogy by Michael and Jeff Shaara. It was heartbreaking throughout the narrative, but, at the same time, very hopeful as we witnessed the key characters in the conclusion of this dreadful war: Robert E. Lee, Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain, and Ulysses S. Grant. And we cannot underestimate the power and influence of President Abraham Lincoln as he did everything in his power to keep the nation together and bind its wounds.

And Jeff Shaara portrays such a tableau of the Civil War through the eyes of these three men and how they left their mark on history. My heart broke for Robert E. Lee as he realizes that General Ulysses Grant has outmaneuvered him in battle, as they both negotiate the terms of the surrender at Appomattox. And the brilliance of General Grant and his friendship with President Lincoln was so heartwarming while knowing what the future held.
