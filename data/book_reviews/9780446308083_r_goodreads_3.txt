It's a shame this book is out of print. I think it just might be my second-most favorite book ever (right afterThe Count of Monte Cristo). If I had read it back when it was first published (in the early '80s), I would have expected it to become a modern classic in a few decades. But at least it sold well enough to get a paperback edition.I bought this book along with ten others at a pretty low price on eBay. I thought I'd read it first because, to be honest, I thought it had the best cover.The Dark Side of the Dreamstarts at the end of the story, after one of the main characters is killed. It's a great start and the author did a great job of introducing all the characters that we meet later when the story is told in mostly chronological order, starting in the 1940s and ending the early 1980s. The book divided into three parts. The first part covers the first half of the book, the second covers the next third, and the third covers the last sixth. As I said, the introduction was great but the rest of the first part did get boring at times. The characters weren't very sympathetic. But I think I read the next to parts of the book faster than I've read any book before. And the protagonists became more sympathetic.Also, and I'm not usually one to care that much about prose style (just adequate writing is enough for me) but I really liked the way John Starr wrote. His prose flowed very well.I think the book's biggest problem is that it does get a bit too ambitious at times. I don't mean it gets pretentious, because it doesn't. What I'm saying is that it deals with some pretty big themes and issues and I had the feeling, when it was over, that they weren't all covered completely.I'm not a fast reader so I'll never hold it against a book for being shorter than it could have been but I might say I wouldn't have minded if a book was a bit longer. And that's what I'll say about this book. I don't think I would have minded if it was a few hundred pages longer. Of course, John Starr might have been too ill to continue, unfortunately, if what the other reviewer says, thatThe Dark Side of the Dreamwas published posthumously, is true. I think this is true because the copyright page says that the copyright is held by the Estate of John Starr, not John Starr himself. Also unfortunate is that I can't find any information about the author online. In fact, Goodreads says that John Starr also wrote a few books that were published a few years ago with some CGI scantily-clad women on their covers that were published a few years ago. That must be another John Starr.I'm going to giveThe Dark Side of the Dreamfour stars because, like I said, the first half is kind of boring. But I really did like it a lot. If the other ten books turn out not to be so great, this book is worth the price I paid for them all.
