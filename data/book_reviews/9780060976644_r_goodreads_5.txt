This one was amusing. The meetings and busywork that appeared to change nothing made me chuckle for awhile, then became tedious. The POV changes allowed other characters to liven up the story as by this time Martha becomes less interesting. Political activism can be a sideshow. Few people are savvy enough to have any lasting impact.On a different note, I've heard that Marxism is capable of gaining fervent followers because it is able to replace that need for religion in society. The Communist party that Martha joined indeed operated a bit like a religious cult. It seems that youth, in their twenties, are prone to fanaticism. In the story, as in life, people generally do not live up to youthful idealism. Martha has herself in a pickle, and her support system has changed, which leads one to open the next book....
