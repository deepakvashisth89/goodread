Janet Mandelbaum is appointed as the first female professor in the English department at Harvard, not because of her feminist views, which she doesn't hold, but because the traditional administration finds her acceptable. However, they are still far from pleased with her presence.The tension escalates during a formal afternoon tea when someone spikes Janet’s Campari. The police later discovered her in a highly compromising state, passed out on the ladies' room floor.Sophisticated sleuth Kate Fansler arrives to help her old friend uncover who is behind the setup. But before they can find answers, Janet is found dead—this time in the men’s room.`````````````````````````Here are my thoughts on this book.I really enjoyed the interactions within the campus and the descriptive strolls around Cambridge, including Mt. Auburn Street, Massachusetts Avenue, Broadway, and Harvard Square. The discussions were plentiful, and the setting felt very real at times.The story takes place during the merger of Radcliffe and Harvard, highlighting the faculty's struggle to truly accept women. The central plot revolves around the murder of an unpopular female professor found in a men's restroom.The book raises interesting questions about the challenges women faced in securing tenure, the historical dynamic of the "good old boys' club," and how those struggles connect to modern issues regarding gender equality and pay equity.
