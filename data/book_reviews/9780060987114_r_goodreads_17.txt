I read this book years ago, but it came up in my feed again today, and I wanted to add to the chorus of people acclaiming it: this book changed the way I approach having OCD. Following the treatment guide outlined in the book (over time) made living with OCD so much more manageable, even when I didn't think a book would really help me overcome the problems I was having. Psychological/psychiatric self-help books really vary in terms of quality -- this is a good book and everyone with OCD should give it a read!

Edit: I see a lot of negative reviews mention that if you're helped by this book, your OCD must be "mild." I've had OCD as long as I can remember, wouldn't describe it as mild and was helped by this book. More and more research is coming out about neuroplasticity and the role of psychology in treating illnesses/diseases that have typically been treated with medication. Unfortunately, there is a contingent of those who resist trying these approaches because they believe their symptoms (perhaps uniquely) won't respond to anything non-pharmaceutical. The idea that even severe OCD symptoms couldn't improve because of the exercises in this book is inane. That's not to say there's no role for pharmaceuticals (which I take!), but dismissing the cognitive aspects of dealing with OCD is wild to me. There is an obvious benefit to learning how to rethink and re-interpret obsessive thoughts in that you reduce the perceived importance of those thoughts -- a tool you carry with you even if you aren't or can't be medicated.

(tl;dr: Weird and presumptuous to say that anyone who benefits from this book has "mild" OCD; more people should give stuff like this a shot.)
