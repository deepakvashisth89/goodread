“RPF is fine.”

-Ronald Reagan

This was a very unconventional biography, and I think a lot of people pick on that because they don’t like the way it portrays Reagan. It’s a mostly neutral perspective, doesn’t glaze him or ignore the bad things he did, and it explores parts of his life and character that most Reaganites would ignore. For example, Morris explores Reagan’s attempt to join the communist party in Hollywood, and his very sexual “single entendre” jokes with George Bush.

I think the reason that Morris fictionalizes himself as being inexplicably linked to Reagan his entire life is because, despite that and similar to people who were physically close to him, he could not get close to Reagan. Almost no one could, not even his own children. That’s a very important point to the book.

There are also some great insights and perspectives of his relationship with Nancy, which are very funny to say the least.

Overall, I really enjoyed this book. It definitely wasn’t what I was expecting when I picked it up a month or so go in english class. If you’re a Reagan freak like me, it’s a great read!
