The novel "The Dogs of Winter" written by Kem Nunn and published in 1997 tells the story of four men who go on a adventure together to take photographs of them surfing in a hidden reef called heart attacks, which is said to be beautiful, wild and marvelous - but it's location isn't identified.The novel does indeed include the basics a novel should cover, in my opinion. It has detailed and authentic characters, whose motives and state of mind are undoubtedly comprehensible, it has a varying story line with a pleasant side of cultural differences and conflicts - but what it really holds is detail. An awful lot of detail.Don't get me wrong, I do indeed see, how the describing a scene can transfer it, moreover: make it come alive, but.. Is it really necessary to sketch an entire setting for about two pages, elaborate each and every microscopic segment - only for the actual scene to pass in about 10 sentences? Do we need to hear absolutely everything about Robbie's abhorrent, abominable piercing?To sum this up: in my opinion The Dogs of Winter isn't a bad book - it does indeed have potential as a part of literature and I might be able to imagine somebody enjoying this style of writing - but I, personally might as well look at some pictures of beaches or maybe take a trip there myself before reading a novel that describes them and holds a few lines about some story about a bunch of boys on a trip and some mad Indians who kidnap a woman in their bizarre house-car.This entire review has been hidden because of spoilers.Show full review
