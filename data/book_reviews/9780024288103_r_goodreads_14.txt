Wittgenstein believed that rules only allow enough exceptions to interpret past words arbitrarily, rendering the concept of following rules obsolete. Language, as he described it, is unique to each speaker and designates sensations only they can perceive. However, he later argued against the possibility of such a solipsistic language existing, stating that there is no distinction between a person who truly follows a norm and one who appears to be following it. Wittgenstein suggested that rules are merely a matter of practice and context created by us. The meaning of a word is only correct when used within the nest of other words that support each other. Language games and the meanings of words change with them. Wittgenstein's resolution of the paradox of following rules is that the rule for following rules is not an interpretation. Therefore, Wittgenstein would likely argue that even a simple sentence requires a deep understanding of the physical world, human perception, and common sense knowledge, which cannot be achieved through rule-based systems or statistical analysis alone. Therefore, machines cannot truly understand language.
