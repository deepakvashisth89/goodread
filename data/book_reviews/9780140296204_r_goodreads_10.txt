Shackleton was a good writer. But, then again, it would be hard not to make something enthralling from his first Antarctic expedition as leader, wouldn't it?

And The heart of the Antarctic is nothing if it isn't enthralling. Whether its the failed journey to the South Pole, the successful journey to the magnetic South Pole, or the various depot-ing and scientific missions along the coastline, the reader is held in awe as to what these men achieved at the turn of the last century. They endured frost bite, snow blindness, dysentery, starvation, exhaustion, countless falls into crevasses; they tootled about in a motor car on the snow; they grieved the loss of their beloved ponies (before, admittedly, eating some of them); they climbed glaciers onto a vast Antarctic plateau they might have described as Lovecraftian had they been there a few years later (indeed, there are serious At the Mountains of Madness vibes throughout the account: I kept waiting for somebody to mention hearing eerie whistling on the wind).

A terrible, wonderful adventure, superbly recounted. If you have any interest in polar exploration, THotA is required reading.
