Just as he was able to useTom and Huckto play with conversations full of false logic and elements of travel writing in that book,Twaincontinues to reveal that his two star characters are incredibly versatile and can fit into a number of different genres. They can mimic the pirates, robbers, and adventurers that Tom reads about in books. In this novelTwainparodies the work ofSirArthur Conan Doyle, who had been popularizing both the revelatory mystery format and the almost supernaturally observant detective for half a decade prior to the publication ofTom Sawyer Detective.In the mid-1890s,Twain, near bankruptcy after unwise investments, returned to his popular charactersTom and Huckto revive his fortunes. He wroteTom Sawyer Abroad, a parody of the travel adventures ofJules Verne, and began a novel to be titledTom Sawyer Among the Indians, which, sadly, was never completed.Twain'sstory, which he wrote in three weeks in early 1895 took place on the cotton plantation of Tom'sUncle Silas Phelps and his wife, Sally, on the Arkansas Delta; both had been introduced inThe Adventures of Huckleberry Finn.The novella published first in a magazine and then as a book in late 1896, has a loosely knit plot; like the previous books in theTom and Huck seriesthe story unfolds in a series of vignettes. On a steamboat voyage from Missouri to Arkansas, Tom and Huck meet Jake Dunlap, a thief who has stolen diamonds from other crooks, who are hunting him. The boys help Jake escape and then reach the farm of Tom’s Uncle Silas, a clergyman as well as a farmer. Silas is harassed by Jake’s brother Brace Dunlap, a rich farmer who is trying to force Silas’s daughter Benny to marry him. Brace also coerces Silas to employ Jubiter, Jake’s brutish, surly twin brother. A body is found, and Silas is accused of Jubiter’s murder. At his trial, Tom cleverly exposes who killed whom. The tale is told by Huck.BothTom Sawyer AbroadandTom Sawyer, Detectivewere wildly successful and, for a time, helped restore the fortunes ofTwain.So why hasTom Sawyer, Detectiveslipped through the cracks of popular reading ~~ especially since it features two of fiction’s most celebrated creations? Well, how could it live up to the Great American Novel ~~The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn.Ernest Hemingwayfamously said,All modern American literature comes from one book by Mark Twain calledHuckleberry Finn. American writing comes from that. There was nothing before. There has been nothing as good since.How could anything that followed in theTom and Huck seriesmeasure up?In my opinion, taken on its own,Tom Sawyer, Detectiveis enjoyable, humorous and allows us another trip downstream withTom and Huck, two characters most of us have grown to love. The bottom line is if you likeTwain, andTom and Huck, then you will enjoyTom Sawyer, Detective, a light, amusing read by America’s best storyteller.Postscript:A few years after the release ofTom Sawyer, Detective,Twainwas accused of plagiarism byValdemar Thoresen, a Danish schoolmaster, saying the plot was taken fromThe Vicar of WeilbybySteen Blicher. In 1910Twain'ssecretary wrote back toThoresenin typicalTwainfashion, diffusing the situation:Mr. Clemens is not familiar with Danish and does not read German fluently, and has not read the book you mention, nor any translation or adaptation of it that he is aware of. The matter constituting 'Tom Sawyer, Detective,' is original with Mr. Clemens, who has never been consciously a plagiarist.
