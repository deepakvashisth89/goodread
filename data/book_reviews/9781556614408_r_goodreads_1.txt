This book simply overdid the spiritual by saying the same things repeatedly. It was incredibly boring with precious few circumstances or situations to move the story along and develop the other characters in Corrie’s life. What was wrong with Rev. Rutledge? What was his illness? We hardly heard from Aunt Katie and Uncle Nick. Where did Zack, Mr. Trumbull, and Pa go for a month? They came back with a great story to tell but the reader never knew it so why was that even included in this book?To me, Corrie who was a firstborn of 5 children, by age 15 had been through much in her young life on the way to California. She obviously handled her responsibility well and early in her story, I believe she was portrayed as a brave and maturing teenager. But as the books progressed, and especially in books 7 & 8, she cried about everything! For her to have experienced all that she did in her life, from being in charge of herself and her siblings after her Ma’s death, for being a writer for a newspaper in the 1860s, for her experience as a traveler, her experiences out East, I could not see a maturity in what I would expect in a 30-year-old woman. I was so frustrated that I almost quit reading this. I also don’t feel she ever believed herself to be pretty and I also question whether she ever really recovered from what her Ma said about her.
