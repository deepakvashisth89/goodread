Sue Hubbell really brings to life the experience of living in the Ozarks and managing her honeybee farm. I really got a sense of place and setting from her and she really evokes the atmosphere of her surroundings.

I like her honesty and her willingness to admit mistakes and shortcomings, but also her resilience and ingenuity in finding ways to try new things and problem solve.

While there is talk of some animal cruelty by locals and neighbours (which would normally put me off reading), I like Sue's compassionate approach to letting animals live and do their own thing. She is tolerant of termites, spiders, snakes and coyotes, amongst many others. She shows a relaxed live and let live attitude which is highly refreshing.

I love her laid back philosophy and her no nonsense approach to rural living.

Great writing, thoroughly enjoyed.

Also, anyone that writes a dedication to their cat, Black Edith (who is a boy cat!) in the front of their book is a winner in my eyes!
