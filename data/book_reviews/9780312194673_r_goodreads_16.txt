An exquisitely told fable by the marvelous Aldous Huxley, reflecting his pacifist/liberal ideology. Jacob's Hands introduces us to Jacob, a ranch-hand who can indeed cure with his hands. His preference is for healing animals and children, not adults. He has long adored his boss's crippled daughter Sharon, and at her pleading, cures her. Word spreads, and at first he offers his services willingly to a church. But is soon lured into the clutches of a depression era huckster, who employs Sharon as an entertainer. They are both miserable with their work, but fall in love. Sharon dreams of stardom while Jacob longs to return to the land.
They try to plan their future, but when he cures the ailing heart of a millionaire's son, their world soon spins out of control and Jacob is forced to reckon whether there is value in curing the body but not the soul.
As a depression-era morality play, laden with magic-realism, it confronts the lure of wealth and fame and beauty, laying it bare for the masses who worship these things without consideration of what they will ultimately cost. Seems appropriate reading for these billionaire obsessed times.
