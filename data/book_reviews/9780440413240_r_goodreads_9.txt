The Incredible Journey was one of my favourite stories as a child, although like many people, my first experience of it was seeing the film at the cinema. There is a later adaptation from 1993 called “Homeward Bound: The Incredible Journey”, but the one which captured my heart was the original film from 1963, which made Sheila Burnford’s 1961 book famous.

I well remember being taken to see this as a family treat at the pictures. It was probably Easter, or some holiday. I knew it was Disney (as are both films) and rather hoped it might be a cartoon, but my flash of disappointment soon went when I saw the breath-taking colours of the Canadian scenery, and the three animal stars. (We did have a television, but it was tiny and black and white.) There was Luath, a young golden labrador retriever, and Bodger (whose full name was Champion Boroughcastle Brigadier of Doune) an old English bull terrier. He was a funny-looking dog I thought, with piggy eyes and a pinky white coat. But I soon fell in love with this old soldier too, although our family dog was a border collie. The third member of the “pack” was a stunning Siamese cat called Tao. I didn’t personally know any cats, although many were to come into my later life.

These three pets were inadvertently left behind on their own. Their owner Jim Hunter had been offered a visiting fellowship at the University of Oxford, and he and his wife left Tao, Luath and Bodger with a family friend. John Longridge, the friend caring for them lived 250 miles away, but he was their daughter Elizabeth’s godfather. Their son, Peter was particularly close to the old bull terrier.



After a while John Longridge goes on a hunting trip for a fortnight, leaving a note for his housekeeper Mrs. Oakes. However half of it falls into the fire, and the impression left is that he has taken the three animals with him.

Outside, where they love to roam, Luath, Bodger and Tao see their new friend go off in his truck. Luath, the young and strong labrador retriever is the leader, and he thoughtfully watches the flocks of geese migrating West. When nobody comes back he decides to set off to the West too. He does not know how far it is, but all three pets can sense that this is where their family live. When Mrs. Oakes and her husband Bert arrive, they do not worry about the animals, as they think that Longridge has taken them with him, as his note seems to indicate.

The bulk of the tale is about the three pets’ incredible journey. The story is set in the northwestern part of Ontario, which has many lakes, rivers, and widely dispersed small farms and towns which they need to negotiate safely, without getting caught. Moreover Bodger is an old dog, and not really up to long distance walking. The three forage as best they can


There are many exciting episodes such as this one. Sometimes they come across humans who will help them, and sometimes they are driven away or shot at. They even become separated
.

The three animals continue to follow their instincts and head west through the Canadian wilderness back towards home, 300 miles away. They have many such life and death adventures, but struggle bravely on
We see the animals’ suffering, and persistence throughout their arduous journey, but allied with their mutual loyalty and courage.



This is a wonderful heart-warming tale, but was it true, I demanded to know. I was a child poised between wonder and disbelief. My father told me there were miraculous tales of animals who had made such a journey, crossing many hundreds of miles to get back to their owners. They were amazing but true accounts, and nobody knew how they did it. But three animals together? We weren’t sure (or so my parents said). It was, of course, a fantasy I realised, when I read the book later.

The Scottish writer Sheila Burnford did not write the story specifically for children, and the language used has no concession to young readers. However, it was initially quite popular, and after the Disney film became thought of as a children’s story. She had based it on the animals she and her husband used to own while living in Canada. These were a bull terrier they had brought from England and a Siamese cat. She described the relationship between these two as . The couple also had a young labrador retriever, who also developed a close relationship with the older dog.

I would happily recommend both the book and the film to any competent readers of perhaps 10-12, or animal lovers of any age.
