We all know this story by now - Ebenezer Scrooge is a miserly old man who spends more time counting his coins than his blessings.

After a particularly egregious display on Christmas Eve, Scrooge is first visited by Jacob Marley (his deceased business partner) who has a dire warning for Scrooge's soul.

"I wear the chain I forged in life," replied the Ghost. "I made it link by link, and yard by yard; I girded it on of my own free will, and of my own free will I wore it."

Scrooge is given a shot at redemption - provided he survive the visitations of three more ghosts. The Ghosts of Christmas Past, Present, and Future. It will be up to Scrooge to decide whether his soul is worth saving - and what changes he needs to put forth to do so.



It took me an embarrassingly long time to actually write this review - I'm talking years between when I read this book and when I finally got around to the review.

That being said, I am here! Finally! And the book was really good - I've read the book and listened to the audio, and have truly enjoyed it both times.

Now, there are probably a million - okay, more likely a hundred - different versions of this book circulating out there in the world BUT this edition is my favorite.

Why?

The meals are selected by Giada De Laurentiis, Ina Garten, Martha Stewart, and Trisha Yearwood! Such a cool way to make the book more magical.

If you are curious, the recipes included are:


--Stuffed Mushrooms
--Spinach Salad with Dried Cherries
--Buttermilk Biscuits


--Herb-Roasted Turkey Breast
--Baked Virginia Ham


--Cranberry-Orange Relish
--Smashed Root Vegetables
--Asparagus with Hollandaise
--Smokey Candied Carrots


--Pecan Pie
--Fruitcake Cookies
--Cran-Apple Crisp



It was really fun to read through this book and see all the foodie decorations and cute layout of the various quotes. I also liked it when we come upon a recipe section and got the next part of the meal.

That being said, I really wish they would've included a couple of sentences about why each cookbook author chose that specific recipe.

It's nice to see the turkey breast recipe but why was Virginia Ham included? Or Smokey Candied Carrots? Are they authentic to the book's time or are they particular favorites of the chefs?

In addition, I really, really think they should've included a recipe for the figgy pudding. I get that that isn't a particularly well-loved recipe nowadays, but it is so iconic to the book that I feel like it should've been added in anyway.
