I’d never read Elmore Leonard before this, but I’d seen a few movies adapted from his work, watched Justified, and just sort of generally feel like there’s a million authors and filmmakers out there who cite the guy as a major influence that I figured I had a pretty good idea of what to expect going in to Get Shorty. And plot wise, that’s very much the case. This book is exactly what you think it is: a gangster goes to Hollywood, decides he wants to start making movies, and a web of hi-jinx and double crosses ensues.What actually ended up surprising me about the book wasn’t what it was doing but how it was doing it. Really minimal prose that conveys a ton of emotion and action, super punchy dialogue, just a style of writing that effectively threads a needle between being cinematic and natural sounding. Leonard’s style is a ton of fun, and picking this up directly after reading a 900 million billion page fantasy doorstop made for the perfect palate cleanser.I listened to the audio book for this one and I am going to take one star off because that audio book is pretty poorly done. It’s read by Joe Mantegna, who spreads about three character voices across the entire cast of characters, uses those voices inconsistently, occasionally just not bothering with character voices at all, and then will often just fall in to using the voice he does for Fat Tony on The Simpsons for long stretches of the book. Sometimes you’ll have a scene with multiple characters talking who all sound like Fat Tony, and it will just be one Fat Tony talking to another Fat Tony about a third Fat Tony while a fourth Fat Tony narrates the whole thing, until it’s just a kaleidoscope of Fat Tonies stretching out in to infinity with no sense of where one ends and the next begins. I love Fat Tony, on a list of secondary Simpsons characters he’s in my top tier easily. I do not love Fat Tony this much. Four stars!
