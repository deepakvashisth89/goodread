Not sure where to start with this review. I can’t lie, I found Light in August by William Faulkner to be a gruelling experience. I oscillated between a 1-star to a 5-star experience at various times, depending on the chapter. My only other Faulkner read As I Lay Dying I found to be a lot more enjoyable. I found As I Lay Dying a little more understandable and predictable than Light in August – I could get into a rhythm.

I achieved no such rhythm with this book.

There were times I enjoyed this story, usually when I was fully engaged and totally immersed, when the writing seemed understandable and Faulkner wasn’t doing anything too clever for me. However, there were many times I became downright frustrated where even re-reading passages proved fruitless, and it just wasn’t enjoyable.

Some of the important themes as I see it are (Be Gentle):



I couldn’t believe how much the “N” word was used. This isn’t a criticism as such, more of a confronting observation. It certainly is a sign of the times (Civil War to the early 1900s) and it did help me understand how ingrained some of the attitudes towards race must be in the US. The early 1900s isn’t that long ago really. The fact I am currently listening to The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, which has an equal dose of the use of the “N” word didn’t help. Huckleberry Finn is even worse in some ways because the Children, such as Huck, use the word freely.



There’s a fair share of overly pious people in this story, such as the sadistic Joe Christmas’ adoptive father Mr McEachern, and the catatonically insane Mr Hines. Well they all seem to be nutcases, horrible, distasteful and violent people. Not sure if Faulkner is making a statement about religion and the hypocrisy of it all. I reckon he might be.



Kindness doesn’t seem to be well rewarded – some of the characters who exhibit kindness include Joe Christmas’ adoptive Mother, Mrs McEachern and Mrs Burden, Joe’s landlady and ‘lover’. I am not so sure their compassion and good intentions were paid in kind. I am uncertain if it’s because they are kind, or women or both. Probably both.


Joe Christmas must be one of the most detestable characters I have ‘met’. Totally misanthropic and equally sadistic. I really felt sorry for anyone he came across – my views are, he was a product of the sadistic, cruel and depraved upbringing he experienced. Poor bastard.

Lena Grove was a delight, a bit naïve though. The book really starts and finishes with her and I must say, considering this book was a miserable experience, I did like the way it finished, gave me some reason to be optimistic.

Bryon Bunch – for some reason his name made me laugh because it was so close and easily confused with the name Lucas Burch. I did love that farcical confusion, and I found it very funny. Not sure if it was supposed to be funny – but I found it so, still do.

Mr and Mrs Hines, What a strange couple, Mr Hines (Uncle Doc) was a complete Muppet with no redeeming qualities at all, whereas his long-suffering wife was really trying to be her best and one could only feel for her regarding the cards she had been dealt in life – poor thing.

I wasn’t sure what to make of Reverend Gail Hightower. I felt sorry for him peering through his window at the street all the time. He was so lonely – however, he wasn’t the only lonely character in this story, that’s for sure. I think he was a good man, but I did get lost in his back story and his thoughts, I really did. But at this time of the book I was suffering from Stage 4 Faulkner Fatigue.




For me this book was difficult. Particularly, as the author flitted between timelines, especially during the back-stories of Joe Christmas and Rev Hightower all this while he was chucking in confusing stream-of-consciousness (I remember that from As I lay Dying) reflections. It was just plain confusing for me at times, and un-enjoyable. I suppose this is what ‘high-level’ literature is all about, it sorts the wheat out from the chaff. I was ready to pack the whole thing in at around the three-quarter mark.

In some ways this book reminded me of my experiences watching films by David Lynch, I am still convinced some of Lynch’s work is not meant to make sense. Is that the case with Faulkner’s work? Maybe someone who knows more than I do can help answer that.

Overall, bloody hard work, a real slog but kind of worthwhile. I do feel like I’ve run a marathon and am happy I finished it.
