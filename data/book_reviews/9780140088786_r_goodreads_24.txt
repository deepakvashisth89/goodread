a beautifully written, erudite work of history/biography. fox's prose is posh and a little old-fashioned, but i think that works in his favor. his knowledge of the source materials is incredible, and he does a good job of alerting the reader when he enters into uncertain territory. his arguments are nearly always convincing. fox is an admirer of alexander, but lays out his faults as well as his better features -- he doesn't get too close to his subject.the best book on alexander i've read.tidbits:* Foot Companions (sarissa troops) less important than alexander's cavalry.* granicus -- the histories are wrong; alexander crossed the river upstream and came at the persians in their camp.* death. argues against poisoning; discusses both heavy drinking (discounted as the primary cause) and illness.* wonderfully detailed segment on the visit to siwah. fox puts zeus-ammon into a mediterranean and near eastern context, and attempts to get at the reasons for the trip and its importance in later years, both to alexander and to observers.* a nice segment on the Successors, hellenism and the impact of greek culture on the east after alexander's death.* only real gripe is his treatment of alexander's sexuality and relationship with men. fox spends a fair amount of time examining alexander's relationships with women, but glosses over his relationships with hephaistion, bagoas and the like. the book seems a bit dated in this regard.highly recommended.
