This book is at the same time so batshit and so intriguing. Is it true? Is it real? Had I been an adult in the 70s I would have liked to make a trip to see this garden and see if it truly is remarkable. My first impression is that I don't believe that these people literally speak to nature spirits (devas). And yet this is empirically demonstrable. Not that they speak to spirits or not--after all, they themselves maintain it's just internal messages that they receive into their hearts. (Though some have visions of elves etc.) But I'm fascinated by the messages they received. Not the new age stuff. And not even necessarily the stuff about nature and humans being in balance (though that speaks to me). But the details of the questions they asked. How much fertilizer to put on each plant. Where to plant them. What to do with the soil. How to cut or not cut them. How to mix them. Could this be mere intuition? Hallucinations? Maybe it's like IFS and something internal just "speaks" and it's accessing a different Part? To have such marvelous results. I'm just super intrigued that a bunch of humans came together all with similar ideas of spirits and their garden bloomed in a notable way.I read about the fallout of Leverage and particularly her points about research into "woo" things specifically the section "Weird experiments and terminology result in sensational claims and rumorsCrystals? Demons? Seances?"https://cathleensdiscoveries.com/Livi...And that definitely colored how I am reading this book. I'd need to see more empirical results (not just read a book by the people themselves saying "yeah it worked!") but if indeed there was a garden for many years that happened as they say, that's very interesting to me and bears further investigation.I really relate to the yearning for ecological preservation and for being in touch with the Earth's needs and for humans and nature to work together in harmony.
