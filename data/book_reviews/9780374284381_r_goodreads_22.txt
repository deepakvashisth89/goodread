This book makes me so angry because 1) it has no point and 2) it just goes to show the complete objectification of women that Eugenides propagates in each and every one of his novels to date. What could have been a poignant and heart-rending book about 5 sisters who commit suicide is instead boring, overly nostalgic, and sentimental. The collective anonymous narrator did nothing other than obfuscate and make the reader question the truth of any one narrative. The viewpoint of a bunch of obsessed, idealizing teenage boys in no way told us anything about the girls or their viewpoints or teenagers or anything in particular. It was simply pathetic and annoying.What should we take from this? That we can never truly understand our neighbors -- or anyone? That privilege means that we will never help our fellow man? That suicide is selfish and meaningless irregardless? That some women are ideals, nothing more? I don't know, and I think the only way to know is for Eugenides to tell us point blank, but you know what? I'm done reading and I'm done listening. Eugenides now ranks up there as one of my all-time least favorite authors but, hey, at least he can do a first line right.-disgusted-
