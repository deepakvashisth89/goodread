“And I half dreamed and half remembered Mom’s never-ending passion for the sea. We were all on our way to Gram’s summer house in Sakonnet, Rhode Island, in our wooden-slatted ’38 Ford beach wagon. What a car!”

Ever since I discovered Spalding Gray in March of last year via a viewing of ‘Swimming to Cambodia’, I have become obsessed with him. He immediately struck me as such an interesting and unique person and I wanted to ingest every single piece of his work that I could get my hands on.

After ‘Swimming to Cambodia’ I watched ‘Monster in a Box’, followed by two shorter obscure entries I found on YouTube, then ‘Terrors of Pleasure’, which thereafter led me to track down Criterion editions of both ‘Gray’s Anatomy’, and ‘And Everything is Going Fine’. Upon winning both bids for a large sum, I received them and watched them and was happy to find that each movie contained what is lost in their entirety online, two of Spalding’s earliest monologues, ‘A Personal History of the American Theater’, and ‘Sex and Death to the Age 14’. At this point I had felt like I did all I could aside from buying his monologues in their book form. Which I did so for a few of them. I also got a dvd of ‘Swimming to Cambodia’, and then to my surprise a blu-ray release was announced a week later which I then preordered. I also bought ‘Terrors of Pleasure’ on cassette tape and ‘It’s a Slippery Slope’ (which might be my favorite monologue of his?) on CD. Somewhere around here I had found out about his very first staged production entitled ‘Rumstick Road’, a highly personal and autobiographical account of his mother’s suicide (like most of his work). Fortunately my college’s library had a copy of it on dvd! I was amazed and checked it out immediately. However, it isn’t a normal recording of the already avant-garde performance piece. It is a Frankenstein’s monster reproduction of it. It is trying its hardest to reconstruct the original version through different video and audio recordings, slides, and many other techniques. I enjoyed it but not being able to see the true version kind of dampened the experience. It was interesting nonetheless.

Now I was absolutely sure that I had seen or heard just about everything there was from Spalding Gray. Then I remembered his novel which is the centerpiece of ‘Monster in a Box’. I ordered it online and was eager to read it after I finished the book I was currently reading.

I am not a very consistent reader so this took me about a month and a half to finish. Part of me also didn’t want to finish it because I was enjoying it so much and I knew that it would end my deep dive into Spalding Gray. I just finished it today and I thought that it was absolutely incredible. I think it is the best thing he’s ever done. I only wish there was an audio book version of him reading it in its entirety just to hear his delivery, when he pauses, when he gets loud, when he gets quiet. I did find an audio recording of him reading three sections aloud to a live audience and that pretty much scratched the itch. Plus he gave some insight into it which was very nice. He is an absolute master at what he did and he is, I think, one of the greatest storytellers ever. He just grabs you and you are enveloped into the story and he has such a great flow. I thought it was hilarious, endearing and incredibly sad at times. It is so beautifully written and truthful. It is clearly a telling of his life except the characters name isn’t Spalding Gray, which he didn’t hide. He referred to it as “Auto-fiction”. In the audio recording of him reading the book he mentioned that he could write a sequel of Brewster North making it to Bali but that he didn’t want to write it just then as he found writing to be a very isolating activity. I really wish that we got that second book. It is unreal to me that he only ever wrote one book!! I mean I am biased because I am such a big fan of him and I don’t think anyone who doesn’t know who he is would enjoy reading this as much as I did but I think it’s still pretty damn good. There were strangely so many references to water and drowning throughout all of Spalding life and work which is eerie given his death. The first and last lines are absolutely perfect and I teared up at the end. Sad it’s over. Such an amazing guy and I miss him dearly.

Release the 1900 page cut!! Time to watch ‘Monster in a Box’ again!
