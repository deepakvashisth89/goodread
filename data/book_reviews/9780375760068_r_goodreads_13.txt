It is a book in which events unfold according to secrets, promises, jealousies, and love, as well as rivalries, all within the purest romantic tradition. What surprised me was the sequence of situations, which kept the reader from letting go of the thread. It is a construction giving pride of place to the psychological tortures of its main character, Michael Henchard, and the repercussions of a heinous act committed under the influence of drink. Nothing is neglected, whether it is the life of a community, the social classes, or the setting of a village created from scratch. I thought there would be a weakness in the construction, only to realize, once I turned the last page, that everything held together. This work is a psychological, societal, and family novel, and almost a page-turner, with a style that is at once descriptive, controlled, and lively.
