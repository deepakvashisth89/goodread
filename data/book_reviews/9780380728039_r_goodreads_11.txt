The final book in the Henry Huggins series! My son warned me that this one was not his favorite, and as I was reading I quickly realized why. This book is a departure from the series in that most of the story is told through the eyes of Ribsy, not Henry. The storytelling is actually compelling and often emotional (with a lot of Lassie vibes and even one specific reference). I don't usually enjoy stories with an animal main character, preferring their human companions to be the driving force of the story, but by the end I actually found that this was one of the best I've ever read.While reading both this series and the Ramona books, I've found that I prefer Ramona, largely because I can see the growth that Cleary herself went through as a writer. In Henry's stories, I often found Henry to be a little harder to sympathize with. His problems often derive from someone else being difficult and he needing to "suffer through" the situation. While there is a lot of value in learning to be patient and kind when others aren't, Henry has a hard time seeing his own flaws.My favorite in this series was book 3, Henry and Ribsy, and I'm so glad that I read the entire series. There are so many fun moments sprinkled throughout!
