SF at its absolute peak. SF doing what only SF can do - sociologically, politically, ethically, morally.

The kind of SF that is exponentially more relevant today than when it came out almost forty years ago.

Cherryh invented a new form of psychology-sociology for this book, a way of thinking about thinking—of understanding ‘understanding’—that is fundamentally different than our own, and that permeates everything her characters think and do. Reading this book is like reading really good historical fiction set in an era before Freud, or before the Enlightenment. Reading these characters’ thoughts feels foreign, in the way that the future will be foreign and in the way that the past is foreign to us.

And yet, these characters are still *human*, are still fundamentally *people* in that way that no amount of language or science or culture or law can prevent. CIT and PR and azi alike.

This is a book about cloning and about genetically modifying people, and about professionalized, mainstreamed brainwashing. It’s a book about slavery and caste and oppression, in other words, and part of Cherryh’s genius is how that’s never really said out loud, not in any way that, to this society, *matters*. But you feel it bone-deep with every character, on every page.

This is also a book about identity, about personality, about selfhood, and about the effect that sexual abuse and other childhood traumas have on all those things. There are no easy answers. The book just follows these characters, for years and years. Some of the time they grow, or change, and some of the time they don’t.

I do think this book is a masterpiece. The steadiness of it, the intricacy of it, the stakes. This book is about the high-wire act of being a person - and the high-wire act of the person standing next to them, who has the power to guide, to play. To push.

And having written a bunch of sincere things, I’m going to allow myself one petty comment. In the midst of the faux-empowerment literary marketing bullshit of the last several years, how great to read a book involving a powerful, morally ambiguous female character where the moral ambiguity is “she’s the clone of a tech genius CEO billionaire sexual predator, and we don’t know if she’s going to turn out to be a horrifically manipulative rapist like her predecessor” and not, like, “she’s rude at parties.”

You know what I mean?
