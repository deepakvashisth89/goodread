Amanda, a repressed English woman stranded in Port Adelaide, South Australia in the 1860s, is forced by circumstance to take a position as governess to three rowdy children in the Australian outback. The children's father, O'Reilly, is a grandson of an Irish convict on his father's side, and on his mother's side, of a wealthy ex-colonel who fought in the Battle of Waterloo. O'Reilly is running a large sheep station in a remote area, while raising his children alone. His wife walked out years ago, when the youngest child was a baby. Amanda is the next in a sequence of failed governesses who either hated the outback, and/or couldn't deal with O'Reilly's hellion children. But Amanda slowly falls in love - with the outback, with the children, and with the man. And although he's been burnt in the past by women who let him down, O'Reilly, somewhat reluctantly, falls deeply in love with Mandy, and they eventually marry.A familiar trope, (falling in love with the nanny/governess) but a very different and beautifully drawn setting. As an Australian, I really appreciated the setting and historical elements of this book. Ms Proctor describes the place beautifully, and the historical elements rang true. It's mostly set in the Flinders Ranges, north of Adelaide in southern Australia, a rugged, remote, dusty, and beautiful place. I'm lucky enough to have visited the area, and I can well recall its magnificent beauty, the extraordinary quality of the light, and the remnants of the dusty ruins of stone buildings abandoned by the early settlers such as those depicted in this book. The settlers naively lacked any understanding that the district was totally unsuitable for the kind of farming they tried to introduce, and most were driven out by drought and difficulty.So the storyline felt quite fresh to me in this unique setting, and the characters' experiences rang true. I liked Amanda and I enjoyed her slow journey towards loving the outback and its people, alongside her romance with O'Reilly. O'Reilly was well-drawn and likeable as well. He felt very Australian, and his love for his children was admirable. His persistence in the face of incredible challenges was also to be admired.So I enjoyed the read, as I've enjoyed Ms Proctor's other Australian-set historical romances. Sadly she only wrote three, but they are all strong and interesting books. I recommend this one if you like HR, whether you are Australian or not. A quality writer.I reread this book for the Historical Romance Book Club's August 2026 BOTM, Enemies-to-lovers trope, and enjoyed it all over again.
