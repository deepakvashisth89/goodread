The 10th in my re-read of the entire Discworld series and the first about the industrial revolution coming to the Disc (though that is a very loosely connected inner-series serie).One reason why I decided to re-read the series is that I can never just indulge in one of the volumes. The other is that the audiobooks were newly produced and the casts looked amazing.Reading this 10th book (in chronological order) are:This volume has a strange idea taking root in Ankh-Morpork. The alchemist's guild is developing pictures that move and tell a story at a weird old place named Holy Wood Hill. Suddenly, very weird things are being invented. Like this mysterious snack that tastes of cardbord and is called banged corn. Surely, the (disc-)world has gone mad!And how could it not what with all those poor tiny demons being ensaved to produce clicks like that. Not to mention that this means the beginning of the industrial revolution.Best of all, an almost entirely new cast (excuse the pun) of characters (such as Gaspode, the talking dog) met some of the best old ones (DEATH, the Watch). First and foremost, Gaspode became one of my all-time favorites after this book. However, I also adored the quirky and overdrawn look at the movie industry and everything that entails - from all manner of unlogical "food" to advertising!Weirdly, while I do enjoy CMOT Dibbler, he never became a favorite of mine. Mostly, I always wanted to see him get his. *chuckles*So many classic movies get made fun of in here but in the best and most erudite of ways, perfectly holding up the mirror about the silliness we humans get up to on a regular basis (I marked plenty of quotes in case you need proof). Looking at the writer's strike we had only recently, all that commentary on how the industry treats actors/writers/crew hit especially hard. Typical Terry.Rounding off this romp was Jason Isaacs (yes, him!) as the narrator of this new audiobook version and he was absolutely nailing it! :D Such a fantastic entry in the series, made all the better by this rendition.
