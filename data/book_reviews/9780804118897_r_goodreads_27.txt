One of the weakest Dexter novels. Extremely derivative, in that a great debt is owed to Josephine Tey - I wonder if royalties were paid to the estate of Elizabeth MacKintosh.It is almost a self-parody in the regular use of Dexter's literary fixations. He delights in a pastiche of literary styles which has become a self indulgence that annoys me.And finally, for a crossword buff, he uses the most obvious plot device. One has to question whether any murderer has ever self identified with an anagram. The early identification removed much of the pleasure in reading
