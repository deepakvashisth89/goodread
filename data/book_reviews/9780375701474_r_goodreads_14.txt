What to say, what to say... It started out great and progressively depreciated.This is one of the top suicide nonfiction books on Goodreads, despite being so outdated. This is telling of how little is published about suicide as a topic. Against the backdrop of a stream of positive psychology and self help bestsellers, I understand that this topic doesn't call to a wide demographic. But still, there should be clear, well researched books on this topic, available to the public. There are academic papers on suicide and depression, so the research on this is alive an well and one could write a pop science book about it. Maybe this is better (I'm not sure because I haven't read it yet):Suicidal: Why We Kill OurselvesProsWhen I started reading I enjoyed the statistics on different facets of suicide, like the average length of a suicide note, how suicidal people often see a doctor in the month before their suicide, that summer is the season when numbers of suicides peaks. The documentation of people's suicidal journals and suicide notes was one of the strengths of the book (to see the insider view of suicide and not the outsider medicalization of it.) There was a study in which students were instructed to write "fake suicide notes" and when compared to real suicide notes, it was found that fake ones are more poetic, descriptive and emotional. Real suicide notes are shorter, straight to the point, often concerned with practical matters like what to do with one's belongings or sorry for the mess.ConsThe general statistics are very interesting, but there are not many deep dives in to the process as it occurs. No exploration into which symptoms start first, which circumstances and mental states are associated with suicide. I remember reading somewhere on a website that the emotions most often reported by people before suicide are: feeling isolated, hopeless, feeling trapped and feeling like a burden. The book didn't look into this, though it seems that this is what is most important to know. If one of your friends is suicidal, you probably would want to notice how they describe their feelings about life.The final chapters were the most disappointing: under-researched, highly opinion-based and totally outdated. It claims that there are no long lasting effects of a parent's suicide on a child, when most recent trauma research shows that death of a parent during childhood ishighly correlatedwith depression in adulthood. This book is old, so the research is outdated. Now we know that death of parents (including suicide) affects people in long lasting ways.The final chapter just keep repeating that suicidal people cannot keep their pain to themselves, so it reaches their friends and families. The author admits that she herself was suicidal once, and unfortunately it seems that this colored the final chapters, making it very opinion based and judgmental. There is little said about the people who commit suicide without sharing their pain with others, i.e the people who show "no signs" before their suicide (I use quote marks because I think often there are signs and people miss them). The author seems to be projecting her own guilt-ridden experiences onto all suicidal people.However the biggest weakness is the author's inability to acknowledge the complexity of the factors associated with suicide. This book treats the disease model of suicide as if it is the only model (it's not). The repeated message is, "suicide is a result of mental disease. Medicate the disease and prevent the suicide." Suicide is far more complex than this message suggests, so by the end of the book I was surprised at how little this book really dived into the causes and circumstances of suicide (for instance: genetic underpinnings, the stress model of suicide, physical health, aging, psychosocial factors like poverty/social status/religion/stigmatized minorities, the role of trauma, the role of social support).There is a blurb about how when the media reports on suicide, it is often followed by a wave of copy-cat suicides, as if people get an extra burst of motivation when they see that someone has done what they wish to do. Similarly, when teenagers read about the symptoms of suicide and realize they identify with them, this may have a backfire effect, because they see it as confirmation that people in their position have no way out. The mention of these phenomena suggest that a suicidal mindset is not just a disease that needs medication. Clearly psychosocial and cognitive factors are part of it."Understanding Suicide" is right in the title, but the book did not deliver.TLDR:Strengths: Interesting statistics, insider accounts of the experience.Weaknesses: Considers only the disease model of suicide, not very nuanced or very deep exploration, some parts are colored with personal opinion rather than research.
