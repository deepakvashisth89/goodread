This book gave me, simultaneously, too much and not enough information. The main bulk of the work was split into long chapters about specific medieval women and the events of their lives. I know all about these women now - and many sections were interesting and exciting to read. But I think I was given too much about them to work with. It seemed like billions of names of their contemporaries were thrown at me, and then brought up 30 pages later as if I was assumed to remember exactly what the man had done, said, and eaten for breakfast in 1397. Most of the time his name was John. Literally everyone, their mothers, and their mother's dogs were named John back then.
I understand that the goal was to depict real women of different medieval social/economic classes as sorts of examples for understanding how women in similar situations lived. I also understand that records were not kept very well by some in the Middle Ages because they could not read, or write, or care to procure ink and write stuff down. I get that. But I think the balance between biographies // information about women whose records were not so taken down was severely weighed down towards the former, and this was tiresome and disappointing.

Still - it was easy to read, entertaining, and oftentimes made me laugh out loud (the authors were strangely funny, and make frequent snide remarks towards medieval people who behaved foolishly, hypocritically, etc.). I would honestly approach this as a beach/fun/leisure read rather than a real academic work.
