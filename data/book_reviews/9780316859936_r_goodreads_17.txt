This is my fourth Linda Grant novel, and I've loved them all. Still Here is no exception, though not what I expected. Reading the synopsis leads one to believe that this is a romance novel showcasing a middle-aged Jewish man and woman who find one another in Liverpool. They secure a second chance at love, wind through their respective secrets and live happily ever after. It is much more complex and nuanced. It is also about being a woman growing older and becoming, or at least feeling, invisible. Ms. Grant poses questions without asking them. Who wins the battle? The surgeon's knife or the feminist mind who continues to pass a mirror and wonders who she sees? She separately questions whether evil can be independent of intent. She weaves this question into the theme of being Jewish and surviving the Holocaust. She asks whether Jews can "distinguish between self defense and aggressive indifference to the suffering of others." The book explores ironies - the irony of escaping the Viet Nam War by fleeing to Israel only to be swallowed by the Yom Kippur War and haunted forever, yet bearing this angst in secret. "Was shot again at work today. I guess only bank tellers and airline pilots can make that claim today."
