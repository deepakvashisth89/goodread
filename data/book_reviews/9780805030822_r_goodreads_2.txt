The best book I've read about the devil because it has no particular pitchfork to grind. This is a straightforward account of the Western notion of a spirit of evil, tracing the influence of Middle Eastern cultures on the core theologies that Europe and America were built on, through doctrine, Dante, Milton, to the age of the Satanic panic. It's clearly written and agnostic in its approach, except for a few places where the nonsense is too much for the author to take seriously and he can't resist pointing it out.

What I came away with was not the author's thesis but the upshot seems clear to me. The devil is mostly invoked by people with something to sell: salvation, canned ham, theater tickets, etc. He's useful for all kinds of monetary endeavors and any lies or cheating can be forgiven when practiced under the mantle of faith. The author is British and I think he underestimates the degree of gullibility of American fundamentalists and the venal sleaze of their preachers, the ease with which the adversary can be invoked in a milieu where knowledge is considered wicked, and where heaven is a commodity peddled by the real devils among us.
