This is a Roderick Alleyn book in which we never see Alleyn. Or do we? At any rate, it's not one to recommend to those who want to see more of the series detective. Still, it's always been on of my favorites.The Clair family owns a natural sulphur mud springs spa in New Zealand, set right against sacred Maori burial grounds. How's that for a unique setting? Taking place during WWII, the destruction of several Naval vessels in the harbor have them suspecting a possible spy within their midst. And, due to a loan being called in they are about to lose the spa, which makes for an even more strained atmosphere. So when a famous Shakespearean actor and his secretary check in, there's plenty to keep them occupied.There are two things I really like about this book. First is the setting, which Marsh does well as she is a native of New Zealand. She writes with authority about the relationship between the Maori, whites and visitors, and describes the countryside well. Also, I enjoy Barbara and Dikon. I love that Dikon doesn't really understand his feelings about Barbara, and that she's not a your typical heroine, but they are both decent people who develop feelings for each other. The added bonus is occasional bits from Shakespeare and discussions about his plays. It's a slightly weird but very enjoyable mystery novel.NB - I tried listening to this, as I've enjoyed other audiobooks in this series, but it felt so slow I eventually switched to ebook. And that was with it sped up, which I generally don't do! So be warned, this is an interesting, quirky story, but not fast moving.
