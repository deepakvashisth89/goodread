I just read three Catfish Bend novels in an anthology, the only Catfish Bend books available in my state-wide library system.When I was 8 or 9 years old, I read this one, borrowed from the paperback carousel at my local library. I thought it was great. It had intrigue, tension, and danger, and it all worked out in the end. I remember borrowing it at least one other time, and it stuck in my head for decades.Thirty years later, I was shocked to discover the book is only about 120 pages long and much more folksy than tense. It's certainly not the psychological thriller of my memory. Nevertheless, along with simplistic characterization and neat, swift solutions to seemingly overwhelming difficulties, these novels have a pleasant, fast-reading tone, light humor, and good morals. I can't really recommend these to adults, but by all means, if you are 8 or 9, you might just love these.(If you do happen to take a break from reading Faulkner to read Burman, be prepared for some really weird dreams.)
