I picked up this 1985 book hoping I could recommend it to my nephew. It's written with an interest level for 12-year-old boys (as the main character is a young teen boy himself), but the reading level (and the small print in my paperback copy) made me think that not many modern 12-year-old boys would be interested. This book is actually the third and fourth story about the main character and his trusty robot Norby, so some of my initial difficulties picking up on the plot at the beginning could be related to the fact that I skipped the first book. However, I found that the stories quickly developed a pattern: the grown-ups weren't as clever as the kids, and were more prone to a violent "solution" to a problem. When the kids (usually the main character and a child version of whatever alien species he's discovered) are left to their own devices, they find out that everyone just wants to be friends. While I might have enjoyed this as the resolution for one plot, it got a bit tired when I realized it was the resolution to EVERY plot.I'll be passing this book on, and looking for more titles to give my nephew.
