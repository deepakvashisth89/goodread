This Modern Library edition has A Christmas Carol (1843), Chimes (1844), and The Haunted Man and the Ghost's Bargain (1848).§ A Christmas CarolIt was not the first time I read A Christmas Carol. It felt meh back then. This time, it's a keeper.You see, A Christmas Carol has this reputation among econ types that it seems to be an attack on capitalism. Perhaps Dickens had this in mind, but I can find a better reading. Being pro-market does not preclude you from being charitable. It just so happened that Scrooge is this miserable being, who needs some realisations.A Christmas Carol fails as a critique of capitalism but succeeds in being life-changing. For one, Scrooge did not stop being a capitalist after his conversion. He merely became more charitable. Also, what made him such a twat is on his backstory.Anyway, what helped me really appreciate this story is the free Hillsdale College Online Course:https://online.hillsdale.edu/courses/...It helped me see important details I failed to notice.If Frieren is seen as a life-changing anime, I believe it draws from the same well as A Christmas Carol almost 300 years ago. Both have Time as an important component.§ The ChimesThe Chimes seems similar to the gimmick in A Christmas Carol. The poor bloke here is Toby. Even if he is not wealthy as Scrooge, he looks down on his fellow working class people as incapable of change.Seeing A Christmas Carol in the context with Chimes, it appears that Charles Dickens attacked unhealthy attitudes and not being charitable, regardless of class.§ The Haunted ManThis one is difficult to read, and I had to read the summary while reading.It's about a chemist, named Redlaw, who has so many regrets and wishes for them to be removed. He meets a ghost capable of doing so, and it also allows him to grant this forgetfulness to others. It follows the trope of King Midas. In both situations, there are unintended consequences.The moral is that even sorrow can be a gift, something we have to keep in mind during Christmas. It's a counterpoint to other stories in this selection as there is a sense of memento mori here.
