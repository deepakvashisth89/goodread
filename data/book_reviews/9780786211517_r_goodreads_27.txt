It is with a heavy heart that I'm writing this review. I have wanted to readThe English Patientfor a very long time, and given that it won the Booker Prize, my expectation was set high. Besides, Ondaatje's roots run to my country and I entertain a kind of loyalty towards the author. However, one must be honest, and I'll try my best to justify why I couldn't be part of the popular view.Ondaatje's writing is so beautiful, almost poetic. That is his forte. And this is the reason behind my endurance. I read it just to indulge myself in the beauty of his phrases, metaphors, and symbolism. The story was lost on me. I didn't understand what Ondaatje was driving at. Nor did I care after the initial struggle. I was groping in the darkness and lost interest midway. The thin light of elucidation towards the end was insufficient to rouse me from my indifference.The story switched between past and present, between characters, and between thoughts and the narrative in rapid succession that I was unabale to properly invest myself either in the characters or their stories. I enjoyed some of the backstories but I can't say I cared much about the story proper. It was boring and repetitive.I didn't care about the four occupants at the villa either. I even disliked the "English patient" until towards the end. I might have felt some connection with Hana had her character been explored more. But the author kept her aloof and mysterious. I felt a little sympathy for Kip but his action at the end felt out of character. The only solid character was the desert which constantly marked its presence symbolizing Ondaatje's concepts of no nations, liberty, and freedom.My overall conclusion onThe English Patient, having taken into consideration my reading experience, is that perhaps it is the kind of story that is better on screen than on paper.More of my reviews can be found athttp://piyangiejay.com/
