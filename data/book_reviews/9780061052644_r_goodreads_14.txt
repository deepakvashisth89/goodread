Upper 3 stars. High 3 stars.Just not quite a 4.I thought this would be another high-tech escapade, but it was more about humanity; traditionalism vs technological progress, or even spiritual progress. Evolving as a species, not physically, but mentally. Breaking out of old moulds and embracing the new, better. The scope of the story started with some grandiose, potentially all-universe encompassing matter, but didn’t quite end up there (for a good reason).Which is fine really. Just the back of the book could have been a bit more honest, or modest.The world building was extensive. Like, 270-pages extensive… maybe that was a bit too extensive. It took its time getting “there”, to the meaty bits, and when it did.. it was ok.The ending was good, but this read more like a Neal Stephenson book that actually concluded properly, and didn’t fizzle out halfway through the story.Not to mention,thatthing which makes an appearance towards the end of the book…”What thing?”Oh, nothing… just the FOUR METER TALL MANTIS-LIKE KILLER ROBOT WITH STEALTH TECHNOLOGY.Yeah… just that.I was like “so, um… book. Are we gonna talk about that?”Book: “nah”We’re not gonna talk about the 4 meter tall mantis-like killer robot with stealth technology reminiscent of the Predator?Book: “..nah”.It’s like a mechanical Predator, complete with cloaking tech, but mantis-shaped and just comes out of no where.. maybe we should discuss this?Book: “nah. Too busy doing a Childhood’s End thing here”So yeah, solid story taking a good look at humanity as it progresses deeper and deeper into a tech-filled world amidst still very traditionally-bound people.Solid 4-meter-tall-killer-robot-mantis/10
