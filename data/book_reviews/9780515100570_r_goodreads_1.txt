I pick this up because of the clever title, but I almost gave up after about 70 pages because as Linda Amos wrote below this is indeed, "a relatively simple whodunnit." Early on I had an idea who committed the crime, or at least one of two people who very probably did it.

I didn't care for the author's style. For example, in the middle of a chapter, after dialog, scene descriptions, etc., there would be a page break where new dialog would begin between people downstairs or in another neighborhood. Yet those people are not identifiable until after several sentences. (Who and where are these new people?) Once or twice would be an irritant, but the author created those jumps often, sometimes several times on the same page.

But I did enjoy the character descriptions and the twist in the final chapter. I'm glad I was able to persevere through this book, but I will not read it again nor can I recommend it.
