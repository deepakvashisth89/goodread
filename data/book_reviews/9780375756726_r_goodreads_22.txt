I had picked this book up to do my book review on, back when I was in middle grade. I think this was my very first classic that I had read and I don’t reread books a lot, but I had read this thrice. Little Women is a timeless classic that follows the lives of the four March sisters- Meg, Jo, Beth and Amy- as they grow up in New England during the Civil War. The narrative is intimate. It captures the joys, sorrows and moral lessons of adolescence and womanhood.

At its heart, the novel is a celebration of familial love. The sisters’ quarrels, reconciliations and shared dreams create an everyday intimacy.

Jo March is someone whose soul I had possessed while reading the book. And it is believed to be Alcott’s character version in the novel. She embodies the struggle between societal expectations and personal ambition. Her literary aspirations and refusal to conform make her one of literature’s best and most enduring feminist figures.

Poverty, illness and loss are woven beautifully into the narrative. But the tone remains hopeful. Here, growth is very much emphasised through adversity showing how hardship shapes character.

The writing style was warm, accessible and deeply engaging. The pacing was well-balanced. The story never feels preachy- it balances moral lessons with humour and drama equally.

I suddenly remembered that I had not reviewed this book which was my very first classic. It is not just a coming-of-age tale but a mirror of human imperfection, resilience and love. I love how Alcott made the ordinary extraordinary, turning domestic life into a stage where triumph and tragedy both are shown with equal grace.
