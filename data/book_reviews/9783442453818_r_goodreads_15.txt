“Da ist auch noch ein anderer Geruch in der Luft, der Geruch von Feuern, die in der Ferne brennen, mit einem Hauch Zimt darin - so riecht das Abenteuer!”

Einfach bezaubernd! Die Lebensgeschichten bzw. die ersten 13,5 Jahre von Blaubär sind sehr humorvoll von Blaubär selbst dargestellt, wobei man oftmals davon ausgehen kann, dass es sich um eine etwas unzuverlässige und zu übertriebene Selbstdarstellung handelt.
Besonders die Art und Weise wie Moers mit der Sprache und Intertextualität spielt, fällt sofort ins Auge und macht den Hauptwert des Werks aus. Die einzelnen Leben und die Übergänge sind sehr kreativ ausgestaltet und absolut unerwartet. Der Humor zielt sowohl auf Kinder als auch auf Erwachsene ab, wobei Kinder Vieles nicht als humorvolle Anspielung erkennen können.

English version:

"There's another smell in the air too, the smell of fires burning in the distance with a hint of cinnamon in it - that's what adventure smells like!" (translated by myself)

Simply enchanting! The stories of Bluebear's life, or rather his first 13.5 years, are very humorously portrayed by Bluebear himself, although it can often be assumed that this is a rather unreliable and overly exaggerated self-portrayal.

The way in which Moers plays with language and intertextuality is particularly remarkable and is the main value of the work. The individual lives and the changes to a new life are very creatively designed and completely unexpected. The humor is aimed at both children and adults, although children may not recognize many of the humorous allusions.

About the content and the chapters/lives:

1: My life as a dwarf pirate (translated by myself)
2: My life with the Klabauter ghosts (translated by myself)
3: My life on the run (translated by myself)
4: My life on the gourmet island (translated by myself)
5: My life as a navigator (translated by myself)
6: My life in the Dark Mountains (translated by myself)

7: My life in the Great Forest (translated by myself)
"The Great Forest owes its somewhat unimaginative name to the fact that nobody likes to deal with it, not even in thought" (p. 211; translated by myself)
"But there was not only a monstrosity of a sound, but also the sound of a monstrosity." (S. 233; translated by myself)

I found the passage with the reflections on the amount of dumplings you can eat without attracting attention and the subsequent revelations particularly amusing and surprising. The chapter really is "woven genius", but not of the spider, but of the author.

8: My life in the dimensional hole (translated by myself)
"Then it fell in on me, and I fell headlong into the dimensional hole myself." (S. 252; translated by myself)

9: My life in the Sweet Desert (translated by myself)
10: My life in the tornado city (translated by myself)

11: My life in the Big Head (translated by myself)
"There is a discreetly criminal feeling associated with entering a head through an ear." (S. 407; translated by myself)

12: My life in Atlantis (translated by myself)
13: My life on the Moloch (translated by myself)
13.5: Half my life in peace (translated by myself)

My favorite chapters are definitely "My Life in the Great Forest" (7) and "My Life in the Great Head" (11). I really love this book, but the Atlantis chapter was really not my cup of tea and also unnecessarily long! The encyclopedia is one of the great features of this first Zamonien novel because it takes up a lot of space and is particularly amusing as the explanations allude to our science of the real world but twist it in such an absurd way that you can't help but at least smile at an encyclopedia article.

Overall: 5.0.
