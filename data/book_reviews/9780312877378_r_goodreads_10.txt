I generally enjoyed the story. It's fascinating to read a fictional story about a tremendously popular historical personage. I find myself wondering if what I am reading is documented in history, or just part of the fictional packaging. The author provided a fairly extensive bibliography.The hero (the great Finn Mac Cool) is rather an anti-climactic and a-historic personality in the story. He was clearly a gifted, charismatic and exceptional man - and yet the end of his life is so disappointing for someone of his potential. Moreover - the story is interwoven with both mythical elements (visions, magical wife)as well as a clear skepticism as part of the plot which causes the telling of the story to feel neither true fiction (because of what feels like a fear to unapologeticcally present the fantastic) nor history (because the story teases with the possibility that maybe part of it may be true).I'm glad I read it, but can't ever imagine being able to make myself read it again.
