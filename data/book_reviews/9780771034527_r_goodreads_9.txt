The Jewish Canadian writer A.M. Klein went silent in 1955 at the height of his career. For reasons no one can explain, he hardly wrote a letter for the next 17 years. Whatever seized hold of him those final years, another demon had gripped him for the previous twenty, when poems, plays, essays, and fiction spilled from his pen.Indeed, his masterpiece,The Second Scroll, rises to a pitch of intensity wholly characteristic of Klein but also perfectly attuned to his subject: a post-Holocaust odyssey of one man in search of his uncle, a survivor. In less than a hundred pages (plus extended glosses), the protagonists trip through half the world and every significant possibility open for the future of the Jewish people.Who knows? Maybe in this novel Klein exhausted everything he had to say. Certainly, seven decades later, we have not exhausted all it has to say to us.
