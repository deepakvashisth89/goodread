The first part of this - the romance between Judy and Malcolm - was great; both characters are interesting and clearly-defined and their banter is fantastic. When the marriage argument comes up, however, the book kind of lost me - Malcolm's digging his heels in for no good reason, and when he finally agrees to a wedding it just gets... awkward, because it's presented as "he'll do it because he loves her so much," and then he's told "no, it can't be a registry office, it has to be a big church wedding" and, like, is he really so wrong to not be super into weddings? His perspective is presented as The Wrong One and he has to completely renounce it in favor of Whatever You Want Darling, which doesn't strike me as a particularly good foundation for a long-term relationship. But what do I know, I got married after work at City Hall to a dude I already owned a house with.
