Oh I do love this book. This is about my third reading, I think, and every time I see something or learn something new in it. This time, I learned that while writing this book, while it was being reviewed and shortlisted for the Booker Prize, Angela Carter (but not the wider world of publishing) knew she had terminal lung cancer. Which either explains why, or makes it more surprising - I'm not sure which - that this is her most joyous, her most accessible and her most successful work.It's almost a literary equivalent of David Bowie's Blackstar album.The work itself takes the them of Shakespeare and runs with it, giving us a world of the stage - the legitimate grand theatre and the illegitimate music hall - of twins (lots of twins) both legitimate and illegitimate (and legitimate twins who turn out to be illegitimate) - of loves lost and found, of loves found and lost - of birthdays - everyone seems to share Shakespeare's birthday - of mistaken identity, of foundlings, of London and of dreams.The story is told by Dora Chance - illegitimate daughter of Melchior Hazard (Chance / Hazard - just one of so many reflections) beginning on the morning of her 75th and Melchior's 100th birthdays and there follows a potted history of all strands of the Hazard dynasty through the traditional 5 Shakespearean Acts / Chapters, culminating in a uproarious and cathartic birthday party where - in great Bard tradition, secrets are revealed and loves are declared.It's a wonderful tying up of Carter's illustrious, but often obscure and controversial, literary career and very much worth a read.
