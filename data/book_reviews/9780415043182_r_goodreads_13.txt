This volume contains much of Popper's more approachable material in the form of lectures and writings intended for a more general audience. While good for clarity, this does mean a lot of repetition and unnecessarily inexact prose.The best part of this volume - though helplessly outdated and falsified by further research in probability theory and mathematical logic - are the addenda. The worst are easily the parts which have anything to do with the history of philosophy, which he butchers even when viewing it in a favourable light.
