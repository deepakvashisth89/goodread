Spent a lot of time with Lessing's 'Children of Violence' series in 2024-25, but Landlocked stands out in the bunch so far and is the only one I'm likely to reread anytime soon (The Four-Gated City is on my shelf forsoon). Some crushing passages about WWII that I'll be thinking about for a long time. All the characters and themes that Lessing has been working with for tens of thousands of words coalesce with this, and a lot of the irritations of the other books have worked themselves out. The bildungsroman process of "Martha Quest" (Book 1) is now nothing more than reminiscences/echoes. The marital issues and appalling parenting skills of "A Proper Marriage" (II) are behind us, and we finally get some unsettling and surreal scenes with Martha and her daughter. The absurdity and tedium of the Communist study groups from "A Ripple in the Storm" (III) are collapsing in on themselves. There's tremendous sadness, wistfulness, beauty, and meaningless cruelty aplenty. I still basically hate Martha as a person, even though I understand parts of her, and I doubt I would have liked Lessing much either. But it's just such...damn...good...writing.
