Final Trace is a competently-written thriller. While the plot was nothing new or innovative, I enjoyed it all the same. It follows David Rosen, an Auschwitz survivor turned nazi hunter, in his quest to get revenge on Mengele- the man who experimented on him as a child. I just want to say that I LOVE David, and if he weren't a fictional character I would totally marry him. He's wonderfully snarky and is an all-around badass. What more could a girl want?

Moving on...The plot is fairly predictable and hits most of the usual notes. But despite this, I really enjoyed it. Towards the end, there's a big plot twist about an international cabal that controls the world and orchestrated the holocaust- and seemingly every other modern conflict. I actually didn't see this coming, and wish it had been developed more. Instead, it's just kind of thrown in there for shock value. It was kind of amusing to see references to Martin Bormann and Heinrich Muller, since it's now known they died in 1945. But this book was written back in the early 80s, so there's no way the author could have known that.

Boylan's portrayal of Mengele- the charming, sophisticated mass murderer- is absolutely chilling. I wish there had been a big confrontation between him and David during the climax. Unfortunately, there isn't. The climax started out strong but turned out really unsatisfying. David ends up dead, and the nazis escape. Their plane is blown up, but they manage to parachute away- evading justice yet again. Even if it's fiction, I got annoyed that after everything the villains went unpunished. Then again, Mengele would die a free man in 1985, so maybe it's a case of art imitating life.

International nazi thrillers are kind of a dime a dozen. While it doesn't do anything new or exciting to the subject, Final Trace is better-written than most. It had good writing and a very likable protagonist going for it. The ending wasn't that great though, which kind of lessened the experience for me.
