I read this almost 20 years ago, but I still remember it was a gorgeous novel, primarily to be seen as a dramatic love story. It is beautifully set in 19th century rural England, in full change, for instance with the introduction of machines on the fields. In contrast with other Hardy novels, the character of protagonist Tess is very credibly worked out, but in a modest way, almost 'sotto voce'. There definitely is a naturalistic accent in the social stigma Tess carries, the genetic legacy and the theme of fate, but it's not overdone, as is the case in his other novels. However, the character of Angel is intriguing, but not quite credible: his social nonconformism clashes with his repudation of Tess for social reasons. And the end of the novel in my view was quite implausible. But this absolutely is my favorite Hardy.
(rating 3.5 stars)
