My First Stephen King Read!! 👏👏👏

I know! I know! I cannot pledge myself completely as a horror lover if I don’t indulge in some of Stephen King’s books. So, this is me getting my feet wet. Though I’ve experienced many of his film adaptations (see below), I have yet to indulge in his literary work. I chose The Body as my entry book because it is lighter and because I remember seeing bits and pieces of the film adaptation Stand By Me and really liking it.

Stephen King Films I’ve watched as a child: (Lol, what? My mom is somewhat of a horror fanatic. Yes, I realize I might have been a little traumatized.)

I’ve seen the early 70s/80s/90s versions of It**, Carrie, Pet Sematary, and Children of the Corn.

**”It” terrified me so bad as a child that I still can’t pass by a sewer drain without being reminded of it. 😂 🤡

I’ve also seen: Misery, Green Mile, Cujo, Christine, and Mr. Harrigan’s Phone

So, The Body is a coming-of-age novella that takes place in 1960 amongst four friends. It’s humorous and adventurous, all of what you would expect from 12 year old boys bantering and causing mischief. I quickly became invested in this story. I can definitely understand the obsession with Stephen King’s writing. I have fallen under his spell. Become one of his followers. His words are like keys to a time machine. They effortlessly swipe away your current reality and pull you back into another time. This book was dripping with nostalgia of old town charm and the pleasantries of 1960. It brought back some memories of my own childhood – riding bikes and thriving on imagination. There is mention of using a playing card against the spoke of a bicycle that instantly brought back times to where I used to do something similar to my bicycle. I used playing cards and a crushed up soda can to make it sound like a motorcycle. It was even cooler when I got a portable CD player to listen to all my latest 90s hits while riding around the neighborhood (that is until you would run over a pothole and the CD skipped 😂🤭Then I’d miss my cassette players!)

But this story was even more than just capturing the essence of nostalgia. It was wholly morbid at times and an intricate exploration into one’s own inner monsters and mortality itself. I really loved the adventure aspect and the friendships. The leech scene will always be unforgettable! I even managed to catch an Easter egg! There is a little boy wearing a yellow plastic raincoat stepping in puddles mentioned. Sounds like a reference to “It” to me! I love when movies and books drop Easter eggs. It’s so much fun finding them!

Well, Stephen King you have another literary fan! I’ve already added a few more of his books to my list that I’d like to pursue next and looking forward to more horror and fun! 🎉
