I read Robert Heinlein's 1959 sci-fi novel "Starship Troopers" at some point in high school or college, I can't remember. I remember liking the book, although I felt that Heinlein was espousing a hard-line pro-militaristic philosophy that I vehemently disagreed with.



Indeed, considered one of Heinlein's more controversial novels, "Troopers" extrapolates a futuristic war with an intelligent species of insect-like creatures. I'm pretty sure the controversial part is that humanity, in the future, doesn't even stop to consider the reasons why the alien species are hostile in the first place. They are just bugs to kill to the point of extinction. In the future, war isn't hell anymore. It's just a 9-to-5 job.



The 1997 film adaptation was a clever parody of Heinlein's novel, subverting a lot of Heinlein's views by intentionally making fun of the blatant jingoism in the novel.



Despite one's views on the military, there is a lot of fascinating philosophy on warfare and institutional violence in this, which elevates it to a sci-fi classic.
