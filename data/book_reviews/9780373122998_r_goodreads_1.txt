This story finished out stronger than the opening, but I still felt that some deeper emotions were missing. So 2.5 rounded up to 3.0The opening:22 year-old hero wants 19 year-old heroine to give up her place in university and marry him. He has one year left of university and once he starts earning she can go to school. Heroine wants to go to school as planned and not alienate her parents They can have weekends together. Hero takes off in a snit.So first editorial:Hell to the no, hero. Heroine was right to not marry at 19. Talk about expectations and objectifying. He wanted his sex doll nearby and couldn't see that she was also entitled to an education and to create a life for herself. The author tried to make the parents out as bigots because he was from Argentina and they were English land owners, but I could totally see their point. Heroine was a sheltered 19 year-old who had no business getting married.Fast forward 7 years. Tycoon hero reads a small notice in the paper that the heroine's family land, house and stables are for sale because of debt. Hero decides he'll look up the heroine by putting in an offer. Then he cooks up a plan for revenge - he'll buy the heroine's house, he'll seduce the heroine and then dump her like she dumped him.And he does just that. The problem is that the heroine is more philosophical then anguished about selling her family home. She never got to university because her mother was ill, then she had to take care of her father until he died and everything has gone to ruin. She knows the hero is trying to humiliate her by buying everything and then making her his employee - but she's so dazzled by his hotness that she spends very little time angsting.Why aren't you angsting, heroine? This is not normal behavior.Hero begins to have pangs of conscience. They have sex every night/day - while the heroine is in his employ. Her job? Do what he tells her to do. In this case, begin renovations on the house. Heroine harps on the idea she is just an employee a lot and it really made their relationship sound sleazy. This was written 15 years ago - so maybe the #metoo is influencing me, but gross.His cousin confronts the heroine and tells her not to fall in love with the hero since she knows he's only seducing her out of pride and revenge.Heroine says she's not in love with the hero - hero over hears that part and tells her that he will never marry her, etc . .Finally, some angst!Heroine is devastated for 24 hours and then decides to take a pregnancy test. Hero's cousin call her to tell her that hero does love her, but will never admit it. It's up to the heroine to make the first move.Heroine does make the move, she tells the hero she loves him and then asks him to marry her. Hero says yes and confesses that he messed up 7 years ago because of pride and was going to mess up again because of pride. HEASecond editorial:I hate when the heroine has to go crawling to the hero - especially when hero is the one who messed up. He cooked up the revenge plan. She was stupid enough to follow her hormones, but she shouldn't have had to say ILY first.Honestly, the only strong thing the heroine did in this story was stick to her guns about going to university. I hated that the author made it all for naught. I don't know what the heroine is going to do with herself beyond the happy ending besides taking care of baby and second baby, the hero. So many loose threads. So much compromise for the entitled hero. *sigh*Heroine had one sexual partner during their separation. Hero had many.
