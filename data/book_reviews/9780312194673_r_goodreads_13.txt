I have worked through most of Huxley in the last few years, and this one is a bit of an outlier, written as a treatment for a film or TV show. I think I recall that Huxley spent time in LA doing movie stuff.

It is an engaging tale of a simple healer, who starts with animals and moves to humans, with lovely descriptions of the Mojave desert in the 1940s or 50s. He has a gift, though not one that modern medicine would credit, as it involves little more than laying on hands and perhaps, with the humans, a little discussion. Being a simple man, he is taken advantage by folks high and low, but he is largely indifferent to his circumstances and so doesn't really notice any of this.

So I thought it was just a parable, with fairly credible characters. Who would believe that a young woman on crutches and crippled all her life would walk and run and frolic within a day of Jacob's hands on her feet? Then at the end of the audio book came a short interview with a woman who knew Huxley well, perhaps his wife, who said that he was fascinated with alternative medicine and miracle cures. I don't know--it seems hard to believe that Huxley wanted us to believe in the powers of the hands of Jacob.

In the end, the human stories are quite credible which make this short book worthwhile.
