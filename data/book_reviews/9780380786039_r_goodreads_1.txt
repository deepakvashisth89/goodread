Cotannah Chisk-Ko, the woman whose "heart" was broken in the previous book of this series when the man she loved, Tay, chose her best friend Emily instead, has grown into quite the flirt. It's been 2 years since then and she now relishes the idea of making men dance to her tune...inciting them, getting them to fight over her and then breaking their hearts. As a result, her brother Cade sends her off to the Choctaw Nation to grow up. There she meets Walks-With-Spirits, analikichi, or medicine man, and one who is quite out of his time - he's in touch with the ancient traditions at a time that the Nation seems to be progressing out of them. He has the Disney Princess touch, communes with animals, speaks with them, heals them (and people too) and more importantly, he is the only man who doesn't show Cotannah that he's enamored by her (even though he is). This makes him all the more interesting to her and so she tries to make him jealous by flirting with other men. She bites off a bit more than she can chew when Jacob Charley tries to rape her, but Walks-With-Spirits rescues her. In his anger, he speaks a death incantation. So when Jacob Charley ends up dead, the Nation believes that Walks-With-Spirits is responsible. Cotannah begins to realize how her selfishness affects people and now may cause the death of an innocent man...so she sets out to prove him innocent and to show him that she can love unselfishly.That's basically the set up here. Cotannah is a spoiled, selfish little girl who wants to exercise her power over men. She wants to use them so they can't use her. I definitely got the impression that she was not a virgin...supported by her frequent claims that she'd never made love to a man she loved before (suggesting that she had made love to men she didn't love). Emily hit it on the spot that Cotannah hated men and the only ones that held her interest were the ones she couldn't control. For me it was kinda painful being in her head because she doesn't have very many unselfish or even big picture thoughts in there. Constant thoughts of how she can make someone jealous and how annoying that man is and her getting mad because he's not falling in line the way she wants or how the cut of this scandalous dress will make men fall all over themselves to compliment her. Gah! I waded through all that nonsense with the understanding that Cotannah's purpose in this story was to mature and to realize her own selfishness and overcome it. I only wish that she'd been successful (although the nonsense does decrease a bit...)Walks-With-Spirits is a major cliche, but he's an interesting contrast to Cotannah. He seeks balance and harmony and recognizes that Cotannah's turbulent spirit unsettles him (I also got the impression that he was a virgin). So it seems that he will be the settling influence and teach Cotannah to calm her spirit and think more outside of herself. I'm not sure Walks-With-Spirits ever finds his balance or harmony again. Instead, Cotannah more or less takes over his life and despite her claims to be finally "not selfish" I didn't see it. Everything she does is for her own gain. She insists on trying to prove him innocent even though he asks her not to - this was forgivable since she was also trying to save his life. However, she claims it's not to assuage her own guilt, but you could totally tell it was. I also felt that it was so that Cotannah wouldn't have to be alone - the possibility of loneliness was more of her driving force than the happiness of the man she claimed to love. Walks-With-Spirits wanted Cotannah to learn to love herself, but she only wanted HIM to love her, and obviously he can't do that when he's dead. For me, this was one of the author's major failings...Cotannah never learns to love herself. She only ever seeks external validation from others...even in the epilogue when she seeks to impress her brother with how domestic she is. There is never any internal acceptance of who she is.Then, after Walks-With-Spirits has given up on proving his innocence he wants to go away to find his harmony again. Does Cotannah give him that last request? No, she insists on going with him because it's what she wants even knowing that she unsettles him. Then he insists they are not going to make love because it would only hurt her and he would feel worse for dying. Does she listen? No, she seduces him into sex (even after promising she wouldn't), not because SHE doesn't want him to die without having sex with him. She also had a thought that she'd make him so addicted to sex that he wouldn't leave her...because underneath it all her goal is to get him to run away. She wants him to sacrifice his honor and run away from his own execution so that she doesn't have to deal with his death (and doesn't have to be lonely). She tries to convince him to do this several times despite his explaining how important this is to him. So you see, she doesn't really mature because she doesn't ever really think about anyone but her. She continues to think about how his death will affect her rather than how HE could die the way he wants to. Yeah, then Ms. I've-Supposedly-Learned-My-Lesson-About-Inciting-Men goes off to flirt wildly with the villain (knowing how evil he is) claiming that she can handle him with her expert wiles and get him to confess (instead of getting raped or killed). RME.So, the author set up a particular goal about proving the heroine was maturing and becoming a an unselfish woman, but then failed to deliver the goods. And unfortunately, it felt like she brought that kinda lovely, ethereal hero, down to earth and to her level. That's too bad.
