This is short but sweet. If nothing else, it lets you into the mind of Highsmith, and into her working practices. I'm so envious of her ability to plot while writing; she likes to surprise herself. She was mainly working in the days of typewriter and she could type out a first draft without continuing to rewrite as she went along, which boggles my mind. I wonder if the inability to rewrite makes the mind sharper and more disciplined? In any case, this little book proves that there are no tricks to writing well; Highsmith's prose and plotting are the result of her incredible mind, and not due to any particular method. I love her definition of suspense: "Stories with a threat of violent physical action and danger." Also, the sections on development and snags were quite useful. Her stories always contain the best complications, and it seems you only need three or four of these, if they're good, to ratchet up the tension and make your stories tense and suspenseful.
