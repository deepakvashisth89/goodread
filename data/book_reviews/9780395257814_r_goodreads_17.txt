Like every Kosinski I've read, this is a loosely connected short story collection framed as a novel. Unlike Painted Bird (plagiarism/authorship question notwithstanding), which felt like a perverse collection of postwar fairy tales, and Steps, whose structure was more elliptical and daring, Blind Date is very much trying to craft a narrative over decades of our protag's life, to its detriment as a cohesive piece imoSome of the vignettes in here are truly horrific, others feel like self-insert authorial fantasy (some feel like both), but generally there's a sense of boundary pushing that extends beyond horny provocateur schtick. One segment featuring an arab trans lover must have been pretty daring in the 70's
