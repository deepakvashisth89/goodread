The boy that you loved is the man that you fear.I started listening to Marilyn Manson when I was about 12 or 13, determined to wear black lipstick and piercings all over my face and ready to shock everyone in my family.Now, almost 20 years later the piercings are gone and the only time I might still look shocking is after yet another sleepless night (teething is hard!).My taste in music though hasn't changed that much.So I really enjoyed reading about Manson's childhood, the events that shaped and made him who he became and of course the heinous tales of the first tours.Journal entries, interviews and a lot of photographs added to the experience of diving into the mind of this shrewd, peculiar, extraordinary artist.
