My opinion only, of course, but it goes against the mainstream!Typically, books characterized as modern classics, whose literary merits must be described by voluminous scholarly analysis and gushing pretentious references to such tools as symbolism, have never appealed to my reading taste. Frankly, I prefer a much more direct story-line and the plot of Ondaatje’sTHE ENGLISH PATIENT, such as it is, is anything but direct and transparent.Maclean’s magazine referred to it as,“One of the finest Canadian novels ever written”. On the other hand, I closed the last page, shrugged and asked myself“What the hell did I just read?”One supposes it was a romance of kinds set against the turbulence of the final days and the aftermath of World War II in Italy but I could find nothing in Kip Singh, Caravaggio or Hana that merited my interest or any deep thought as to their motivations. Indeed, the only parts of the novel which rescued it from being set aside unfinished were the very compelling descriptions of the war against Rommel in North Africa, the technical aspects of the mechanics of bombs and the hair-raising description of Kip Singh’s work as a bomb-disposal engineer.One more book checked off that had languished for many years on my bucket list. But I can’t say I’d recommend it particularly heartily to any future readers.Paul Weiss
