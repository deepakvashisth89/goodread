Arghhhh...this is the second Voyager book I've tried to read, and it just isn't working out for me. What the hell are these jumps-around-randomly-right-in-the-next-paragraph narrative shifts?? I don't care about Janeway's childhood in the middle of an action scenario! And her talking to a therapist--huh!?? Where is tough Janeway?

But then in the show she was reduced in narrative and captainly prominence when they brought on Spandexed Seven--the younger woman dressed so all her woman's parts are clearly shown. So sexist and juvenile; I only kept watching the show because the overall writing in the episodes was IMO probably the best of all the shows. But viewers like I am FINALLY got a woman in charge on a Star Trek show, and then a few seasons later, every few moments, we have to see the silhouette of an implanted-looking woman in tight spandex. She would have been a very good character had she almost always been dressed modestly like the rest of the show's characters.

Voyager's creators ruined a woman being in charge by in the next breath objectifying women the way sexist hollywood has always done. And then by the end of the show, SEVEN winds up with
and NOT JANEWAY! More stereotypical nonsense and ridiculous wish fulfillment on the part of the sexist old men in charge of hollywood. (Not that all old men in general are sexist.)

Sorry for my I-am-woman-hear-me-roar rant (...naaaa, I'm not sorry lol...), but I don't know what the hell happened with these Voyager books. I don't know if I'm going through a phase and I personally just can't read them or what. I'm just kind of irritated, too, that I've had five more of the Voyager books on my Kindle for a few years now, and I think maybe a few Voyager comics too--but if they all suffer from the same stuff as the last two I tried reading, then I blew my money. I might not even bother trying to read any of them.

And what the hell happened to the characters in this book, especially Janeway? There seems to be too much sentimentality in the narrative. One of the reasons the crew of this show is my slight favorite protrayal out of all the old ST shows is that the characters in Voyager are each so unique, and, because of that, there's a lot of tension between them.

Eh, Amazon kept offering my husband and I deals to buy the ST shows at a reduced price, and now we have this show on Prime. I'll probably just go back to watching my favorite episodes and give up on the books. Maybe the show just doesn't translate very well into another medium.
