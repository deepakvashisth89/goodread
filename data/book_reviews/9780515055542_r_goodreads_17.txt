The only reason I'm not giving five stars is that I worked out the method of the murder early on. This is my first Marsh novel and even coming late to the series it was easy to get the hang of the central characters. Unlike more recent crime fiction which seems to need a corpse in the opening chapter and several more thereafter this one takes its time and is the better for it as the author takes 50% of the pages to setup all the horrible suspects in this diva-filled family in their country house. Thankfully I had Agatha Troy as a sympathetic character to root for. I also enjoyed learning about the process of portrait painting.Unlike others here I enjoyed the whole married couple reuniting after the war subplot (perhaps because I'm married to somebody who is away for work regularly?) but even if you dislike that, it's easy enough to skip.If you enjoy Golden Age British detective fiction you should enjoy this one.
