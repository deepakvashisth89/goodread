I thought I would really like this, as usually books with a theatre theme are good, but this one got too frustrating. The H took "cruel to be kind" a bit too seriously, broke the h's heart when she was only 18 (she was an actress just getting started, while he was a playwright who wrote the play she had a part in), then does the same thing again several years later, by accusing her of cheating she tells him she's pregnant, because he's under the impression that he shoots blanks! (One doctor's statement that it's highly unlikely he'll father a child was enough for him, apparently.) That was the reason he dumped her the first time (he felt he'd be depriving her of having children) and why he's acting like the betrayed lover this time around. (I guess it never occurred to him to get a second medical opinion???? Not to mention being honest with her: just because she said she dreamed of having children someday, didn't mean she's dump him or stick with him out of pity when she found out he was supposedly sterile! Anyway, was adoption a "no way" option?)And the fact that she was a virgin after their years apart didn't tell him anything???? She didn't want any man but him, yet he believes that right after they become lovers she was going to do an about face and sleep with another man??? OH, COME ON!!!And the OM in the story, who was a good friend to her naturally gets blamed. He does play White Knight, offering to marry her because he did have some romantic feelings for her, but they were later transferred to her BFF, and I would have liked more time devoted to this SC.As for the MC, after a time the h stopped trying to get him to believe her and gave up, and who can blame her? She also got angry when the H said he'd help out financially, assuming the OM wasn't stepping up to the plate and taking responsibility, and who can blame her for that either? Later, he suddenly decides he was wrong (gee, you think???) and goes a bit overload trying to make it up to her, while she's not too receptive at first (again, who can blame her).He even decides not to do what he should have done a long time ago and saved them both a lot of mess: get a second medical opinion. He wants her to know he trusts her when she says it's his baby and she's never been with another man, and not looking for proof will be a way to do that.It tells you something about their true feelings that when each had a turn at not feeling well (in her case, not being able to afford proper medical care and suffering from anemia, in his, working too hard on a script, and not eating or sleeping properly) the other one was there to help and see that they got well. A sign of love, though neither would admit it at first.But some things were a bit much, like the h collapsing on stage and everyone in the cast/crew, etc. taking care of her medical bills, watching out for her, and keeping a doctor backstage in case she overdoes it. in the real world, the understudy would have taken over the part until she was better. But in this book, she's onstage until she's ready to pop out the kid, just too ridiculous!That's about as believable as that whole mess with her investing all that money on her manager's (very bad) advice and losing a bundle, as well as owing taxes, so she has to move out of the fancy Park Avenue apartment she shared with the BFF and move into a dump in Queens instead.Where the book rally goofed was turning the birth of the baby into a rom-com farce, with things getting a bit berserk, the cab driver turning out to be a man they had a chat with in a deli (where he gave his advice on parenthood, along with another guy and a waitress, who stopped serving tables to sit and chat with them, if you can believe that), and he, the other guy, and the waitress end up at the hospital waiting for the baby to arrive. Altogether now: "WTF!!!!"So DUMB!!!There were two things that were never touched on. The storyline makes it sound like the h was determined to forget her small town roots, but the book itself never gives that impression. Also, the h mentioned more than once that her BFF hates men, and at first didn't think it would work out between her and the OM (though she was glad it did), but you're never told why she felt that way. Did some guy break her heart in the past? Was she sexually assaulted? Did she have an abusive or perverted stepdad or some other creepy relative? We're never given any info, just the idea that whatever it was that made her feel that way, the OM changed her mind.There was just too much silliness to be able to take this seriously.
