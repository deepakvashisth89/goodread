REVIEW….

I went into The Girl Next Door expecting something disturbing, but I was not expecting it to break my heart like this.

It is very rare for me to hate a character enough that I get genuinely upset, but Ruth managed it. This book made me anxious, angry, and completely helpless because I spent so much of it wishing I could just reach into the story and get Meg out of there.

Jack Ketchum’s writing is incredibly straightforward, and I think that’s exactly why this hit as hard as it did. He never overdid it or tried to force the emotion. He simply told the story, and that was more than enough. Having him narrate the audiobook made it even more impactful because every scene felt exactly the way he intended it to.

Finding out this was inspired by the real-life case of Sylvia Likens made it even more heartbreaking. The real horror wasn’t the violence itself., it was the cruelty, the indifference, and the people who saw pieces of what was happening and still allowed it to continue. That was the part I couldn’t stop thinking about. It made me think about my own kids and hope that if they were ever in a situation where someone needed help, they would do the right thing without hesitation.

This is easily one of the saddest stories I’ve ever read, and I know I’m going to be thinking about Meg for a long time.

I’d recommend it to readers who appreciate emotionally devastating horror, stories inspired by true events, and books that stay with you long after you’ve finished them.
