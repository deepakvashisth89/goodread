this book is about following someone brilliant into battle, about early programming culture and the northeast’s (now forgotten) culture of computing, about working very hard with people you love on something you are too naive to believe is impossible. there’s so much rich description here about what it means (and feels like) to do great work, about squeezing the ideal into the actual, about working for glory and love of the game rather than fame or money. “soul of a new machine” is such an apt title - some of the beauty of this book is getting lost in crunch or minutiae, feeling constantly on the cusp of failure and that nothing is working, and looking up to notice a nearly-built, whole computertracy kidder is an excellent writer and I love this genre wise too (as “literary journalism”). shocking to me that this isn’t as firmly in new journalism canon - this feels like a computing-oriented version of The Right Stuff, but I think I liked it even more. pretty instantly this is one of my favorite things i’ve ever read and I recommend without reservation. shoutout to matthew and linus for recommending, obviously I was going to love this
