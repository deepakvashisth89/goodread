Finishing this felt like stepping out of a cathedral I didn’t realize I’d entered. A monumental book — and the welcome Balzac gave me was so dazzling that I can say, without a tremor of doubt, that his storytelling brushes perfection. Those opening chapters were wickedly funny, laced with a sophistication worthy of the glorious mustache he carried like a badge of excess.Is it worth reading as much of La Comédie humaine as one can? The clear answer is yes — with the small caveat that life will either draw you closer to Balzac or pull you away from him, depending on how much reality you can stand. But I’ll never forget the legendary creator of “les Marguerites,” nor the way he turns ambition into anatomy and vanity into prophecy.Balzac doesn’t describe a world — he dissects it, laughs at it, and then hands you the scalpel.
