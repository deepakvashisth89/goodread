Happy New Year! 2020 is looking to become an exciting and suspenseful political horror show (impeachment, presidential election year, all that jazz: can ya feel it?)*, so I decided to re-read a 20+-year-old graphic novel by Stephen King, illustrated by Bernie and Michelle Wrightson, based on a film by George Romero. What a way to start the year, huh?



Actually, other than the terrific artwork by the Wrightsons, the book is merely okay; a blatant homage to the EC horror comics of the '40s and '50s. King, I feel, dumbs it down for the kiddies (which is hilarious, given that 90% of the stuff in this book would probably never be published today given all the "emotional triggers" and "offensive" material that far left-leaning watch-dogs would inevitably find within), even though it is clearly not meant for children.



Still, I enjoyed it this time around. My 47-year-old self definitely found it less terrifying and more silly than my 12-year-old self found it to be. I remember having vivid nightmares after reading it. That was then. I've now seen worse. Hell, I've lived through the first three years of the Trump presidency, so nothing much scares me anymore.




Don't ask me why I picked it up to re-read. It was just a random choice. Or perhaps I felt that it was an appropriate way to ring in a year that I know is going to be one wild ride, politically. Personally, it sets the tone for my personal reading goals this year, which includes a lot more graphic novels (a trend I started last year), history, politics, and horror (a genre that I find is underrepresented in my reading list, for some reason.)

Anyway, I hope 2019 was good to you, and I wish you all a wonderful 2020!

Horror film auteur George Romero made this into a pretty cheesy film (good cheesy, not bad cheesy), but what really makes the graphic novel (I'll call it that, even though it's more of an homage to the classic EC horror comics of the '40s and '50s) "Creepshow" isn't the fact that Stephen King wrote the four original stories (although that helps) but Bernie and Michelle Wrightson's awesome artwork. I used to own a copy of this, but it became so dog-eared from reading that it eventually disintegrated into nothingness. (In truth, I think I hid it under the bed because it scared me, and my mom cleaned everything out from under it and accidentally threw it away.) Honest to God, this book actually did scare the bejeezus out of me and gave me nightmares, and much of that is due to Wrightson's creepy artwork. A definite collector's item...
