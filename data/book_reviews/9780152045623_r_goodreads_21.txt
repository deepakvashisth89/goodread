I read and rated the earlier books in this series favorably. It was an interesting concept and I liked that there were gay mentors in the first book. When one of the following books said those two were “just roommates” I hated it but I chalked it up to being because the books were published in the 80’s,90’s. I also cringed and overlooked the clear religious subtext because I have trauma related to religion and am not a good reference for what is symbolism versus someone trying to push their beliefs.This book however I can’t excuse. I was on high alert as soon as Kit said he could tell Darryl is autistic just by looking at him. Much of the conversations surrounding what autism is are filled with over generalizations and straight lies. At one point Kit’s mom, who is a nurse, tells Kit that sometime people “come back” from being autistic and that’s how we know what it’s like.I really tried to give the book a good go but I stopped in the last chapter. The idea that Kit and Nita help “fix” Darryl just by being there is ridiculous. It’s overt ableism and pushes the idea that something evil makes you autistic. Nita even says she thinks that Darryl was made autistic by the lone power.I can understand and appreciate that the book was written in 2002 by someone who is not a specialist. However, I won’t be giving this author any more of my time.In summary, I won’t be finishing this series and won’t be recommending it either.
