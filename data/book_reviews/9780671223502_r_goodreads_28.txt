"Think pink. A better way of life."I was first introduced to Eloise, the most famous little girl to live at the Plaza Hotel, through the absolutely iconic Disney special,Eloise at the Plaza.It quickly became one of my comfort watches as a child and even today as an adult. If I'm having a bad day and want something cute and cozy to watch, I'm putting onEloise at the Plaza.Further, it was one of the most influential movies for me growing up because as soon as I saw it, I had two realizations: My favorite color was pink (and it still is to this very day), and I absolutely MUST visit New York City and stay at the Plaza Hotel. My father lived in New York City for two years when he was in the Army, and he used to tell me stories of all the fun he had there. Skating in Rockefeller Center, attending Broadway shows, walking around the vastness of the city and stopping to eat in one of the thousands of restaurants. He made it seem like an adventure, withEloise at the Plazaencouraging my dreams as well. New York City was Eloise's magical playground that invited me to join in her rawther marvelous escapades.I didn't even realize back then thatEloise at the Plazawas based on a book. It wasn't until much later that I stumbled upon that nugget of information. Needless to say that once I learned that, I needed to read the book based on the special that had such a big influence on my life. And let me tell you, the main, prevalent thought I had in my head is...Eloise is exhausting, LOL!!She's running around the Plaza hotel doing rather dastardly things, such as torturing the elevator operator and pouring water down the mail chute. Some might call her a spoiled little brat (as evidenced by the reviews.) I call her a nap waiting to happen. She's a child that, when it came to parent teacher conference time, the teacher would be describing Eloise as having "a big personality" (in teacher speak that means she's a handful and poorly behaved, teehee!)It pains me to say this, but I prefer the little Eloise of the Disney special over her literary counterpart by just a smidgen of a bit. Maybe it's because the human Eloise had that quality of being naughty but oh-so-lovable that you couldn't stay mad at her for long, but honestly I think it's just because of the memories attached to the special that I have. An unfair start, I know, but we're being honest here!That being said, I still enjoyed Eloise and reading about her various romps and dastardly deeds around the Plaza. There really is not plot to this book. It's more of a look inside the day in the life of Eloise. Now that I think of it, it kind of reads like a literary version of those DITL videos you see lifestyle influencers post. Come to think of it even further, Eloise would make a GREAT influencer if she lived in the present day.Kay Thompson's narration is sparse and blunt, just how one would imagine a six-year-old would talk. However, some of the language used is definitely more on the book for pretentious grown-ups side. I find this rather ironic (and Kay Thompson herself did as well), that Eloise is considered a children's classic and children's brand as a whole. Eloise is clearly meant to be a parody of a children's book for grown-ups, and yet in the marketing world she is clearly advertised to be for children. We got Eloise talking about martinis and charge accounts at Bergdorfs and AT&T stocks, and yet Disney family films created two of the most iconic TV movies of all time around her (taking out the more adult parts and conversations of the film). At my local Barnes and Noble, there's a wooden cutout of her displayed in the store. Where, you ask?In the children's section.Stranger things have happened, I suppose. It's just something I find funny. She really is in a book for precocious grown-ups, but children have claimed her as their own.And of course I have to give a shoutout to Hilary Knight's stupendously charming illustrations. They're one of the reasons, if not THE reason, Eloise is as iconic as she is. He really captures the precociousness and mischievousness of Eloise without making her a caricature. He humanizes Eloise and makes it so you can't be mad at her for long (though whoever is at the other end of the mail chute would say otherwise, haha!) He also didn't set out to make Eloise the most angelic looking child possible, something that's clearly visible in his illustrations. Her hair isn't perfect, her clothes are askew, and she has some baby fat, but that's part of her charm. She isn't meant to be perfect.Overall, this was a rawther charming read and had me itching to rewatch the Disney special that started my love of the color pink and obsession with visiting New York. I happened to read the 60th anniversary edition, and this version made me loveEloise at the Plazaeven more than I do now. The way Disney stayed so faithful and paid homage not just to the book, but the character and personalities of both Hilary Knight and Kay Thompson just shone through. In the 60th anniversary edition, you get a behind the scenes look at the development of the Eloise character, biographies of the lives of Kay Thompson and Hilary Knight, and how Eloise continues to have an impact on both children and adults to this day.Because, after all, pink really is a better way of life.
