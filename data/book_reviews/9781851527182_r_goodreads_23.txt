I have been reading this book for the last four weeks. Jane Eyre has been a long time favorite of mine, but I hadn't reread it for years. I have had a love/hate relationship with Wuthering Heights since high school. I have read it 3 or 4 times before, but not for at least 20 years. I have never read Agnes Grey before. This fall I have a plan to read the complete novels of the Bronte sisters, and these are the first three.Of the three, Wuthering Heights was definitely my least favorite. The overwrought emotions of the unlikable characters swing between telenovella and child throwing a tantrum level for me. I pity all the characters, but they also infuriate me. They do feel real, however, and the descriptions are mesmerizing. Reading this book sometimes feels like watching a train wreck you can't tear your eyes away from. If I train wreck could be somehow beautiful despite it all.Jane Eyre was even better than I remembered. I noticed many connections to the fairy tale Beauty and the Beast, which I found quite enjoyable. Masterful use of tense and compressed emotional tension.Agnes Grey started a bit aimlessly, I thought. But it really became compelling about half way through. It is the shortest of the three, by far, and the only one with a romantic hero that any reasonable person would actually want to marry. The romanance is yearning and subtle. There is a moment that reminded me strongly of Mr. Darcy's hand flex in the 2005 Pride & Prejudice. I loved the romance. The narrative is biting and quite resentful at times, and at other times is lyrical and lovely. I think this is an unrated gem!
