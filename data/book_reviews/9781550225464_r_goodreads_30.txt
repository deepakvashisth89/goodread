This book sneaks up on you, what starts as an interesting travelogue soon becomes a moving story anchored by the unthinkable grief and heartache that Neil was enduring after the loss of his 19 year old daughter and then his wife less than a year later (soon followed by the imprisonment of his closest friend and travel buddy).He sets off on the road on his motorcycle with no real destination, his only need is to have constant movement, the road passing under his two wheels somehow keeping him together as he grapples to understand his place in the world now that his little family is gone.The fact that he's considered one of the best drummers in rock and roll history with the band Rush only serves as a small footnote here. I found him to be humble, even self deprecating at times but with a certain wholesome humor as he reads letters written to those who were worried about him on the road.His journey comes full circle at the end of the road as he's faced with confronting his unknown future, where he's to live, if he'll ever play with Rush again and his search for some form of ballast to his life now unmoored.While reading this book I was saddened to learn that Neil passed away just a few weeks ago, which only made this book that much more poignant. I enjoyed every mile traveled with him and can only hope he's got good weather and some open road to ride up above.
