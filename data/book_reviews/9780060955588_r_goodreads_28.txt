It's really hard to pin down a rating for this book. It has passages that are pure brilliance... deep images and explorations that I hope to remember and reflect on. But these are intermixed with so much that is jarring and all over the place. One example: towards the end, there are deeply insightful thoughts on death and absence. But then the book immediately switches over to some odd subject I already can't recall, even though I just finished Eternal Echoes.

Frustrating. I am going to go with the word frustrating... I wanted to give this book four stars, but can't. The writing is a tad too disconnected, as if dashing all over the place somehow makes the text more "poetic." It also gets far more explicitly Christian than Anam Cara did, which really isn't my cup of tea. But the Christian influence surfaces in ways that are scattered and disordered. Mr. O'Donohue was definitely not a fundamentalist, at least, and there's an almost precognitive chapter on that very subject towards the end.

One thing that jumps out is how little of his own experience the author really brings into this book, though it is allegedly about the intimacy of participating in life and its mysteries, rather than merely observing life. The tone is often "from on high." I found this style of writing very tiresome after a while.

Which is sad, because there are insights in here that are beautiful and would really benefit our world. There are thoughts here that are even more poignant and relevant at the start 0f 2026 than when they were penned back in the 20th century. The deeper parts of this book are sort of prophetic.

I don't regret reading it and yet I struggled to finish it. Yeah... frustrating.
