Romance novels by Kathleen Woodiwiss tends to follow the same pattern. Delicate, beautiful female lead who gets into trouble and rescued by the tall, muscular, male lead. Instant physical attraction, followed by a developing romance. A twist that separates our lovers, which is resolved at the end. During the process, the inner strength, compassion and courage of our female lead is revealed.
Come Love a Stranger follows this pattern. On a stormy night, a deliberate fire is set to destroy a madhouse. A mysterious woman wearing a nightgown is riding her horse away from the madhouse and gets into an accident, which results in her rescue by the handsome, rich, Ashton Wingate. Ashton is convinced that the mysterious woman is his wife Lierin, who fell overboard during a pirate attack three years earlier and thought dead, though her body was never fount. Unfortunately our heroine is suffering from a loss of memory due to the accident. Although she has no memory of her past life, she falls in love with Aston. The twist is a man, Malcolm Sinclair, who pops up and insists that our heroine is Lenore, his kidnapped wife, and Lierin's twin sister. As she gets back flashes of memory of her previous life and home, the memories strengthens Malcom's claim.
This book is a little slower than some of the author's previous novels and the black servants and workers on Ashton's estate are offensive caricatures that are submissive to the whites, while adoring their master and mistress. Granted, the story is set in the Mississippi in 1833, but these minor characters could have been been fleshed out a little bit more, rather than having them be a racial stereotype.
