Hawk Hunter the Wingman has survived the events of the previous book only to end up in a parallel universe. Here, World War 2 has never ended; the Russians had been conquered, Japan never entered the war, and England had been conquered. What's more, all of the principal casts were now in different roles except for our hero.Maloney also likes to re-fight World War 2. In Book 9, Maloney uses Operation Mincemeat. In this book, he recreates 617 Squadron's attack on the Ruhr valley dams. This works in a science Britain was already occupied and thus unable to mount the attack with BRITISH bombers in real life. Maloney also likes to use of the paranormal and sixth sense. It can get weird at times and repetitive after 14 books.Another thing about this book are the planes. Most of them seem to defy physics and look like what the planes in the 1950s would like described by 1940s pulp scifi writers. Twelve engine prop pusher planes, jet powered hybrid P-51 Mustangs, etc.What surprised me was the arch-villain. We know there was Viktor Robotoy and yet he doesn't appear in this one compared to the events of the last three books.
