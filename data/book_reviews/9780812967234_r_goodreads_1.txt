No. No this is not a good bio for anyone to read, and certainly not for Rockwell fans. The author seems to actively dislike her subject, and that just isn't a good start for anyone's life story.
Frequently, when she cites a statement that Rockwell made about himself, she follows through by stating that there are inaccuracies in the details, and then sets out reasons why Rockwell may be trying to cover up some unpleasantness. She does a lot of amateur psychoanalyzing and few of her conclusions are gentle or forgiving.

Eventually, when she's forced to describe NR as a good friend or a charmer at parties or attractive to women, it's almost impossible to recognize this person as the unreliable, sick, obnoxiously selfcentered fellow she's presented so far.

(Which is not to say that he was without fault, just that Claridge dwells on his faults at length without giving sufficient attention to his virtues.)

And good god! She repeats at least three times throughout the book that he had a randy uncle who died of syphilis.
