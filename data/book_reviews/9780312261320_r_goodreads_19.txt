What I LikedBy reading other reviews, it looks like I was not the only one who accidentally checked out the wrong book by this title from the library! I expected this to be the same title by Laura Whitcomb and did not double check until I began reading and thought, "What is this about again?" and realized my mistake.However, I was pleasantly surprised by Thayer's writing. She is a lovely writer and creates a sad, slowly gripping tale about a widower named Peter who lives as a virtual hermit after the death of his family. When a heavily pregnant Jehovah's Witness woman named Elaine shows up on his doorstep and asks for a place to flee, his warring emotions, embarrassment, and awkwardness are all incredibly true-to-life.What I Didn't LikeI wish the ending had a bit more of a hold to it. The conclusion fell somewhat short, with several characters and storylines being left somewhat unresolved.Elaine as a character often drove me crazy. Her willingness to just depend on everyone financially and for full support seemed incredibly selfish and over-dramatic.I had never heard of the RH blood type issue with pregnancy. Since I am pregnant when I was reading it, that was very intriguing to me. However, Thayer seems to breach a very fascinating topic with the JW's resistance to accepting blood transfusions - and then the issue just drops without any resolution or moral conclusion, even from one character or another. The discussion seemed so unresolved in the end.
