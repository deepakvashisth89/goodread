I really liked this book, I think it has a lot of interesting outlooks and conclusions on femininity, sex, love and power. It forced me to evaluate myself and women as a whole in that process. I do however, think it has its flaws - I find the MC selfish, privileged, as well as dismissive and almost envious of other women in the book. Noticing the judgemental and sometimes downright misogynistic internal dialogue really brought down the book for me.Stuff I liked: I found it apt in its description of the common desire among women to yearn for being hurt. I like the fact it acknowledges how drilled into us bathing in disrespect and submission is, as well as how hard it can be to free yourself, not only of the physical desire of domination in sex but also the mental prison of patriarchal thinking. Powerful and successful women as shown through Leila, also, and in some ways, exceed the average woman in this thinking. It is natural for someone with power to want the release of submission and paternal care, but what if it hurts you? What if female submission is built into and props up the destructive violence of an oppressive system? Is it, then, wrong to desire it? Why does it still feel so good and necessary to give in this power? Leila wrestles with these questions in a new world of sexual liberation and feminism, treading new ground. (I found myself relating to these thought heavyyy, felt extremely understood.)Interesting to note is the portrayal of men in this book, they are not all one dimensional and evil (though I view some as such) but they are just as complicated and confused by this new exchange of gendered power as Leila. I particularly enjoyed, perhaps a tale as old as time, our main man, Dart, wrestling with his ego in relation to a woman with more money, more power, and more intellect than him. He cannot stand it, and it rips the relationship apart. She builds him up, offers him a helping hand, which only makes the rift of resentment wider. This subtle reversal of the creation tale and traditional gender roles (built around the creation tale) reveals that men hate being treated as women. Ever since Eve was built from Adams rib, men cannot fathom being given to, and provided for by a woman, and that is why, Dart, despite his love for her, betrays Leila.Now with what I found frustrating. Leila constantly sees a situation ending badly but let's it play out anyway, or downright ignores her findings or intuition. I also found the middle of the book quite repetitive and a little on the boring side as you kind of have to watch Leila make the same mistakes over and over again. I think I disliked it, not just because I found it boring to read but also because it does shield an element of truth, because at its core, this is a book about addiction: addiction to drugs and alcohol, addiction to sex and addiction to love. So of course its frustrating to watch a woman fail to better herself over and over again, thats life! It's very true and relatable but extremely hard to put down into words because, if you're angry with yourself for making the same mistakes you sure as hell get angry watching someone else do it.I found leila very relatable in her struggles, but also hatable. I hate the language used to describe the wives, girlfriends and mistresses of the books' main men, often referred to as 'bimbos' or 'cold and callous' 'unloving', when most of the time the reader never interacts with these women, unlike the men Leila interacts with which, despite doing some pretty awful things, are rarely given the same amount of vitriol. Leila's cynicism (outside of the dream of the perfect love) while a realistic reaction to the hardships she has endured prior in life, is extremely exhausting to follow for the whole book. I hated the way she talked about those she deemed as 'below her' when I thought, more often than not, those characters deserved sympathy and understanding, but I reckon thats pretty subjective.I took a lot from this book and I think I read it at a time when I was reevaluating my own relationship with sex (and sex addiction) as well as male sexual power.
