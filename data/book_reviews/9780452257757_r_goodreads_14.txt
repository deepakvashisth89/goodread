Jumping in at number 4 of this 5 novel series, I was rather at sea for a while. Rather a lot of female characters with initial M to identify along with narrator Martha!The writing style is dense and labyrinthine, sometimes the references are unclear. I think there is a mix of approaches - partly conventional narrative, partly an almost cubist approach to viewing situations from different angles. An odd preoccupation with the details of how human bodies are inhabited, somewhat diverting from the narrative. The narrator, socially committed, is curiously also quite self-centred. The depictions of the women characters are I think more convincing than those of the men on the whole. The men seem to carry a lot of cultural baggage, and have a general tendency to be broody and sullen.The exterior themes of colonisation, world war, and a communist movement in crisis, emerge through a fog of personal and circumstantial detail. No character is especially likeable. There's an odd mixture of lots of detail, mixed with a sort of vagueness or equivocation, in Lessing's writing.Sometimes I wish she would just get to the point, but that isn't her style or approach. I don't entirely like her style, but there is merit in this book, though I couldn't call it a very enjoyable or satisfying read. Along the way, though, I did learn a little about southern Africa in this period, which helps in understanding subsequent events.
