In the 1930s there were people in Britain who supported fascism, and some of them gathered regularly at Hallam, the country house owned by a family whose subsequent generations were much less involved in politics but no less insensitive to the lives of the people around them. At first Sarah Causeley is somewhat starstruck by the gracious hospitality shown to her as a recently hired governess to care for six-year-old Phoebe. But her upbringing as a village vicar's daughter makes her feel more at home with the working class folks, and leads to romance. Then a campaign of harassment ends in a murder which is never solved. We know this because there are frequent references to the "present" and how Sarah's opinions and feelings changed. Not his best, but interesting.
