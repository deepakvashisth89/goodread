Two mysteries unfold in the opening pages of "Eighty Million Eyes" (1966). First, a bully walks into an office building, accosts a woman who doesn't know him, and then beats up a cop before leaving. Detective Bert Kling is on this case, though the accosted woman doesn't like it because she's interacted with Bert before.Second, in the main storyline, a live television comedy show is interrupted by the death of its star while forty million viewers (the titular eighty million eyes) watch it happen. Detectives Carella and Meyer catch this case and start wading their way through the quirky and obnoxious production affairs of the television industry.As an aside, this TV murder must have been a fun bit of "poking the bear" by the author Ed McBain who I'm sure had his own annoying experiences dealing with such folks in his time as a screenwriter used to trying to convert crime novels - his own and others - into television and movie projects. Among his hundreds of credits, McBain wrote Hitchcock's "the Birds" screenplay from the Du Maurier short story, for example, and wrote for the "Columbo" series and I assume later had some hand in converting the next of the 87th Precinct novels "Fuzz" into the 70's comedy starring Burt Reynolds as Detective Carella, which sounds just awful and I have to see it right away.A smart cop procedural takes us through the machinations of these three detectives investigating the two cases, interrogating witnesses, checking alibis, and setting suspects against each other in attempts to find the culprits. A generally light tone is offset at about the halfway mark when a brutal spoiler-removed occurs that had me skipping ahead. The tonal shift was jarring enough I didn't want to read it.Verdict: The two cases wrap up nicely at the end and "Eighty Million Eyes" is another good, short, mystery in the 87th Precinct series but don't get too comfortable with the early tone because it gets rougher.Jeff's Rating: 3 / 5 (Good)movie rating if made into a movie: R
