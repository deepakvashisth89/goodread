Biographer Andrew Morton set out to prove to people with a preconceived image of Monica Lewinsky as spoiled, pushy and slutty that she is actually a deep and complex person led astray by her heart and victimized by a treacherous friend, corrupt legal system and merciless media machine. My experience was the opposite: I began the book knowing few details about Monica and predisposed to give her the benefit of the doubt, yet found as I read that it was very difficult to like the shallow, whiny, self-destructive and self-entitled character that emerged despite Morton's forgiving interpretations of her behavior.Form as well as content contributed to my dissatisfaction with this book: Morton inserts far too much of his own voice for my taste, judging each player as good or evil in a very repetitive and overdone way. I started this book by reading the sample pages on Amazon.com's "look inside" feature, which were some of the most interesting sections -- the moment when Linda Tripp's betrayal becomes apparent, Monica's first attentions from the president -- and decided to read the rest. As it turned out, those were the only interesting sections in a book bogged down by boring detail and repetitive editorializing. The sneak peak is all you need to bother with on this one.
