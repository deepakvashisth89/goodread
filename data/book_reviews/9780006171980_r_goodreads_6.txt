I have to stop grading these on a curve because the action scenes are so good. To that goal this gets the lowest rating I have yet given to a Sharpe book albeit Sharpe's Honour is not the worst in the series. I dub this Sharpe's rebound after following the best installment IMOSharpe's Enemyin the series. Sharpe's Honour falls short of it's predecessor by a considerable measure.Sharpe's reckless personality is explored more than anything else here. the idea that Cornwell is trying to impress is that Sharpe is a fool! a fool yes but a honorable fool. I do not enjoy this aspect of Sharpe's character. Although i acknowledge it makes for interesting fiction reading, makes me just say "common man, what are you doing". I will cut Sharpe a break and say his actions are a result of his grief over Teresa's death. The two main villains a set of Spanish brothers a vile inquisitor and a violent partisan are sort of common types in the series. not to say they are bad characters or poorly written but we have seen their type before. Sharpe's main adversary Pierre Ducos continues his recurring role as French spy master who has a grudge against or hero (good luck with that). Ducos uses Sharpe's former lover La Marquesa yada, yada, yada fromSharpe's Sword: The Salamanca Campaign, June and July 1812as a pawn to set a trap for Sharpe. La Marquesa is a willing participant in Sharpe's downfall because she's that type of woman. I do not like La Marquesa character mostly because she is the best example of the rampant misogamy in the series. I'm sure it's a accurate historical depiction but she's portrayed as literally a Spoil of war at the end of the book. being compared to gold though-out the book. Due to Ducos schemes Sharpe is forced out of the army and has to fake his own death. He is forced to become a fugitive to clear his name which turns out to be really easy. All along he is manipulated by La Marquesa who uses him as a pawn. it all turns out okay as he end up with her in his bed, as it should be..... Yeah right! there is action through-out with an exciting historical battle. Cornwell set up to the battle is widespread involving first person narration from the French commander, Napoleon's Brother Joseph, and Wellington himself. Cornwell sets the battle up quickly and effectively but of course he had to since it only takes up a less then a third of the book. A heart-throbbing knife fight between Sharpe and the violent Partisan is everything a Sharpe reader expects. The Inquisitor is left a loose end maybe to return if Sharpe ever returns to Spain. There is a great scene where the plunder the French has accrued in Spain changes hands to the British. Cornwell literary talents are on full display here almost enough to gain another star by itself!i only hope this was the rebound that Sharpe and the series needed to return to their previous prowess!
