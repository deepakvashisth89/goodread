My favorite MEB yet! This one was so sensational and soooo funny!! I laughed out loud so many times.

I wanted to add more to my review because this book deserves it. This would never pass in the Golden Age of Detective Fiction. The detective, while utterly delightful, is way too improbable and the whole story lacks realism (though it does have a fun Count of Monte Cristo vibe to it). I didn't know this going in, but I quickly realized it (given that the town name is Slopperton!). I found this offputting at first, but after the first several chapter (probably as soon as the detective came on the scene--seven-ish chapters in), I was hooked. There are some moments of real pathos in the story and the villain is positively chilling at times, but the overwhelming feel is a delightful romp. Don't worry about all the storylines and characters too, especially if a character pops in and you have no idea who he is. MEB wraps it up brilliantly and nothing is wasted. I seriously think I laughed as many times while reading this as reading Wodehouse. That is really saying something!
