I'm not 100% sure what to make of this short book of essays chronicling the seasons in Weweantic, Massachusetts. It was quite difficult to get into. The shortness of the essays (two to three pages each) made for a staccato reading rhythm that was annoying. It wouldn't let you in to inhabit this world. Additionally, one of the early essays, "The Standing Stones", I had heard on the radio a decade ago. In fact everyone at work heard it. I worked for the Massachusetts State Archaeologist at the time and the essay was about our office, but it wasn't a place any of us recognized or full of people we knew and we were left scratching our heads wondering if the story were true or fiction. And that's what I wondered as I read. Was this really a biography? How could you best categorize this odd work of 56 essays? Then half way through I tipped over into the world that Wasserman was describing, seeing the poetry of everyday life In difficult situations. The abruptness of the essays no longer bothered me and I longed to return to Massachusetts myself and eat the summer peaches and smell the salt air. All of a sudden I understood what I still could not categorize.
