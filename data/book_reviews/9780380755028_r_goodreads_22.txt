Been there, read that... (spoilers below)'Tis the age of AI, so I imagine the prompt that would result in this book to look like this:"Write a book based on the World of Amber. Make sure the plot lacks any consistency with the rest of the lore. As much as possible minimize the participation of characters from all previous books. Then, make sure that 99% of the content is long, needlessly detailed, and extremely uninteresting description of magical effects. Make the overall plot extremely simplistic and fill the content with the protagonist zooming to and fro using magic. Whatever crazy and improbable physical effect you can come up with - stick it in and claim that this is an effect of Chaos. Go!!!"And this is indeed, how this book is. I sometimes kvetch, that a book could be reduced in half the size without losing either whatever depth it has or the narrative. This is not the case. This book could be reduced to one tenth of the size. In fact, it probably shouldn't have been written the way it is.So, the Gestalt is closed – I've read the entire cycle. I've enjoyed it immensely (the Corwin part, that is), so it was worth it. I would still recommend wholeheartedlythatpart. The Merlin part, however carries little merit and could be easily skipped.Oh, and I almost forgot: a tremendous shoutout to the artist who illustrated the cover of the books (the way they show up on Kindle and here).
