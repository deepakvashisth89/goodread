I was right to listen to my gut feeling by postponing to continue reading this book series. What I heard from some people who read this was that this is a book you either absolutely love or hate—no in-between. They were right.Anne of Windy Poplars was not just underwhelming but it also didn't respect the reader's time. With over 250 pages it makes it a longer entry in this book series. Therefore it's even more outrageous that it's full of filler. Nothing of importance happened. I'm also sure that nothing in this book will be relevant for the later installments.Another reason why this ended up being a 1-star read is 'cause of the epistolary structure throughout the novel. This type of writing technique was most likely used to show that Gilbert Blythe is busy studying medicine at college and can't be around for this installment, but it doesn't change the fact that I dislike epistolary structure in books.L.M. Montgomery wrote Anne of Windy Poplars many years after the fifth book, making her unable to include anything in the fourth book that could potentially make room for plot holes or the like. What I'm wondering is why she wrote Anne of Windy Poplars in the first place? Perhaps it's the same reason why she wrote more than one book; social pressure. Originally the author only planned to write Anne of Green Gables and leave it as a stand-alone novel, but many readers wanted more. It wouldn't surprise me if this fourth book was solely created 'cause of the high demand for more.
