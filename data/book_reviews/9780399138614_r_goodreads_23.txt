_Curfew_ (titled _Crybbe_ in the UK), is a horror/dark fantasy novel from 1993 by British author Phil Rickman (1950-2024), who also published under the names Thom Madley and Will Kingdom. It’s a long book (625 pages) and can be at times a slow burn, but is very atmospheric, has a phenomenal sense of place, and has a very enjoyable build up to a catastrophic ending.The setting is and around the small Welsh town of Crybbe, a border town that at different times has been in England or in Wales. Described as not just sleepy but sullen and drained of life, it’s a joyless little town that were the people more welcoming, might be quaint with such things as its nightly ringing at 10pm of a bell one hundred times as part of a curfew (a curfew not really obeyed and meaningless to people from Off, that is outsiders, which can be people from just a few miles away). Though the town used to have a number of megalithic stones, all but one was removed centuries ago, the remaining one dominating the scenery and called the Tump and much disliked but also Not Talked About by the people of Crybbe.We have two disruptors appear in a town that “doesn’t like clever people.” We get Fay Morrison, a radio reporter who has moved back to care for her widower dad, a retired canon of the Anglican church. Fay is a curious woman, continues to work as a radio reporter for itty bitty Offa's Dyke Radio, and who doesn’t just accept certain things are a “Crybbe matter” and Aren’t Talked About, including weird things like the town has no dogs and we Don’t Talk About It and maybe some people regularly see ghosts and We Don’t Talk About It. The other is a billionaire record producer named Max Goff,, CEO of Epidemic Records, who wants to make Crybbe a New Age tourist mecca with things like restoring all the megalithic stones the townsfolk had removed, restoring an Elizabethan era mansion/farmstead known as Crybbe Court (rumored to be haunted, definitely a place where Many Murders were committed), and bringing in a bunch of New Age types like astrologers and Tarot card readers to become residents. Between Fay’s questions and Goff doing All Sorts of Things with his fortune, not only are the people of Crybbe unhappy but the two have awoken something very dangerous, both very human people with skeletons in closets and something supernatural.There are other characters that become important but all are ultimately entangled in the orbits of either Goff, trying to apparently undo things that were done in the past and ignorant of why they were done or of Fay, trying to makes sense of it all and if needed, fighting back against a rising supernatural threat and the all-too-human people benefitting from it.Sense of place of Crybbe was extremely well done and read true to me. I got the sense the author had researched quite a bit and I see he has written other works that used elements from this novel. There are a few loose ends at the end (one even being commented on by Fay and another character), but it has for the most part a definitive ending.
