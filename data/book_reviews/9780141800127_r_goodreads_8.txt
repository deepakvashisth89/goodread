“I never had any friends later on like the ones I had when I was 12 - Jesus, did you?”

I've been thinking about this book ever since I woke up, and I'm struggling to put into words what this book made me feel.

I think this book reminded me a lot of that invincibility we all felt when we were kids. How you take it for granted and assume it will still be there during teenage years and adulthood, but it's really not. And even though the four boys in The Body are no strangers to hardships and abuse, they still very clearly felt this protection, at least until they grew up.

I think King has a wonderful talent with words. You might be in a subway, on your way back home, but suddenly you are in a small town in Maine, following four boys on a journey from childhood to adolescence, remiscing about the innocence you've already lost.

There's nothing quite as powerful as the friendship between four boys, and the friendship between Gordie, Chris, Teddy and Vern was such a beautiful reminder of how simple my own friendships were when I was a kid. I miss that, I'm glad I read this book, I'm glad I got to remember parts of my childhood and its simplicity through this book.
