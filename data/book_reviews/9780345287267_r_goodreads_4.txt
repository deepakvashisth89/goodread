Thorne Smith'sThe Nightlife of the Godsis the literary equivalence of a Frank Capra's screw-ball comedy film. Smith loved his drunken debaucheries, his mean-spirited pranksters, his heathenistic mythological creatures and of course all that drinking and sex. Classy double-entendre styled sex of course. Thiswasthe early 20th century. Yet he was also making lots of social commentary, much of it skewering the American upper-class and their often hypocritical morals. Thorne Smith certainly knew the territory, having hung around Dorothy Parker and James Thurber, usually with way too many cocktails. His writings were often in the same territory as his colleagues. Smith is at his peak inThe Nightlife of the Godsas he successfully mixed zany humor with the battle of the classes and sexes. This is one of his best and I give it four and a half stars only because I enjoyed theTopperseries just a bit more.
