Stories of "hippies" spitting on soldiers returning from Vietnam circulated in the latter years of the war and after it ended. Syndicated newspaper columnist Bob Greene was curious about the spitting stories so he wrote a column asking Vietnam vets to write and let him know how they were treated. He got hundreds of responses telling how individual vets were greeted, including a number who were spat on. Many others weren't spat on, but were treated horribly. Some returning veterans told of being greeted happily. Greene was able to substantiate many of the stories and wrote "Homecoming" in 1989. Cheap copies are still available online.On a personal note, a few months after returning from Vietnam I was discharged and started college a week later on a conservative Midwest campus. How conservative? President Nixon chose that campus to give a speech defending the war in 1970. Most of my fellow students reacted with indifference or mild approval when they learned I was a Vietnam veteran. But a substantial minority treated me with open contempt. I was called a war criminal once in class by a male student who knew I was a veteran, but had no idea I had been to Vietnam. Another time a girl snarled "How many babies did you kill?" Nobody spat on me, but there were enough bad incidents that I carefully hid my veteran status for many years. To this day there are co-workers and friends from my 20s and 30s who have no idea I'm a Vietnam veteran.When the first Gulf War happened there was a complete reversal in public attitudes towards the military and veterans. From indifference or contempt, attitudes changed overnight to gratitude and respect. It was crazy! The sudden and complete turnaround made me deeply distrustful of public opinion. It can turn on a dime.Vietnam veteran and anti-war activist Jerry Lembcke wrote a 1998 book titled "The Spitting Image: Myth, Memory and the Legacy of Vietnam." In it he tried to prove the spitting incidents never happened because they were not documented in the media and because veterans and anti-war activists were natural allies. LOL! He tried to prove a negative.I haven't read the book, but will comment that anti-war demonstrations were so frequent and disruptive at Los Angeles International Airport that planes carrying soldiers home from Vietnam were diverted to military bases after 1968.I think Lembcke wrote his book to deflect guilt about how the anti-war movement treated Vietnam veterans. No doubt some in the anti-war movement considered veterans allies, but IMO most of them considered us either dupes or criminals.
