If a transplanted, confused, and hungover Victor Hugo or John Steinbeck, hard up for cash, agreed to reboot a class-conscious “Trailer Park Boys”…The Milagro Bean Field WarI treasure the very few books that have actually got me to laugh out loud. More importantly, the story offers a compassionate picture of dispossession, politics, generational guilt, and Mexican-American identity in the 1970s Southwest.Nichols clearly takes pleasure in the challenge of describing what is inside any character from his Russian-novel-length cast as he throws them into seemingly endless (and very believably absurd) combinations. The best of this novel is its dialogue and a narrator who never truly detaches from characters’ thoughts.Published in 1974, this book has a blind spot for strong women, with the exceptions of Ruby Archuleta, Nancy Mondragón, and Ursula Bernal. Neither are there any consistently heroic male characters, except for 93-year-old vigilante Amarante Cordova. The great and the disappointing visit each character as they stumble through a snowballing disaster of corporate water rights and agriculture schemes. If you read for human portraits and conversations, it is worth your time.Que viva Snuffy Ledoux!
