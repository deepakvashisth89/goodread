This book was fascinating. At first, I had a hard time taking in all of Lawrence's anger. Fortunately, Lawrence does more than just rant. He discusses the events in the Book of Revelations and then presents an alternative way of viewing the world.Despite the dozen years of private, Catholic schooling, I am fairly certain I have never read the Book of Revelation. I surely don't remember ever discussing it at school. This was really my first exposure to it. I don't agree with everything that Lawrence says, but I do think he creates a compelling argument. And, I certainly like his final commentary: "My soul knows that I am part of the human race, my soul is an organic part of the great human soul, as my spirit is part of my nation. In my own very self, I am part of my family. There is nothing of me that is alone and absolute except my mind, and we shall find that the mind has no existence by itself, it is only the glitter of the sun on the surface of the waters." He directs readers to "re-establish the living organic connections, with the cosmos, the sun and earth, with mankind and nation and family."
