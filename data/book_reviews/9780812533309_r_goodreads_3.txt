The concept sounds like alternative version of Elric of Melniboné but this really isn't Eternal Champion story written by Cook. The sword is alive, it eats souls and grants the wielder power. But that's about it. Lore is different.

I started to regret opening this book almost immediately. First chapter has more kingdoms, regions, cities and families than entirety of Black Company. There is no map and no glossary so the info dump was confusing and made me wonder what is the point - the book is a 250 page long standalone. Well, I guess author planned sequels but the book wasn't popular enough. The ending is conclusive but also open. Also, as the story goes every important name will be re-explained in context and so the confusion dissipates.

Story is mediocre. It does pick up after 50-ish pages but it's never a page turner. True to Cook's writing style, there is not a single proper description of a scene or of a person. It's a bit more clunky than what I remember from Black Company and annoyed me sometimes.

Our main character is a chosen one but unfortunately for him the chooser is a very nasty, jealous and blood thirsty goddess. The sword comes with its own dwarf as a companion and if you misbehave he will stab you in your sleep. The duo goes across kingdoms there and back fighting another ancient evil that seems to be just as nasty, jealous and blood thirsty as Suchara, the goddess who picked Gathrid to be her champion.

As is tradition there are no good guys. The story flows and bends as plot demands and I didn't bother to find plot holes but some of the things were very simplified (characters never lack money or food for example).

Overall it's alright. If it had just a little bit more engaging story or more dark humor (there's barely any) I would've liked this book a lot more. It just doesn't feel very inspired. Although had I read it in the 80's I'd probably rate it a bit higher. But fantasy has evolved a lot since then and later Cook's books were big part of that process.
