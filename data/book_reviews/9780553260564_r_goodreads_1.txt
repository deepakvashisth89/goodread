THE CLEANUP (2011) by John Skipp (Author), Craig Spector (Author)
My Review 3.4 Stars

These guys are amongst the frontrunners of modern horror. Skipp and Spector take you to the limits...then one step more. -- Clive Barker

This novel which was co-authored by the horror legend John Skipp was a Group Read selection for the month of January 2026 (Horror Book Club ). Craig Spector co-authored Skipp’s first novel, , which was snatched up by Bantam Books in 1984. It was published two years later in 1986 and sold over a million copies around the globe. Skipp’s dizzying list of accomplishments are too lengthy to enumerate in this review.
Skipp and Spector collaborated on a number of written works and several novels. This novel followed the fan favorite . It is not unreasonable to assume that fans of this writing team were expecting the same feverish and fiendish excitement which had thrilled them the first time around. My personal reading experience with I am afraid did not come close to my time spent with their first co-authored novel.

So okay? It was vampires the first time around. What topic got the creative juices flowing for this dynamic duo on their second meeting of the minds? The descriptive blurb on Amazon does not tell you much. The story takes place in the streets of New York City. A man named Billy Rowe is an eyewitness to the brutal murder of a young woman. The word in caps is bandied about The reader of the brief paragraph is left with the notion that this “Billy Rowe” dude is going to acquire some kind of power and subsequently go about

The main character is Billy Rowe, mid-twenties, deadbeat musician and witness to the bloody spectacle of a young woman’s bloody demise in the street. He and an acquaintance named Larry share an apartment which could be a photo op for the TV Show “Hoarded”. For those reading who are unfamiliar with the TV series “Hoarded” let it suffice to say that no free floor space existed. All items contained within the confines of the apartment were pitched, thrown, smashed, or ground into the floor. Billy’s hair, manner of dressing, shoes, and overall demeanor were in sync with the appearance and general sloppiness of his environment. A stunningly beautiful young woman is deeply in love with him (Mona). She is successful with a music career in high gear.

It is not my intention to reveal spoilers, plot twists, or any other information that could potentially spoil your reading experience. I will say that (as stated in the Description on line), there is a point in the story wherein Billy Rowe (described above) is made privy to an incredible “power”. Likewise, as stated in the short Description, Billy decides to do something about the crime in the city and the illegal activity going on in the streets that is harming innocent civilians.

This is a long book close to 400 pages in length. I liked the fact that it is formatted in short and clearly designated chapters, which is easier to read than some other formatting especially for older novels only digitalized in recent years.

This is not a new finding or something I did not notice and appreciate in their first collaboration () but the descriptive narrative in the novel is very well written. What was it the old timers said? The writer had a colorful “turn of phrase”? The writing style has a characteristic manner of expression which I truly enjoy. The way sentences are structured is never dull with these guys.

I was literally approaching the 50% mark in the book when I consciously decided that I was going to read the entire novel. It was my early opinion that the storyline was more suitable for late teens, early 20’s. There were additionally some pivotal parts of the plot that required suspension of belief that posed a genuine hurdle for me. Juvenile, DC and Marvel Comics, and just not material that would ordinarily entertain an adult reader. Conversely there were some pretty graphic depictions of sexual assault that rendered the narrative more of a mixed bag. Murders and mutilation were given explicit descriptions for the reader to pour over as well.

I already mentioned this is a long novel, and if the narrative was intriguing, and much of it was exactly so, I would read and cover a lot of pages without concern or thought. But much of the book darts around like a mouse in a maze and as a reader I became at first bored and second aware of the page number. In the final analysis the plot was undisciplined, and this resulted in a chaotic narrative a significant portion of the time.

Another issue I had with the storyline was the bleakness of it. I know that many readers will opine I am crazy to say such a thing. However, I found myself feeling empathy for characters a lot of the time, including the main protagonist, Billy. This was a paranormal plotline and the plot twist was not difficult to figure out. That said, I really did not like the ending despite the authors’ attempt to end the novel on a poignant note.

I rated the book 3.4 Stars for the reasons outlined above. I have no qualms about the talented writing of Skipp & Spector. I have already bought and intend to read it this year sometime.
