This book is meant to be an essay on how and why we (the readers... and humanity in general) should only give our unconditional affection to God, while everyone else should only receive a very limited amount of it. C.S. Lewis then goes the scientific route in trying to prove that such a mentality is in fact not only the natural order of things, but also a very healthy one.

Things start out well, with the first two parts debating parental affection and friendship vs love for God. Specifically, the need for a fine line to be drawn between loyalty and healthy critcism. Granted, I did skim the part where goes on and on about keeping the ultimate expression of love for God, but I liked his overall reasoning of not putting anyone on a pedestal.



The 3rd part was meant to talk about the limitations to be put on Eros (romantic love), but instead got bogged down in how it is sex. The final part of the book, arguably the most important of all, was meant to explain how (and why?) our love for God is meant to surpass every other type of love. Unfortunately it completely missed its target audience, ending up preaching to the choir. I'm sure that all the poetic waxing about ultimate rewards and such would've hit great for a true believer. As a very skeptical atheist however, of the arguments presented here seemed even marginally appealing to me.
