This book was a marathon to read. I was longing to be done with it while also feeling proud of my accomplishment at having made it however far I was at the time. Finally today I sat down and read 100 pages. It was “easy” to do in a way because all the plot threads were coming together with an awful finality, and I was feeling agitated until I knew the fate of each character. There is no doubt in my mind that this is a masterpiece of a book. And if I knew anything about 15th century Italian history, I’m sure it would be an even more compelling read. 😆 But boy did I bog down in the political chapters! Whenever Romola’s name appeared on the page again, I felt my interest perk up immediately. Romola is a masterpiece of a character and it was worth reading the novel simply for her.It was worth reading the novel for other reasons too, and I think I’ll suspend any rating until the group that’s reading this together has its discussion. I think hearing from other readers about this complex novel will help immeasurably.The historical figure Savonarola plays a big part in the novel and his portrayal and the moral/religious/ethical questions surrounding his character are fascinating. I had never heard of him before, and now I’m intrigued. His relationship with and effect on Romola are also well worth contemplation. Is he a positive or negative figure? It’s complicated. I enjoyed Eliot’s nuanced portrayal of him.Is it bad to say I could have done without Tito? Honestly I found him rather boring 80% of the time. He’s shallow and cunning and his treatment of Romola is cruel. His own lack of greatness of soul is painfully obvious in contrast to Romola’s beautifully depicted greatness of soul. Romola’s comparison to the Madonna is poignant in the novel and I think would be even more so on a re-read when I have more mental space to focus on details rather than simply on plot.I felt much more personal connection and investment in both the characters and plot in Middlemarch and especially in Daniel Deronda. In that respect, this novel is a far third for me with Eliot’s novels. But again, I eagerly await the discussion which I’m sure will illuminate the richness of this novel that is perhaps just beyond my ken.
