This was a well researched book about the British Royal Family “The Firm”. Donald Spoto does not provide us with any new or unknown details about the Windsors, however, what he does do is give us a detailed picture of the Royal Family and how stuck they are in: their history; their traditions; their sense of destiny; and their sense of their importance. It is unsettling how none of them from Victoria to Elizabeth II had a well grounded education or wide experience of the world that they lived in, to prepare them for the life that they would eventually lead. This was really only broken with Diana and how she tried to bring up the Princes William and Harry. In addition, no one in the Royal Family learnt from history, Elizabeth like Victoria before did not allow Charles to have any real involvement in the workings of the monarchy, there was little real thought given to what the role of the Princess of Wales would be in the 20thC, and any mental health problems that Princess Margaret had (and I am sure she did) were ignored and just brushed aside. It was even worse to realise that none of the monarchs from George V down to Elizabeth II had good relations with their children which has just perpetuated the patterns of distant aloof parents who really don’t know how to relate to their children (and this goes for Charles as well).
Two points of reflection. Firstly, the relationship between Charles and Camilla never stopped when he and Diana got married. It was well known within their circle, Diana’s famous comment “there were 3 people in this marriage and so it was a bit crowded” was not just hysteria, it was the truth. Secondly, I wonder what Donald Spoto would make of the Windsors now in the 21stC, with a new King, the decision by Harry and Meghan to leave the Firm and all the scandal surrounding Sarah and Andrew.
