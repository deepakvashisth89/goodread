I am so happy now, after spending some time in Maine this week, and getting to know the people who inhabit Dunnet Landing. I had no idea how much I was going to love this and I highly recommend it for anyone who loves a quiet, lingering, restful and pleasant book. You will get to know the people and the place so well and you will envy the unnamed narrator who has the pleasure of introducing them to us and fall in love with them just as she did while she was visiting.



This quote really sums up the mood and the atmosphere of this short novel and stories. We meet herbalist Mrs. Almira Todd, the landlady whose herb garden is central in concocting the many elixirs that help to heal the town’s bodily and spiritual ailments. Her mother, Mrs. Blackett, lives isolated on a nearby island, and is strong and independent. Her hospitality is truly one of a kind. Joanna Todd is Almira’s cousin who was spurned in love and has left the town to survive on Shell-heap Island, where she lives her own grief, silence and solicitude, as if in exile. There are the male characters who charm their way into our hearts. Captain Littlepage, a retired shipbuilder, recollects his memories of a sublime voyage to the Arctic. William Blackett is Mrs. Blackett’s son and Almira’s brother, who seems to be timid in society and lives with his mother. Elijah Tilley is a widower who seems to be diminished by the death of his wife whom he loved deeply but never fully appreciated in her life. He sat knitting while he spoke of his dearly departed.

Most of the characters are elderly who remember old loves and lost loves who are self-sufficient, prideful in their labors, and unburdened by their years. The narrator is a woman, a writer who befriends these inhabitants. I would love to have spent the summer in her place getting to know this lovely grouping of townspeople and hearing them talk and tell their stories. A bonus would be to be surrounded by the lush landscape and to sample to fresh catch each and every day!
