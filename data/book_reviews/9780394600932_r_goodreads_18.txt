her true identity. and we begin to understand that the real tragedy doesn't lie in the letter itself, but in the impure eyes that look upon it. as the townspeople watch their own sins reflected in hester’s body, the letter slowly turns into a mirror. in the piano, too, ada’s silence was in fact the loudest cry of all.we keep waiting for a confession, a scream, a moment of revenge. yet that long-awaited moment drifts away from us like a foggy harbor. hawthorne traps us inside this delay and the waiting begins to tick within our conscience like a clock. faith ceases to be a refuge and becomes a rusted shackle that binds the soul to its own desires. and the more we try to escape, the more the sound of those chains echoes through the depths of the forest.when i reached the end, i thought the weight in my chest might finally lift, but that relief never came. because in the silence echoing through that town square, we all know that no one is truly without sin.●okurken her satırda ruhuma batan o dikenleri hissettim;
