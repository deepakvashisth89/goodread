As always, it's 4 stars if this is your bag, maybe a 3.5 if it's not.The book gets points with me for being concise. No one ever needs 10 examples of a given point, and this text made it's argument and moved on. Understanding the origins of the LLC was interesting if for no other reason than to understand the world it left behind.I picked this book up because of the history of the company across time, and on the score it delivered. How culture affected LLCs in different countries was interesting, and the business case studies here were interesting though I'm not wildly impressed with that sort of thing.It's important to remember this was published in 2003, and in the past 22 years the role of business in the American mind has changed dramatically, so the book overly focuses on Sarbanes-Oxley, doesn't know about the coming financial meltdown, and is maybe a bit more politically tilted towards Bush-era talking points than it might have seemed at the time.Maybe the most interesting argument the book makes is this:"Hegel predicted the basic unit of modern society would be the State. Marx that it would be the commune. Lenin and Hitler that it would be the political party. Before that a succession of saints and sages claimed the same for the parish church, the feudal manor, and the monarchy. The big contention of this small book...is that the most important organization in the world is the company."From there they lay out a good argument for their case (I don't agree with it, but it is a good argument). If that sort of thing intrigues you, I'd pick up a copy.
