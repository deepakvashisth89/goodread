TW: SA

I appreciated the frank retelling of the events recorded from the trials for the majority of this book - the facts alone portray the level of tragedy.

However, I found myself becoming increasingly frustrated at the author’s presumptions cropping up towards the end of the book. Especially so regarding the experiences of the women on the island. None of the small minority of surviving women, most only kept alive as s*x slaves (which the author cowardly refers to as “kept for common service”), were permitted to be heard and recorded during trials, yet the author infers that their suffering was presumably better than the men on the island. Where is the accuracy in a making a comparison between which group suffered the most, when one group’s experience was suppressed from records? Even if all survivor accounts were recorded, comparing who suffered less out of those who lived in fear of potentially being murdered and those who were forced into becoming s*x slaves is an unfair one to make.

This one unfair comparison overshadowed the entire book for me. I found it difficult to finish after that.
