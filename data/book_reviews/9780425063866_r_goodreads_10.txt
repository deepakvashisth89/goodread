I’m a sucker for the “big, dumb object” trope in science fiction; two of the first science fiction novels I read as a kid were rendezvous with Rama and ringworld. This being written less than ten years after them gave me some expectations.

There’s a lot to like about this book and while I definitely enjoyed it, there were some things that diminished the overall experience.
The set up is great, a mission to Saturn discovers this structure and gets forcibly taken into it. Once the crew is inside, some aspects of the book reminded me a lot of Phillip Jose Farmer’s book: to your scattered bodies go, which I did not like. These people wake up inside the structure, naked and changed in some ways. There’s a real over emphasis on sex that seemed unnecessary, and continued to the point of graphic sexual violence. I’m not clutching pearls about it, it just didn’t seem to make a lot of sense to the overall story.

The world building was interesting, there was a fever dream/fantasy aspect to it that was enjoyable. What I really liked was the quest undertaken by the main character: captain cirocco “rocky” jones and her lesbian simp sidekick.
She is determined to get to the control centre of the structure; a long and dangerous journey.

Maybe if I’d read this when I was younger I’d appreciate it more but I think the main thing I liked about Rama and ringworld was that they end the books with huge questions intentionally unanswered. They make you think about all the possibilities for different kinds of life. In the conclusion of titan, many of the mysteries of the book are answered in an imaginative but expository way that I’m accustomed to reading in more current science fiction.
So, I’m on the fence about this one; overall I liked it, but I’m not sure I’m going to read the sequels.
