“ALL I HAVE IS MY TYPEWRITER AND MY FEAR”

Mountain Climbing in Sheridan Square (1988), Stan Leventhal’s first novel, and Skydiving on Christopher Street (1995), his last novel, are significant bookends to his literary output. In Mountain Climbing in Sheridan Square, AIDS is just beginning to rear its ugly head. In Skydiving on Christopher Street, AIDS is a given. The two novels give matter-of-fact accounts of an unnamed narrator’s life in Manhattan during the late 1980s and early 1990s. The narrator’s apartment that overlooks Sheridan Square is his ground zero. Stan Leventhal lived in an apartment above Sheridan Square. In Skydiving on Christopher Street, the narrator says, “All I have is my typewriter and my fear.” This reminds me of Robert Giard’s photograph of Leventhal at his typewriter looking out the window that appears on the cover of Mountain Climbing in Sheridan Square. You can’t separate Stan Leventhal from his narrator.

Leventhal’s writing is clear, direct, and unpretentious. Skydiving on Christopher Street makes an impact in spite of (mostly because of?) its understatement. The word “skydiving” never appears in the text. Leventhal doesn’t have to develop the metaphor. After the narrator finds out that he’s HIV-positive, he says, “An abyss opens before me and I can feel myself falling.” The novel is not without humor, particularly the scene at Jones Beach, where the narrator and his friend Lenny hilariously encounter a flock of hungry seagulls. “This is like a Hitchcock film,” Lenny says.

Leventhal includes wonderful period details; for example, “The clock on the VCR tells me Jon will be home from the gym very soon.” And, the narrator refers to Jon as his “lover,” a word that is rarely used today, if at all. The narrator’s adventures in editing porn magazines titled Manmeat and Big Boys are fun, but he shows us how this supposedly sexy job can also be boring and aggravating, as any job can be. In real life, Stan Leventhal was an editor on those glorious magazines Torso, Honcho, and Mandate.

As the narrator vividly describes, AIDS drastically changes Christopher Street: ”[L]ike a tsunami of polluted water, something washed over Christopher Street, destroying its vibrant colors, leaving behind the detritus of tragedy and despair.” But he doesn’t despair for long. Soon, he proclaims: “[N]ow the streets belong to us again . . . Our blood fills the cracks in the pavement. It’s ours and we’re never going to give it up." The narrator, and Stan Leventhal, come to terms with, and bravely accept, where their lives have led them.

Stan Leventhal was lost to AIDS in January, 1995, a couple of months before Skydiving on Christopher Street was published by Hard Candy Press. Copies of the novel have been scarce and expensive on the used book market. Kudos to ReQueered Tales for bringing this novel and other works by Leventhal back into print.
