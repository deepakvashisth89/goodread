**MINOR SPOILERS**
This book is the part of the Amsterdam Cops/Grijpstra - DeGier series.
The commisaris of a murder unit in Amsterdam finds out that his sister’s husband has died in Maine. Suzanne, his sister, asks him to come and help her move back to Amsterdam. He agrees, but his co-workers worry about him going alone. They go to the police chief who helps them set up a police training situation where DeGier goes to the same area. It is interesting to read about the contrast between the police in the US (Maine specifically) and Amsterdam.
At first, the book has a deceptively simple prose style; very straightforward and clean. But as you read, you realize that the commisaris and DeGrier are both clever and see hidden meanings and they also see the ugly motives of people in the town. As the book continues, the ugliness becomes more apparent as more motives and crimes stack up. It was an interesting writing style.
Once they are both there, they realize that there is a serial killer active in the area and that Suzanne’s husband is likely a victim. They begin to investigate.
Suzanne is a whiner and the commisaris wants to be with her in small doses and, as a reader, you do as well. She cries constantly and is a bit impractical with how she wants to handle her husband’s affairs.
Spoiler… There is a scene where DeGrier has s*x with Madelin, who was a suspect. I did not agree with that action. He found her very attractive and she reminded him of a damsel in distress from a favorite childhood book. But she was not a damsel in distress in any way but was a bit of a self centered predator and he was aware of that. I did not think it was good police procedure.
The book fell to 1 star just after that as DeGrier tells the sheriff he had s*x with Madelin. The sheriff asks: “She was a good lay, was she?” DeGrier responds “yes” (which might not have been completely truthful as the author describes Madelin as passive during the encounter and that DeGrier felt he was in an ad). But then the sheriff says, “I haven’t had the pleasure… Maybe I’ll have it later, but I’ll have to get in her way. You sure got yourself in her way.” (The investigation may have triggered Madelin’s seducation.) Yes, this might reflect how some people would behave but I found it distasteful and unprofessional. I like the detectives in a book series to act with a bit of honor or I won’t continue to read the series. A few pages later, the commisaris wondered what it was like when DeGier had s*x with Madelin but then thinks about the fact that he was not jealous. I had been thinking that the commisaris was a good fellow, but this made me question that understanding. Finally, there is a reference to a case in Amsterdam where the prosecutor did not pursue a rape charge because of the way the victim had been dressed. Put all together, I do not recommend the book.
At the end, DeGrier knows that the commisais has figured out who the villain is. He asks for the answer and the commisaris says that he should figure it out himself. This can be a type of teaching style, but I thought it was an interesting way to handle the situation.
I read this book about 45 years after it was published.
I do not plan to continue reading this series.
