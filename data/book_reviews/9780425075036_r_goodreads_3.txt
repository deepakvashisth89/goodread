They were all listening to Duke Anderson as he plotted the heist: NYPD, FBI, Federal Bureau of Narcotics, Secret Service, IRS, Bureau of Customs, FDA, FTC, SEC, NYS Liquor Authority. Bugs were everwhere: Taps on pay phones, a transmitter concealed under a car dashboard, a voice-actuated tape recorder installed in the neighbors linen closet, a telescopic microphone concealed in wooded high ground in Central Park, etc. This might seem a far-fetched, but law-enforcement went wiretap-crazy in the late 60s in response to organized crime (including the yippies and Black Panthers).Duke came from Kentucky mountain-man stock which gave him certain advantages over the shifty New Yorkers. But Duke didn't know he was being bugged and he also didn't know that the 251st Precinct was run by Captain "Iron Balls" Delaney.I have little patience for gimmicks and conspiracies, but by the time I finished the 94 short chapters, I was completely sold. Sanders truly created a one-of-a-kind crime thriller.
