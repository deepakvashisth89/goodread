The second of the long-running Brunetti series and here we see Leon introducing a more politicised element to her emotive story-telling: issues of the American army in Europe, of toxic waste and high-level cover-ups merge with more domestic scenes in Venice. Especially potent are the portraits of a Sicilian mother and her petty criminal son; and the ambiguous activities of Brunetti’s own father-in-law.Offsetting the darker elements are scenes of comedy in Brunetti’s ongoing feud with Patta, his boss; and the wonderful warmth of his family life. Nuanced and atmospheric, this is crime writing lit up with wit, intelligence, subtlety and humanity.
