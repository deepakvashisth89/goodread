Started a little slow and heavy-handed. It is quite obviously meant to be discussing 9/11 through Vietnam, which made me roll my eyes and set the book aside for a bit. However, as Molly begins thinking through the war and seeing it from many perspectives, she sees how complicated the war is and begins to draw her own conclusions, rather than be influenced by her peers or parents.

I was really emotional--like lump-in-throat, tears welling emotional--when Patrick finally gets to call home. It just reminded me of my mom talking about her dad calling from Vietnam.

Loved all the VA hospital stuff. They were the best parts. The school stuff was terrible, but mostly because it reminded me of the ways adults fail kids by giving them simplistic answers, e.g. "Communism is bad," without contextualizing it for them. Teachers especially fail their students when they don't take their questions seriously or nurture their curiosity. Also, the book touches on the strain and real emotional toll on students w/ family in Vietnam. I would have liked more of that.

Anytime Molly discusses race, it's just awful. It's not clear if that's a failing on the author's part or the view of race from a teenager in a highly racist city or what, but...oof.
