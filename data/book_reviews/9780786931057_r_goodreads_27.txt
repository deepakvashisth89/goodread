I've been reading all the Forgotten Realms novels as nostalgia for the ones I loved as a child. Finally got back to this after accepting that maybe once I've read a couple books by one author and hated them it's okay to not read the rest if they're so bad I struggle to read them... This isn't one of the ones I read back then, so there's no nostalgia for this one in particular.Overall, this is better than some Forgotten Realms novels, worse than others, thus earning its middling score. First, the characters. For the non-spoilery version, I do have enough lore knowledge to know what certain characters would become, so that may have colored my expectations a bit. I found Cyric and Adon interesting, Bane was exactly as expected for a fantasy dark lord style pure evil villain in a good way, Mystra and Kelemvor were insufferable but did actually have personalities, and Midnight was bland in that way you get with the stereotypical strong female character of older fantasy novels.For the more spoilery, in depth version-Kelemvor is a deeply frustrating character.Knowing who he becomes, I'd honestly expected a very different character. He's a sexist who has very strong ideas about what is and isn't women's work. Note that this is someone we're supposed to root for, who works in a city run by a woman and has an old ex-friend with women in that ex-friend's adventuring group. Yes, there's likely still sexists in that scenario, but the only time he reflects on this later in the novel is to go 'Midnight isn't like the other girls, she's badass and feminine!' Secondly, he's got a greed/curse plotline that ultimately falls rather flat. Specifically, if he performs a selfless act without compensation he turns into the beast and is forced to kill. The first time we see this fully happen it's clearly painful but he basically takes down a deer and returns to normal. It's unclear if he would have even harmed the person who came across him if they hadn't fled, or if he has enough control to direct who he kills. Anyway, he obsesses over getting his rewards and will not do anything without a reward, including, in a flashback of his, save the lives of people he cares about multiple times over. The issue is, becoming the beast is not a terrifying enough event to justify his behavior if he can get away with killing a deer for 'killing someone' and if he has control. It makes him ultimately fall rather flat. Hopefully he improves later on.Midnight is annoyingly bland, and honestly I don't think there's even anything I can say about her worth spoilering. She's ambitious, and there's a little bit of spite to her (desire to cook terribly for the party to get back at Kelemvor for being sexist) but other than that she's a very hollow character. She doesn't really seem to feel emotions other than being horny for Kelemvor.Cyricis actually a reasonably interesting character, and I didn't expect to like him so much going in knowing lorewise what becomes of him. He serves as a look into the system of the Zhents from a character no longer associated with them, which is a rare thing in the Realms, and even rarer is that he didn't also do a full sail 'I'm a perfect good guy now' flip. He's still a scheming rogue who is very cynical with some bloodthirst, but he's also someone who had a rough childhood in Zhentil Keep, was indoctrinated into the thieves, and is trying to avoid his past catching up to him. Sure, it wasn't a novel story even at the time, but that's because it's an intriguing story. I don't love that we fill in the pieces with a lore dump to Midnight, but I do like that the reason for the lore dump is an odd sort of reciprocity with Cyric basically going 'hey I accidentally overheard one of your secrets here have mine now we're even.' It just says a lot about how he thinks. I hope that his fall to what he becomes is done well, because in this book, he has nuance.Adon has layers to him. Not all of those layers are great, but he's genuinely a pretty neat character. He's vain and talks too much and a bit of a playboy (one element of the initial backstory is that he literally seduced a member of the conspiracy's wife to get information out, a role typically only played by female characters even in modern novels), but he's also genuinely kind-hearted and loyal.He's the first to figure out Kelemvor's secret and chooses not to reveal it until it becomes a safety issue. He's also grappling with what it means to be a cleric in the Time of Troubles, because he can't heal anyone anymore, or cast any other spells, only use his warhammer, and while he's good with his warhammer he frequently feels useless, and falls into a deep depression only worsened by when the one thing he really seems to value about himself is taken away from him. He's experiencing a crisis of faith, and is in general one of the characters that seems to experience emotion the most strongly in this novel, which is important when your gods literally left the heavens and nothing makes sense anymore.As for plot and pacing... Uncovering the Zhentish conspiracy in Arabel probably should have been part of the text instead of backstory. This would have given Kel, Adon, and Cyric reasons of their own to pursue Bane, then they could have hooked up with Midnight and Caitlin later on or something. As it is the plot starts kind of weakly. Next, the random monster attacks are kind of weak story-wise. Either use them to reinforce 'oh no we need to stabilize magic' or 'oh no Bane is after us'. Then, the scenes in Bane's Castle and immediately following should have been a climax, not a midpoint. I mean, you're seriously going tokill off a godin the middle of a novel? Yeah, you'd have to change the name if 'go to Shadowdale' was the sequel hook, but it would have made for a better story. Instead the actual sequel hook relies on the people of Shadowdale being stupid enough to think thatElminster is dead. Let's be real here. In other novels/ sourcebooks / whatever these are intelligent characters that have lived in Shadowdale a long time and are fully aware of how powerful Elminster is (it's not like he isn't already a badass going into this story), and they didn't find a body. If anything they should be reassuring the party members that there's no way Elminster is dead.. Anyway, yes, the second half doesn't really mesh cleanly with the first. But there's a big battle, and you have to finish with a big battle, right? There's a bit of character development in the second half but not a lot of plot.They never did figure out who killed the clerics of Tymora did they? I suppose finding the spies or infiltrators would have been inconvenient for the sequel hook.Other nitpicks I don't know where to put-Why the fuck do people keep using magic when magic is going wrong? Seriously, you'd think after a few times they'd start checking themselves no matter how much habit it is. But instead you get Midnight at several points in the book going 'oh magic is going wrong maybe I should stop using it' and then using it again in a couple pages. Seriously? There's still someone who uses it towards the end and then is surprised when it misfires on him. Also, why doesn't magic go wrong for Elminster? Yes, I realize he's Elminster, biggest badass ever and whatever, but seriously, if magic is going wrong for the literal goddess of magic it ought not to be fine for him, that's just cheap.Don't name one character Thurbrand and one Thurbal if you can get away with it. It makes it hard to follow.Also don't name a character Tempus in a setting with a Tempus god where the gods are walking the earth. I spent ages wondering if there was more to Tempus Blackthorne...Dumb injury timelines. You're telling me thstKel losing temp use of his arm and Cyric taking a bad injury himself are either no or a short period of unconsciousness, while a slash to the face puts Adon out for well over a day? Nah you just didn't want to deal with the fallout so you decided he'd remain unconscious until it became convenient for the story.
