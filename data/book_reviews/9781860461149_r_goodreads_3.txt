This book has been called "The Danish Gormenghast," which intrigued me. I love Denmark. I loveGormenghast. Sign me up!It started strong. The opening stories had a fairy-tale quality that felt very much like a journey through a nation's collective unconscious. To my delight, there were echoes of Andersen. And it was because of Andersen's influence that I gave Høeg the benefit of the doubt when he started talking shit about spoiled princesses...which segued into talking shit about eating disorders...which segued into the same old, same old about sexual abandon being the true mark of goodness and authenticity in a woman, at which point the book had become just a boring story about a boy going off to school, and if you want to know what happens in the last 50 pages, you'll just have to ask someone else.(So I guess it's the DanishTitus Alone.)
