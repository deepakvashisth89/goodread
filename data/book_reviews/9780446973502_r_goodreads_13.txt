Robert Jastrow was an educated man who played an important role at NASA. In this book he gives a good, readable history of how the "Big Bang" theory came into being. His explanation of the expanding universe and how the red shift shows the speed at which a galaxy is traveling away from earth are excellent. On page 113 he admits that "There is a kind of religion in science; it is the religion of a person who believes there is order and harmony in the universe. Every event can be explained in a rational way as a product of some previous event; every effect must have its cause; there is no First Cause." What the "Big Bang" theorist believes about the beginning of the universe is exactly like what a Christian believes about the beginning of all things; neither of the beliefs can be proven by scientic experiment. The atheist and agnostic have theories that sound right to them, and that they believe. The Christian has the word of God. What is the first, eternal Thing? Not the Universe, for it had a beginning both for the atheist and the Christian. But what about before the beginning of the universe, before the Bbig Bang"? Only the Bible provides a reasonable answer. The First Cause was of infinite power and infinite wisdom that can be seen in what He created. "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth," i. e., the universe. Jastrow' s book is good and of value for what it teaches about the rise of the "Big Bang" theory and the men whose discoveries led to formulation of the theory. But remember, the "Big Bang" theory is just that - a theory, not fact.
