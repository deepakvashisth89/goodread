The serpent's egg focuses on realistic character behavior and a magically adventurous plotline - this fantastic and suspenseful story was incredibly difficult to put down, and I often caught myself laughing aloud as I read it, or fearing for the characters.A (thought to be) normal girl, Miranda, works out to be a wonderful heroine in the novel. Her closely knit friendships with other courageous characters, along with their interactions and development make this book much more enjoyable than the average fantasy.The world is deceptively 'normal,' until the release of a great malevolent demon sparks the interaction between two previously separated worlds - one of the humans, and one for those who possess magic. There are limited ways to access each world, but they do exist, and a perfect example would be the one located beneath the parliament buildings in Ottawa, Canada.When a mysterious man comes from this other world, which the humans had long forgotten about, Miranda finds herself the target of not just him (who is trying to save her), but that of the blood-thirsty demon as well. Not only that, but her forever-loyal friends chase after the pair in an effort to save Miranda from something they do not yet understand. This is the beginning to a tale of, classically, the forces of good combating those of darkness, intense battles, brave and empathetic heroes, and cold-hearted villains.This story will leave you wishing that you could have been included in the adventure, and hurrying to pick up the next novel, The Burning Crown, as soon as possible.J. Fitzgerald Mccurdy is a most brilliant writer, and the only complaint I could fairly voice would be that her story ended far too fast!The Serpent's Egg Trilogy is a series I would highly recommend; not only to younger children, but teenagers as well.
