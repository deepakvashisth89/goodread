This review is of “Hudson!”, book #1 of 5 in the “Whitewater Dynasty” series by Helen Lee Poole.
The Story: The book begins with an introduction to the heroine of the book and series, Abigail Brewster. Abby lives with her mother and father and hopes to marry her sweetheart, Daniel Williams. Those plans, however, go horribly awry when Abby’s village is attacked by Onandaga Iroquois Indians. While Abby endures emotional, mental, and physical degradation from her Indian captors, on a ship heading to New France-Canada- is Edouard de Fournet, the hero of the book. Edouard had a reason for leaving his native France; hint; it wasn’t voluntary. Edouard arrives in what is now Quebec hoping to establish a trading business.

Abby earns the respect of the Onandaga, becomes their blood relative and marries a Iroquois brave, Corn Dancer. Things go well for Abby for a while, until she refuses to participate in the torture of two captives, one Indian, one white. Abby is shunned after this, and later escapes the Onandaga.

Abby and Edouard don’t meet each other until nearly the end of the book, when Abby nurses him back to health after he is shot. Later, Colonel George Washington “encourages” Edward-now using an anglicized version of his name-and Abby to marry. They do, and Edward, now using the last name Forny, begins building his business.

Upside: The book is well researched, and I learned about Iroquois Indians as well as colonial America and what is now Canada.

Downside: Ms. Poole’s writing style is very “on the surface”. I never felt a connection between Abby and Edouard/Edward, either as individuals or as a couple. (To be fair, the book is not categorized as a romance, but as “historical fiction.) This made the book a chore to read, which is not a good thing at all.

Sex: The sex scenes are pretty mild. There is no real heat in them at all.

Violence: Assault, shooting, killing and 2 fairly graphic torture scenes.

Bottom Line: It’s not my normal fare, but I have all 5 books in the series and I will plow through them. Just not with much enthusiasm.
