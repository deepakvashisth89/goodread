Nice action/suspense thriller that was certainly timely when it was first published back in 1992. Christie, a former Marine officer, begins the story with the bombing of a U.S. warship, which predates the attack on the U.S.S. Cole in 2000. Flush with the success of the first Gulf War the Pentagon believes itself completely in charge of the world situation. A handful of Iranian terrorists demonstrate that this is not the case.It was probably difficult for readers, back in those pre-9/11 days to believe a scenario such as Christie devised. Over a dozen foreign nationals, using safe houses and equipment (much of it acquired here in the U.S.) able to plot and carry out a terrorist attack on military and civilian targets. While the plot here is one of a commando attack on a well-known American landmark, and not the use of commercial aircraft, the idea that this sort of attack could take place would seem too far-fetched. I can only assume that back then many readers would have put the book aside and not given it a second thought. As things stand now, the events in the book take on an additional impact, since it’s easy to visualize a scenario much like that which Christie puts together.Remarkably, Christie is actually able to make Ali, the chief of the commando unit, come across as both genuinely threatening, yet at the same time sympathetic. Christie allows Ali to reveal how he came to hate America and Americans with such vehemence. Having grown up in poverty in his native Iran, Ali is finally able to attend an American college following the down throw of the Shah and the Iranian revolution. Attending school at the time of the Iranian hostage situation, Ali finds himself the target of bigoted fellow students as well as the police. The attack on American soil is more then a political act for Ali it is personal vengeance against those who humiliated him years before.In this his first novel, Christie displays great skill as a writer and as someone able to understand both the military mindset and that of those who would bring “the war to our shores.” As a Navy vet myself, it was easy to visualize the scenes on the American warship both before and after the explosion. The adrenaline rush and sheer panic that sailor’s feel when the alarms go off in the middle of the night. Rushing about in almost total darkness in an area normally familiar, suddenly to find it now full of dangers and the possibility of death. My own sea-going deployments allowed me to personally experience the marine troop spaces that Christie describes. They were claustrophobic even in daytime, so it’s easy to imagine how they must seem to inexperienced men in such a situation.Christie’s writing is fast-paced and keeps the action moving throughout. His personal knowledge of military equipment and strategy brings everything to vivid life.
