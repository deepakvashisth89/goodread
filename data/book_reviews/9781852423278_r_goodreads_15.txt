“Do your daughters wake up sweating with nightmares of black savages when all along it was you.” In American Dreams, Sapphire neglects the hand of the reader held out asking for a tour. Sapphire drags the reader into not only her truth, battle, struggles and conflicts but the conflict of being a Black Woman in a country that cast doubt on Black Woman victimhood. She brings readers into Black Voices that never shined and have since faded with the headlines or lack of headlines, and she brings them back with the demand they deserve. The demand like LOOK AT ME. YOU MUST LOOK AT ME. And Sapphire uses brute and direct language that is necessary to not only get the reader to pay attention but America as well.
