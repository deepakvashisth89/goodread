I was looking for a historical mystery series to replace Ellis Peterson’s Brother Cadfael books, set in 11th century Shropshire. I came across this book by Bruce Alexander placed in the hubbub of a messy, poor, and rich, clean and dirty London of the 18th centurpy London. Here, at least one of our heroes is the blind John Fielding, magistrate of the Bow Street Court and a kind of detective-in-chief. The other hero is the enchanting narrator, Jeremy Proctor, who is reading law under Judge Fielding and is also his intelligent, active, and self-possessed, but sometimes easily embarrassed 17 year-old assistant. The great strength of Jeremy’s depiction is not only his excellent performance of his duties, his straightforward stalwartness, but also his prickliness and feelings of insecurity about how his master regards him. Additionally, he is charmingly shocked and embarrassed by his fascination with the opposite sex, which is perhaps, in his view, perhaps unworthy of his position. This all adds up to a remarkably accurate portrayal of a hardworking and serious person who is also still a teenager.

One of the characteristics of the book that is ambiguous is that, while the narrator is in the present and accurate, the narration is clearly meant to be regarded as written. And the question is whether it is story that Jeremy tells some years after the events. While the story is enjoyable and in a great voice, this has me knocking about somewhat.
