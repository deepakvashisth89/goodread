3rd person/some swearing/racial or class disharmony

My rating for this book is for the series as a whole. This book, the first in a trilogy, gets a 2/5 rating, mostly because of the quality (or lack thereof) of the writing. It's written 3rd person but not in the modern way where we follow the story from one person's POV and get their thoughts and feelings. Instead we get anybody's thoughts, feelings, descriptions of things the main character could not see from her vantage point, etc. It reads very blah and meh. It's like we are watching TV or a movie and the camera will show us stuff that the main character can't see. I guess I'm spoiled from the modern type of 3rd person that is from one person's POV and we only get to see what that character can see, hear, etc. We only get that person's thoughts and feelings. So reading this book was sometimes a chore. I almost DNFed it but I'm glad I stayed the course because there's some interesting world-building throughout the trilogy.

Our main character is Lily who runs away from home because her father wants her to get a job in the family business and she doesn't want to. She goes to see her teacher of martial arts but arrives just in time to see some aliens capture her teacher and take him off in a ship. On the spur of the moment she decides to go after them and save her teacher.

On her journey she meets and befriends a young Ridani girl. The Ridani are the down-trodden of society, given the worst jobs, given negative slurs. They are distinctive because of their full-body tattoos that are part of their sort-of religion, their belief that one day a certain man will lead them back to the green grass lands (which is Earth, though they have forgotten through the generations and only have folk songs).

And a ton more stuff happens that I think I won't spoil for you. I will say that there is a certain character who is more than they seem. And that Lily discovers tatters of information about the past that comes to the fore in the other books.
