I can’t say if I liked this book, but I read it in one sitting. I was between a 2 and a 3, but based on how I felt reading it I went down to a 2.Following the story of an older man who lost his family many years ago, we see how he lives a self-sustaining life in rural Maine, attempting to subdue his grief with routine and isolation. When a young pregnant woman with a bruised eye stumbles upon his cabin while fleeing her husband, what started as a temporary stay evolved into cohabitation.I read another review that I find myself agreeing with. I hoped it would be a story of healing, which it is in a way. However, when he began (and kept) having sexual fantasies about the young girl half his age who was still married, I feel like the story completely lost me.The writing style is extremely detailed, and almost too graphic at times when it came to the animals and the girls backstory. That’s not something that bothers me normally, but I think the overall feel of the book was dark as a result of how consistently graphic everything was.I enjoyed the details about how he lived a sustainable life living off the land, with his own sheep, gardening and the focus on self-sufficiency. I feel like those moments highlighted how when things are difficult, enjoying the little things can help you get by. When he started getting back into the bag pipes, it was a great way to show how much he was going back to the person he was (and the level of detail on the topic was also interesting). Her struggle with the decision to stay true to her beliefs or not was a major theme that kept me wanting to see the story through. Their transparent conversation about what each had been through was also a big highlight, and I did enjoy their dynamic when it wasn’t about his fantasies.Sadly, it was just was not my cup of tea. I’m sure others would like it, but it was not for me.
