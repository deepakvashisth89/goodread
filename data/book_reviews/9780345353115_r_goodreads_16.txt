This is the second Heinleinian juvenile. While formally standalone, they informally show the expansion of mankind in the universe. I read it as a part of the buddy reads for November 2025 at Hugo & Nebula Awards: Best Novels group.

In this novel I guess RAH decided to evade problems of his first juvenile Rocket Ship Galileo. He makes heroes older (so they don’t need parents’ agreement to do stuff), puts it further into the future (at the same time anchoring it with the date) and replaces an ‘old wise man’ with an institution and its representatives, quite possibly borrowing from his time in the Navy.

This is year 2075 AD and young Matt Dodson applies to join the prestigious Interplanetary Patrol. The Patrol, whose motto is and which is probably built upon the UN world police force briefly mentioned in the previous novel. This is an idea RAH actively backed (there are his several articles on the question) in 1945-1949: to give all nuclear arsenal to the UN to guarantee peace. In this book, one of the duties of the Patrol is to monitor nuclear warheads orbiting Earth (and we can suppose that they were used at least once - ). So, back to the story. Matt befriends fellow recruits Texan William "Tex" Jarman, Oscar Jensen from Venus, and Pierre Armand from Ganymede. They pass a number of physical and psychological tests, are accepted as cadets and have a few missions, varying from searching for a missing Patrol ship to establishing diplomatic relations with Venerians.

A great nostalgia read!
