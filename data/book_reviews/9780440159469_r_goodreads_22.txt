The vicar is not surprised when the most hated man in of St. Mary Mead is found dead. Even the vicar agrees that the world is a much better place without Colonel Protheroe in it. Two people even confess to committing the crime, and yet they both appear to be innocent. With the list of suspects being so long, Miss Marple decides to help the police investigation by doing what she does best.


This is exactly what you’d expect from a cozy mystery story. We have a slow but steady pace that allows us to slowly familiarize ourselves with the setting and the characters. There’s a small village setting where everybody knows each other. We know the killer is amongst the people we meet. And sometimes the characters stand out more than the murder mystery itself. Like, there’s so much gossip that the dialogue overflows with it.


Miss Marple is very different from Poirot. She’s the smart and cunning amateur sleuth who manages to stay under everybody’s radar because she’s an old and gentle soul. She seems to aspire to be nothing more than a fly on the wall at times. While she doesn’t always stand out, she’s still noticeably there. And she’s become very good at observing people over the years, noticing little details that others may miss. She lurks in the shadows looking for breadcrumbs that will lead her to the killer. She always seems to be snooping around at just the right time too, because her curiosity demands that she knows about everything that happens in the village. So you can leave it to her to crack the case.


Not a murder mystery that is designed to grab you by the throat. The first Miss Marple full length novel is a cozy mystery novel in heart and soul. I must admit that it feels a bit too long for my personal taste. But it’s a good introduction to Miss Marple as a character. And it’s both a relaxing and interesting story for readers who like to challenge their inner sleuth.
