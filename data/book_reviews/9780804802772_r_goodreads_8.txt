Kawasaki spends most of the book really going into detail on all the flaws and failures of Japan and the Japanese, it's a weird book. He devotes a lot of time to deriding the Japanese inferiority complex in their attitude to the West, you can't help but think his thoughts are coming from the same place.

At the same time, it's pretty informative on a number of historical points and not exclusively negative. Even through the negatively, you can form a reasonably coherent view of how Japanese society evolved through the 1900s, and understand how it was functioning prior to the economic success of the 70s and 80s.

Worth mentioning that it's openly racist at many points in a quite manner-of-fact way (towards the Japanese mostly, but not exclusively) - much more so than other books I've read from the 60s.
