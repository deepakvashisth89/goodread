Those of us who are addicted to courtroom dramas will find this book highly satisfying. You’ll have to suspend disbelief in a place or two, but I think that’s normal for this series.The second subplot in the story was less-than rewarding to read, but it fortunately wasn’t a large enough piece that I felt distracted or interrupted much.Ben Kincaid’s boyhood episcopal priest didn’t kill female members of his congregation, but the cops said he did, and they came up with enough evidence to arrest and try him for the murders. This priest is progressive even for a bunch of Episcopalians. He engages in what some would insist are unpriestly behaviors with members of his congregation, particularly women. Admittedly, you can engage in unpriestly behavior without committing murder, and poor Ben Kincaid and his staff worked to convince a jury that the two can be separate.I spent a quiet unstructured Saturday reading this, and I thoroughly enjoyed the experience. The audio narration was excellent.
