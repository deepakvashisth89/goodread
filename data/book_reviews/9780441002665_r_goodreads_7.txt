This book has '18 new tales of vampirism' emblazoned on the front cover, but in the forward the editor states 'that these tales were 'intended to see just how far I could take the concept without any actual bloodsucking.' Huh. If I'd read the forward, I might not have bothered picking this book up in the first place.

Out of 18 stories (and I confess I didn't manage to finish them all), I think about three are vampire stories in the traditional sense. The others are occasionally weird metaphor or alternative vampire tales. there are some decent stories in this collection: I particularly liked Karl Edward Wagner's , Silverberg's (originally published in magazine in 1957(!)), and by Melissia Mia Hall, and by Elizabeth Massie is going to live in my memory because I found it so disturbing.

It was interesting, but not vampiric enough for me - I like genuine bloodsuckers in my vampire anthologies, so I won't be reading this again. Recommended for readers who like weird horror, not really recommended for vampire lovers.
