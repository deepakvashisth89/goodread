Winner of the 1948 Pulitzer Prize for Drama, it is widely considered to be one of the masterpieces of 20th-century theater.

I've been to see plays on Broadway countless times, but A Streetcar Named Desire was my first experience reading a play! This American classic consists of 11 scenes and is set in the two-room home of Stella and Stanley Kowalski in the poor section of New Orleans. Stella’s older sister, the uppity Mississippi Southern belle Blanche Dubois, comes for a visit, where conflict arises. Each scene is filled with action and meaning, maintaining a high level of emotion and drama throughout. Williams uses his characters to express the darker aspects of everyday human existence. The play dramatizes a clash between two cultures: the traditional Southern culture and the emergence of working-class immigrants.



Williams used themes that were very revolutionary for its time, as he was the first person to address certain topics so directly. The play depicts mental instability, homosexuality, alcoholism, domestic violence, sex, and even rape, which were realities for many Americans at that time, but these topics were swept under the rug and not talked about.

My reading adventure for this classic consisted of a paperback copy, a dramatized audiobook, and the 1951 film starring Marlon Brando! I absolutely adored the experience of reading a play and look forward to reading another.
