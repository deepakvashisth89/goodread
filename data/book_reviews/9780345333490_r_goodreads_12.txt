Well written TAS novelizations by a respected sci-fi author and contains a large portion of the plot of Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979).

These novelizations differ from the finished episodes that aired because Alan Dean Foster expands the story and also because the stories are based on the original script for the episodes, not the final shooting script of the aired episodes. One neat addition we get from Foster is an attempt at continuity. In my experience, Trekkers love this. Speaking for myself, I absolutely love it when a series is serialized. This was done very well later in Deep Space Nine and Enterprise.

For example, in the climax of the first episode novelization, “Beyond the Farthest Star”, Kirk burns his hands; at the beginning of the second story, “Yesteryear”, Kirk muses at the new skin on his recently burned hands. I love that Foster did this, and I love that Foster not only novelized the episodes, but also expanded them into novella length.

I love James Blish’s episode novelizations, but Blish stuck to the script pretty closely and, as a result, the novelizations Blish wrote for The Original Series (TOS) are great, but they are also very short compared to these by Foster for The Animated Series (TAS). These are fleshed out by Alan Dean Foster and he did a masterful job in doing so.

Perhaps the biggest piece of trivia related to this book and a large draw for every Trekker to ensure they read it at least once is the third story, “One of Our Planets is Missing”. A full two-thirds of the plot of Star Trek: The Motion Picture is stolen without credit given to the original writers of “One of Our Planets is Missing” and “The Changeling”. If you take the cloud from “On of Our…” and have Nomad from “The Changeling” be the living entity in the middle of that cloud, then you essentially have Star Trek: The Motion Picture minus the character dialogue. The iris-like opening that leads into the heart of the cloud is here, the Ilia-probe is here except that it’s Spock instead. Spock learns about the universe from the creature at the heart of the cloud a la Will Decker at the end of the movie.

I gave it 5-stars because it’s a 4-star book but it’s the first based on the Animated Series, so I threw in an extra star cuz I’m a Trekker nerd.

Written by the man who ghostwrote the Star Wars novelization for George Lucas, and the story for Star Trek: The Motion Picture for Gene Roddenberry, as well as novelizations for the 2009 reboot and the official novelization of Alien (1979). In other words, Alan Dean Foster is legit, and is a bonified member of the Star Trek universe.
