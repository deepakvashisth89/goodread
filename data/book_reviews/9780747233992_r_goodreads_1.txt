She got me.I thought I knew all of Edith Pargeter's tricks (I've read dozens of her books), but by the end of this last installment in her brilliant WWII trilogy, she had me in tears.As with the previous two books, Pargeter doesn't pull any punches in telling the awful truth about war. This books follows her main character through D-Day right into Germany at the end of the war. There is plenty of brutality and absurdity and pain to process, including the almost unreadable discovery of a concentration camp.And yet there is meaning there, and hope, and hints of God. And storytelling so good that I found myself weeping on the last page. Recommended but be forewarned.
