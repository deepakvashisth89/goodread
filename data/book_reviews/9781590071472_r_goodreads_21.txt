They don't write books likeThe Wind in the Willowsanymore.Today's books for children are sly rhymes, action and social engineering.Windbelongs to an older, more innocent time when even accomplished men such as Kenneth Grahame, A. A. Milne and J. R. R. Tolkien invented stories for their children.Stories which over the years became classics of literature.Windisn't a fairy tale so much as it's life told for those who will inherit it. Told by those who love the inheritors.Even if you've read it before—especially if you've seen Disney's Bowlderized revision—read it again. Pause along the way to consider the world Grahmane portrays. This is England; this is childhood; this is life as we remember it, or wish it was.
