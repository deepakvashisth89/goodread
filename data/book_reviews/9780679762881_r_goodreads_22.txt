I recently read and enjoyed Andy Grove's Only the Paranoid Survive: How to Exploit the Crisis Points that Challenge Every Company and Career about organizational agility, so I was eager to read Andy Grove's treatise on management in this classic early 1980s business book. I think in general the advice is good and still relevant in the mid-2020s, with some caveats due to the inevitably more tech-oriented culture we live and work in today.

The parts that resonated with me the most were that it's the manager's job to train their direct reports (rather than letting costly mistakes that cause major financial harm or customer loss from lack of supervision constitute training), and that performance reviews shouldn't just be rubber stamps of approval and should be deliberately constructive.

I agree with other reviewers that the breakfast factory model presented throughout the book never really comes full circle and isn't a great analogy for a book about people management -- it's more suited to a book on optimizing a manufacturing production line. It's somewhat similar to the production line analogy Eliyahu Goldratt uses in his contemporaneous business classic (first edition published in 1984), though the analogy works much better in Goldratt's case.
