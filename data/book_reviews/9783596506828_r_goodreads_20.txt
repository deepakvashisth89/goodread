After the Reading of the Will
A review of the William Morrow Kindle eBook (January 25, 2005) of the Collins Crime Club (UK 1953) & Dodd, Mead & Co. (US 1953) original hardcovers.
This time it was in the big drawing room that Hercule Poirot assembled his audience.
Timothy was not far from voicing the general feeling when he remarked in an audible sotto voce to his wife:
“Damned little mountebank! Entwhistle must be gaga!—that’s all I can say.”
It looked as though Hercule Poirot would have to work hard to make his proper effect.

What a relief it was to get back to reading classic Agatha Christie again after enduring the alternate-universe version of Hercule Poirot in Sophie Hannah's (2023), part of her ongoing continuation series.

Poirot is called in on the case by his lawyer friend Entwhistle. After the funeral of a family patriarch, his surviving sister hints that the old man was murdered. A few days later she herself is murdered as well. If the culprit thought that would stop speculation they obviously didn't count on Poirot's investigation. Entwhistle actually does most of the legwork of interviews and such, but it all ends with the now classic scenario of Poirot gathering all the suspects in a room for the final reveal.




This edition includes a very respectful introduction by Sophie Hannah in which she names this as her favourite Poirot novel. It is unfortunate that her own pastiche version of the character does not show the same respect.

I continue to enjoy my binge of 2025 re-reads and/or new reads of the classic Agatha Christie books from the 1920s through to the 1950s.
