I was gifted the first three of this series by a good friend, so recommended by a source I trust. Was not let down.This is a bit slower paced, especially at the beginning as things get going. Definitely on the wordier side, but it fits with the academic setting. A bit of a more conservative feel to it - again, works with the private academic setting (and apparently it's marketed by a Christian publishing company, though it's not particularly in-your-face about religion, at least not in my view).For an amateur detective, at least it felt like Ben Reese had the intelligence and background to make it work. He was an archivist by trade - detail conscious and methodical - and had a background in intelligence during WWII (still only 15ish years previous to the 1960 setting). Pulled off the amateur investigation better than some other ones I've read. Not perfect, but not ridiculous either. Even better, he's a likable character, not one just charging in bullheaded and opinionated into the investigation.And as for the period, I've watched more historic mysteries in the 1960s than read them. Made for an interesting shift from the contemporary or 1920s-40s mysteries I usually read.Some downsides - not as complex a mystery, but not glaringly evident either. I did have a fair idea, but it wasn't to the point where I was shaking my head impatiently waiting for the characters to catch up to the obvious. Also, not a fan of the 1st person/3rd person POV shifts. Yes, it can be done effectively, but doesn't make it any more enjoyable for me. Much prefer one style, stuck with through the book.Overall, has a Golden Age sort of feel to it. Rather introspective, but not too dark and not violent - more about the mystery. If you likeDorothy L. SayersorNgaio Marsh, it'd be worth at least giving this series a try.
