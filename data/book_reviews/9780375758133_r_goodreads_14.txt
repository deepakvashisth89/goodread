”It was, she cried silently, enough to suffer as a woman, an individual, on one’s own account, without having to suffer for the race as well. It was a brutality, and undeserved…”


that quote just minutely displays how beautiful and touching this book was. how authors like nella larsen and Dostoevsky can create such short stories that mess with your mind and stare uncomfortably deep into your soul and speak to it is beyond my comprehension.

‘’ follows two biracial black woman, Irene redfield and clare kendry, that are both able to “pass” as whites, but lead complete opposite lives. Irene embraces her culture and background, while Clare is married to her racist, white supremacist husband. at first, it’s easy to dislike Clare, because on the surface level she seems ignorant and honestly, quite annoying, but at the end you’re almost warped into the mind of Irene — who deeply disliked her, but also could never say no to her. i have absolutely no idea how Nella Larsen was able to make me feel that way without spoilers, but that just proved to me how sensational of a writer she is.

not only is this book interesting to read, but it also resonated with me. as an Asian American myself, i’ve experienced, and a lot of my friends and I have discussed at least one point in our life where we wanted to be white, just for the sake of not having to face the atrocity of injustice every. single. day. Clare and Irene are such unique and interesting characters because they represent two different lives that mixed POC, or POC, could lead. it was so fascinating to read how their lives played out alongside both of their thoughts, deep desires, and regrets. on one hand, embracing your culture and marrying someone Black, but being almost violently envious of your friend, who, on the other hand, married a white man for the stability, but is also envious of you, and the vibrant culture that she left behind.



P.S. THE ENDING?!? i crave more.
