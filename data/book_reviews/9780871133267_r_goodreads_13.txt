Meh. I really wanted to read Patricia Highsmith and this was the first book of hers I was able to stumble upon. Found at a charity book sale, I decided to give it a chance even though the blurb on the back cover really didn't interest me. Turns out my instinct was correct. Slow and boring, and about artsy types. I don't particularly like books about artsy types, as the books are capable of being perhaps even more pretentious than the people they are portraying. Add to that the characters in this book, none of them relatable except maybe the intolerant Ralph, but even he is taken to the extreme being a racist, self-righteous bigot who just happens to be an atheist (that is an interesting twist, I must say).Overall, I feel this book didn't contribute anything to me as a person. It wasn't beautiful, wasn't insightful, it didn't teach me anything about the human psyche nor the human heart... It did paint a picture of 1980's New York, which let's be honest, probably wasn't the best decade of the Big Apple's... And it was something to read, a time filler, and not even the best one at that. I was in a hurry to finish it just to move on to something better. Not what I expected to say about such a highly acclaimed author.As a side note, I will definitely read Patricia Highsmith again, but something that catches my interest. There is one thing to be taken from this book, actually. The main character professes to not liking Hemingway, yet is afraid to give her opinion on the subject. She is encouraged to stick to her guns and say she doesn't like Hemingway and explain why. Bravo! I'm never afraid to admit when I don't like something or someone no matter how many accolades they have. I may read a highly acclaimed author or book because it's culturally important, but that doesn't mean I'll like it. Personally, I agree about Hemingway being a lightweight!
