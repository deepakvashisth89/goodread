First and foremost, especially having read the Coming Storm, I am getting wigged out by Russel's seeming fetishization of Black men. Maybe I misread irony especially with the Greek gay guy? Even if I did, the characterization of Kai is very questionable.

That said, Jonathan was the driving force of this novel. Not just the tragedy of AIDS, but also thge beautty of the wold in its abbility to creative divine energy made his story compelling.

I understand that telling the tale through Jonathan's own voice, as well as three others, was a strategy to show him as three dimensionally as possible. It didn't work. The alternating narration was distracting at its worst, and trite at its best.

There were odd moments too -- Stayon's dad becoming a Jerry Falwell-type was too fantastical as it really only existed to set up a very weird and fun sex scene.

I loved both the political critique of the space race, and the unbridled passion for thinking about space and the universe. The line about how dark matter makes up the majority of the universe, and that our conception of reality might be a tiny minority of what's really going on stuck with me.

This was a page turner, and something that I enjoyed. Just a lot of other flags. It felt so dated and yet the level of repression around queerness represented happened very much in my lifetime. Weird how time works.
