As a kid growing up in Colorado, I had no idea about East Coast summer camp culture and relied on Laura's Luck as a guidepost. But now that I've been here for over 25 years and have raised my kiddo here, I get this book on a deeper level.

To be clear, this was one of my favorite books when I was a kid: we loved family camping but I had no blueprint for single-gender all-summer camp. The drama of being sent away to girls' camp for eight weeks, the injustice of not being in your age appropriate cabin because your school year was not aligned with your age, the chores that the girls had to do as jobs and as punishment: I wanted to experience all of it.

Rereading it as an adult didn't take away from my childhood memories. I enjoyed immersing myself in this period novel and felt the injustice of Laura's non-sporty introduction to camp all over again. This is an older read but a perfect story.

(Coda: I reread this book before my daughter left for overnight camp three years ago. We were both nervous but she loves her girls-only overnight camp. She has gained so much confidence and independence and I am so happy that this book imprinted on my soul the benefits of camp.)
