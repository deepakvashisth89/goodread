Entering Space is a must-read for anyone in the space industry and perhaps even if you are a science fiction enthusiast. One might think it being from the year 2000 would make most concepts dated, but I can assure, as someone who’s read myriad space science non-fiction and fiction books, that this masterpiece is the best I’ve come across.Robert Zubrin brilliantly delineates the complexities behind the real adoption of revolutionary space technologies, from spaceplanes (then called rocketplanes) to antimatter photon rockets for interstellar drivers. Beyond the technical aspects of the tech, such as Deuterium-Tritium fusion chemical reactions or the mathematics of swing-bys in the Jovian system, Zubrin does a stellar job with putting it all in perspective economically. Therefore, for the space entrepreneur or investor, there’s no better way to get a grasp of how the technology works while understanding what trends are currently infeasible (such as solar power systems) and which ones are viable alternatives (power relay systems).
