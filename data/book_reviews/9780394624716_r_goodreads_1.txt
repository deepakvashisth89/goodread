GANDHI (3 hours 11 minutes)

Gandhi is far from a gaunt script written by John Briley, spanning 181 pages including some production photos in the middle. Despite italics being used to emphasize words in dialogue AND winning the 1983 Academy Awards for Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Screenplay, and Best Cinematography (among others), the cinematography directions are not stylized within the text and are significantly harder to distinguish.

These directions include: coursing, camera is moving toward, high and far away, approaching, move nearer, the camera keeps moving closer, point of view is always Godse’s, full shot, dissolve to, his point of view, camera picks out, camera is high, sweep under the camera, reverse angle, another angle, their point of view, the shots are varied, intercut always with, camera panning slowly, intercut, close shot, camera pulls back and back, camera comes to rest, long shot, dissolve through to, the camera has been moving in, camera is low, panned off, camera picks out, camera pulls back, medium shot, panning, fade out…, fade in…, camera pans, camera zooms, pans to, second close shot, intercut, long shot, high, the camera lifts and pulls back, whirr of a camera, swift cut, widescreen, but slightly under-cranked with the bad cutting and predictable music of the old newsreels, H., I., reverse angle- close, long shot- high, extreme close shot, the camera is high (helicopter), camera levels and speeds along it, camera sweeps, the camera angle has changed, we are high, the camera zooms, wide angle shot, slowly the camera begins pulling back.

Directions for dialogue are also rather specific. The words "gaunt" and “taut” are used frequently. Other words used multiple times include pranam, ashramites, sepoys, acidly, solemnly, soberly, and pyre.
