Perhaps one of the least original books every written; even the Goodreads catalog includes at least a dozen platoon leader memoirs from Vietnam. A newly trained infantry lieutenant becomes the leader of 30-40 men in combat for a six-month period. Many are killed or wounded, some are found unworthy, some receive a practical education in survival and move up. McDonough's story is as good as any; though late in the war, he was assigned in an active [read: dangerous] sector and he took his mission to foil and destroy the enemy seriously. Many men under his command were killed or seriously wounded, but in the paradox of war, it is likely that even fewer would have survived if McDonough had tried to avoid combat.
The book is a microcosm of the war: out of a desire to protect everything, everywhere, American defenses were adequate only while the enemy chose to not concentrate. That McDonough and some of his men survived in their isolated outpost, guarding a small village, is a testimony to the Viet Cong's lack of commitment to destroying them. McDonough's platoon scored many minor victories against Viet Cong units too small to resist for long. One lesson of Viet Nam is that thousands of minor victories, like those in this book, do not add up to winning the war.
