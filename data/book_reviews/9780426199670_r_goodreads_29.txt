This didn't work for me. I think the novel is too short for its own good, condensing seven TV episodes into a 140 page novella is just not enough space. Especially seeing as each of the episodes has a beginning, middle and climax in of themselves, condensing them down leaves the action and peril of the book to feel quiant and unimportant. At one point, Barbara gets kidnapped and they all go out to find her - which they do about a page later. There's very little suspense to proceedings and the book suffers because of it. I also find the story to be very monotonous - Marco Polo's allegiance is constantly shifting, something I didn't find to be terribly interesting. The plotline with Ping-Cho's unwanted marriage is given a little more room to breathe, but ultimate is just her repeatedly saying she doesn't want to, more and more kver the course of the book.Its still and interesting read, especially given how different this style of story is - the travelogue aspect is one very rarely seen today and Marco Polo should definitely be commended for that, but I don't think this book is the incredible read a lot of people say it is.
