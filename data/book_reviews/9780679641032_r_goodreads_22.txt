It looks like my paperback version was copyrighted and printed in 1961 due to an introduction by L. Jesse Lemisch. Besides the autobiography itself, this book contained a sampling of his writing and letters on the following topics: wealth, doing good, his scientific endeavors, the revolution, the family man, and his religious views. He wrote the autobiography in later life after he was finished with most of his public duties, but it ends in the late 1750's more than 30 years before his death. I think he ran out of time and didn't finish his entire life. His writing style mostly was engaging, especially for someone writing in the 18th century. The autobiography focused on his efforts to determine what a virtuous life was and how he endeavored to achieve that. He attributed his material success to hard work and frugality. Of the other writings, I was most interested in his writings about wealth, doing good and religion. Overall he accomplished a lot and contributed a lot in his lifetime, and was a trusted person because of his continual efforts to be virtuous. His greatest failing in my view was not being a good husband, leaving his wife alone for decades while performing service abroad. He had a "family" that he stayed with in London and he fell in love while in Paris. I am conflicted about whether he failed to be virtuous on these accounts due to the long time and distance he was away from his primary family. Interestingly, his list of virtues that he was striving to attain did not include faithfulness/monogamy so I guess he felt what he was doing was acceptable. The included writings certainly didn't express any regrets about it. I will be recycling this old paperback.
