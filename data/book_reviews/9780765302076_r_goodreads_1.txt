Shhhh... Listen... Hear it?That's the sound of J. R. R. Tolkien spinning in his grave over the fact that this anthology is said to be in his honor.Please.None of the stories in this volume are Tolkienesque in the slightest. While one does mention Hobbits and another mentions Halflings and there are Goblins and Elves and a Troll, that's where the similarity ends.Granted, I've only read The Hobbit, Lord of the Rings, and The Silmarillion, but I don't recall Leprechauns and Sugar Plum Fairies. And the story about time travel and zombies? And the one that took place in Normandy?Frankly, this volume reads like a collection of stories that otherwise failed to reach publication. Their pointlessness is only surpassed by their plotlessness.With the exception of one gem -- Terry Pratchett's "Troll Bridge." But even in this case, the story belongs to the lore of Discworld, not Middle Earth. (The presence of this short story did, however, garner an extra star for the volume overall. Without it, the rating would have been a solid "one.")Overall, this volume was painful and, at times, offensive.
