This book has been on my thoughts in the last few weeks and I was happy to be able to find a copy of it even if I had to settle for digital.This interesting book takes a look into what it may have been like if circumstances had allowed the Virgin Mary to be taught to read and write thus allowing her to keep a journal. As a result we get a fictionalized account of one of the most interesting women in Christian history with ties to the story told in the Bible's Luke.Evelyn does such a great job in portraying the heroine of her story. Young Mary is full of awe, excitement and eagerness but you can see the gradual change in her emotions when she knows how much trouble she is going to be. And slowly as every event unfolds an older, wiser Mary starts to come out - a woman whose affection for her husband is strengthed by their trials, whose love for her children overcomes the miracle of her younger years and the eventual resignation on having to finally let go of those one loves so they can live their own lives wherever God may lead them.The author combines into this emotional tale verses from the Old Testament, events from the New Testament and cultural additions from the actual setting of the book. As a result the book stands for one of the best biblical fiction books I have read even when there are spots that I don't believe would have been Mary's actual thoughts.All in all this is a good and quick read that ends on the note of the start of Jesus' ministry. Sadly the reader isn't given any further insight into what Mary must have dealt with during her firstborn's career, her pain during his persecution, the Crucifixion and his resurrection. Unfinished the reader is allowed to read the Bible for the rest of the story and ponder what it may have been like for this favored woman.In the end this is a book in which many women will be able to relate whether it is as a daughter, an excited bride-to-be, a new or tested wife, an expectant mother or a mother raising her children. And that in itself allows the reader to be able to relate more intimately with the faces that come to us from the New Testament's greatest miracle story.
