The metropolis of Isola is suffering from a brutally cold winter and a crew of painters have planted themselves in the 87th Precinct's detective squadroom, adding disorder to an already tense atmosphere, when someone calls the squad line demanding $5,000 or the city's Parks Commissioner will be killed. Efforts to figure out if this threat is genuine or a crank fail when the city official refuses police protection. Then the cops are unable to bring in the culprit when they try to exchange the cash at the designated time. The commissioner is then actually killed and now there's a murder to solve at the same time that the culprit calls again and pushes further with a greater monetary demand on the deputy mayor's life next.

Escalating this case to higher levels for 87th Precinct readers, this being the 22nd in the series, is we now finally see the return of "the Deaf Man," the evil genius antagonist from "the Heckler" (my review #680, series ep #12), a Moriarty-style supervillain who crossed paths with these bulls when he set off a city-wide chaotic furor to mask a bank robbery eight years prior.

"Fuzz" (1966) is a good police procedural on its own but I'm going to pause here to say it is strange to see that it was turned into a comedy heist film in a 1972 movie adaptation starring Burt Reynolds, Yul Brynner as the Deaf Man, and Raquel Welch, with a screenplay written by McBain himself. The book cover for the used book's printing I found is cringe as heck, a movie poster style pulp cover with Burt Reynolds playing the super cop Carella, laying on his side and smiling around a cigar with only a towel to cover himself, a buxom Raquel Welch running at the camera half-clad and with a pistol in hand, and some clumsy men dressed as nuns with guns behind her. I know the 60's and 70's were weird eras but my parents' generation now has a lot more to answer for lol.

Update: After writing my review above and before posting it, I actually watched the movie on Prime with my wife. It is what we might call a very 70's-ish cop cult classic, meaning, it is pretty bad. She fell asleep and I was reminded why books beat movies every time. A young Tom Skerritt holds up pretty well as Kling, and Burt Reynolds is Burt Reynolds and Raquel Welch is cute as can be, but Yul Brenner is embarrassingly horrible. And the scripted cop banter on film isn't half as funny as when one reads it on the page.

Verdict: "Fuzz" - the novel, not the movie - is pretty good, a fun mystery centered on the 87th squad of detectives in a winter cityscape with a few divergent threads all holding parts of the story.
