Simply perfect.Review below after a recent re-read:Let me begin by saying how much I love this book, despite a host of politically incorrect plot points and attitudes that reflect the era's norms for the genre, but absolutely wouldn't fly today, for good reason. So despite all my love for this book, please be warned that if you are triggered by Old Skool tropes such as rape, and forced seduction, this isn't the book for you.The story is about a couple who married too young and made many mistakes.The girl let her horrible, manipulative parents destroy her marriage without even noticing it, she was having so much fun playing the bride. The parents’manipulations caused a lot of problems in the marriage. The hero and heroine started having verbal duels on the daily, with more and more unkindness until it disintegrated into hurtful and unforgivable insults. In a fit of rage, the hero did the unpardonable: He sexually assaulted his wife in the most brutal, revolting way.Follows a separation and more misunderstandings. Somehow, hero and heroine realize despite all the toxicity, they still love each other. The man tries to contact his wife to ask for a second chance but is thwarted by the parents. The wife has finally woken up to the horrible nature of her parents when they try to force her to abort her baby and divorce the husband, against her wishes. She decides to move away and they turn on her too, since they can't control her anymore, spewing the worst insults on her and basically disowning her.The heroine built a life for herself and her son. She supported herself and her child by becoming a reporter. She is in Greece doing a story on a reclusive millionaire, when lo and behold she runs into her estranged husband. Lots of hostility and sexual tension between them. She imagines he must have been sowing his wild oats all these years, maybe even having an affair right now with the very married French tart whose boat is moored next to his on the harbour.He imagines the same about her, and is especially jealous of her relationship with her interview subject, a notorious playboy before he walled himself into a private retreat a la Howard Hughes.Things come to a head when the hero kidnaps the heroine on his boat. There is a forced seduction that ends with the heroine liking it as is par for the course in Old Skool Harlequin-topia, and many misunderstandings, especially the ones caused by her parents, are uncovered.The hero made it pretty clear that he spent any free time he had, in between his job engineering for various construction projects around the world, desperately looking for the heroine. He never forgot her. When he saw a byline for an article she had written in his local newspaper in Australia, he finnagled her whereabouts from her employer, rented a boat, and hightailed it to her Greek island, in the hope of somehow rekindling their relationship. He expressed contrition and remorse, and a great, vibrant love for the heroine in a believable way. (He also denied being involved with the French tart and implied celibacy during the years of separation. Maybe I am drinking the Sally Wentworth Kool-Aid but I believed him). In my eyes, he redeemed himself beautifully.The heroine put him through one more test, running away from him after their blissful reunion, but leaving a trail that he could follow. He did so and within three days was at her door, meeting his son for the first time. Their joyful reunion as a family was a big tear-jerker for me and a beautiful end to a story filled with turmoil and tragedy.There was nothing earth-shattering about this plot or the tropes used in it. The reason I love this book so much is that the author wrote so compellingly about this estranged married couple that I felt elated for them at their reunion, after both of them suffered terribly for years. It really brought a tear to my eye.Sally Wentworth has written many campfests and many turkeys in her extensive backlist but sometimes, she reminds you of what a great writer she is. Not only is the pace of this book just right, there are as many light-hearted, humorous moments as well as all the angsty ones, making for a good balance. Most of all, she has created two compelling characters, flaws and all, that you can really root for.This isn't going to be everyone's cuppa by any means but for me, it is one of the best books SW has ever written. Loved it!
