The primary reason I read this book was the fact that it was read by Jane Austen, herself, and it was a favourite of hers. Imagine holding a book that was read by a favourite author of mine!

This book was written in 1778. I think this may be the first book I’ve read pre 1800. I was worried I’d find it a difficult read due to the language of the time, but this was not so. In fact, quite an easy read in which the text easily flowed.

This is an epistolary novel. Everything that occurs is conveyed through letters. This format worked well- I had no qualms with it. What’s surprised me was that the principal recipient was the elderly Reverend/ stand in father of Evelina and she shared absolutely everything. Maybe, young women were different in those days.

As the title indicates, this book is about Evelina, a 17 year old young lady, who is making her debut into society. She is well educated, but oh so naive of social expectations. She is beautiful, shy and demure and she attracts a lot of attention from men, much unwanted. She makes quite a few faux pas during her outings, having had no instruction on what was correct by society’s standards.

Evelina has a secretive parentage. Her mother died giving birth to her, her father had denied being married to her mother and in so doing, Evelina was unclaimed by him as his daughter.. So this novel is also about Evelina reclaiming her birthright.

This book is a satire about society and its classes. It is about societal expectations, social norms and manners and recognizing sincerity versus lies.

There is humour in this novel, mostly by Captain Mirvan. He took an instant dislike to Madame Duval, Evelina’s grandmother and he loved to torment her. I wondered if this had to do with the English/ French rivalry and animosity.

What didn’t work for me: it started to feel quite repetitive- one letter after another with a similar subject matter.

Overall, I enjoyed this book. Has it usurped Austen as a favourite? Definitely not. I found it very interesting to experience what Austen did though. I can see that she did receive some inspiration for her own books after reading this one.

Published: 1778
