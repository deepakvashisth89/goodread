I loved it! The Passion Bargain was a sexy, fun and romantic read. I smiled stupidly throughout the book and continued to hold that expression all the way to the satisfying conclusion, at times even laughing out loud at the smitten antics of the lead characters who were equally likable. Their first onscreen meeting in the book appeared early, and it was scorching hot.
All they did was talk—at the longest traffic light in the world—but their smoldering, mutual passion for each other was palpable. Poor virginal Francesca was so overwhelmed by this desire she literally had to stop her scooter and calm her trembling body. But it's not all about sexual attraction even at this point, at least not for Carlo. Unknown to Francesca and the reader, he's already smitten, having fallen head-over-heels in love with her at their very first, off-screen meeting prior to the traffic light rendezvous.

Carlo was the typical Italian, HP hero: sexy and charmingly arrogant. He was also very passionate about protecting Francesca, and swooped in like a knight in shining armor to offer his support when Francesca really needed it. Being a trusting and loyal person, Francesca was blindsided when she discovered her best friend and roommate had been doing something she shouldn't be doing.
There's more to the fortune subplot, but I won't get into it here. However, it did create more conflict and caused Francesca to distrust Carlo's interest in her.

As Francesca fell for Carlo a few meager days after her broken engagement, she tormented herself for being so fickle and with doubts over Carlo's intentions towards her. I certainly believe it's possible to love two people at the same time (in different ways), so having Francesca fall for Carlo so soon after her broken engagement didn't bother me. And really, her attraction for Angelo was never on the same level as they were for Carlo, who was worthy of Francesca and proved it by the time the novel ended.
