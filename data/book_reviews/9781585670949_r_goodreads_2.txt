This too is another book on the so-called Lost Generation of 1920's Paris. However, in this case, it is fiction. And although the title leads you to believe that it is a murder mystery, murder is only a small part of the book. It is actually about a reporter who falls in with many of the famous British and Americans who lived in Paris at that time.

Interestingly, the other main character are given fictitious names although the author tells you in the introduction who the people really are. The other major character in the book, for instance, is Ernest Hemmingway, under a different name, of course. The Fitzgeralds and others are also given different names, while some keep their own names...such as James Joyce and Gertrude Stein.

Because it is fiction, we can enter into the lives of the artists and writers who make up the story. There are many scenes in the famous cafes of Paris such as Lilas, The Dome, and others. We here their conversations and take part in their little games. To be truthful, these great artists of the 1920's hold the same kind of banal conversations that we hold in today's bars.

Oh yes...the murders. There is a person running around Paris killing women in the style of Jack the Ripper. One of the characters we meet in the cafes is a victim about halfway through the book. But, then there are little hints throughout the book that this person was not murdered by "The Ripper," but rather it was made to look as if he did it.

It all comes out in the end and the murderer is revealed. But, don't let the title fool you...this book is much more about the daily lives of the Lost Generation than it is about murder...which is why I really enjoyed it.
