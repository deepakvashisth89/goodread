I read this novel a few years ago and thought it was a pretty good kids' story, and have just finished reading de Lint's adult Newford collection Tapping the Dream Tree, in which it's collected as the final story. The version in Tapping the Dream Tree is reprinted from the first appearance, which was as a Subterranean limited edition from 2002. It struck me as more of an adult tale, so I compared the two versions. The Little Brown is lovely; it's lushly and lavishly illustrated by Charles Vess, but the characters are not how I pictured them from the text-only collection; the Dillard girls appear much younger, particularly the main character Sarah Jane Dillard. Aunt Lillian appears more aged and fragile, some of the fairy creatures are a bit creepier, but the main difference is in the ages of the seven Dillard sisters. There are some changes in the text that simplify descriptions, and age-references are removed, and altogether I'd say the revision is much more directed to a younger audience of reader. Ironically, the newer version is over a hundred pages longer, but that's mostly typesetting differences and a whole lot more artwork. It's a good coming-of-age fable about family and fairies in either binding, but I preferred the older one. It's listed as a Newford novel, but the text only vaguely mentions the town a time or two, and the setting seems to me to be much more of an Appalachian one more suited to Manly Wade Wellman than to de Lint. It's a fine, fast read in any event, suitable for kids of all ages.
