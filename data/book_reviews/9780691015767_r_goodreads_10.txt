Jung on Active Imagination is a very interesting and valuable compilation of Jung’s work, especially thanks to Chodorow bringing the different texts together in a coherent way.What I found most fascinating is the process of active imagination itself and how Jung works with inner images and symbolic material. However, I sometimes felt that the interpretations of the patient’s drawings were a bit too general and symbolic in a fixed way. Because there is limited context provided about the patient, the explanations can feel more “objective” than they actually are.And that is exactly where the tension lies for me: Jung himself emphasizes that meaning is always individual and context-dependent, while some of the presented interpretations can feel more universal than intended.Overall, a very insightful read, but one that also invites a critical reflection on how Jungian symbolism is presented and interpreted.
