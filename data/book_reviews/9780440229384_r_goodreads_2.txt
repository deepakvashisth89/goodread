✰ 3.75 stars ✰“Whale talk is the truth, and in a very short period of time, if you’re a whale, you know exactly what it is to be you.”One might argue with me that it was riddled with stereotypical cliché, built on jock aggression and unseemly violence and blatant racism that felt more contrived than convincing. And I can attest to that. But, I can also counter that I was wholly engaged and totally immersed in the story itself. The writing pulled me in with a unique protagonist that had me care for his convictions and strong core of goodness. 👍🏻It may have to do with T.J's (aka The Tao Jones - yes, that's his real name) own tragic backstory that made him such a fresh voice to listen to - his raw candidness and sincerity. How he felt it was unfair that wearing a lettered jacket could only be for athletes, which is an odd thing to see at Cutter High School, cuz I've always seen in movies that wearing your other half's jacket was always a sense of ownership or even pride.But, I digress.“He knew that we take what the universe gives us, and we either get the most out of it or we don’t, but in the end we all go out the same way.”And yes, it has its fair share of offensive comments, racial slurs, and a foreboding touch of trauma and abuse, but it is the dark and ugly shadow of life, we choose to ignore or pretend to exist? Does not mean it's still not real, or that you can dismiss it as a Young Adult novel of 2001, so it's okay. You cannot deny that there are those who simply lack the sense of moral decency.T.J had my entire attention. 🙂‍↕️ From his commitment to building up a swim team of misfits simply in his defense of his mentally disabled classmate, Chris, who created an uproar simply for wearing his dead brother's football jacket. It's amazing how things can just spiral out of control or even twist into other parts of your life, like T.J's did.How his defiance drew the ire of one Rick Marshall, who's had it out for him ever since he came in his way of a form of animal cruelty that was unnecessarily cruel. 💔 A loss of innocence, whose own helplessness was a reflection of T.J's own abandonment by his real mother that the love and support his parents give him - his white parents, mind you - is something he never takes for granted.“You have to see everyone in relationship to you. Just because you understand the shit in someone else’s life doesn’t mean you don’t stand up for your own.”So how do these points align? How does building up a team from scratch become a bond of friendship and brotherhood to those who never had anyone to listen to their grievances, a place to unload their guilt or shame.Everything is relative.How does his defensive streak for protecting the weak against the atrocities of injustice push T.J to act, all the while his father serves as a gentle reminder of how rash decisions can lead to unfathomable regrets that you cannot simply wash away? 😢Being a half-black, half-Japanese, half-white eighteen-year-old senior (the cover does question that, though) isenoughof a reason to spew hatred and arrogance, made only worse when Rick's own mixed-race step daughter becomes the target of his violence and cruelty. 🥺 Something T.J just cannot stomach or fathom this level of awfulness that his parents have to rein in his anger to direct it towards other means, because they love and care for him and want to protect him.“He didn’t want me living a life of what might have been.”It's a tough act to pull together, but somehow, it did. 🤌🏻 It's more than just a story about swimming. T.J's quick-witted retorts, how he pushed forward to solve his problems, his relationship with his father, the friendships being forged with genuine feeling and heart, his innate sense of goodness with even that sharp edge of grittiness and fierce commitment to not back down.As tensions flared, tempers aggravated, steadily building that trepidation of unease sinking in that something awful was going to happen... and when it did, I felt such an emptiness. Knowing the character's backstory, I felt also a peace - a delayed sense of retribution finally granted in a way T.J never would have wanted, but it taught him so much. My heart hurt at that pivotal life-altering moment, one that would leave its impact on him forever. 🥀
