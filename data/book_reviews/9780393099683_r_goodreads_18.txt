The novel is timeless, only Melville’s convoluted sentences give an indication of the period in which it was written and set. The story(ies) take place on board Fidele, a passenger steamer plying its way along the busy river. People are coming and going all the time, the flotsam and jetsom of life on the Mississipi. The reader has to be constantly on guard, along with her/his fictional counterparts in order not to be “taken in” by the fraudster, ‘con’ man, call him what you will. Such people can come in many guises, financier, politician, clergyman, all are “salesmen”, one way or another. Today they may be money laundering, selling or fighting Brexit perhaps?Is it healthy always to be on one’s guard, always distrusting. Is man essentially good or evil and what of Christ’s teaching in all of this? He too is on the boat somewhere, it seems to me.
