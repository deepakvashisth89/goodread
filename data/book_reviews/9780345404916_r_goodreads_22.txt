Shaara does a good job again of bringing the characters of Robert E. Lee and Joshua Chamberlain to life while introducing the awe-inspiring General Ulysses S. Grant. A lot of action is packed into this final installment of the trilogy, but Shaara manages to handle it all pretty well. It begins with the aftermath of the catastrophic Gettysburg campaign and goes all the way through to Lee's surrender at Appomattox. In between, the slow but sure destruction of Lee's army under the boot of General Grant's force is well-narrated.

The return of the stateliness of Robert E. Lee and of the idealistic scholarliness of Joshua Chamberlain are welcome bits of continuity that follow in the footsteps of the first two books. However, having General Grant as a new face was what I enjoyed in particular about this piece of the trilogy. His dealings with many selfish and competing generals within his own army on the road to victory was commendable. In addition to that, his close relationship to President Lincoln during the final year of the Civil War was critical to his success and was given special attention in this book.

As an avid military history buff, I appreciate the attention to detail Shaara gave to most of the major conflicts that fell within the timeline of this book. The Wilderness, Spotsylvania, Petersburg, Five Forks, and Appomattox are of particular focus. I wish a little more mention would've been given to the Battle of Cold Harbor in order to give a more comprehensive picture of Grant's Overland Campaign (for only the aftermath of that battle is really included in this book). However, I cannot complain much as this final piece of the trilogy measures up closely to the previous two installments.
