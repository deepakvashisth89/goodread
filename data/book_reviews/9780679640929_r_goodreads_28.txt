Hans Küng is a provocative figure in Catholicism and his short history stays true to his reputation. I, though, (a protestant with Catholic sympathies) appreciated his honest recounting of the historical origins and development of the more recent Catholic dogmas—papal infallibility is something of his calling card of course, but he also provides quick overviews of the Marian dogmas (immaculate conception and ascension) as well as the Sacred Heart devotion. Overall, it was an easy-to-read, broad scoping history of the Roman Catholic Church. Is it filled with Küng’s personal biases which are often times painfully overt? Sure, yeah. But as long as this isn’t your only church history book you ever read I think it’s a fine one to pick up and consider.
