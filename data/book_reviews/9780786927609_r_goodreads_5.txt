Hand of Fire picks up right from the end of Crown of Fire. When I say that, I mean this book is more of the same for the trilogy, and unfortunately that is not a compliment. The chase continues as the bad guys, and there are so many more bad guys, look to steal Spellfire from Shan.We find the group in the final leg on their journey to Silverymoon. In attempt to gain safe passage, Shan and Narm get a spell to hide their true identities, which is quickly lost because Shan cannot keep her composure, which just fits her character so well as this point. Once the Shan makes it to the caravan leader, the team is off. Throughout the journey, other players in the caravan are discovered to be Zhents, Red Wizards and the like.In the end Shan realizes that she can't control this power and she is under the assumption that she got Narm killed. In response, self immolation. For three books, we got an over powered wizard who was dropping dragons, beholders and the like with a blink of the eyes and in the end it was all for naught.Like the first two books, this convoluted and busy. Far too many characters, especially villains. The character development remains lackluster.Overall, this book is a 1.2 at the best and lacks on reasonable conclusion to characters in this trilogy.
