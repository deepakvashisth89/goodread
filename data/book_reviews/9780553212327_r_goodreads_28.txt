When a child reads this book, they feel like they're in a fantasy world. Indeed, looking at the book, the giant man and the tiny people are just figments of imagination for many. When an adult reads it, they actually see a person's passion for adventure and the wonder of being in new lands. Gulliver's kind, understanding, and conciliatory demeanor, despite his enormous size, contrasts sharply with the harsh, disciplined, uncompromising, and rule-bound attitude of the Lilliputians, leaving the reader astonished. One can't help but wonder if the author is creating irony by using this contrast. Gulliver, with his enormous size, hesitantly asks for favors, while the Lilliputian king, who is tiny in comparison, doesn't care. He does many good deeds for the king and his country, and the king later fears his power and decides to punish him. Gulliver could inflict unimaginable harm on Lilliput, but he remembers his promise to the king and gives up. The powerful one, by keeping his word, becomes even more exalted in his own eyes. A humble giant man, arrogant little people, and their mysterious world – the journey begins here. Have a good trip.
