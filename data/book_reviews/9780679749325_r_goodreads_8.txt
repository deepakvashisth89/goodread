This novel tells the story of a young Egyptian woman, Asya, from her late childhood through the early part of her adult life (years covered are 1967 to 1980). The story is set in Egypt, her homeland, and England, where she is pursuing a PhD. This is a very long novel, and the author paints Egypt in wonderful detail – from details of family life, to food, to buildings, and to culture. Of course, there were a number of political events in Egypt and the Middle East between 1967 and 1980, and these add to the descriptions and the plot. While reading, I was reminded that virtually all the same issues are being brutally fought over today. The novel is also the story of Asya’s marriage, which was complicated – loving but unsatisfying; calm but tumultuous. A great deal of time is spent on Asya’s introspection, which was interesting (and nicely done), but seemed to me to go on forever. I did like the fact that Asya was not always a likeable heroine – sometimes I just wanted to say “Really, Asya, wake up and see what you are doing/destroying.” I loved this author’s previous novel, Map of Love, and although I liked certain aspects of this one, it dragged a great deal in many places (for me).
