Generally good actionable advice, but the philosophy behind it is flawed. It places full blame on the individual for their internal experiences, even uses contemptuous language when giving examples of REBT not working for individuals as if REBT should apply to *everyone* and if it's not working, the person isn't trying (which would be an iB according to his own philosophy).

In one example, he has a woman do REBT for months to stop her insecurities that came from watching her husband stare at beautiful women instead of teaching how to effectively communicate in asking him to gawk less in front of her.

It really doesn't cover the complexities of dealing with negative circumstances that, when used naively or maliciously, can teach someone how to tolerate unhealthy circumstances (such as IPV) by inflexibly assuming that the recipient's belief systems are the cause of their misery when, in reality, it's not as black or white that the book states. Although even Ellis doesn't say it's black or white. He says it's 100% the onus of the person in misery.

I think the book can legitimately be helpful in the actionable tools it provides when used appropriately. It's easy to get carried away by his charisma in the way he writes, but there are so many limitations that don't get addressed and would be important for laymen to know before reading.
