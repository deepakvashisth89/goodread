M. Elaine Mar (b. 1966)'s 1999 memoir Paper Daughter is a detailed recounting of her experience immigrating to the United States from Hong Kong in the early 1970s. Her father, followed by the author and her mother, moved to the Denver, Colorado area for economic reasons, and heavily relied on other family members established in the area for employment and housing. Their experience was far from glamorous; Mar and her parents lived in a spare room in a relative's basement, and her parents and later Mar worked in the kitchen of a Chinese restaurant in what sounds like an under-the-table arrangement. Money and resources were very scarce, and Mar recounted her difficulty with fitting in and facing discrimination among her largely white classmates. When Mar became a teenager, she rebelled and had a rocky relationship with both of her parents, and instead used her intelligence and academic skills to get accepted into a prestigious summer program and later Harvard to basically flee from her family. The book abruptly ended right when Mar started her freshman year at Harvard.This book was published in 1999 when Mar would have been 33 years old, so I'm disappointed that she doesn't go into what happened in college and beyond. Often it takes decades of adulthood to properly contextualize and learn from difficult childhoods and understand what our parents and other adults in our lives were going through.
