Started the year off right with a book that asked, “What if your hot legal guardian was a murder suspect with intimacy issues?” and I said, “Go on.”

Nicholas Bragg is basically if Mr. Darcy had a criminal record and the emotional range of a brick wall. Society thinks he murdered his wife (he didn’t), and he copes by being allergic to feelings and trying to auction off his teenage ward like she’s an inconvenient houseplant. Enter Jane, who’s like, “I want love, not your weird guilt proposal, thanks,” and proceeds to vanish into the night and reinvent herself as a secret stage actress. Icon behavior.

Meanwhile, Nicholas is busy doing everything except dealing with his feelings — keeping a mistress he doesn’t want, brooding, aggressively refusing to grow. When he and Jane finally cross paths again, it’s all lies, hidden identities, sexual tension, and Nicholas trying to express love through the medium of moral obligation. Spoiler: Jane’s not into it.

Add in a surprise child, a scandal resurrection, and a frankly hilarious amount of dramatic miscommunication, and you've got one of those old-school historicals where the men are a mess and the women are tired.

It’s trope soup: guardian/ward, hidden identity, forced proximity, "I hate feelings" hero, the whole historical angst buffet. And yes — I loved it. I have no shame.

Why four stars and not five? Because even though it hit, it didn’t leave shrapnel. Also, I read Off Balance right after, and frankly I’m still recovering from the whiplash of that chaos.

Would recommend if you like your romance with maximum emotional constipation and heroines who actually walk away when the man starts talking about duty instead of love.
