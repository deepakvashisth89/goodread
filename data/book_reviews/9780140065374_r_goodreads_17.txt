There's so much to like and little not to. If I were only a slightly different kind of reader this would be 5 stars for sure, but I do try to save those for my absolutely tops.First of all, it's not really a mystery. I thought it was, but I was wrong. It's a social satire with a mystery element, rather than the other way around. It's shaped quite like a mystery (a death, lots of suspects, a detective (or two!) and so forth) but it isn't really one. You would not be able to figure out whodunnit until the point that the writer tells you whodunnit.Maybe this is my lack of understanding Australia at play, but I had trouble placing the time-period. There was the casual racism of Agatha Christie in the 1930s, but the motel would (I'd think) suggest the 1940s or later, while the cocktail party hinted at the 1920s, 50s, or 80s. It was quite startling about 95% of the way through to find a reference to Burt Reynold and know for certain when it took place. This isn't a bad thing, but I found it unusual to be so at sea, personally.And (don't skip ahead!) the last line's a corker. If any of you feel like once you know who did it, the rest of the book can go hang, please, not in this case. Read to the very very end, it's worth it. It'snota twist where everything you knew before gets subverted, it's just, Wow, that last sentence. I promise.Most of the characters are awful in one way or another (the racism is outrageous, not on the part of the author, it's clearly the characters) and the police detective is nearly as bad as Inspector Dover. Maybe worse, actually. Yes, worse, just better-looking, which can trick you when you're superficial like I admit to being. In a way it's a bit likeA Confederacy of DuncesmeetsNice WorkmeetsGaudy Night, with some Inspector Dover tossed in.Which all comes down to saying well worth reading. Terrific book. High 4 stars, can't bring myself to rounding it up though.(Note: I'm a writer, so I suffer when I offer fewer than five stars. But these aren't ratings of quality, they're a subjective account of how much I liked the book: 5* = an unalloyed pleasure from start to finish, 4* = enjoyed it, 3* = readable but not thrilling, 2* = disappointing, and 1* = hated it.)right, Elizabeth.
