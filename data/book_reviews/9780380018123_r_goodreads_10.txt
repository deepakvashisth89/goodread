Imagine the depth of Leo Tolstoy, the humor of Woody Allen and the absurdity and originality of Douglas Adams, all under the same roof -- this is how I picture Stanislaw Lem.The Star Diariesis the brilliant example of his genius, and the English translation by Michael Kandel doesn't disappoint.I read lots of Lem in my teen years, but after rereading this book, I want to reread all he wrote. Back then I thought of his writings as funny and entertaining, but now I can see so much more here, including scathing satire of the Soviet regime and its key figures like Stalin.Too bad Stanislaw Lem isn't well-known outside of the Eastern Europe (with the exception ofSolaris, popularized by Tarkovsky's film and many times more by a later Hollywood version). Lem truly deserves a world-wide fame.Read in 2012.P.S. So I read this in 2012, and now, in 2025, as I stumbled upon this tiny review and couldn't help rewriting my lame 2012 sentences into a better shape, I see that in spite of my good intentions, I read only one more book by Lem, in 2016. Besides, in 2020 I inherited from my elder brother a whole bunch of Lem's books in Polish, bought in our local bookshop in Belarus ages ago. Back then my brother and dad read all of them, and I somehow stumbled through one, half-guessing what was going on. I now look at them wistfully every morning as I do my morning exercises...
