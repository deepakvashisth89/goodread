It seems like this book was published under another title “Mavreen.” My edition on open library was named “Scarlett.” Not sure why this editing change was made. Maybe the publisher decided Mavreen was not an enticing title for “modern day” readers. I don’t know if editing changes were made to the content. Anyhoo…

“Scarlett” is the story of the illegitimate daughter of an English aristocrat, in the late 18th century. I know that authors can take liberties with historical fiction but this quickly became historical fantasy and not the good kind. Not only is the titular Scarlett beautiful beyond belief, but she is also educated like a Nobel Prize winner, endowed with Mozart-like musical talent, can tame a wild stallion at the age of five, can dance like a ballerina, can discuss geopolitical issues of the day, can speak several languages like a native etc. This was of course the trend for 1970s bodice rippers to make their heroine into an incomparable Mary Sue perfect heroine but this book really took it too far. At least with the whimsical books of Virginia Henley and Beatrice Small, you can kind of tell that the authors are in on the joke and you just enjoy the ride at the amusement park. This book is too earnest and clumsy for that. Especially as the heroine becomes more and more of an annoying Mary Sue and her love interest is a limp lettuce leaf, weasely cad who is definitely unworthy of her martyrdom. I stopped around page 200 of this 627 page doorstopper! Claire Lorrimer aka Patricia Robins has written some very entertaining non-Harlequin romance books published by the Linford Romance Library. This foray into historical fiction was not as enjoyable.
