A nuclear storage facility is robbed and some employees taken hostage by the robbers, who have packed them into two vans and disappeared. California cops Detective Ryder and his Highway Patrolman son Jeff learn that their wife/mother Susan Ryder is among those kidnapped and are on the case. Why the kidnapping? Can these terrorists build a bomb with the materials they took? And why the foreword about how fragile the fault lines are around San Francisco Bay? And why is the title of this book "Goodbye California?" Sounds ominous...The super-villain in this disaster thriller is a polite cult leader who holds an expansive estate in the mountains of California,"Goodbye California" (1977) struggles quite a bit with prose and fears that are pretty dated now, with large sections of text dedicated to the weaknesses of US nuclear materials security, how easy it would be for someone to steal and build a bomb, and how often computers and security personnel disagree on inventory and processes, stuff like that. Modern technothrillers might touch on the same idea but this one is based on 1970's security and technology and reads that way.The Ryder characters, especially the Dad, are also written a bit silly; this guy is so no-nonsense he doesn't bat an eye at the fact his wife is abducted and instead throws insults around deadpan at other investigators as he works the case. He reads like a cross between Officer Poncherelli and Dirty Harry. Okay, reading that it sounds like it might be fun but it isn't.Also taking up a lot of print space are scientists discussing fault lines and the Richter scale and predicting big earthquakes and I wanted to just kill myself.Verdict: Quit on page 166 (of 315) for boredom. I know there's a disaster coming, and I'm sure it will be epic in scale and terror, but I'm an impatient man and quite-literally nothing happens in this book for the first 166 pages except the hero breaking in to his boss's house and his boss's lawyer's house, scientists talking nonstop about pet causes, and a megalomaniacal bad guy telling his prisoners he means them no harm.Jeff's Rating: 1 / 5 (Bad)movie rating if made into a movie: PG
