Surprisingly as iconic as Chinua Achebe is, he is not an author that I read until now.Things Fall Apartis arguably the most famous African novel of all time and is usually the first one people go to when they want to explore writers from the continent but it’s actually the first part in a trilogy that is aptly named”The African Trilogy.”However, I’m not getting into that right now. Instead, I readGirls at War and Other Storiesby Chinua Achebe in one sitting on a Friday night while waiting on a thunderstorm to strike my city.To put it simply, there were some stories that were standouts among a lot of “okay” stories. None of them are reallybadand I was able to read through this without spending too much time confused about the language. On the back of the book, the New York Times referred to this collection of twelve short stories as “first-rate fiction” and I have to agree with them. Achebe’s writing possesses a level of authenticity, power, and innocence that is so rare for writers these days. Achebe’s work takes on the problems that take place in modern Africa during his time and digs deep into an infant Nigeria, not even a decade independent from British rule, and the problems the country faced during the Nigerian Civil War.At times funny, poignant, and downright bizarre, these stories were a joy to read. Some of my favorites were:Girls at War,Sugar Baby,The Madman,Vengeful Creditor,The Sacrificial Egg, andMarriage is a Private Affair. Through these stories, we see a titan of a writer who is flexing his wide breadth of knowledge and range. The stories showcase tragedy and resilience while introducing people to a part of the world they may be unfamiliar with. While we are a bit far removed from times like this in the year 2023, these stories are about humanity and the things that we all do for love and protection.
