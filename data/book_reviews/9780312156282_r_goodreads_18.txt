Before I get into my review, I want to state that the subheading of “Tales of Tenure and Terror” is very misleading as the stories aren’t exactly what many would describe as ‘horror’. The third story is the closest to ‘horror’. Dark academia, dark comedy, etc would be better descriptors.Still, the author is a great writer, and while the subject matter would have otherwise bored me, I enjoyed his prose and the way he wrote these characters to be so pompous and insufferable lol, I looked forward to the inevitable car crash that awaited.I was pleasantly surprised to see that this book was published in 1998 because the stories are indeed blatant criticisms of misogyny and racism in academia. Not to say that there weren’t progressive people in the 90s, but compared to many male authors at the time, this is in contrast very refreshing to read.While all of the stories feature egotistical men walking themselves confidently into their own demise, the first really lacked the payoff to match the journey, especially compared to the other two. In terms of strength, I think #3 > #2 > #1.I did like that all of the stories were loosely connected, something I personally find to be a nice extra touch in short story collections!
