Don't bother reading this book - much better management advice is found on watching an old re-run of The Office. Simply put, the author breaks down a management 101 class to some really generic principles and just leaves it at that. If you've been a manager or have had a manager (so in otherwords represent 99% of the employed class) there's absolutely nothing new here. While there are a few interesting aspects of how Andy's day went (such as he prioritized the new employee orientation and I give him a lot of credit for doing that), it's not a memoir, it's a management book, and it's positively one of the most boring reads on the subject. There's a lot of attention to Mr. Gove lately, since OKR is a hot topic these days thanks to books like . But this isn't his opus and there's little relation to the principles of OKR and what Mr. Grove writes in this book.

Like other reviewers, I'm quite surprised that 's view is that this is one of the most influential books of the millenia. There are many books which stand the test of time - management books included - and this wouldn't have even stood in it's own time if I would have originally read it then. is a great book. , writes the new foreward to High Output Management as being a great tome, but his own book is much better.
