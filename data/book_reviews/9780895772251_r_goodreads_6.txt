Unfortunately, I’m not very impressed. But I suppose I should’ve known better since this is an American author in the 1800s.
The endless word babble that starts out nearly every story can put a man to sleep easily. Once the actual story gets going it’s usually more interesting but my god, the trouble getting there.

Of the 19 short stories in this collection I did have a few I enjoyed.
The Cask of Amontillado was great, insane and no answers, I loved that.
The Black Cat did horrify me in a way, but just because I hate reading about animals getting hurt.

The others I found more of a struggle, but each did often have its own certain imagery or intrigue to make it interesting. Just a lot of them fizzled out in the end, less horrifying unknown and instead just some coked out guy. Oh well. I understand at the time this was brand new and unique.

Personally, Poe strikes me as a depressed, mopey guy who likes to pretend to give in to his ‘perversions’ and has a great fear of death. Not someone I’d hang out with.
