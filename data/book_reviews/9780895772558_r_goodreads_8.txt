In a small market town commonly referred to as Tarry Town is a glen known as Sleepy Hollow, thought to still hold 'witching power'. The main haunting of the region is an apparition of a headless horseman, said to be the ghost of a Hessian trooper who lost his head to a cannonball in battle.Ichabod Crane is a teacher at the local schoolhouse who finds temporary lodging by flitting from one parent to another by helping out on their farms. He is also quite taken with a beautiful woman named Katrina Van Tassel, whom he hopes to marry, but he has a love rival in Brom Bones. When Katrina eventually rebuffs him at a party one night, he heads out for a ride, only to never be seen again.A gloomy, atmospheric gothic tale that is written in wonderful, descriptive, poetic-style prose. The story itself is quite basic, and the headless horseman is little more than a bit part, which was surprising given it's what the whole book is known for. It always surprises me how much these classics get watered down with the various adaptations, so when you do get around to reading the original story, it bears scant resemblance.The ending is left with a touch of mystery as to whom it was Ichabod Crane encountered on his journey and where he disappeared to, but given the story is heavily based on a love triangle and there are hints of foreshadowing earlier on with regard to pranks and mischief, it certainly leads us to a particular conclusion.I did like this for the most part, but it does veer off into irrelevant rambling at points. I also don't think the postscript at the end was needed, as it deviated from the ambience of the rest of the book. Plus, it didn't really add anything to the plot arc other than to let us know a man is narrating a tale from what has been passed down through the grapevine, so it may or may not be true. Which only reinforces the ambiguity and, to be fair, was probably the author's intention.
