This is a well-written, quick and easy read that on the surface is entertaining. My favorite bit was a description of the health food store and the child's name being "Earthshoe." I did chuckle at that.

The book IS funny, that's not the problem. The problem is that somehow as the vignettes unfold, the protagonist becomes increasingly unsympathetic. He turns his high beams on both country neighbors and city folk, and is snide about both, yet somehow it just isn't a pleasant "laughing with" type of humor. He comes across as condescending and arrogant, unpleasant and struggling with a superiority complex that no amount of faux self-effacing humor can quite distract us from.
In contrast, I have enjoyed David Sedaris's work over the years, and find him to be a master of dismembering a person or subject with alarming and hilarious accuracy. Yet he doesn't come across as an unpleasant person. Mullen does not achieve this.

By the time I was finished, I felt that the author, while a fine writer with a sharp wit, is not someone I'd want to have a beer with, no matter how good the beer.
