This is my first experience with author Peter Lovesey. The premise is that two former WW@ colleagues are linked by their urges to rid themselves of their husbands. Complications ensue.So the book started in the direction of Strangers on the Train, but it rapidly diverged when paperwork began to complicate the plans. And for me, while the paperwork necessarily is a facet of actual 20th century bureaucracy/protocols, it doesn't make for compelling storytelling, at least as told by author Lovesey in this book. A little bit of this would have gone a long way.I liked that the principal storyteller's descent into the rabbit hole of her unexpected accomplice produced the psychological turmoil that one might, contrarily, actually expect. She's not in control of the situation, but wants to be and tries to be, but is thwarted at (almost) every turn.I didn't like that the ultimate hurdle was the British civil service and funeral requirements. As I wrote above, while it's probably true, it's not an entertaining story.So, points to Lovesey for coming at the Strangers on a Train premise from a different angle. But points deducted for failing to complete that journey in a satisfactory way.
