Ginny is a pretty normal teenage girl: she feels out of place in her new school, her parents are too wrapped up in their own problems to notice her existence, and so on. Her frustration makes her a possible tool for Loki, who wants her to unleash Fenrir (currently held immobile by “the Mjollnir”) and destroy the world, or at least Oshkosh.

As so often happens, this story tries to make Loki the bad guy and utterly fails.

“Loki was the loner always - independence was his byword. Rules and regulations grated on his nerves; the social glue made him want to vomit…. Ashes and splinters - he still ached after them, leaning into the morning breeze.”

“’Safety,’ Loki said. ‘And danger. That was it. Sitting behind fences, peeping through the slats at life going by. Or coming out into the open, taking risks. The edge of danger that gives you and me the idea we’re having fun. Screeching that bike just to the edge, feeling your stomach in your socks and your blood roar. Being alive.’”

“Despite herself, Ginny felt the power in the weight and the shine of the hammer, so strong and definite in its lines. It had to be the opposite of Loki. This was form and direction. Loki was slipping and sliding and coming around from the side. The shape-changer. Well, what was so wrong with that? Ginny remembered his easy jokes, the blue shine to his eyes.”
