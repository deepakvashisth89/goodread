In December 1984 a toxic gas leak, from a Union Carbide Pesticide Plant, massacred between 18,000 and 30, 000 people. Over 500,000 people were exposed to methyl isocyanate (MIC) gas. When water is introduced to this toxic gas the result is cyanide.UC was a gigantic American Corporation. Among other products, they made Sevin, a pesticide. By introducing this product to the Indian farm, the company felt they would be enlightening the farmer and also the saving their crops from wide spread insect infestation. No one in the chain of company experts took into account droughts or Monsoons. Eventually the cash cow UC envisioned failed. The plant was slowly being shut down and only operated with a small maintenance staff. Unfortunately, the maintenance necessary was not being done and the tanks holding the MIC (which were never suppose to be filled to capacity) were deteriorating,Doninique Lapierre tells a very even-sided story. Laying out the facts without making any judgments as to who was at fault. He introduces you to all the main characters and I became attached to so many of the people that lived in the shadow of the Pesticide Plant.This was not only a story of the disaster, but a story of the people. I would recommend this highly to any non-fiction readers.
