‘F2F.’ A random mass paperback amongst rubbish old pop fiction on an unfortunate op-shop bookshelf.

How it handles joking computer references is good, though they are clearly classic social touchstones.

Ellis is Elon. Kate is the creative spouse. He lives on Tesla St.

What begins as ambitious speculation on the power of computers and telephonic potential for MURDER, ends up as a short-sighted failed human approach, ultimately reskinning a tried serial killer thriller.

It has made me think back on the disturbia of serial killer-type minds in literature, and how intense it used to be at my first brushes with it. As my first brush with mature storytelling in novels, I read a thriller like this. It was called 'Impulse' and was standard without character. Scenes of crop black hair wigged pale sex workers riding their charge and then getting their head shot in the middle of it. It must have been the author's deepest idea of depravity in sexualised murder. That and the opening scene are the most memorable, aside from the beat where the protagonist gets his head shot by the killer but he survives so as to conclude the mystery. That was more intense than all of 'F2F' manages.

Another example is 'Orphan.' Watches at early tween age in a motel living room on the small TV in the corner by the roof with low volume so as to not wake my family. That was a twisty and surprising story of evil.

It’s the same premise as my exciting hyperpop slasher film concept, only, in 1994 ‘f2f’ was a bit more sci-fi. This much I could tell from the synopses, so I rushed back to the op shop to grab it and educate myself on a path less travelled. It played to the genre. With the book's ending, Finch should've gone harder on the contrast of the all power of internet and computerised thought, versus the frailty and smallness of humans. It was a lame end, without thinking deeply on many of the speculative themes it appeared to engage in. I can't believe there are VR headsets at the end. It seemed like some extra developed levels of technology with the video game but then they bring out an immersive VR headset at the end. It should've had the power to shock the victim, it begs the question of who created it, and why it hadn't been mentioned until now? 'Virtual Light' was almost this disappointing. But at least there was character in that.
