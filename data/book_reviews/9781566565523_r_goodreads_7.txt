Griffin, a professor at Clarement Graduate School in California, has written a nuanced, well-documented summary of many of the questions surrounding 9/11. An ethicist, he carefully distinguishes between several levels of culpability as regards the main events of that day, namely, each of the two towers, the Pentagon and the aircraft downed in the Pennsylvania field. Too often, authors dealing with 9/11 have lumped everything together, proof of governmental lies (and there have been many) in one instance being extended to suggest intentional lying in all instances and even direct involvement of governmental personnel and agencies in the planning of that day. Griffin avoids such appeals to emotion, relying instead on documented facts and claims--enough to draw the certain conclusion that there was govermental deception and cover-up and to adduce a whole host of plausible hypotheses as regards the actual events at variance from "official" reports.
