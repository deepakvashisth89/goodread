I was blown away by this book which I’d never heard of before buying it in a charity shop (thank you Oxfam Bookshop, Skipton) earlier this year.

The Haldmarne family are scraping a living from farming during the Great Depression. Marget, the middle daughter, is the narrator looking back over a ten year period. She has a low opinion of herself -



- but her love of nature evidences a depth of feeling that she is unable to express outwardly. She and her family are as much part of the landscape as the failing crops, dying trees and struggling animals. Even though I have no knowledge of many of the plants, flowers and natural features she describes, yet I could see them in my mind’s eye as I read. Family life was claustrophobic so the only escape was to wander and wonder at the natural world for which Marget has a deep love which is matched only by her feelings for Grant, the farmhand.

This is not a feel good book. It is depressing, as it should be given the nature of the environment and the period in which it’s set, but there is an element of admiration for those who carried on trying to make a living in the face of intense hardship because they had no choice, or because it was all they knew. Many issues are covered such as mental health, unrequited love, the submissiveness of women, and the drive towards more modern systems which were leaving the poor behind.

For me, this is a straightforward 5 stars. I’m surprised that many reviewers have only given it 4 or fewer. I know star ratings are spurious and everyone has their own personal ratings system but still, this is simply a wonderful piece of writing.
