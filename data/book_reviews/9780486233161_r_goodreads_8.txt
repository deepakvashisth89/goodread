I think anyone who’s read this classic would agree - it’s totally bonkers.

From start to finish, it’s a wild ride through pure nonsense. You’ve got mice and ducks arguing about grammar, a hookah-smoking caterpillar asking existential questions, a turtle who cries nonstop, and tea parties that never end. And don’t even get me started on the Queen who wants to behead everyone for no reason. What is going on?

And if you think Alice is the voice of reason in all this chaos - think again. She’s out here worrying about how to curtsy while falling down a rabbit hole and writing poems mid-conversation. She’s just as weird as everyone else. Absurdity is in her DNA.

But here’s the thing: for all its madness, ’s world isn’t totally unmoored. The tea parties and croquet games were real parts of Victorian life, and the Queen of Hearts is clearly poking fun at the era’s obsession with rules and authority. Fun fact - Queen Victoria supposedly loved the book.

Even the nonsense has roots. The bit about the 3 sisters eating treacle is based on the real Liddell sisters, especially little Alice, who inspired the whole story. That grounding in reality is what makes the book feel oddly believable, even when it’s at its most ridiculous.

Now, compare that to a lot of modern absurdist lit - super serious, kind of joyless, and seems more interested in making you work for meaning than actually telling a good story. It’s like they want you to feel smart for “getting it,” kind of like . You’ll find a lot of existential dread, alienation, and not much fun.

, on the other hand, just wanted to tell a fun story. Wonderland is weird, yes, but it’s also magical. The conversations make no sense, but they’re charming. Sure, you can dig for deeper meaning if you want - but you don’t have to. The joy is in the ride.

And ’s writer is just insanely brilliant. Alice’s rambling thoughts, ’s cheeky little side comments, the parody poems - it’s all super smart. The wordplay, the puns, the rhythm of the language - it’s not just silly, it’s skillful.

What makes Alice special to me, is the balance. It’s playful but thoughtful, chaotic but somehow coherent. Just when it’s about to go completely off the rails, pulls it back on track. That’s the magic of nonsense - not everyone gets it, but when you do, it’s kind of wonderful.

That said, I wouldn’t hand this to a toddler. The language is tricky, and the concepts are pretty out there. Even adults might miss some of the layers. But if you’re up for a strange, smart, and surprisingly deep adventure, it’s worth the trip.
