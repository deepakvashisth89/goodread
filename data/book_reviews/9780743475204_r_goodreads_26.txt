Why did I not know this existed before Nemira published it?... Is it possible that "Game of Thrones" hid everything else George R.R. Martin ever did, since it's "not at all like Game of Thrones, so ignore"?Anyway, to call "Wild Cards" a short story anthology would be a bit like saying "A Superman omnibus is a short story anthology". Technically, that's what's going on: you have stories written by different authors, collected by an editor and sold together. Practically, you have a single world, characters who reappear in multiple stories, different writers all adding something to a bigger story - and, of course, you have superheroes.The bigger story is this: there's an alien planet where people are genetically very similar to humans. They're also way bigger jerks than humans, and some of them have paranormal powers and want to increase their numbers, so they devise a virus which would genetically modify people to become superpowered (1% of the population). Or die (90% of the population). Or be horribly disfigured (9% of the population).And, to test it, they send it to Earth - as a sort of bio bomb. An alien who's less of a douchebag tries to stop it, but it goes off anyway over a crowded city - and he sticks around on our planet to try to help the afflicted (especially the horribly disfigured ones).The stories are mostly about people who have been changed by the virus, whether they're superpowered or disfigured - and they're realistic and gritty and entertaining.
