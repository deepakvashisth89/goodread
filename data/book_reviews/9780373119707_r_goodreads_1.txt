The specter of heroine's late step-sister hovers malevolently over the protagonists of Daphne Clair'sLover's Lies, wrecking their courtship from beginning to the bitter end.As a child, the heroine worshipped her glamorous, beautiful older stepister. She was too young to realize that said stepsister was a sociopathic, lying, weaselly, cheating, home-wrecking, adultering, rapacious, selfish, cruel, nymphomaniac, self-centered, gold-digging hall of famer. Well, she knew about the cheating part, because the stepsis enlisted the help of the 13 year old heroine to act as go between between her and her lover, the stud gardener who apparently serviced both the house and the stepsis, during that long, hot summer. (No cliche was left unturned in the making of this story).Older stepsis was married to a nice, wealthy, older, career-oriented man. Stepsis confided in her little sister about how unhappy and neglected she was, and how she had finally fallen in true, romantic love with the hot stud. Heroine was a worshipful 13 year old who thought her stepsis was the bee's knees, so she overlooked the immorality of the adulterous affair, sided with her stepsis against her supposedly mean husband, and vicariously lived through her sister's romance with the stud. However, that same summer, the stepsis killed herself, and the heroine believed it was because the stud dumped her stepsis after stepsis begged him to run away with her and live their love in the open. The heroine grew up blaming and hating the stud for what she thinks he did to her beloved stepsis.Which is why, when she accidentally bumps into the stud ten years later, on a tour to China, she still believes he is the Anti-Christ and proceeds to revenge herself upon him in a cold, callous, completely calculated manner as to make even the most Old Skool, Alpha, revengeful Greek tycoons of vintage HPlandia shake their heads in disbelief.The stud-hero has no inkling that this gorgeous woman is the gangly 13 year old who acted as a go-between messenger between him and his mistress ten years ago. Heroine romances him, gives him the best sex of his life, then dumps him spectacularly by making a public toast at the tour's concluding banquet, in front of all their fellow travelers who had watched what they thought was a sweet holiday romance developing before their eyes. If you thought getting dumped by a post-it note was humiliating, you have to experience this bitch doing her dumping via toast, wine glass in hand, to really understand what humiliation means.Even AFTER the Great Big Terrible Misunderstanding is cleared up, namely that the hero did not dump the stepsis, it was the stepsis who dumped him, and that in fact, she was cheating on her husband with numerous stud-muffins, heroine only does a half-hearted apology that doesn't even come close to satisfy my grovel-needing heart. And on top of it, she asks the hero, on the last page of the book, if he wouldn't mind naming THEIR future daughter after her stepsis.Yeah, you read that right. Cause nothing could make hero happier than gaze at his sweet baby girl's guileless eyes and cherubic face and call her by the name of the married woman who amused herself with his teenaged-self while also having an affair with the married-with-children, next-door neighbour, called the hero a gigolo that she would never run away with when he told her he was in love with her and begged her to leave her husband, and then crashed her car into a bridge when she found herself pregnant but her married lover refused to leave his wife and kids.Un-freaking-believable. DC definitely reversed the roles in this one. The so-called hero had no pride and the heroine was disgusting and irredeemable.
