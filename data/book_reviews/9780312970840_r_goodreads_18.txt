This is the best Ngaio Marsh I've read, and it's a bit amazing that it's from such a late date (1968). But it has so many things going for it: strong and compassionate guidance from Troy for the first two-thirds; a really clever structure that plays up the mysterious elements and teases the reader; an isolated group with many many believable suspects; a perfect river cruise setting described with a painter's eye; fog and creepy alleyways; a well-considered (for a white writer in 1968) look at racism in England; and Alleyn at his most elegant and adroit. It would be interesting to read it at the same time asDeath on the Nileand spot the differences between Christie and Marsh. I give this one the edge. On to 2023!
