I’m aware that to some extent this is a worse-than-usual Heinlein book, but it’s far and away my favorite. Its model is just as much Horatio Alger or even Hardy Boys as it is science fiction, and its science fiction models are very much in the Gernsback mould. But this lack of ambition means that Heinlein cannot fail (as he so often does for me). And furthermore the didacticism (which is not wholly absent) seems less like Heinlein and more like a Alger / Stratemeyer didacticism. Maybe that *shouldn’t* make a difference, but to me it took the edge off my teeth (or whatever). It just felt like part of the genre, and not Heinlein’s heavy hand.And what you have left is a wonderful Boy’s Own story with lots of science. The hard parts are too easy (always a problem with Heinlein, I think), but there are moments when I thought it was going to be really hard, and that was exciting to me.
