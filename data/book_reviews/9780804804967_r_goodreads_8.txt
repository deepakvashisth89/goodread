After reading the 1973 text, I can certainly say that the Chinese characters which form the backbone of Japanese writing are much more visually comprehensible to me than they were before. The author does a great job of demonstrating the patterns of how individual characters are constructed, though some of the etymologies had me a little skeptical. On the whole, it effectively demystified a major component of Japanese orthography for me, even if I would have to study much harder to actually learn it. Pronunciation, however, could have used a more thorough explanation.

I had to laugh at the inclusion of such critical example vocabulary as "cannibalism" and "anti-communism." I also had to cringe (hard) at the description of the hypothetical model of the character for "woman" as "a pregnant young thing." I will assume that post-1970s editions of the text incorporate more dignified language.
