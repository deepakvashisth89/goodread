Someone thought the movie based on this novel was better. That’s often not the case, but may go to explain my less than enthusiastic review, centred around the story of a vinyard in France.

I don’t recall who gave me this German edition of ‘A Good Year’ (Ein guter Jahrgang). None of my friends have owned up to it, but it was good opportunity to try another German text (admittedly with my online dictionary and Google translator close at hand) as it has been a long time between drinks. Pun intended.

I wondered if it was the language barriers that contributed to my overall dissatisfaction, but no, I have come to the conclusion that it was the fault of its light-on-substance storyline. To be honest, it felt a little silly at times.

There are some passages that seemed to spring from another era; descriptions of women that fitted more in an outdated James Bond film, and some unfortunate serotyping:

‘…eine Gruppe Zigeuner, hager und dunkelhäutig, die wie Haie in einen Schwarm tropischen Fische durch die Menge glitten…’ = ‘…a group of gypsies, gaunt/lanky and dark-skinned, that glided through the crowd like sharks into a school of tropical fish…’

Having said that, learning new German words made it an entertaining read:
die Sauregurkenzeit= slow/off/silly season.

I always have fun with idioms. Many I needed to look up:
einen (kleinen) Schwips haben = to be (a bit) tipsy.

But not this one:
in Morpheus’ Armen ruhen = to be in the arms of Morpheus.

It does mean the same in English (sleeping), but I’d never heard of it until one of my German Goodreads friends enlightened me. When I read the following it brought a smile to my face:

‚Zu Müde, um auch nur an Essen zu denken, nahm er ein langes heißes Bad, bevor er beinahe umgebend in Morpheus sank.‘ = ‘Too tired to even think about food, he took a long hot bath before he fell almost completely to sleep.’

Because of the German, it gets a little extra. 3.5 stars
