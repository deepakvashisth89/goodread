I read most of Iles' work back in the day and decided to reread some after hearing he passed away just a few days ago. Like many of Iles' novels,The Quiet Gametakes place in Natchez, Mississippi, and here he introduces Penn Cage, a lawyer turned novelist, who returns to his home town Natchez after the tragic death of his wife. Penn hopes staying with his parents will help his 4 yo daughter cope with her mother's passing.Well, upon returning to Natchez, Penn soon finds a mess of problems. I will not go into great detail here, but first, Penn's father is being blackmailed by a lowlife in town. Second, Penn gives an interview to the manager of the local rag; Penn now is pretty famous as an author and has the notoriety of a 'home town boy made good'. In the interview, a side topic concerns the secrets of old Natchez and Penn mentions the case of a black man who was murdered in 1968 via car bomb, but the case was never solved. Caitlin, the manager of the paper, publishes an article about the black man, Del, digging up the old dirt. This quickly becomes a flashpoint in town, which is divided pretty evenly among black and white folk...Iles tosses in enough plot twists, action, and political intrigue to keep the pages turning rapidly. Penn quickly becomes a 'Southern Man' hero of sorts, not being racist but tough on crime and an advocate of the death penalty. I liked the Southern Gothic feel of the novel, the social/political dynamics and the occasional subtle tweaks of those who see Mississippi as basically a racist wasteland. My only complaints concern who Iles made Penn into a Gary Stu at times, but that sometimes happen with thrillers. 4 quiet stars!
