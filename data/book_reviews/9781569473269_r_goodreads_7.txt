As the copy I was reading did not say on the cover that it was a Peter Diamond story, I thought at first the main police character was Henrietta aka Hen Mallin of Bognor Regis CID, assisted by her capable Detective Sargent Stella Gregson. So I was a bit irritated by the intrusion into the case by the bluff Diamond due to the victim turning out to live in Bath, the area he covers. He tends to take over, even where a particular aspect is definitely Hen's jurisdiction, and despite being tough and decisive, she defers to him and even says he was right towards the end of the novel despite his having not always made good decisions.There's a mention at one point that he misses his own capable female subordinate, Julie, but accepts that she is never coming back as she had had enough of working for him (this is a few books on from volume 5 which I read prior to this and saw exactly why). "Julie, the side-kick who'd taken one kick too many and asked for a transfer." He again dresses down a female subordinate, but at least doesn't do so in front of the rest of the team, so perhaps has learned something.Some aspects of the story did not convince, such as the victim conveniently leaving a computer journal that mixed frank details of her love life alongside her work notes. There's a gosh-wow reaction to computers, but I put that down to this originally being published in the early 2000s. The lack of social media at the time contributes to the delay in identifying the victim.I won't be continuing with Diamond, but have ordered the two other books in the short series featuring Hen (this volume apparently counts as 0.5 in her series). Overall, I would rate this as an OK 2 stars.
