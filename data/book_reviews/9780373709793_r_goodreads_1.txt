Did I pick this book up because the male protagonist is named Levi, which so happens to be my name as well? Yes. Did I look forward to the fact that he is an Architect which is similar to my career in construction and what I went to college for? Oh yeah! Did it speak to me because I was a new father just as the protagonist is a new parent? Yes. Did I read it after four years on my shelf because I needed something to fill a gap until my next book came to the library and was oddly excited to read this? YUP! But, did I enjoy it? ...meh.

There was some good in this book. I did like some of the little things designers and owner's representatives can't agree on. I liked the strange attachment the female protagonist had towards the baby, DJ. I enjoyed the ending, despite the main conflict of parenthood came in the last 75% of the book, the fallout, and the ending were summarized to come to a close all in 50 pages or so. I feel a little betrayed that the baby was the plot device rather than letting characters grow in the book.

I hoped this book would be humorous filled with the new parental experiences parents can relate to. I hoped that there was some depth to the characters other than a tragic backstory that was only briefly explained or heavily leaned on in some moments. I don't think the characters had to grow other than just realize that love was an option they could feel or wanted.

Everyone in this book is a surprisingly talented first timer who never really fails. The Sister, Ruth, is less developed than the brief fling she met or even a random child of Levi's old client. I wish there was more to this book, more depth to characters and more challenges they had to overcome, but alas, I was disappointed.

This book was not terribly written, but did we need the random Look Who's Talking moment of baby dialogue?

I feel conflicted, but I didn't look forward to finishing the book, but it was not so bad that I had to put it down and leave the fate of the characters to the void of disinterest.

This is the Burger King of books.
