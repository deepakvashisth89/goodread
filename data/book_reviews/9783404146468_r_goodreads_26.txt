When asked the common dinner party question: “If you could dine with anyone dead or alive, who would you choose?” I often found myself unable to answer the question. How on earth could someone be able to pick just one person in all of humanity? Well, after reading this book I can confidently say that I would choose Queen Elizabeth l. I would love to learn anything and everything I can about her life, her childhood, ascension to the throne, her relationship with Robert Dudley, her relationship with her court, her duration of her reign including most notably the war against Spain, and of course if the rumours of Arthur Dudley being her child were true.‘The Queen’s Bastard’ by Robin Maxwell was a great historical fiction novel that I think mostly needs to be taken with a grain of salt. You can tell Robin put her heart and soul into her research to create a believable case about the existence of Arthur Dudley, and others who nitpick historical fiction might just need to let the small things go. This novel was insightful, well researched, thought provoking, and overall greatly entertaining. I’m a fan of the Tudor period and it was amazing to get to read a fictionalise perspective of the Queen as well as a myriad about of other characters, see what their lives were like in the 16th century and read about significant events as well, like Englands victory over the Spanish Armada. This novel was amazingly well paced and very descriptive as it told the story of England over the years. Not only was it a tale about the illegitimate child Arthur but a tale about love, royale life, 16th century Europe, war, spying, and more.Maybe one day we’ll find out the truth about Arthur Dudley, or maybe i’ll get to find out myself ;).
