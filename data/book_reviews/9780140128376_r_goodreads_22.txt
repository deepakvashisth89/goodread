My Goodreads friend Marquise is a self-taught expert when it comes to fairytale retellings. And she described the retellings in this book as some of the best she’s ever encountered. How could I say no to such a recommendation?


The Bloody Chamber - 4/5

A young girl happily marries a much older French Marquis. But there’s something fishy about the Marquis. He’s already been married three times. And his former wives have all died in mysterious circumstances.

Their marriage takes a weird turn when he gifts her a choker with red rubies. A choker resembling a slashed throat.

When he’s away, the marquise starts to explore her new home with the keys he gave her. It doesn’t take long before she finds herself in a certain chamber and she realizes that she’s opened Pandora’s box. But did she close it in time to keep hope alive?


The Courtship of Mr Lyon - 3/5

Beauty’s father’s car breaks down. He spots a perfect rose in a garden and takes it for his daughter, Beauty. But the home owner is furious when he sees this stranger stealing a rose from his garden and confronts him about it. And that home owner is a lion-like creature called Beast. But appearances can be deceiving. He’s actually a lonely but quite gentle and kind person.


The Tiger's Bride - 4/5

Beauty is pretty much forced to take her clothes off, because her father lost her in a game of cards. When she refuses to do so, her new master Beast locks her up. But she doesn’t give up hope and tries to break out of her doll-like shell. Because she doesn’t want to be his property, she wants to be his equal.


Puss-in-Boots - 3/5

Figaro the cat wears boots and is always on the prowl, looking for some romantic quality time with anyone of the opposite sex. His owner, a young man, falls in love with a girl. Unfortunately, she’s already married to a very controlling and jealous husband. But that doesn’t stop Figaro from trying to help his owner get into her bedroom.


The Erl-King - 3/5

A young woman wanders into the woods and comes across the Erl-King, the king of the woods. They do the dirty deed and then he wants to turn her into a caged bird. But the little bird yearns to be set free. She wants to spread her wings and fly high in the sky.


The Snow Child – No rating/5

AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH! I’m truly sorry but I needed to get that out of my system.

Hey, what’s that floating away in the distance? Must be my innocence. Because I think I just lost it and I’m probably never getting it back again. Seriously, what did I just read?


The Lady of the House of Love – 2,5/5

A female vampire wants to suck a young soldier’s blood but instead invites him into her gothic bedroom.


The Werewolf – 3,5/5

A girl goes through a forest to visit her grandmother. But then she comes across a wolf.


The Company of Wolves - 3/5

A girl goes through the woods to visit her grandmother, not caring about the threat of the dangerous wolves. She instead comes across a hunter who can’t wait to take her clothes off.


Wolf-Alice - 3/5

A girl raised by wolves is trained a bit by nuns before being given to a duke.

_

As you can tell, I didn’t review these stories individually. I just didn’t want to repeat myself every time, as these stories are all quite similar in nature. I just want to say that I was expecting to find some dark fairy tales, because of the title. But when I actually started reading it, this book really took me by surprise.


Long story short: dark erotica. Now to be fair, this book probably pales in comparison to real dark erotica. There’s nothing overly graphic to be found here, well, apart from maybe one story. But I’m just not that big a fan of sex scenes in books. And there are a lot of them in here. They’re also quite dark. For example, there’s more than one story about necrophilia in here. If I’d known this in advance, I wouldn’t have bought this book to be honest. But I will admit there is still a lot to enjoy in this book. The thing is, these are all stories inspired by the original fairy tales but with a feminist twist. The female protagonists basically explore their sexuality to find light in the darkness and overcome it. And their character arcs are really strong. But the dark sexual nature of the stories is just not my cup of tea. Not even remotely to be honest.


Overall, a strong and well written character-driven and very very very adult fairy tale retelling anthology. Though it might not be for everyone.
