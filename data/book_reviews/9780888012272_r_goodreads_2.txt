I wanted to explore religious themes in science fiction as a fun spring break diversion, so I read this book along with stories from the fifties through the eighties. This collection of stories was published in 1998 and it shows its generational difference. If you could ask the book, it would tell you it is "spiritual, not religious."The selection of stories leans heavily on fantasy rather than fiction that has anything to do with science and the theology runs the gamut from trendy New Age fuzziness to indistinct mixtures of Buddhism, Taoism, and a vague conviction that everything happens for a reason. In case the reader can't extract a clear point of view from just reading the story, the authors write little essays to tell you what they mean.The editor of this anthology thanks "the One who started me on this path and encouraged me do it." She continues, "It's been a worthwhile and terrific experience with unseen and unexpected ramifications that far outreach the confines of a single, simple book." I agree. It's kind of like the problem of evil: If God is omniscient, how could He have encouraged the editor to put together such a poor collection of stories?
