You don’t know what intensity is until you’ve experiences a day in my life. Internally belaboring over decisions about going to the gym or reading, traversing long distances amidst a hellish landscape of bumper-linked automobiles, navigating the intricacies of corporate politics and socioeconomic brokering, struggling with six grade math while helping with homework and teaching lifelong lessons about discipline and emotional regulation. It’s all in a day’s work.

But being kidnapped by a sadistic psychopath, getting shackled to a kitchen table, and fighting a pack of bloodthirsty, violence-ridden, battle-trained Dobermans while making a pulse-poundingly precarious escape is pretty intense too.

This book was a lot of fun. I haven’t read Koontz in years, but this makes me want to go revisit some of his other well-regarded work. Let’s be honest, the man is wordy. This book is in-cred-ibly verbose. Each scene is described in more detail than is necessary, and there were moments I became exhausted with his explanatory excess. But this story is excellent. It’s a truly great thriller, and is amongst the most memorable I’ve experienced. It will probably be one of those I become even more fond of as I look back with the perspective of elapsed time…
