Wayne Care's novel, "Vietnam Spook Show," is a solid war story centered on the aviation-based intelligence campaign to gather information on the North Vietnam (NVN) air war against American. The information gathered on flights of the Navy aircraft was used to alert rescue forces to the location of downed American planes, and to identify the location and type of enemy defenses. The intelligence crews used radio and electronic means to identify and track NVN aircraft and missile crews within North Vietnam. These real-time reports led to the rescue of many downed American airmen.

Navy Petty Officer Craig Nostrum is a highly skilled linguist and listener who becomes obsessed with his job. It is a difficult job, made even harder due to a Queeg-like Navy Commander who, striving for promotion to Captain, is at war with the flight crews. These stressed crews, ill-disciplined, are driven by competition with one another and the conflict with their leader. The author, who is a veteran on such service, deftly describes the numbing effect of this phase of the war on the young, enlisted men flying against the enemy in their unarmed reconnaissance planes.

An excellent and engaging introduction to a little known aspect of the war in Vietnam. Well worth reading.
