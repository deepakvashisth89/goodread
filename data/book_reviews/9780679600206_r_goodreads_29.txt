White Nights: 4/5. The most traditional story of the collection, with some lovely moments and lines. A pretty capture of people who are only in our lives briefly but impact us forever.The Honest Thief: 3/5. I didn’t have much to love or dislike about this one.The Christmas Tree and a Wedding: 3/5. Same as above, neutral.The Peasant Marey: 5/5. The sustaining power of the memory of kindness in the midst of harsh conditions! Loved this.Notes from Underground: 4/5. Ah, the human condition. This guy’s got it bad. Brilliant, chaotic, and memorable.The Gentle Creature: 3/5. Overall I don’t love how Dostoevsky’s (all male) protagonists write about/interact with women and this was another example of that. I liked the final paragraphs though.The Dream of a Ridiculous Man: 5/5. The final insight of this story is the thing that sticks with me most from Brothers Karamazov - that active love of neighbor is the most certain way to strengthen faith and see the kingdom of God rightly. Only through a vision in which this is possible can life be lived well.“For I have beheld the Truth. I have beheld it and I know that people can be happy and beautiful without losing their ability to live on earth….The main thing is to love your neighbor as yourself…Once you do that, you will discover at once how everything can be arranged.”
