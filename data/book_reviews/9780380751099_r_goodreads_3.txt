If you like stories with no cohesive, organized world view, this is the book for you. It's what I imagine a bad acid trip would be like.This book was published the year I graduated from high school. If they bothered to read it, the woke folk would be shouting about appropriation, right after they got done shouting about invisibility and lack of representation. But one of the things I admire most about Arnason's generation of female speculative fiction authors is that they incorporated all different kinds of humans, and human ideas and stories, into their work well before it was fashionable.Arguably the best part of this book are the three appendices, where Arnason starts to demonstrate the skills she wields later in "Ring of Swords" and "Hwarhath Stories" -- brilliant virtuosity in extending scientific and ecological reality into shining possibility.
