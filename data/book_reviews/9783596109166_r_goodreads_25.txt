A haunting tale about four passengers on a plane discovering the lost paradise, Shangri-la, hidden away in the distant Kunlun Mountains.

I did not expect to like this one as much as I did. My first time hearing about it (and discovering), was at my local hospital's books by donation center. I liked the vintage cover, and that's also where I got my copy of the "Red Badge of Courage". Other than the cover, I knew nothing else of it.

I'm kind of glad I went into this blind, though. It read like a Twilight Zone episode and was able to hold my attention all the way through. Unsettling, yet intriguing. It was a fantasy novel without all the knights and dragons and a mystery without the murder.

I loved the twists and turns sprinkled throughout, too. It really has dated well. That's something you can't really say about a lot of older books. This was a great escape of a novel from the 1930s.

Highly recommended.
