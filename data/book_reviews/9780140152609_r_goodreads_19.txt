No Founding Father speaks to contemporary American more intimately than Benjamin Franklin: a man of science that wedded practicality with Yankee ingenuity; a politician who devoted himself to local causes that drive progress forward in his neighborhood, city, and colony; a fervent Englishmen who untethered himself English rights to cast about for a wholly new conception of power and liberty in the New World. Carl Van Doren’s biography is heavy on Franklin’s writings, but none the lesser for it, as Franklin’s word come alive with a wit and candor uncommon in the formality of George Washington, the cantankerousness of John Adams, or the soaring liberality of Thomas Jefferson.

Van Doren makes the case for Franklin as the man with the widest vision for what America could become, and actually became.
