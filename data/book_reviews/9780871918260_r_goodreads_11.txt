I read this story on the strength of Theo’s recent review. Theo (and H.G. Wells and many others) was right. It’s an outstanding story.

Four men in a small boat in the Atlantic, off the Florida coast, struggle to make it to land after their ship has sunk. The sea is rough and mighty, but with just two flimsy oars and a sail made from a coat they persevere and eventually spot land.

Land, yes. Safety, not so much.

It is unsurprising that Crane develops the man vs. nature theme in this story primarily from the perspective of the Correspondent, since its genesis is his own experience in a similar situation when he was a journalist in the Spanish American war. It’s an interesting blend of the physical challenges he faces with his internal struggles to come to terms with what is happening.

By the way, my copy of the story is in a collection of Stephen Crane’s works and so I didn’t have a specific link to use. I looked over the covers on the various English editions on GR and picked the one that seemed to capture the feeling of the story best. So many of the options looked as if they belonged with different stories completely!
