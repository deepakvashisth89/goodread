September ReadathonFine and for me no better.Everything bobbed along like a toy boat on waves far too big for it (or maybe a toy boat in a bathtub). The image of drama and tension but no substance.Many of the bigger moments of the leads' revelations or spelling out their feelings were internal, told to the reader and not each other. Along with a lot of telling about their chemistry and fond memories and very little building it actually happening.Heroine was a type that can sometimes get on my nerves, which is entirely on me but still true. lol. I am glad that amid her pratfalls and broad brush personality she got some wins. Hero was a decent chap and pretty flat for being a childhood crush grown into man-of-her-dreams.The deceit wasn't nearly big enough or centered enough to resonate and the big reveal was almost rushed. And the whole plot is built on one too many contrivances that I think could have been dispensed with or tightened -- we open with our heroine interviewing to be a columnist but we have zero idea why so she blunders at the interview but gets a "I like your style kid" from the paper editor, and then is reintroduced doing singing telegrams, and then finally we get to the devices that throw her and the hero together -- but I got the sense the author liked her humorous and big wink innuendo moments too much to make them work or pare them down.The secondary mystery plot was similar and not fleshed out enough to be either a romp or a red herring. I was relieved and did like that the heroine lampshaded "maybe it's a romance novel" when they found the terrible writing.
