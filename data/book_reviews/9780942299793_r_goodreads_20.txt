I have long wanted to read about the Situationist movement, after having read some radical socialist texts and some radical postmodern texts... what better synthesis is there to be found? "Society of the Spectacle" is a philosophical work that describes the working of the "spectacle" as a product of modern consumerism that exists in social interaction through means of pseudo -representation (if that doesn't mean anything to you, dont worry it didn't to me either at first). In essence, everything that was once lived, had receded in consumption, with eventually only the representation of consumption being important in social interaction.



This was written in the 1960's! far before the storm of social media that literally reduced all social interaction to simple representation through images. The work takes the implications of this phenomena and relates them to: the commodification of time, the isolation of the individual, the banalization of space, and the effect of modern economic production on the cultural domination of the proletariat. Another prediction was made that the production of wealth on the basis of knowledge instead of material good will become increasingly the economic engine of modern economies. We are now living in an economy where a large percentage of wealth is extracted from purely skill based abstract application onto material wealth.

I have long regarded my books as holy scriptures not to be: bent, scratched, torn, neglected, and certainly not written in. But in this dense scripture, where so many of what was of the critiques echoed as true in my ignorant perception on inherent cultural value, I was forced to make connotations and highlight passages that held importance to me.

I will give a short example, in which Debord applies the phenomena of the spectacle on the creation of urban civilization or the idea of 'urbanism'. This is known as the reactionary effect of modern society to most efficiently make use of its space in an exponentially growing population that seeks economic welfare in cities. However there is an inherent threat to the safeguarding of class power when workers are brought into close proximity in an urban working environment (the city). Sprawling isolation (urbanism) was then a more effective way in controlling the population. This individual isolation does however allow for a controlled reintegration of the working class for only the purpose of production and consumption, after which all return to their isolated existence far from the threshold of power or collective coercion. Only to now live their social lives in pure abstraction on an ether reality that is purely based on representation of consumption (having a job, having a family, a nice car, a big watch). The time spent participating in a regulated role of production and consumption can itself now be seen as a commodity, ("time is money").

These ideas are naturally much better explained in the book itself, yet even then are hard to wrap your head around. . This too was for me hard to wrap my head around, yet one of the last passages nicely summarizes why this is the case,



I will leave it at that, and strongly recommend that you pick this book up at some point in your life. Although it says here that I have read it only once I feel as if I have read it at least three times. The ideas are so dense and at times hard to grasp, but grappling with these ideas and negating certain structural assumptions in society are what make this book a joy to read.
