I want to start this review by saying I have read quite a few different biographies on Mae West. I get the feeling that this author doesn’t actually like Mae West that much. They focus on everything and everyone but Mae. If you want to know about who Mae actually was as a person, look elsewhere. This entire biography is purely about her work, and nothing more. With an almost pathological focus on her time in vaudeville, her time in movies makes up maybe 1/4th of the book. If you want to know anything about Mae’s life after what wasn’t even her last movie, you get a quick wrap up in an eight page epilogue. Paul Novak is mentioned twice and it’s only in the epilogue. It’s so, so incredibly dry as to be stifling. It’s a 352 page book and it took me a month to slog through. Also, I don’t need the history of the the third theatre that ran a vamp skit she did in her 20s. I chose to read the book for Mae, not the history of theater and the Hayes code. All in all, it’s really not a good biography of Mae West. I’d look almost anywhere else if you actually want to know about her as a person. Mae wasn’t a reader but I couldn’t imagine what she would have to say about this dry, plain toast of a biography.
