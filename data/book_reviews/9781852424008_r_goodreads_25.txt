Lynne Tillman is sooo good. The most exciting prose stylist I've read in ages. There's something cool and superficial about her writing, in the way it keeps you at a distance. And is primarily concerned with people, rather than the world around them. It doesn't run away with itself. and there are parts where you do feel like it's keeping you too much on the surface, but then she'll write a sentence which manages to capture a personality sharply in a remark or gesture or object, symbol or something. and you're like - yeah. and it's deep even though it's superficial and you feel it. I guess it's about trauma - the "we are all haunted houses" epigraph is a giveaway - and all of the characters are fucked up, and overt, objective bad things happen to them. But it's not A Book About Trauma. LT writes about experience through a matter of fact lens, and so much of the way the characters are feels... unexplainable or, like. it doesn't care to explain. It just wants to show, and record. and besides this is the default state. In that way, it feels adjacent to Raymond Carver, but how he would write if he was a woman, and chic. I also thought it was interesting how the girls are all quite different, yet also the same. Like they could be parts of Lynne Tillman, but also like, anyone. The writing is economical and cool but not try hard or detached, or boring, for the most part. Very dry, mean and funny in an insecure sardonic cool girl way. writes about sex and sexual experience in a way that feels as sex can often be, equal parts exciting and sad, and is queer with queer characters whose queerness is written about through the same matter of fact lens, neither sensationalising or stripping out. just being real.basically I love Lynne Tillman.
