A Passage to India is a classic that must be read, and was written as taking place in the early twentieth century set against the British Raj by Edward Morgan Forster chronicling the English occupation of India in the mid-1800's. The book is divided into three parts with the first part The Mosque focusing on the city of Chandrapore where we first meet Dr. Aziz, a Moslem doctor practicing at the government hospital in Chandrapore. Then there is the introduction of Mrs. Moore visiting her son, Ronny Heaslop. Accompanying her is Miss Adela Quested, possibly there to decide whether to marry her son. At a party there is discussion to visit the Marabar Caves, a fictional recreation of the iconic Bababar caves located in the Jehanabad District of Bihar, India. Magical and mysterious, indeed. This sets up the plot that propels this book through a lot of cultural and racial issues.

The crux of the book explores English colonialism and all of the attendant issues, namely the racial tensions and prejudices between indiginous Indians and the British colonists. Is it possible for an Englishman and an Indian to ever be friends? The racial stereotypes are deeply embedded in the culture of India. There is the undertone of racism that spills over throughout this book. It is no accident that this book has survived all of this time and was selected as one of the hundred great works of English literature by the Modern Library.
