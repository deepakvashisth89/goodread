My copy is a 2nd edition Vintage paperback, from an unknown year, because Vintage strangely seems to have forgotten to add a copyright page to the book. It is 424 pages, if you don't count the "bonus" 144 pages of notes, index, appendices.

424 was far too much. Nicholl comes close to naming every single human being who lived in Elizabethan England, to flush out his tale of who killed Christopher Marlowe. I chuckled on the last page of Chap. 33 when he writes: For these reasons, and perhaps as some kind of respite from the claustrophobia of these last chapters, I offer now for the first time a brief biography of Thomas Drury...
isn't quite the word I'd use, but wow, the insane level of detail is claustrophobic or oppressive or asphyxiating or SOMETHING.

This is a random passage from the Thomas Drury chapter just mentioned above... and keep in mind that Marlowe was killed in 1593, so this 1603 melodrama is a decade after Marlowe died:
Page 397

In this instance, as he does with what feels like dozens and dozens of side-characters in the Marlowe murder, Nicholl is trying to paint a picture for us of what kind of man Thomas Drury was, but Above it is Lady Sheffield and the Earl of Leicester, but in what feels like hundreds and hundreds of cases, Nicholl dredges up little Elizabethan England soap operas that do nothing but muddy his Marlowe storyline.

Nicholl and his editor (if he had an editor!) must have had conversations that went like this:
So this entire chapter on what's-his-name, the Earl of Northumberland, where you mention 785 different men, can we trim this down a bit? By which I mean, just delete it?
OH I trimmed it already, it was 18 chapters on the Earl originally, you're lucky I got it down to 1 chapter.

The level of detail, and the amount of research, is certainly impressive, but Nicholl should have used all this research to write a book about espionage in Elizabethan England, which is the book Nicholl seems like he really wanted to write.

In the end, you slog through 400+ pages to reach a conclusion which makes you kind of shrug and think, "yeah? well, sure, maybe?"
