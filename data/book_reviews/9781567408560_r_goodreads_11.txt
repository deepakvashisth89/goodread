Quick read, like most of Michael Lewis' books, but it felt less engaging than others. But, despite this book being published over a decade ago, it's still relevant and provides a lot of interesting context for the beginnings of the tech industry as we know it today.One main takeaway was how Jim Clark helped to shift the startup value system to favor labor (founders) and their ideas/execution over capital (VCs), but that's only true for some companies (Google, Facebook, etc.) but not for others, so this book is more a biography of Jim Clark than a complete history of Silicon Valley. Also, while I knew Jim Clark had founded Netscape, I hadn't known much about his involvement with Silicon Graphics or Healtheon (which later bought WedMD), which supports the idea that he really is unique in his sometimes blind grasp towards the next "new thing".
