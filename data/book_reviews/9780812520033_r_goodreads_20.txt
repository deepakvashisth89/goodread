I read most of the Borderlands books decades ago, and loved them, especially Will Shetterly's contributions. I'm not a re-reader, but somehow decided to read this, probably again. I remembered the setting and the vibes, but not the characters or story. It was still absolutely delightful. Young teen runaway Ron goes to Bordertown, falls in with other young humans and faerie, is an asshole much of the time but learns a lot.

I read Borderlands long before discovering Charles de Lint, and I loved Will Shetterly's writing as much as I've loved de Lint's since. Borderlands and Newford have a very similar basis in community, found family, and people with good hearts. This book doesn't seem dated; there is a lot of inclusion, and the lack of cell phones and computers would be explained by the unreliability of electronics in the Borderlands.
