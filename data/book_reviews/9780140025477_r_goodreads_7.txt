Published in 1937, Summer Moonshine is one of P.G. Wodehouse's classic 1930's country house farces. Moonshine in this context meaning nonsense, foolishness, or absurd delusion, all of which are present at Walsingford Hall. Walsingford Hall being an ancestral seat that has been converted into a glorified boarding house for paying guests by its cash strapped owner Sir Buckstone Abbott

The novel contrasts mercenary or superficial courtships with real emotional chemistry. Adrian Peake woos women for financial advantage, Tubby falls for idealised romantic illusions, whilst Joe and Jane develop a rapport through mutual wit, honesty, and a shared amusement at the absurdities around them

The plot is the usual contrived and faintly ludicrous flim flam which matters not a jot. Of course it all resolves itself most agreeably however crucially the intervening sections include the usual sparkling writing, comedic mishaps, high literary allusion, Edwardian slang, and vivid metaphor. It's a treat from start to finish

I listened via Audible, and Jonathan Cecil's narration is superb. As always. He remains my favourite Wodehouse narrator.

5/5
