This is the first story concerning Cerryl and our first look into how the White Order operates as the flip side of the coin from the Black mages, who are very similar in their moral makeup (black being better as we see over time in this series in my opinion). You are born with what you have and then you make the best of it. Obviously, the story of Cerryl and Jeslek cannot possibly be over (not with that kind of tension within the guild). This takes place roughly 300 years after Creslin and therefore I believe in the middle of the timeline created by the prior novels in this series. You know what is strange about these books that I am starting to notice? All of this attention to the background details of these stories and the people encountered as they travel, even with the large descriptions presented, and they are still just passing images to the overall story. I am hoping that the next book is the sequel to this one, but I recall that you can never tell where and why this series moves in terms of time. Modesitt will not paint a linear picture from A to B , and his movement from book to book certainly brings this fact out. Onwards!!!!
