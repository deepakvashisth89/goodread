"The law... should be accessible to everyone and at all times."The title story is very short and has been published on its own, as a chapter in his novelThe Trial(see my reviewHERE) and in the collectionThe Country Doctor(see my reviewHERE).It's an allegorical tale on two of Kafka's key themes: judgement and bureaucratic process. (He studied law at university, and went on to work in insurance, investigating personal injury claims.)A man comes seeking justice. The reason is not stated - this is Kafka, after all! Nor is it clear if he's seeking it from the highest human legal authority or from God. Perhaps he doesn't know himself, as he's stuck outside with "the most lowly gatekeeper" of many.The door to justice is open, but the doorkeeper won't let him pass. There is never an outright "no", nor any reason given, just prevarication and the implication (and it is only an implication) that one day it might be possible.The man waits, and waits. For years. The doorkeeper takes bribes:"So you won't feel there isn't anything you haven't tried."You can probably guess the outcome more-or-less.Image:Waiting at the door...(Source)Some of Kafka's stories have humour; this is not really one of them.
