As a Yank, it took a period of adjustment to become comfortable with O'Brien's phrasing. Some was easy to figure out ("he...puts his hand on mine and steers me to his shorthorn") but some took a bit of research (I was temporarily thrown by words like "dapping" and "toff" but actually enjoyed confirming their meanings). Once beyond this brief acclimation, the stories revealed their full power. The struggle between religious tradition and immediate human emotions such as desire permeate the tales. The writing can be dazzling, but also blunt in its beauty and profundity. One such gem: "perhaps pity is a luxury and deliverance a thing of the past." Once idiom is captured and set aside, the sense of kinship with O'Brien's characters can be fully appreciated. As the narrator of "Epitaph" explains, "You see, everyone is holding on. Just." Yes, yes we are.
