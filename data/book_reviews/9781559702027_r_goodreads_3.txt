Auschwitz: A Doctor's Eyewitness Account by Miklos Nyiszli

5/5 stars

I have no idea how to start this review. It made such an impact on me. I normally don't read books like this. But this one was free on Audible and for some reason it drew me in and the second I started listening to it I couldn't stop.

The book starts with a foreword where the question is being raised of why didn't the Jews who walked off the train when they arrived in Auschwitz didn't protest or overrun the Nazi's? The question kinda shocked me and for a second I was like where is this book going?

Another question that gets raised is whether it is up to us readers to decide if Miklos was a good person or if he did participate in things to save his own skin. So my brain instantly started turning and it didn't stop turning throughout the entire book. 

The book starts when Nyiszli arrives in Auschwitz in 1944. He is a doctor and Dr. Mengele is impressed by his medical knowledge and accurate assessments. His main job is working in the crematoriums and performing autopsies on the Jews Mengele experiments on.

Nyiszli's tone is very matter of fact throughout the entire book. It's a very factual retelling of what he did during certain autopsies and a very factual retelling of what happens around him. And that gave the book such an eerie, fearful and scary feel. I think it was sort of a survival mechanism. He could relive, go through these memories purely by distancing himself from the actual emotions. 

This book showed me how the Nazi's very effectively ruled Auschwitz. It was a well oiled machine where millions of people lost their lives.

The second the Jews stepped off the train you could feel their spirits were already crushed. It's harrowing how cruelly they are treated and separated. It's disgusting how greedy the Nazi's were for their belongings and jewelry etc. I got sick with how they treat them as things and not as humans.

The fact that Nyiszli could see from the smoke what was being burned broke my heart and soul. How he factually describes how the Jews get killed. How vicious Mengele was and how deranged he and his co-workers were. Believing they were scientists, believing that their skeletons were more important than their lives.

But one of the most insane things was... feeling that Mengele also had a human side. It was so confusing and I felt some sort of anger towards myself for even feeling it at all. Mengele cares for the people around him and even helps Nyiszli see his family who are imprisoned in another section in Auschwitz.

Seeing how the Nazi's revelled in their power was so hard to read. How they collect things for the Reich but how they also fill their own pockets. Eyeing shoes of the people who just arrived and stealing him the second they were gassed.

And seeing how they increase the crematoriums functionally to cremate even more people than before was so painful to read.

The fact that entire bunks were killed due to diseases and how easily they killed off people all in order to stack the new arrivals was just insane. They didn't just kill the sick or weak ones. It was insane and awful to read how practical the Nazi's were regarding space and the effectiveness of the camp. 

The ending was so disgusting as well. Seeing how they are burning evidence, destroying buildings and trying to hide what truly happened at Auschwitz.

My heart broke for every single one that got hurt. I felt hatred towards the Nazi's and my mind kept reeling at the things Nyiszli was forced to do. And it made me wonder. How far would I be willing to go to save my own life? And the lives of the ones I love.

We also see the kindness in Nyiszli. He truly tried to help the people he treated. He treated the bodies with compassion. And he left medicines for people so they had a change to heal from whatever they were suffering from.

I just cannot imagine what he went through. But mostly. I was horrified by what the Jews went through. I want to say I can imagine what it felt like. But the truth is... I can't. I can't actually feel what all those innocent souls went through. The fear, the power struggle, trying to live, wanting to die, seeing yourself waste away, your loved ones waste away, seeing the cruelty of the nazi's, feeling the fear of when are they gonna hurt me?, can I truly know what that feels like? No. I could never, ever feel that. 

So I am grateful books like this exist. They tell us the absolute factual truth of what happened. And it reminded me we should never, ever forget what happened. It shows how cruel humanity can be. How selfish humans are if they think they are worth more than other humans. This should never be forgotten. And all of us should strive to never let this happen again. Unfortunately atrocities still happen all around the world. People still use each other. People still die innocently and get shot down for having the wrong race or nationality. It's insane how cruel humanity can be. I just hope we will get to a point that we truly learn from what happened and fight for every single one of us. But that might be a pipe dream. Still, I personally am going to strive for that. I will treat everyone with love and respect. No matter where you come from or what the color of your skin is or what the nationality on your passport says.

My heart is still in pieces. The holocaust.. I.. I am at a loss for words. 

A harrowing book. But I think it is such an important read. But I will never, ever forget this one. My heart is with every single victim. ♥ And I won't ever forget them.
