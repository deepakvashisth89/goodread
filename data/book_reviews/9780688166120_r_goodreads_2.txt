I wasn't really taken with this book. Not because of the author--I love Wood's "The New Whole Foods Encyclopedia" and refer to it constantly. Not because of the topic--I love grains and eat various whole grains almost daily. And not because of the format--I often 'read' and enjoy cookbooks that are merely recipe after recipe, and this book isn't quite like that either, with introductions to each grain and brief commentaries on buying and storing.It just didn't have the "it" factor for me. Most of the recipes were too basic (3 recipes for steamed rice?), too heavy for my taste, or just sounded...dull. Much of this is almost certainly because of the book's age, published 15 years ago, and changing trends in food (even the few photos feel heavier, darker, and more cluttered than contemporary food photography).So I think I didn't like it because of me. I don't eat like this, and I know how to steam rice, and I have latched onto many of the current trends in food culture for light, fresh fusion foods. When the book came out it was understandably lauded; many of these grains were anything but mainstream in 1997. And I think it can still serve the same purpose--an educational tool for those who want to broaden their horizons past Quaker oatmeal and Uncle Ben's rice, and need some guidance. But for people already cooking with whole grains regularly, better to turn to contemporary food writers who are still innovating.
