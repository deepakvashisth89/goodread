T.H. White's writing style is to provide extremely detailed descriptions of the characters, environments, and action in his stories. This can be seen in The Once and Future King, and it is present as well in the Elephant and the Kangaroo. In this book, the main character, Mr. White, is instructed by the Archangel Michael to build an ark. White does go into lengthy, detailed descriptions of how Mr. White actually builds the ark. I do question the necessity of these parts of the book. But in a way, I enjoyed them because they gave me insights into White himself. I think providing those details and thinking through how a poor, modern farmer would build a giant ark on short notice is something White actually enjoyed thinking about and figuring out himself.Aside from the detailed descriptions of ark building, this book had some great thought provoking ideas in it. Mr. White had to consider what he would take with him on the ark and if he would leave anything out (like guns or alcohol) in starting the world over again. Also, the contrast between Mr. White and the O'Callaghans provides challenging thoughts about what it means to be an atheist and what it means to be a person of faith. At certain moments in the book Mr. White, the atheist, shows greater faith than Mrs. O'Callaghan, the devout Catholic.If you enjoyed reading The Once and Future King and you are looking for more writing by T.H. White, this book is worth your time. Even with the excessive detail he provides on ark building, the book still contains great humor, excellent character development, an interesting plot, and thought provoking ideas on faith and morality.
