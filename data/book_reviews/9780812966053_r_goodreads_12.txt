Mosses from an old Manse by Nathaniel Hawthorne is indeed a great read.
Published in 1846, Hawthorne poured his thoughts into the form of short stories and sketches, which are beautifully narrated and woven together. If one reads his work closely enough, one can find gems hidden in each of his stories.
He travels , 'through the gate of dreams', through 'The Hall of Fantasy..by the universal passport of a dream.' He encounters 'the old apple dealer, whom...unconsciously to myself and unsuspected by him, I have studied.... has become a naturalized citizen of my inner world.'
In Buds and Bird Voices, he writes:'These blessed birds. Their music...They are discussing the economy of life and love and the site and architecture of their summer residences, and have no time to sit in a twig and pour forth solemn hymns, or overtures' and he calls them 'our feathered citizens' and adds 'Politics, certainly must be the occasion of such tumultuous debates, but still, unlike all other politicians, they instil melody into their individual utterances and produce harmony as a general effect.'
It was a pleasant read, and the detailed endnotes were very useful too.
