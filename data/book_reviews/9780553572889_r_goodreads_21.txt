This novel has me torn. I'm trying not to be biased against it just because I don't like the direction Claremont took the storyline from the point where the film Willow ended. I'm trying to judge the book on its own merits. However, I find the author, in his attempts to show just how expansive and grandiose this world he extrapolated is, just ends up making it harder for the reader to understand exactly what's going on.Most of the story makes sense on a basic narrative sense. There are very few parts - although there are a few - where the author gets so enamored with his descriptions of magic or non-corporeal interactions between characters/forces that the reader loses track of the narrative. Unfortunately, these lead to the overall theme of the story. I'm still not exactly sure what Elora Danan was fighting against, fighting for, or trying do, just that it was very important.Thus my dilemma - am I being too tough on this novel just because I don't like how it launched off from Willow? I've read countless fantasy novels where the magic got muddied sometimes, or I was confused exactly why spell X or ritual Y fixed problem Z, but gave it a pass as long as the adventure was fun and the characters interesting. So why am I giving this novel an extra-hard time about that? Does it deserve it? Am I being biased?I don't know. I do know I do not like this as an expansion of the Willow movie, and I urge people who have that nostalgic love for the 80s film NOT to read this series so as not to ruin it. It's a dark story, and honestly has nothing to do with the Willow film or its characters or feeling.
