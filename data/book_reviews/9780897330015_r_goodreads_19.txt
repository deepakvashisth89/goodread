3,5 // Barbie (2023) if it was about the NEP.

Did you know Alexandra Kollontai wrote fiction? The result is a didactic read at times. What strikes me more, though, is that it's never as didactic as you might expect from a Bolshevik functionary. In that respect the main novel is better than the two short stories that precede it. Wouldn't say it's truly a great work – some parts were clunky, the general direction of the plot was predictable. But it's a good read regardless, even setting aside its value as an amazing time capsule.

I particularly like the psychological dilemmas Kollontai paints. Her exploration of how you can get estranged from someone on a personal and political level still resonates a century down the road. It's also a novel full of empathy, even directed towards people who were very much "the enemy" in those days. Ironically the book is a true product of the NEP era, as much as there is a red thread of anti-NEP frustration woven into it: I doubt something like this would have gotten past the censors post-1928.
