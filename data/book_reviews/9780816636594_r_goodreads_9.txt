Leslie Heywood is a professional body builder, and recovering from an exercise compulsion. All throughout high school (pre title IX), she was obsessed with being someone. Being the absolute best was her priority, and she wanted to train with the guys. She had the trifecta-food restrictions, over exercise, and the need to be the best. She was so obsessed with being the best, that her body couldn't take it any more, and it started to work against her. For her, the struggle has always been to prove herself, to take up space and to make herself visible. Towards the end of the book, she takes a reflective stance, and talks about her change in outlook. Teamwork, cheering on your teammates and realizing that winning isn't everything are some major lessons she learns later in life.
At some points I found this book to be a bit self-indulgent, but I think it was nothing but honest. She focuses on her high school athletic career, at the first outset on title IX. I realize that the times are different now, with female athletes as prominent as males. But I would argue that the issues facing Heywood in the 1970's are not that different from the issues facing female athletes today. We are so over loaded with the need to be the best, the focus has shifted away from the important stuff-how to work as a team, and enjoy an experience with others.
