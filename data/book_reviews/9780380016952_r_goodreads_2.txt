I had to sit on this a bit before I could write a review because my feelings around the story are complicated. Let me start by saying I loved this book. There was just something about it that hooked me and kept me hooked the whole way through.

That being said, so much of the feminism, gender roles, and sexuality found in this story feels -and is- outdated. I found this to be especially true in terms of how the story navigates gender binaries, the idea of lesbianism, and expectations of the masculine and the feminine. However, there also exists ideologies in the novel that are still so painfully relevant that it almost becomes disturbing.

I think what ultimately saves this book from its own pitfalls is just how well written it is, and how masterfully Gould has managed to utilize her time. The pacing is astounding, and I especially loved how the author intersected all the timelines and perspectives. It kept the reading fresh and interesting. I also adored how messy and complex the characters were, and how much their dynamics shifted given the short time in which we had as readers to enjoy them. They were bizarre, and frightening, and deplorable, and so human that it was impossible not to relate to them in some way, shape, or form.
