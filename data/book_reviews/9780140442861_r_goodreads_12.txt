Plutarch isn't exploring history, but writing and parable, through the medium of historical figures and lessons that can be learned.Characters almost represent moral ideas - reflected as their actions - that drove these larger than life figures . Sometimes he raises questions regarding complicated decisions rather than declarative statements on moral fact - othertimes, omens and gods intervene to teach lessons.History as a means of allegory or teaching lessons . The constant presence of omens and the karmic cost of ignoring them or disdaining the gods. An omen to sacrifice a redheaded virgin , which he (as a moral man) does not want to do, and a red horse arriving at the exact right time. Callippus spurning the gods and oaths, murdering Dion, and then being killed later by the same dagger.Stories often strike down or push back on aspiring dictators - while also lamenting democracy and the stupidity of decisions by the masses.THE CONTENTArtaxerxes - conflict with Greece but largely to moralistic about the corruption and manipulation of the Persian court , betrayals of mothers , wives, sons and brothers .Pelopidas - exemplifies the values of trust and great friendship. First to defeat the Spartans , then “throws away his life” in battle which is not becoming of a general. Like most myth is personal where the antagonists liberate their city with 10 friends.Dion - Plutarch is fundamentally not a supporter of democracy , and therefore minimizes the asks of the common people like land redistribution. Maybe an idealized version of Plato’s philosopher tyranny is presented here , but Dion ultimately inflexible and unable to win the masses similar to Brutus the Roman life.As written , Dion selflessly sacrifices for the ungrateful citizens, where others constantly undermine him despite threat to the city . The stupidity of the masses results in the sacking of their city which only Dion can save them from.TimoleonBack on Sicily attempting to save the city of Syracuse from tyranny. Continued use of omens to show favor or disfavor from the gods .Dionysius eventually concedes defeat and winds up in Corinth - Plutarch seems to greatly enjoy exploring the change of fates from ultimate tyrant to lowly common citizen, which Dionysius takes with good humor.After defeating the tyrants, Good fortune follows Timoleons cause - they beat the barbarian carthaginians back less through his own genius than good omens . Eagles carrying snakes , wreaths of parsley , fortuitous winds secure his victory and democratic rule for Sicily. Truly , one loved of the gods.DemosthenesGreat orator compared with Cicero. Never spoke off the cuff but carefully prepared his speeches , believed that was the ultimate respect of democracy.Loses the ultimate battle against Philip with Athens.PhocionThe orator general , a backdrop for decades of Athenian history .
