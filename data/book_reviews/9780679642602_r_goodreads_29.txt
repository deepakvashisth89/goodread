The 16th emperor of Rome, Marcus Aurelius, wrote the most well-known book on Stoicism: Meditations. I will attempt to emulate stoic clarity and lack of ornamentation, and keep this straightforward and without fripperies.The book is superb. Its lessons are basic and common sensical: be useful, be good, care for others, care for yourself, don't be a drama queen, don't crave praise, avoid self-indulgence, avoid anger, know that everything is cyclical and know that everything must die. Consider your inner self to be your most important retreat but do not avoid the family of humanity. Recognize the demarcation between things we can control (our thoughts and actions) and things we cannot control (everything else, including the opinions of others). Be calm, be tranquil, even when in pain. Understandlogos: the fundamental, rational principle that orders and connects everything in the universe.He repeats various iterations of these lessons throughout 12 rather randomly titled sections. I say "random" because all of the lessons can be found in each section. I did not mind the repetitious nature of the book; lessons must often be repeated to be truly understood.The book was a soothing, sometimes melancholy experience. Best read with a pleasant view in sight, to further inspire contemplation in between chapters.My edition was from Ryan Holiday's small press. The translation is blunt and unpretentious. An excellent gift.
