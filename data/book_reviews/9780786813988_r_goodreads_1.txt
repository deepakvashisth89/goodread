Out of all the episodes from the television show, I never cared for Shelter. The same applies to the novelization of the episode. It is probably because I don't find the Bigfoot legend particularly interesting, to begin with. However, the adaptation to the book was better than the actual episode.Shelter is actually the fourth episode of the show, and it goes by a different title: Sacrifice. The story is essentially Fiona (Fi), her brother Jack, their rock star mom, Molly Phillips, and manager/roadie family The Bells decide to go camping in Shiloh National Park. Legend has it that the ghosts of the Battle at Shiloh are haunting them there forests, and Fi wants to run into those old fogey spirits. However, while collecting wood for a fire with her and his friend Clu(eless), Fi gets lost in the woods and runs into Bigfoot!What made this book really interesting was that the episode that aired did not talk about a possible connection between the dead soldier and Fi. It is mentioned in the book he had dreams and visions of her, and she had a dream of him. The episode didn't really explore that, which is a major disappointment. That would have been quite the episode, but of course, you only get about actual 20-22 minutes of actual story in the episode. And of course, this mysterious plot line could simply have been Paul Mantell's own creativity when he was writing the adaptation. Furthermore, the book provides more depth as to the relationship between Fiona and her brother. Essentially, he is Scully to Fiona's Mulder. And, of course, what's a tween show without some down-to-earth sibling rivalry?Overall, as I had mentioned, this was probably one of my least-favorite episodes, due to my disinterest in Big Fuzzy Foot (as Fi mentions, his middle name is Fuzzy), as it is also slightly reflected in the book adaptation. While the book was (of course) better, I know why they were written, and that was to encourage their young viewers who love the show to enjoy reading, in the hopes that those young'uns become lifelong readers.Dear Disney, it worked!
