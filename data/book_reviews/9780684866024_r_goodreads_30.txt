Much has not dated well. A shocking story of teaching.Having been there myself, briefly, seeing the 'trade' school system of the 50s, the students populating it, the teachers attempting to keep control... it felt very disconcerting.The story of a young teacher's first job, from interview to the classroom. Stopping a crime, failing to discipline his classes, trying to get through to them and actually do his job. Fascinating stuff, very tense.The narrative style gives us hints of what is to come. The book itself more than hints at the social morals and expectations of the time - some awfully graphic representations of racist and sexist language and behaviours (I won't elaborate too much). The low aspirations of these students. A truly disgusting male teacher obsessed with getting a job at a girl's school. Physical assaults. The depictions of the only two female characters, so markedly different but both quite stereotyped and derogatory.Dadier remains sympathetic throughout, I felt. He really earnestly wants to teach. He begins more idealistically than readers know is going to be good for him.I can only imagine that this book caused waves at the time it was published. I may have seen the film decades ago but can't remember. The book was shocking though, today more than ever for the changes that have happened in terms of our expectations of language and expectations.Hard to read at times, due to this. And a shocking, distressing tale in itself.Possibly won't win any converts to the teaching profession!
