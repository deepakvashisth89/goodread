I’ll read anything by F. Scott Fitzgerald. He’s the writer whose prose has moved me more than anyone else’s. There’s just no flatness with him. His characters always feel deeply.Pain, something unavoidable in our lives, is something Fitzgerald clearly understands, and I really like the way he writes about it. Not in an overdone way, but in rich, almost bejewelled sentences that make even sadness feel beautiful.Of course, Babylon Revisited gets a lot of attention, and rightfully so. It’s one of his best short stories. But in this Alma Classics edition, there were several other stories I really loved: Six of One, What a Handsome Pair, More Than Just a House, and One Intern, which was my favourite.There’s this one scene I really liked.“He met her by accident or contrivance half a dozen times in the next fortnight, before he judged he could ask her for a date.‘Why, I suppose so.’ She seemed a little surprised. ‘Let’s see. How about next week either Tuesday or Wednesday?’‘How about tonight?’‘Oh, not possibly.’When he called Tuesday at the little apartment she shared with a woman musician from the Peabody Institute, he said:‘What would you like to do? See a picture?’‘No,’ she answered emphatically. ‘If I knew you better I’d say let’s drive about a thousand miles into the country and go swimming in some quarry.’”It’s a really small exchange, but it feels loaded. He starts off careful, then suddenly wants to see her the same day, like he can’t quite wait for the “proper” timing. And she doesn’t just go along with it either, she kind of resists the usual date format and throws out something way bigger instead.With Fitzgerald you just feel the weight of things, even in the smallest moments.
