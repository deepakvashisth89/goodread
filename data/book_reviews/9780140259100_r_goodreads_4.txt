So, there's this guy who from childhood who is terrified of the extreme likelihood of nuclear war. Everyone thinks he nuts. He has an obsessive personality and frankly he is nuts. Over the years, which include many fascinating events, such as going underground to escape the draft, he slips in and out of psychosis. As a child he built a fallout "shelter" under the pingpong table in his parents' basement to the dismay of his parents. Later on he tries to build one in his backyard, to the dismay of his wife and kid. Really, what he's doing is digging a hole for his fears, which can't be escaped.But what about us, as we go merrily about our lives, as if the bombs aren't coming. "No one knows," he keeps telling us. But of course we do. Doesn't this level of denial approach psychosis? Aren't we all living in a very deep hole?(BTW, he has several intense sessions w a Milton Ericksonian therapist that are to die for. Erickson had it going on.)This book is one of many ways O'Brien explores the impact of Viet Nam and different responses to it.
