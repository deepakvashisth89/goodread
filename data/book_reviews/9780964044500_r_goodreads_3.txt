This book provides a lively and opinionated look at the commune of Twin Oaks in Louisa, VA, from the perspective of a woman who was one of the founders, left for awhile, then returned and has lived there for many years. It's particularly interesting because in a lot of ways the author is not a "typical" commune dweller. She makes very clear that these are her own personal opinions as an insider, and doesn't try to create an objective history, which I think is good. She has a lot of really good commonsense thoughts for anyone who's interested in living in or creating an intentional community, though I also think that it might be of interest to others.Unfortunately, I didn't get to finish the last couple of chapters, because I had to return it to the people I borrowed it from.
