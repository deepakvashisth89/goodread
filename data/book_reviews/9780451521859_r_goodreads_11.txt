This work was my first Jane Austen novel, and I enjoyed it. However, afterward, the story didn't thrill me as much as Jane Eyre, another Victorian tale that I had devoured and that deeply moved me.
There, I moaned and sympathized with the Dashwood sisters' disappointments in this basket of heartless and unscrupulous vipers, more interested in the size of their dowries than in their undeniable human qualities.
But it is pretty long and monotonous: the trips between London and the various residences, the inter-family gossip, the marital maneuvers, the sweet lines, and the sneaking shots. In short, this atmosphere is weighty, but the style is remarkable since I wanted to know how it would end for Elinor and Marianne at all costs. And finally, I am a little disappointed with this ending, which looks like dramatic bells have fallen.
It is nevertheless a beautiful read because of the characters' psychological depth and what it teaches about the customs of the time, as well as the disregard for women and their future.
