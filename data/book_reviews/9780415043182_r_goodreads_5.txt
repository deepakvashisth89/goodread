ENGLISHYear 2026 started with exciting news in the Brazilian health sector. A new hope for the treatment of spinal cord injuries gained widespread attention in the national media.The substance — polylaminin, a derivative of laminin present in the body tissues — was developed by Brazilian scientists after almost three decades of research promising to revolutionize the treatment of paraplegia.Initial studies allegedly showed an impressive rate of recovery especially among those injured within 72 hours from the accident. A Brazilian lab filed a request for government authorization to start trials. Authorization was granted in January 2026 for the initiation of Phase I trials.The fact awakened the interest of the press and then — boom! — Brazil had a promising breakthrough treatment for a serious health condition, giving hope to millions of people in the entire world.During this period, the news made the front page of several media outlets. “Patients recovered from spinal injury after administration of polylaminin,” “cure is underway” were frequent headlines in the first couple of months of 2026.The lead researcher became a celebrity. A statement she made in an interview, saying that the substance could be nicknamed the “God molecule” because of its cross shape, reinforced the hype.In this scenario, it wouldn’t take long for patients and their families to seek and get court injunctions to receive the treatment even if it was still far from any conclusion on its efficacy.Some scientists, however, began to question the initially announced results. They were called envious and accused of rooting against Brazilian science. In a few more weeks, the criticism began to gain traction. The chorus of critics grew louder given that the stage of studies was still too early to announce any cure. Moreover, the study supporting the claims of the substance's extraordinary performance was unavailable.The lead researcher responded to critiques by saying that the initial study was in preprint, but the results were encouraging. The researcher revealed that it had been rejected by specialized publications, but the reviewers were wrong. Critics also pointed out that the sample of patients who had been administered the drug was too small and the success rate was far from representative. In a TV interview, the researcher said that it wouldn't be appropriate to conduct a clinical trial with a control group in situations like this – and told the critics to go study. The researcher's reasoning was: if the substance is effective, the study will deprive the patients in the control group of receiving treatment that could save them from paraplegia (the researcher believes that polylaminin is more effective when administered as close as possible to the accident).As trials are expected to commence, to date (March 2026) at least four main problems have been pointed out by critics of the initial study: (i) very small sample size; (ii) spinal cord injuries already have a certain rate of spontaneous recovery; (iii) “post hoc ergo propter hoc”: just because recoveries occurred after the administration of polylaminin doesn't mean it was due to the substance's action; (iv) clinical trials with a control group can (and should) be carried out: in this case, the control group should not receive a placebo, but the standard treatment for spinal cord injuries.In the face of such hype, it is always important to remember Karl Popper’s theory on the importance of criticism and refutation in science, which he exposes in his “Conjectures and Refutations”. In essence, the resistance against the use of a control group to test the efficacy of a medicine in this case (and in most cases) would not allow one to distinguish the medicine’s effect from spontaneous recovery; in other words, that wouldn’t be possible to attest the medicine’s efficacy.“Conjectures and Refutations” puts together many lectures the author gave from 1940’s to early 1960’s. It is a great walkthrough of Popper’s thought from his enquiries into ancient Greek philosophy on the quest for truth, through the demarcation system of science based on falsefiability (his main theme), to fierce criticism of Marxism and the defense of liberalism, another issue dear to him. The result is a great book for those interested in Popper’s philosophy, since it brings his entire system of ideas in clear terms. Unlike most of his “Logic of Scientific Discovery” (my review here:https://www.goodreads.com/review/show...), “Conjectures and Refutations” brings Popper’s ideas in an all easy and even slightly repetitive way, which helps the reader to fixate the contents. Once the chapters are lectures he gave at different moments and to different audiences, the reader can choose which ones to read per issue covered. In my case, I read about 2/3 of the book. I concentrated my read of the chapters on methodology of science, i.e., the first ones on ancient philosophy, those on demarcation of science, and one of particular interest on the method of social sciences.Popper explains the idea of “conjectures and refutations” by rejecting the view that science is produced through induction. Science begins and ends with problems. Humans do not simply infer laws from repeated observations; instead, they actively search for patterns and form hypotheses. Even Newton’s laws did not truly arise from induction as was stated by Newton himself. Many conclusions come from abstract reasoning rather than observation, and some even contradict immediate experimental impressions. Observation alone cannot generate laws or theories; it must pass through the intellect, which formulates theories. These theories are then tested through experiments — this is the essence of deduction. Observation’s main role is therefore to attempt to refute theories.Popper also addresses the problem of truth in science. A theory can be considered superior to another when:(i) It makes more precise assertions than another and these more precise assertions resist more rigorous tests. (ii) It takes into consideration and explains a greater number of facts than another, and with more precision. (iii) It describes and explains facts with more detail than the other. (iv) It passes tests that the other theory didn’t. (v) It suggests new experimental tests not previously considered by the other theory and successfully passes them. (vi) It unifies or connects several problems that were previously unrelated.Such superiority between theories characterizes the advancement of science.Popper also accepts the idea of approximation to truth through the notion of truth likeness. A theory may remain useful even after being refuted if it stays closer to the truth in relevant respects. This often occurs in the social sciences, where theories function as approximations despite their limitations. A common example is the rational agent assumption in neoclassical economics: although humans are not fully rational in economic decisions, the model has long been used as a simplifying premise. More recently, behavioral economics has challenged this assumption.Popper also proposes three requirements for the development of knowledge: (a) a new theory should arise from a powerful and unifying theoretical framework; (b) it must be independently testable; and (c) it should survive rigorous tests, though this last condition is less indispensable than the others.In a chapter dedicated to the social sciences (“Prediction and Prophecy in the Social Sciences”) — something relatively unusual in his writings on scientific methodology — Popper criticizes what he calls historicism. Often advocated by Marxists, historicism underpins their claim to be able to predict revolutions if certain historical conditions are present. Yet for Popper, predicting solar eclipses is very different from predicting revolutions.Thus, he criticizes two ideas he calls “vulgar.” The first is the belief that the social sciences study collective entities as such. He labels this “naive collectivism” and argues that social phenomena should be analyzed through individuals, their actions, and their interactions, rather than through competing classes, for example. The second is the belief that society is governed primarily by conspiracies. Conspiracies — whether attributed to secret elites (such as the “Wise Elders of Zion”), monopolists, capitalists, or imperialists — are, in his view, rarer and less effective than commonly believed. Even when they occur, their results often differ from their intentions. The central task of theoretical social science, therefore, is to study the unintended consequences of intentional human actions.Popper's theory can be criticized. His emphasis on scientific progress through criticism, combined with his explicit liberal commitments, still encounters resistance from many scientists and left-leaning intellectuals. Ela revelou que o estudo havia sido rejeitado por publicações especializadas, mas os revisores estavam errados. Os críticos também apontaram que a amostra de pacientes que receberam o medicamento era muito pequena e que a taxa de sucesso estava longe de ser representativa. Em essência, a resistência ao uso de um grupo de controle para testar a eficácia de um medicamento, neste caso (e na maioria dos casos), não permitiria distinguir o efeito do medicamento, da recuperação espontânea; em outras palavras, não seria possível atestar a eficácia do medicamento."Conjecturas e Refutações" reúne diversas palestras proferidas pelo autor entre as décadas de 1940 e 1960. Trata-se de um excelente panorama do pensamento de Popper, desde sua análise das raízes da filosofia grega na busca pela verdade, passando pelo sistema de demarcação da ciência baseado na falseabilidade (seu tema principal), até a crítica contundente ao marxismo e a defesa do liberalismo, outro tema caro a ele. O resultado é um livro excelente para os interessados ​​na filosofia de Popper, pois apresenta todo o seu sistema de ideias de forma clara e objetiva. Diferentemente da maior parte de sua obra "A Lógica da Descoberta Científica" (minha resenha aqui:https://www.goodreads.com/review/show...), "Conjecturas e Refutações" apresenta as ideias de Popper de forma simples e até mesmo um pouco repetitiva, o que facilita a fixação do conteúdo pelo leitor. Como os capítulos são palestras que ele proferiu em diferentes momentos e para diferentes públicos, o leitor pode escolher quais ler de acordo com o tema abordado. No meu caso, li cerca de 2/3 do livro. Concentrei-me na leitura dos capítulos sobre metodologia da ciência, ou seja, os primeiros sobre filosofia antiga, os sobre a delimitação da ciência e um de particular interesse, sobre o método das ciências sociais.Popper explica a ideia de “conjecturas e refutações” rejeitando a visão de que a ciência é produzida por indução. A ciência começa e termina com problemas. Os seres humanos não inferem leis simplesmente a partir de observações repetidas; em vez disso, buscam ativamente padrões e formulam hipóteses. Mesmo as leis de Newton não surgiram verdadeiramente da indução, como afirmou o próprio Newton. Muitas conclusões provêm do raciocínio abstrato, e não da observação, e algumas até contradizem impressões experimentais imediatas. A observação por si só não pode gerar leis ou teorias; ela precisa passar pelo intelecto, que formula teorias. Essas teorias são então testadas por meio de experimentos — essa é a essência da dedução. O principal papel da observação é, portanto, tentar refutar teorias.Popper também aborda o problema da verdade na ciência. Uma teoria pode ser considerada superior a outra quando:(i) Ela faz afirmações mais precisas do que outra e essas afirmações mais precisas resistem a testes mais rigorosos.(ii) Ela leva em consideração e explica um número maior de fatos do que outra, e com mais precisão.(iii) Ela descreve e explica os fatos com mais detalhes do que a outra.(iv) Ela passa em testes que a outra teoria não passou.(v) Ela sugere novos testes experimentais não considerados anteriormente pela outra teoria e os supera com sucesso.(vi) Ela unifica ou conecta diversos problemas que antes não estavam relacionados.Essa superioridade entre teorias caracteriza o avanço da ciência.Popper também aceita a ideia de aproximação à verdade por meio da noção de verossimilhança. Uma teoria pode permanecer útil mesmo após ser refutada se permanecer mais próxima da verdade em aspectos relevantes. Isso ocorre frequentemente nas ciências sociais, onde as teorias funcionam como aproximações apesar de suas limitações. Um exemplo ordinário é a hipótese do agente racional na economia neoclássica: embora os seres humanos não sejam totalmente racionais em decisões econômicas, o modelo tem sido usado por muito tempo como uma premissa simplificadora. Mais recentemente, a Economia Comportamental vem desafiando essa hipótese.Popper também propõe três requisitos para o desenvolvimento do conhecimento: (a) uma nova teoria deve surgir de uma estrutura teórica poderosa e unificadora; (b) ela deve ser testável independentemente; e (c) deve resistir a testes rigorosos — embora esta última condição seja menos indispensável do que as outras.Em um capítulo dedicado às ciências sociais (“Previsão e Profecia nas Ciências Sociais”) — algo relativamente incomum em seus escritos de metodologia científica — Popper crítica o que chama de historicismo. Defendido frequentemente por marxistas, o historicismo os embasa na afirmação de suas capacidades de prever revoluções se determinadas condições históricas estiverem presentes. Mas para Popper, prever eclipses solares é muito diferente de prever revoluções.Assim, ele critica duas ideias que chama de “vulgares”. A primeira é a crença de que as ciências sociais estudam entidades coletivas como tais. Ele rotula isso de “coletivismo ingênuo” e argumenta que os fenômenos sociais devem ser analisados ​​por meio dos indivíduos, suas ações e suas interações, em vez de por classes em disputa, por exemplo. A segunda é a crença de que a sociedade é governada principalmente por conspirações. Conspirações — sejam elas atribuídas a elites secretas (como os “Sábios de Sião”), monopolistas, capitalistas ou imperialistas — são, em sua visão, mais raras e menos eficazes do que se acredita comumente. Mesmo quando ocorrem, seus resultados muitas vezes diferem de suas intenções. A tarefa central da ciência social teórica, portanto, é estudar as consequências não intencionais das ações humanas intencionais.Pode-se criticar a teoria de Popper. Sua ênfase no progresso científico por meio da crítica, combinada com seus compromissos liberais explícitos, ainda encontra resistência por parte de muitos cientistas e intelectuais de esquerda. No entanto, sua contribuição para a metodologia científica permanece altamente relevante e deve continuar a informar a reflexão crítica sobre a falta generalizada de letramento científico — especialmente quando supostos medicamentos ou tratamentos milagrosos frequentemente capturam a atenção do público.
