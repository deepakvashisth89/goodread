Outstanding historical fiction chronicling roughly one year (the last year) of the 'Iron King', e.g., Philip the Fair (IV), who reigned in France from 1268 to 1314. So readable it hurts! Druon tosses in her intrigue, love affairs, power dynamics and a real taste of life in the early 14th century; I can see why R.R. Martin deemed this the 'original Game of Thrones' for it is captivating.

First, I loved Druon's prose here as he draws the reader into the story. Druon utilizes many POVs, but the main is King Philip. As represented here, Philip managed to unite France (via marriage and other means), and even drew Flanders into the realm. Philip also promoted a loosening of the feudal laws, granting de facto citizenship for people living in the cities; he even promoted Roman law there. So, in effect, while still citizens of France, these people no longer were bound to the land as serfs or any other feudal obligations except to the king. This transitory period I have always found fascinating and he represents it so well. Of course, the titled elite (other kings, barons, etc.) largely opposed it, but Philip got his way. Philip also brought the pope to France (in Avignon) and brought the Templars to heal.

I have always been intrigued by the Templars; formed as a warrior/monk sect to protect pilgrims in the Holy Lands conquered during the crusades, they expanded their influence to eventually become major power brokers/bankers in all of Europe. At one point, they controlled half of the capital in Europe! Philip himself (or France) owed them a bundle to say the least; they extended loans to the nobility at first to help equip for the Crusades, but then moved on to lending for reasons of state (at high interest rates I might add, violating Canon law). In any case, Philip, with his 'pet' Pope, managed to get them convicted on heresy and after seven bloody, torturous years, the book begins with the 'trial' and final say regarding the four 'big wigs' of the Templars, including the grand master himself.

While this proceeds, Droun takes us to England, where Philip's daughter Isabella reigns as Queen; unfortunately for Isabella, her royal husband prefers boys. That brings in the various sex scandals that seem to embroil half the nobility which Druon almost gleefully portrays. The focus here are the King's three sons, two of which are married to the daughters of a noble house of Burgundy (thus bringing that kingdom into France proper). Both of these daughters engaged in love affairs behind their noble husband's backs. A friend and cousin of Isabella, Robert of Artois, knows about the affairs and wants all involved to face royal judgement (long story, but due to the marriage of the women, he was deprived of his estates).

Finally, Droun turns to power, with the rich Lombards (Italian bankers) playing a major role. So, we have the King and his royal interests of France proper, love interests and scandals, money politics, all taking place in a richly developed 14th century France. Druon provides numerous footnotes (as he states, for those so inclined) and this seems very well researched, but nonetheless, the author must have taken some liberties. Fortunately, these liberties turn this into a page-turning thriller! If you love historical fiction, or even fantasy, you should really dig this. 5 royal stars! I cannot wait for the next installments (assuming I can track them down). Bravo!!
