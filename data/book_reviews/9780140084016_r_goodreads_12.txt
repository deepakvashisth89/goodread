Once, in high school chemistry, we studied smelling salts. I still remember that particular bracing whiff, and was reminded of it during _Household Words_. Joan Silber has a way of nailing her characters' inner thoughts and feelings with such clarity that it causes that same sharp inhale. You sit up and think, "Jesus."I especially love that this is a novel about that dreaded subject of unlikable women. Rhoda is stubborn and hard. She is an uncompromising snob. She is lonely and angry, funny and sad. We feel for her, judge her, condemn her, weep for her. Silber was ahead of her time in focusing on her and making us sit with her. If you are interested in liking your characters, this is not for you—but, then, you're missing out. A devastating and beautiful novel.
