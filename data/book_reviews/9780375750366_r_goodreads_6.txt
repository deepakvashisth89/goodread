I have no difficulty understanding why I haven't read this in the 16-17 years this volume has graced my shelves. It's much harder to understand why I bought it in the first place. I must have bought it on a whim, soon after having read "Sophie's World" by Jostein Gaarder. I also had to study philosophy of sorts ("theory of knowledge") in highschool, so maybe I was inspired. This was no good, despite a fairly promising start.The book is about Bryang Magee's discovery of philosophy and his career in the field. Most of the time it is mind numbingly boring, but occasionally it is informative. The biggest problem I had was with what how arrogant the author is as he describes himself in this book. No one is as intelligent or understands philosophy as well as he does. *eyeroll* I bothered until about page 400, when he came with the remark that teaching didn't give him anything because none of his students could bring any new questions to the scene. Poor Mr. Magee, too clever for this world by far.NOT RECOMMENDED.
