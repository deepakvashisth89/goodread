i recently rescued a dozen or so old books from the recycling bin (!!) in my apartment building’s lobby, and this was amongst them. personality typology was one of my most fervent quarantine-era hyperfixations. i had started with mbti (didn’t we all), fallen down a jungian dichotomy rabbithole, read about enneagrams and tritypes and socionics, and then abruptly lost interest one day (and so it goes :,) let the record show that i truly believe personality theory/typology is pseudoscientific at best and junk psychology at worst. however, i think it holds a little more water than astrology given that it uses evidence based reasoning, and i think it’s ‘real’ in the sense that perception shapes reality. also it’s fun! humans have this quirky quiz-taking, box-sorting, label-making internal drive that i am not immune to i fear.

of the popular typology modalities, the enneagram is the most fluid and the least prescriptive, and in this regard vaguely truthful to human nature. each of the 9 personality archetypes staged in this model are described not as a set of fixed, persistent traits, but as patterns of behaviors that emerge under different conditions. because people are unique but generalizable, this model is easy to identify with.

despite my haterism i can’t deny that it’s an elegant system, and many of the lines of growth and disintegration are uncannily precise. as a scientist, i like that it reads less as “all humans are born with 1 of 16 different brain types” and more as “humans are different but alike and we’ve acted so predictably for millennia that we can cluster 9 major common behavior circuits.” 2 stars bc the author’s research was lazy and his writing style was corny lol.
