Overall: The book over-indexes on childhood issues. I find that often to be the case, when the book is written by a clinical psychologist.That said, the exercises, relationship perspectives, and practical how-to's were really insightful. I ended up ignoring 50% of the book, but the 50% that I didn't ignore was really useful. I got a lot of value out of this book.Here are some of my notes:==> Key Idea - Focus on the OtherYour relationship(s) work best, when both partners focus on meeting the other partner’s needs (rather than their own.) By committing to what’s best for the relationship, each partner paradoxically gets their own needs met.==> Exercise - Focus on the OtherEach partner makes a list of things, big and small, that has their needs met. These can include things like receiving flowers, the partner doing the dishes, reading a book together, etc.Each partner commits to doing 3 to 5 things per day that meets their partner’s needs. DO NOT KEEP SCORE. Focus exclusively on making your partner happy.==> Triggered - IntrospectionWhenever you (or your partner) feel’s triggered, it’s often because your partner reflects a part of you that you’re critical of. For example, if you view you’re easily angered by your partner’s messiness, there’s likely a part of yourself that judges your own messiness.Take a moment to reflect on how your partner’s “flaws” may in fact be reflections of your own flaws.Conversely, this can also happen when your partner exhibits an attribute you wish you had. In other words, it reminds you of something you don’t have.==> Amago ProcessA conflict resolution process.1 - Listen, then reflect back, then confirm that what your partner is trying to say has been heard.2 - Validate what they’re saying. You don’t have to agree with them. Just show that you can see how, from their world and their side, what they’re saying makes sense.3 - Reflect how your partner feels, and check if they feel heard.==> NegativityAll negativity boomerangs. When you yell at a partner, their cortisol level increases, and so does yours. All negativity is self-abuse.Being negative with your partner is always counter productive.Go cold turkey. Each partner commits to no negative statements. If someone says something negative, they must say 3 positive, specifix, unique things to their partner.==> Positive FloodingWrite down all the things you love, cherish, or appreciate about your partner. Flood your partner with those appreciations.Each partner can also write down the things they want to be appreciated for. Trade lists, and consciously flood the other partner with appreciations for those things.Physical traits, character traits, things they’ve done for you, overall feelings of love and appreciation.
