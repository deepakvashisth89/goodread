Very comprehensive. I enjoyed reading this. It starts off with historical information, immigration of Chinese in late 19th century and American responses in the West, and then moves to arrival of Japanese immigrants and how they were compared to Chinese, and eventually earned their own negative stereotype in 20th century.The second book is start of the war, and applications of all war department orders, life in internment camps.The last book is perhaps the most incredible. The authors move through each argument American public offered as to how this occurred, and looks at historical evidence to support or deny. I was a little shocked to learn the whole movement can be tied back to one general, along with a DOJ which saw itself as impotent during war time. This section also looks at the main three supreme court trials connected with internment, and explains how each is detrimental to American freedoms.
