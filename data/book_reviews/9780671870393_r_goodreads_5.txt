a fun summer read about two girls vacationing in greece and making unexpected discoveries about their uncanny connections to a pair of demigodesses.pike's novels always have some problematic elements, as is to be expected from 90s YA horror. this one isn't the worst in that regard, but an unsettling sense of vague misogyny runs through it. josie and helen are best friends, but they kind of hate each other. their relationship is superficial - it revolves wholly around their conflicts over boys. it's an unfortunate representation of female friendship, especially because the narrative and the characters don't seem to realize how toxic it is.josie and helen are noncommunicative as they both pursue the same guy, and josie is remarkably mean and callous about it, despite the face that helen is recovering from a suicide attempt. yikes.this one also reminded me that pike's work is truly geared for an older audience thanfear street. underage binge drinking, ogling at nude beaches, talk of safe sex and AIDS, and teenagers who are openly frisky - they're going wild on this vacation.the greek mythology element is fun, but it's disappointing that pike creates fictional demigodesses to mirror the jealous rift between the MCs, rather than using actually mythological figures.and it's funny that he uses the screenwriter dad to shoehorn in a story about aliens - pike just can't resist including space creatures!toward the endjosie and the male love interest are "poisoned" by being fed powdered glass. i recently learned frommy darling dreadful thingthat this murder method is a myth, and any glass fine enough to be eaten easily will not damage your insides. i love when books cross paths like this.not horrendous, but definitely not a favorite pike novel for me. there are a few memorable moments though:The fish, of course, made me feel kind of horny.
