This book is sadly disappointing!The remarks on Adams life, work, and POV were very interesting, but the choice of photographs was incredibly limited and repetitious. The book speaks of his most famous photograph "Moonrise, Hernandez, New Mexico", but doesn't include a print of it. The same goes for mention of his official portraits of President and Mrs. Jimmy Carter and of Vice President and Mrs. Walter Mondale (the ONLY official portraits that are photographs, not paintings)! Not a print of either in the book!Much is made of the fact that Adams photographed nearly all of the national parks in the US, yet every photograph chosen is from the western US (Grand Teton, Grand Canyon, Rocky Mountain, Glacier National Parks, Canyon De Chelly, Carlsbad Caverns). None of his work east of the Rockies is shown. None of his work from Europe is shown. Also, very few of the photographs were dated - the few portraits in the book would have been made more interesting by listing the era in which they were shot. Were these Navajo people photographed prior to WWII when they still lived a traditional lifestyle? Or were they photographed later when the modern world had begun to change their way of life? Who knows!This subject deserves a better pictorial representation (especially as that was his bailiwick) than this book gives it.
