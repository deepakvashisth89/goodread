This one of Wentworth not-Miss-Silver books and I'm of 2 minds about it. 3.5 stars would be more accurate. The first third seems pointless, the middle is full of interesting characters and the final quarter is a fabulously fascinating page-turner. Always, always, Wentworth supplies a ton of information about how life was lived in England between the Wars, when telephones were an expensive rarity, boarding houses were common, and mail came twice a day, but if the message was urgent, a telegram could be sent. And if a car stalled, the driver just got out and cranked the engine up. Good breeding was taken for granted, clothing could be pawned, servants abounded and 5 pounds was a year's wage for some.I tend to think the encounter of the man on the run with the little old lady abed working the crossword puzzle full of classical allusions was worth the plodding first chapters, but I am a fan of Wentworth's sense of humor, world view and sudden insights into the meaning of life.
