This book is curious in that it is most interesting in its most speculative early parts, where a somewhat abstracted picture of warfare is given. As it gets more historical it might lose some of its interest with readers that are familiar with the time periods. I also got a feel that the overarching argument, that war is endemic and that we should not be complacent about the lack of a nuclear conflagration so far, is a bit of an afterthought. This really felt like a highlight reel of interesting things to say about the waging of war.
