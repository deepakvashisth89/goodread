Küng's A Short History is less a strict history book on the Catholic Church and more a commentary thereon. It is the author that makes this book intriguing, for his account of history is far removed from the biased hagiographic antihistories so often promulgated by what I call the Catholic-Apologetic Complex. You will not find Küng reading, for example, papal supremacy back into early church politics (nor would any expect this to be so given his history), nor does he ignore developments in conciliar theory such as Haec sancta. I think Küng particularly excels at demonstrating the impact of medieval law on the development of the modern "Roman system" and on how the histories of the First and Second Vatican Councils, often conflictingly, interact with it. On the other hand, one wonders if Küng is not sometimes too quick to condemn, if he does not paint too wide of a brushstroke with his depictions of historical ecclesiastical periods which do not agree with him. Overall it is a good read for those interested in the person of Küng as well as modern conciliarism and nouvelle théologie.
