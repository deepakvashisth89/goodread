Update 10 March 2026Finally saw the film. The visceral intensity outweighs anything in Platoon or Apocalypse Now. It was great to see one of the minor characters ‘Too tall to fly.’ In keeping with the book both sides are portrayed in heroic terms. Mel Gibson as Hal Moore comes over well with conscience and love for his men. A remarkable man. Given the Iran situation now it’s a pity that more haven’t watched this. As the futility of War continues.‘No one starts a war-or rather, no one in his senses ought to do so-without first being clear in his mind what he intends to achieve by that war and how he intends to conduct it. (Clausewitz)My understanding of the Vietnam war has come from documentaries, films and books like Karl Marlantes ‘Matterhorn.’ For firefights - ‘13 hours…’ by Mitchell Zukoff - not Vietnam but Benghazi. And of course books like ‘Stalingrad’ by Anthony Beevor. All an amalgam of what is packed into this book.The focus here is on two battles - Ia Drang where for 3 days 450 men of the 1st Battalion 7th Cavalry held off 2000 North Vietnamese soldiers. Later the incursion at Albany a disaster by any standard.General Hal Moore who commanded the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry with Joe Galloway a reporter at the time tell the story. Detailed maps. Starting with the roll of the dead. Finishing with stories from the families that give context to those who died. In between, detailed narratives from individual soldiers and officers. Including comments from the Vietcong generals who were all interviewed for the book.The battle scenes are visceral and disturbing. At the end there is a brief description of different views on the how the war was lost. But I think the focus here is just on the bravery of men from both sides. The Vietcong are not denigrated in the story. In fact only one reference is made to ‘gook.’ It’s just a true story about real bravery. The dust smotes again affected my eyes reading the family member comments. I have not yet seen the Mel Gibson film. Although I understand it has been given the Braveheart type embellishment.Some pullouts:‘I went back to firing my M-79 grenade launcher and got shot in my right elbow…then I got hit in the neck…now I couldn’t talk …I was shot a third time…blew up my right leg and put me down…then two stick grenades blew up right over me and tore up both my legs.’ (Arthur Viera).‘At first light I pinched off a small piece of C-4 plastic explosive…to boil up a canteen cup of coffee. If you lit C-4 very carefully you would be drinking hot coffee in maybe thirty seconds; if you were careless it blew your arm off. (Joe Galloway reporter and co-author).Death is the price you pay for underestimating this tenacious enemy. ( Hal Moore)Captain Ed Freeman Pilot - 4 inches taller than the height limit for pilots. So always known as ‘Too Tall to Fly.’‘It was the final act of a North Vietnamese soldier who was killed. Before he died he took a hand grenade and held it against the stock of his weapon. Then he had gotten on his knees and bent double. If anybody tried to get his weapon they were going to activate that hand grenade. When I saw the dedication …I said to myself: We are up against an enemy who is going to make this a very long year.’ (Dennis Heal)Toby Braveboy from Coward, South Carolina. Amazing bravery. Saved from death by a young Vietcong who pointed his gun at him and then walked on. I looked him up on google only to find he had died in 1971…in a road accident. Later in the book are listed the survivors and what they were doing years later. Braveboys auto accident is mentioned.Well that’s enough from me. Heroism (soldier jumps on a grenade). Bravery of all including medics and helicopter pilots and crew.Hal Moore comes over as a true leader of men. Can’t praise him and the book for revealing the true horror of war enough.
