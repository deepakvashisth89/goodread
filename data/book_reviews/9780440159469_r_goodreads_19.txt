Murder at the Vicarage is my first Agatha Christie. Yes, I know.

Set in a typical English country village, Colonel Protheroe is found dead in the local vicarage. Vicar Leonard Clement is our likable narrator and due to his position of trust in the village, he is present during most key investigation points and meetings as the police bumble their way through trying to solve this crime. We have a list of suspects, some more obvious than others – and luckily one of the village inhabitants, Miss Marple, is around to help solve this case. She is good. Very good indeed.

This is a pleasant enough jaunt through life and gossip in an English rural village. It kept me interested, but this waned in the last third. Not because it was poorly executed, of course Christie was a master craftsperson, this is as polished as you would expect. It’s all about my antipathy towards .

The issue with whodunnits I have is – the culprit is often someone who is a fringe player and makes some sort of key appearance towards the end (engendering a feeling of being hoodwinked), or it ends up being the most obvious person (in which case, it engenders a feeling of being anti-hoodwinked??).

To be sure, this was pleasant enough.
