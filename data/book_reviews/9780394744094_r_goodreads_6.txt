While Richard Russo has long been one of my favorite contemporary novelist, somehow I have missed reading his first novel, Mohawk. Mohawk is a small decaying mill-town in upstate New York that has fallen on hard times. And it seems its citizens have fallen on hard times, too. This is essentially the story of two families, the Grouse family and the Gaffney family and how their lives have intersected over the years, sometimes in dramatic fashion. This is a tightly plotted, compelling portrait of small town life in the United States, taking place in 1970 with Vietnam and the draft in full swing. There is also the realization that the town has been poisoned for years after a long awaited, federally funded study linking the high incidence of cancer and leukemia in Mohawk County to the toxic waste and chemicals that have been dumped into the nearby Mohawk River by the local leather tanneries and mills.

All in all, this is one of the darkest novels that I have read by Richard Russo. And it confirmed once again why I prefer life in the big city. Although, Russo has long been lauded as an author who has an uncanny ability to reflect the rhythms of small town life and that of its people.
