Although I found the premise of a condemned man's escape from Death Row by the same authorities who put him there difficult to comprehend initially, once I accepted that at face value, the rest of the story was excellent. Simenon, and by turn, Maigret, never disappoints, even when the task at hand seems insurmountable.Simenon provides readers with an atmospheric, character-driven story told fully, yet briefly. His economy of words stands in bold contrast to other writers of his era, yet I never find myself feeling cheated of necessary detail.Although part of a series, this book would work as a standalone.
