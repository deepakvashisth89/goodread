"I've stayed in the front yard all my life.I want a peek at the backWhere it's rough and untended and hungry weeds grow.A girl gets sick of a rose."From "a song in the front yard" in SELECTED POEMS by Gwendolyn Brooks. Poems written and originally published 1944-1963, this edition by @harperperennial 2006.This volume gathers poetry from 20+ years of publications by Brooks, the first black Pulitzer Prize winner (1950) and one of the most renowned 20th-century American poets.The slice-of-life poetry from her early works, "A Street in Bronzeville" and "The Bean Eaters", were my favorites, and as well as her later elegies to some of her favorite poets, Robert Frost and Langston Hughes. There's such power in her words, showing the lives of people in Chicago, Illinois, her life-long home, her activism, and her interpretation of 20th-century events like The Little Rock Nine, and the murder of Emmett Till.This edition included some fantastic supplemental materials: a biography of Brooks, a commemoration by poet Nikki Giovanni, and the transcript of a radio interview and poetry reading with Studs Terkel from 1961.From that interview, Brooks' clear and simple message:"I myself have only tried to record life and interpret it as I have seen it."
