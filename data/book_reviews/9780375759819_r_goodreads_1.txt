This book sells itself as the story of a perfume genius (not THE Perfume Geniushttps://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EPiiY...) who sought to reinvent our scientific understanding of why humans smell, a biography of a maverick wronged by the traditional science world.Burr is a nimble, witty writer, and he obviously has great affection and respect for his subject, Luca Turin. Perhaps too much affection: THE EMPEROR OF SCENT comes across less as a biography and more as a hagiography. To Burr, Turin is unassailably brilliant, ahead of his time, so far outside the box that the box can't recognize him as a geometric form. To me, Turin seems like a hyper-intelligent, self-taught polymath who does slightly junky science and then is annoyed when other scientists point out what he'd need to do to transform his junk science into the real deal. After I finished, I felt obliged to pick up my laptop and do a dive into the other named scientists and the fate of Turin's smell theory . . . which turned out to be a worthwhile and entertaining time in general.Ultimately, a fun, provocative read that asks "what if Howl fromHowl's Moving Castletried to get a piece published inNature?"
