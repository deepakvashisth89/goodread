This book was headed for the local rummage sale....but before I pitched, I paged through it. I found I couldn't put it down. I haven't read much about the battle of Gettysburg, considered to be the pivotal engagement of the Civil War. Foote's "Stars in Their Courses," (a biblical passage) told me what I'd been missing. Everyone's takeaway about Gettysburg might be different, but Foote makes a case for thinking twice about Lee's strategic acumen, or the success of his opponent, George Meade for reasons other than numerical superiority. But this book is more about boxes on a map;there's blood on these pages...pardon the drama...but rather the intensity of men going into battle with the hopes, fears, expectations, their concern for their comrades' well being, and their place in history. Foote lays out the three-day battle for readers through the eyes of those who were there...the planning, the execution, the carnage, all described through what had to have been some painstaking and quite possibly gut-wrenching primary source research. In the process, he provided what the butcher's bill looked like after the smoke had cleared. Horrendous numbers, and an aftermath in which in some cases the living might have actually envied the dead.When I was younger, I used to collect drawings of Civil War battles. One of them, Pickett's Gettysburg Charge, portrayed a Confederate officer ( I thought it was Pickett; it wasn't) leading the attack, his hat balanced on the tip of his sabre. But nothing in that picture could even begin to tell the real story of that charge...the knowledge that it was both heroic and hopeless, doomed from the start by poor planning, poor reconnaissance and poor execution. It was ordered by Lee himself, thinking it would be the blow that would collapse Union forces. Instead, it delivered a blow to Confederate forces from which it would recover anytime soon.I'm keeping the book.
