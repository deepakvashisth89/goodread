This is maybe one of the classics defending Calvinism. Although well written, and trying to be convincing Palmer don’t convince in any remarkable dagree. Surely it gives one an eagernes to study the bibleverses he gives to his defence, but some of them you don’t even have to look deeper into to see the exegetical fallacies he presents. Some of the presented points are simply too shortly defended, and in a bit arrogant way.I’m far from a calvinist but was further shocked when he defended all sin and evil as God’s will and a result of something God forordained and decreeded. A conclusion that is natural when you assume that Calvinism is true. The book also contains some strawman arguments about arminians and arminianism. It’s although a great book if you want to understand the TULIP of Calvinism. I’m sure to dive deeper into the verses Palmer presents on behalf of many calvinists.
