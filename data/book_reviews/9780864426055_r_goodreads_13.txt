I picked this book from a little community library near my house, so it was a random choice.

The book is an account of the author and his wife buying a rural cottage in Italy, and their life there. It is interesting, funny and quite sentimental. It gives a view into the countryside, the life of the people that live there, and their traditions and rituals. It’s also quite good on nuggets of local history.

There are a couple of things that I think make this even more interesting. Firstly they purchase the house in the sixties, so most of the book takes place then and into the seventies. This is before most of the tourism boom so the book is a look into this part of Italy at that point in time. Lots of traditions, trades, and routines the author describes are already gone by the nineties when the book finishes, as TV, affordable cars, and economic changes drive new ways of life.

In fact you can get a sense of the book as a slice of history even from the fact it was written in the nineties. The author describes what things like pesto and focaccia are. Things I think today in Britain would just be assumed knowledge.

The second thing that makes the book more interesting is that the author was captured and taken prisoner in Italy during WW2. He escapes from his prison and hides in Italian mountain ranges, with a lot of help from locals. His recounting of these experiences, and his revisiting of some of the places, are another slice of history.

It was close to being 4 out of 5, for these reasons.
