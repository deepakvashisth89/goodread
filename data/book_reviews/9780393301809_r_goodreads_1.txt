This book is concerned almost solely with the political history of the state, from the first colonial settlement to the time of writing (mid 70s). And a dreary history it is--one set of greedy corrupt high-living overlords being replaced by demagogues mobilizing popular revulsion, who quickly become a new set of greedy corrupt high-living overlords. The other interesting aspect of New Jersey history--the change from wilderness to subsistence farming to market farming to industry to suburb--gets mention only insofar as it affects politics. Thomas Alva Edison is mentioned only because his son was governor.
