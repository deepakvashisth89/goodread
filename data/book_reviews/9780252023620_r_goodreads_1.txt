It was okay.I picked it up because Taylor Swift quotes his phrase "where the spirit meets the bone" in Evermore, although I'm thinking she might have gotten it from Miller William's daughter who I think was a notable singer that used her father's phrase as the title for one of her songs.The inauguration poem and the poem 'compassionate' with the line TS used were the best ones. They were pretty good! Both would be worth revisiting now and then. I really like straightforward poetry when it still has some real soul to it and a pretty turn of phrase here or there. The first poem in the collection was also good too. There were definitely a few other nice lines among the bunch.Overall through, the style of this collection is mostly very straightforward, even for prose poetry.Some of the themes deal with Christianity in a way that is sort of about the constraints of the religion, which I don't believe in to begin with, so there wasn't much there for me. Some of the poems were also inexplicably shallow, like making fun of someone's personal ad in the newspaper even though the ad was actually quite witty?? (ex lines from ad:45 year old male who could pass for 60, sober now and then) like why was Miller taking it at face value and also so seriously? There's another poem where he laments that poets are so quickly forgotten by history, and writes that it's from a place 'nobler than greed' that he wishes poet's works were remembered for thousands of years. But to be honest there is truly nothing in the rest of the poem to actually suggest that anything deeper than greed is actually motivating these thoughts ?A notable lowlight was the poem calledThe Light in the Eyesand was simply two lines:"Who knowswhere it goes?"
