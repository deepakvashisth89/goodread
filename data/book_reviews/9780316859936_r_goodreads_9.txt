Still Here is, at heart, a romance, a tale of attraction between middle-aged Jewish professionals, one who's built a family in an enviably long-term marriage and another whose serial monogamy has left her alone and craving intimacy. It's also a meditation on aging and our responses to this ubiquitous human curse that is, proverbially, better than the alternative. Who better to appreciate this than the immigrant children of those who dodged the death camps or soldiers who've seen first hand what an anti-tank missile can do to the vulnerable human body? It's rich in reflection on family history, the power of parents to control or free their children and the ultimate control we have in choosing to make outrageous demands or dignified concessions. It also manages to weave in great sense of place in its Liverpool setting, some great observations on modernist architecture and reflections on ideals of beauty. The plot never drags and there's warm warm intelligence on display on every page.
