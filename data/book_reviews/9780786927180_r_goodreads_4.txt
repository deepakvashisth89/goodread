Just like Downfall, this book is side-quests upon side-quests. This would have been completely fine to me (because, honestly, any books in this series that aren’t the main storyline are side-quests), but for the fact that I didn’t care about most of the characters. We learn next to nothing about newcomer Varek and he was very bland. I didn’t like the implication that less makeup and more modest clothing was an “improvement” for Rikali, though I did like that she was less annoying than in the first book. Maldred barely does anything in this novel, and I dislike him anyway, and Dhamon is the same self-serving, asshole he was in book one.

YET, we have my boy Ragh. I knew there was a badass Draconian in the series and he finally arrives. Granted, the fact that he’s ten feet tall seems improbable to me. The ceiling of my main floor is only nine feet, so how Ragh later fits into certain places, his shoulders rubbing aside, seems unclear to me. Seriously, I love the Draconians, so I recall a lot about them. In my mind, I altered him to be seven feet instead, since that made more sense for most of the situations. Anyway, his height question aside, I liked Ragh because he had personality.

I also liked the villain, Nura Bint-Drax. The previous book didn’t have an antagonist, so it was nice in this one to have something to tie it all together. I wish we’d had more scenes with her, though the ones we get are interesting.

The writing in this one was less jumpy because we tended to stick to Dhamon’s third-person limited, and the fight scenes were well-described. It’s a more engaging novel than the previous, but there were some continuity questions I had, such as how (not why) the knights made it to the ships and how Ragh escaped one situation. Yet, Rabe has some really inventive and super cool locations and situations in this novel, which made it much better than the first.

The best part of the novel was the section with the sorceress, as it went a completely different direction than I expected and Dhamon was passed out for a lot of it.

I’ll read Redemption, but only for Ragh. I wish Dhamon would just die and the story was about our poor flightless draconian instead.
