This was such a strange book. Had a reference not been made in the story, I would have guessed the book had been written in the 50s, yet it was published in the 90s. The protag is a lawyer who is in his 40s but refers to young men (anywhere from 16 to guys in their 20s/30s) as "sonny." Women talk about being liberated yet, ugh. It kept reading to me as a book about white male privilege.Here's the thing: I read books all the time not only from different decades but different centuries. I am usually only momentarily troubled by sexism, etc, and let it go as being from a bygone era. For reasons I don't get, this book really bugged me and took me out. Oh, and the protag commits a rape (in my opinion) and we are to think it is justified. yech!So, I'm done with this series. I can't read Travis McGee either; couldn't get past that first book. Lovers of McGee think I'm nuts. This book about Brady Coyne really rubbed me the wrong way, but politics aside, I think I'm most bothered by this weird uncomfortable quaintness.
