This is one of the saddest books I've read recently. 
This is one of the funniest books I've read recently. 
One feeling does not cancel out the other. Vonnegut has seamlessly mixed the two, page by page by page.

In 'Deadeye Dick' tone is everything. If you find yourself unprepared as to what to expect going in, you might start believing the book is depressing as hell. And, in a certain light, that could very well be. But not in Vonnegut's true, somewhat side-splitting sardonic light. He is not so much writing a book as he is holding up a funhouse mirror to the mind-numbing unreality that is this reality we call 'life'. 

'Deadeye Dick' is rather like a bipolar, vintage Warner Bros. cartoon. 'Tongue-in-cheek' gets a whole new look.

There's no real plot. Capsulizing would be next to impossible, since the entire 'storyline' shifts from chapter to chapter (though it actually feels more like from line to line).

Essentially, narrator Rudy Waltz tells us all about the black cloud that has hovered over him since birth. In the first line, he refers to himself as "an innocent wisp of undifferentiated nothingness". A cypher. But that's not just low self-esteem talking. Rudy feels that includes all of mankind. We're born without meaning, then live and die that way.

Rudy is something of a natural storyteller. And a writer. He's known for writing one play. A really bad one, one that nevertheless opens in New York City - and closes in one night, playing to a receding audience. 

In retrospect, Rudy's life has been cursed since the day in his young life when he was shooting off one of his father's many, many guns and, by way of a rogue bullet, accidentally killed a pregnant woman (thus earning himself a moniker).
