Do I think the Imperial Legend could be possible? Yes. Does Alexis Troubetzkoy do a good job of swaying the reader to thinking so? Not really. The author can't seem to make up his mind how things happened and the book is full of contradictions.First, he tells us Alexander planned his disappearance before leaving St. Petersburg. Yet when the royal party is in Taganrog, the author relates incidents (such as the candle, the old hermit monk, etc.) as if they are swaying Alexander's mind toward disappearing on the spur of a moment decision. Pick a hypothesis and stand behind it. Because his argument loses a great deal of credence with the waffling.He also contradicts the information about the empress. First, he suggests that Alexander told his wife during their six hour private conversation, but later he has the doctor trying to get rid of the empress during the pertinent time, as if she is not privy to the plot.And why does he harp on the exhumation of the body or bodies, as if that will bring closure? How, when he tells us the grave might hold a different body, be empty or that Feodor Kuzmich was transferred from Siberia to the royal tomb? Even if they did do DNA testing today, the author has a story prepared to support his hypothesis no matter the outcome.So in short, an interesting legend but not very well defended here.
