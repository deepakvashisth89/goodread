This a pleasant enough read, but rather dull. Why? Because there’s no suspense. Usually, in a romance, the suspense is going to be about when the conflict between the protagonists will be resolved, when and how they will overcome the obstacle keeping them apart, when they’ll realize they’re in love, etc. Or, there’s some kind of a mystery/spy plot to add suspense. You know the mystery will be solved, you know the villains will be identified and probably punished, you know the protagonists will survive any peril and end up together, but the particulars of how all that will happen are unknown, and, ideally, draw you in.In this case, everything was so obvious that the predictability far outweighs the unknown. From within a couple pages of the tragic beginning, it’s obvious that the uncle was murdered, and it’s obvious who did it. Only a little while later, you can make a pretty good guess for how.Within a couple pages of meeting the heroine, the hero’s declaring himself. The author (to give some credit where it’s due) does give some flashback scenes that seem intended to show the building affection, but they seem awkwardly placed, or too long to be flashbacks. In any case, despite them, I didn’t get a compelling sense of why he’s so enthralled. It’s too easy.The predictability is somewhat reduced by the way the good guys decide to entrap the villain, but unfortunately, the characters (especially the heroine) reveal themselves as TSTL at this point, because the plan is really silly.The plan to catch murderer: set up a horse race to lure the killer into reusing the same exact same method used to kill the uncle, but with the new earl as bait. The heroine decides to tie him up so that he can't drive in the race, and drives the curricle in the race herself. Of course she nearly dies. Because of course the villain would be dumb enough to kill someone the exact same way.Ultimately, despite a couple of nice scenes, it wasn't wholly satisfying as a romance.
