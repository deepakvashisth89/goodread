The fascination with our own lives–problems, love, friends, work–tends to take up most of the space in a person's mind. And though this makes sense, a book like this reminds you that every person you walk by–the baker, the train conductor, the gas attendant– all have their own lives. That baker who sells you their Italian treats learned that recipe from his aunt, who was born and raised in Italy, but moved here so that her kids and grandkids could choose the life they want. That train conductor is on his last day of OT before Christmas because his kids have been begging for a new toy, and there is nothing he won't do to see a smile on his children's faces. That gas attendant is saving up money to go to college to be a doctor–studying flashcards on what the appendix used to do, why insulin is important to the body, what signs are important to detecting cancer–and is currently the highest-ranked student at his local community college. All these people have stories, and when you take a second to consider someone else's, the world grows–and so do you.
