Within Gregory Benford’s Galactic Center series, the first two volumes are hard-sf near-future. However, with Great Sky River (#3), the story jumps forward some 35,000 years, to an initially unrecognizable story universe. While still adhering to known principles of physics, the nature of the writing shifts from hard-sf to more of a post-apocalyptic style. I’m now in a re-read of the entire series, because it is mentioned in Lecture 4 “Evolution and Deep Time in Science Fiction” of Gary Wolfe’s video lecture seriesHow Great Science Fiction Works.On the barren world of Snowglade, we meet Killeen and his son Toby. They are living with a band of humans known as Family Bishop. Their history has been one of gradual loss of technology under assault of aggressive and climate-changing Mechs - from starships to archologies, to fortress citadels, to a dwindling population of homeless scavengers. Just six years ago, they were driven out of Citadel Bishop, and have been on the run from the Mechs every day since. For about half the book, the narrative seems a familiar trope of lost technology, and desperate conflict. Each human life lost is not just a loss of that person, but also of “aspects” of ancestral humans that are carried within the memories of the living survivors. But, to be honest, Killeen doesn’t even understand what his aspects are talking about a lot of the time.A new type of Mech known as a Mantis begins hunting the Family Bishop, and propels the plot into the beginnings of an exposition of the deep history that has occurred during past millennia. The Mech civilization is revealed to be not as straight-forward as it first appears, and neither are the humans. There is a timeline summary of the Galactic Center chronology in the final pages of the book, but you should not read it ahead of time, if you want to avoid concept spoilers. It is an engaging tale, with plenty of reversals, and a more conventional romantic situation than Benford used in the sexual experimentation of the earlier volumes. I enjoyed the book, and will be going on with the series – but did not find it exceptional.
