Originally titled "A New Wonderland," so it wears its inspiration on its sleeve--it's a little weirder and more surreal than The Wizard of Oz (although it will be retconned as being part of the same "universe" and appear briefly in The Scarecrow of Oz).

They're a trope, but I find "candylands" viscerally ghastly. This is a crank opinion about something that doesn't actually matter of course, but ... can't children enjoy a rainy day without the rain being lemonade? I spent so many happy hours as a child on the river with my Dad; in Mo, the river is apparently rootbeer. What a rancid thought.

Putting that aside, some of these stories are quite fun; if you stripped out the candy stuff and told me that "The Wizard and the Princess" was a traditional fairy tale from a country I wasn't familiar with, I'd believe you; it's great, and the princess shows a lot of fight. L Frank Baum loved his little girl protagonists, since that was his target audience, but aside from Ozma periodically solving everything with magic, they're often quite passive. "Timtom and the Princess Pattycake" is another good adventure story, even if I side-eye the premise of "suitor must find magical potion to cure his love interest's personality"; its vision of her temper as a sort of possessing demon who is not "actually her" is reminiscent of how some alcoholics see their alcoholism, although it probably doesn't bear in-depth thought.

Definitely a few to many "royalty visits foreign clime and finds they're all crazy and everyone should stay at home" stories; weirdly provincial in ways the Oz books don't tend to be.
