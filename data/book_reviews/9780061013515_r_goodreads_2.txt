How do men act on a sinking ship? Do they hold each other? Do they pass around the whisky? Do they cry?These were the reflections of Sebastian Junger at the very beginning of the book. It is something almost too terrifying to consider: standing on a sinking ship and knowing you will soon be plunged into the unforgiving abyss.Originally, my next shipwreck book was intended to focus on the SS Arctic, but I realized that my blood pressure would be too high if I were to read about that event, so I decided against it. Why, you ask? For those who aren't familiar, the SS Arctic is notorious for the mutiny of its crew and for ignoring the principle of "women and children first." Out of all the individuals on board, only 85 people survived - 24 passengers and 61 crew members, all of them men - while all the women and children perished.In the past, it was predominantly women and children who died in shipwrecks. The "women and children first" principle was not a formal law or written rule; rather, it was a silent agreement - an honorable practice of the time. Unfortunately, not all ships adhered to this principle. The Titanic was one of the first ships to implement this unspoken agreement, and it was only due to the honor and gentlemanly conduct of the crew members and male passengers that women and children were given priority during the sinking (and also because the officers threatened to shoot the men who attempted to board the lifeboats forcefully).Now that I’ve shared some history, let’s move on to the story I am writing.The Perfect Storm is the true story of six fishermen on the swordfishing boat Andrea Gail, which set sail from Gloucester, Massachusetts. It also follows their family members, three people aboard a sailboat named Satori, and the New York Air National Guard's 106th Rescue Wing during the convergence of weather conditions known as the Perfect Storm.Some of you may have seen the movie based on this book, which stars George Clooney. I wanted to read this book mainly because of Sebastian Junger, whom I greatly respect as both a writer and a person. I have seen his documentary Restrepo, where he was embedded with the Second Platoon in Afghanistan's Korengal Valley, and I've read his book War, which addresses the psychological struggles soldiers face after returning from war. I also enjoyed the movie The Perfect Storm, which was adapted from this book, so I had high expectations going into it.SPOILERSThe year is 1991. 30-year-old Bobby Shatford, played by Mark Wahlberg in the movie, is about to board the swordfishing boat named the Andrea Gail. The Andrea Gail, as Junger describes, is one of the most profitable fishing vessels in Gloucester Harbor. The only other sword boat in the harbor that could surpass the Andrea Gail in catches is the Hannah Boden, captained by Linda Greenlaw. Linda (played by Mary Elizabeth Mastrantonio) is one of the few women in the industry and is recognized as one of the best captains on the entire East Coast.Bobby is set to embark on a month-long trip to the Grand Banks, located off the coast of Newfoundland, Canada. Bobby is in a committed relationship with Christina Cotter, an attractive woman in her early forties. His mother, Ethel, works as a daytime bartender at the Crow's Nest, a popular hangout and drinking spot for fishermen.Bobby and Christina are both divorced and have children from their previous marriages. He was bartending and fishing to pay off child support debt. Chris was friends with Bobby's sister and met Bobby when her friend took her to Crow's Nest one evening. As Chris remembers it he doesn't look back, not once, and he keeps his face hidden the entire way.Swordfishing fishermen are often seen as the high rollers of the fishing industry. They spend most of their time at sea and have limited opportunities to be with their families. It's a challenging and dangerous profession, but the financial rewards are substantial.37-year-old Billy Tyne, the captain of the Andrea Gail (played by George Clooney in the film), was a divorced father of two daughters who had a deep love for the sea. His ex-wife divorced him because she believed he would never quit fishing, despite his assurances that he wanted to do so.'If he had to pick between me and the boat he picked the boat.'Other crew members include 30-year-old Dale Murphy from Florida, 36-year-old Michael 'Bugsy' Moran, also from Florida, 29-year-old David 'Sully' Sullivan, a local from Gloucester, and 32-year-old Alfred Pierre, a Jamaican from New York.They are setting off for the Grand Banks, known for being a rich fishing ground but also one of the stormiest areas on Earth.Since 1650, an estimated 10,000 Gloucester men have died at sea, far more Gloucestermen than died in all the country's wars. As dangerous as the Grand Banks were, though, Georges Bank was even worse. There was something so ominous about Georges that fishing captains refused to go near it for 300 years. Men talked of strange dreams and visions they had there, and the uneasy feeling that dire forces were assembling themselves.Accidents occur frequently, and it is not uncommon for fishermen to get injured or even fall overboard. Once someone falls overboard, their chances of survival diminish significantly due to their heavy clothing, strong waves, and the cold water. Even if a crew member successfully brings them back onto the boat, it does not ensure their survival, since the vessels are located far out at sea, which significantly delays the Coast Guard's arrival to airlift the victim to a hospital. There are times when rescue isn't feasible. Most vessels are located several miles offshore, way beyond helicopter range.The trip wasn't going well. They had been at sea for several weeks and had not caught nearly as many swordfish as they needed. The more fish they catch, the more money the crew will earn. A small catch results in little income for a profession that is both dangerous and difficult. The crew is starting to feel angry and frustrated, as this situation means they will have to remain at sea longer. To make matters worse, the ice machine is malfunctioning. If the fish spoil, all their hard work will be in vain.However, their fortunes take a turn. By the end of the month, Captain Billy Tyne has 40,000 pounds of fish in his hold, valued at approximately $160,000. We know this because fishing boat captains regularly communicate with one another. A captain named Johnston said he spoke with Billy, who sounded happy and was getting ready to head back home.Billy had no idea the Perfect Storm would hit North America on October 28 and last for days.Heading home can be challenging for a captain, as it's crucial to select the right route. This decision affects not only the time it takes to return but also the level of danger posed by the weather forecast. The Andrea Gail couldn't afford to take several days to avoid the storm because all the fish they caught would spoil, so they had to stay on course for home. But when the storm hit, Billy unexpectedly changed course without informing the other vessels.While the crew of Andrea Gail fought for their lives, several other dramas unfolded. One involved a sailboat named Satori, which was successfully rescued. The most thrilling part of the book occurs in the later chapters, involving a downed National Guard helicopter from the New York Air National Guard's 106th Rescue Wing. Major David Ruvola, pilot; Captain Graham Buschor, co-pilot; Staff Sergeant James R. Mioli, flight engineer; and pararescue jumpers TSgt John Spillane and TSgt Rick Smith find themselves adrift in a dark, stormy ocean. In the film, the helicopter attempts to rescue Andrea Gail. In reality, its mission was to save a Japanese yachtsman (he was eventually picked up by a Romanian freighter). Pararescue jumper Rick Smith, a father to three young daughters, was lost at sea, and his body was never recovered.What bothers me now that I've read the book is the portrayal of Sully in the film. Movie Sully comes off as a rude hothead who spends most of the time arguing with Murph. In reality, Sully was a kind and considerate man, and it bothers me that Hollywood tarnished his reputation to create more drama. I can't fathom the emotions his parents and siblings experienced while watching the film.The Andrea Gail vanished just a few hundred miles from where the wreckage of the Titanic lies.
