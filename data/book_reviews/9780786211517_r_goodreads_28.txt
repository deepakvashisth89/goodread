Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ½Genre: Literary FictionFour damaged lives meet and live together in Italy at the end of World War II. In an abandoned villa, a nurse (Hanna), a thief (Caravaggio), an Indian sapper (Kip), and an English patient, meet and live there talking about their present and about what they have suffered in the past. They relive those memories (good and bad) that still cheer them up or haunt them all the time.This book was the Booker Prize winner in 1992. I have watched the movie adaptation many times but this is the first time I read the book itself. This is not my first Michael Ondaatje book though. The story is very captivating. I think the idea behind the story is brilliant to bring four strangers who suffered many hardships together in an isolated place especially at a time the war’s dust has not settled yet. The whole setting of the story is fantastic.The author has given all the characters their due. Each one of them is well developed whether it is their present or their past. I feel this is a great accomplishment because the book is not long. The writing is stunning, to say the least. You will see beautiful prose and many poetically rich text. As for the timeline, keep in mind that this is not a linear story. It goes back and forth between the past and present for each one of the characters.The English patient’s identity is gradually revealed throughout the story. Not only him but all the other characters as well. However, the reader will be more interested in him at first due to the title of the book which makes you think it is all about him. In reality, the story is more about all the characters but the English patient is the one that is kept mostly ambiguous for the sake of story-building and giving the plot more intrigue and vagueness. Highly recommended for all literary fiction fans.
