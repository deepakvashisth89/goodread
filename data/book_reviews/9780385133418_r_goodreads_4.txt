The author Robert Bright would not ring a bell but as soon as I saw this book's cover, coming to me from outstanding children's books my parents stored, familiarity rang right away. I even recognized a pivotal scene of “Georgie And The Robbers”! A little friendly ghost stands atop a billowing sheet, to make himself look larger and bolder. He aggressively shoos away a threat to the animals and family with whom they lived, while that couple was out, in town. I love stirring a memory, of a book cover and of a page I have not seen in thirty years. How rewarding!

This was published in 1963 and I had no inkling that this is fourth in a series, that started in 1944. It is in recent years that I am filling in stories that belong with bargain novels I bought. I would enjoy reading the rest of Georgie's stories. The idea that a friendly spirit has the attic of a house and is known to the household pets and other animals, with whom he accomplishes deeds, is creative and empowering. The night environments, challenging situations, and villains are downright unsettling, certainly for me, as a tot. Our animal protagonists have cheerful faces, thus it is a safe suspense, that generates excitement about how this story will come out. Robbers attempting to steal from a kindly farm family was disturbing. I loved seeing Herman their cat, Miss Oliver, an owl, and the cow, with a rabbit looking on, work together to scare the heck out of these bandits.
