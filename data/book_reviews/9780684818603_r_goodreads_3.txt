Set in Coastal North Carolina in the summer of 1968, "The River to Pickle Beach" is an enjoyable portrait of men and women, their need for love, and their ability to handle change. It's a realistic story of Bebe Sellar, a kind-hearted soul with a deep sense of faith, her husband Jack, a gentle man with a tragic childhood secret, and the summer they spend running a colony of beach rentals on Pickle Beach. Bebe and Jack encounter several people that summer, and stereotypes are broken. They also become involved with Mickey McCane, a tormented man, whose inability to change with the times, and own tragic past leads him to psychopathic behavior and a violent crime.
I enjoyed this book. Betts' characters are well developed and her settings are realistically drawn. As I read I could hear the surf and see the children playing on the beach. I could also experience the beach just as Bebe did. Excellent writing! I am surprised Betts is not better known for her work. I'll be reading more of her work and am just disappointed that I didn't discover her sooner.
