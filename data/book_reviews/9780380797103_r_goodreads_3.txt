This is not a bad book, but it was definitely elevated by Beverly A. Crick's narration, and it's the first Maxwell book that hasn't landed in my DNF pile.The writing was satisfactory but it was very trope-y!Tropes include:a spinster vicar's daughter whose parents are deadan alienated from his now-dead father, former scoundrel, self-made 2nd son of a dukecaught in a compromising situation marriage of conveniencerescue of scruffy street orphans (a weird side plot that never really went anywhere)one room in an inn (multiple times)"I'm not good enough for her/she'd be better off without me"you get the idea.There were some comic bits (not many) and a cast of bizarre hypocritical villagers who I was glad to get away from.Also, super hard to get excited about a MMC who owns a shipping company that is basically a colonialist enterprise á la the East India Co.A mid-level, written in the late 20th C, historical romance all around but I made it to the end, which I guess says something.
