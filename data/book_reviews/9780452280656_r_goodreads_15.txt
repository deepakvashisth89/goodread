This book was not only a great pleasure to read but also gives the gift of a theoretical framework which extends far beyond the book itself. Werner’s use of the Gospel, Blues, and Jazz impulses within Black American music are not unique to him, of course, but by tracing how these three interact and were expressed through the music of the 50s-90s he helps us see just how ever present and necessary they are. It was especially striking to read his analysis of the post-civil rights movement, and how politics and policies betrayed the promise of this movement. Werner shows how some artists managed to resist the economic and culture darkness, while others merely reflected the dismal realities. It’s not exactly a hopeful story, but perhaps the examples of artists resisting in the midst of darkness is a more realistic kind of hope and example.

I wish Werner would update the story for the 20 years since, as I would love to read his analysis of Kendrick and Drake, BLM and MAGA, and Bruce Springsteen continued activism. But I suppose the story never really ends, and the gift of this book is that he helps us understand this story as it continues to unfold.
