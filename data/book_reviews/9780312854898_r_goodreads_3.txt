This is a good read for anyone who's tired of the "awesomely charming prince has to go save a princess, slay the dragon, and have trivial things get in his way that he can easily get around" books that sound like repeats of stories one tells to children.I read this book when I stumbled across it in my father's study and read it in Sr. High. I must say, this book is hilarious to me.A boy who must become a man, travel out into the wilderness alone, a conartist, traitors, the big city, royals, thieves, dragons, and maidens so old they can't be good for anything anymore. The pace of this book was excellent, the story was refreshing, and the ending isn't what I expected, and yet, even twisted as it is, I loved it.Nijon makes me laugh because he has all sorts of adventures throughout this story, and each is as real and improbable as the last. It's hard to pick a favorite character, from the protagonist Nijon, Brother the mongoose, the cunning trickster Mika, the near-blind dragon... The list goes on and on. Each character is interesting and all have reasons to make one laugh and contemplate the serious issues of life and how we deal with them.I'm not sure if I'm disappointed there was only one volume of this book or not. It leaves plenty to the imagination, ends on its own terms, and doesn't turn into a drawn out series. As far as modern tales go that stretch out into ten books or more, I'm actually glad this is a forced stand alone. I think I would have been disappointed if it continued, even though it does say "Magic of the plains: Volume 1" on the cover.
