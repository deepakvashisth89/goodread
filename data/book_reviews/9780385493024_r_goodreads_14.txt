First, it's a little outdated, as medicine is constantly changing. Without sounding overly critical of her birth experience, it annoys me that she generalizes the care that women get at hospitals. That all hospitals want to get women delivered ASAP with as many interventions as possible. Not true, especially if they have midwives and nursing staff trained in natural births. I feel like the author along with so many new moms did not educate herself on the birth process and did not understand that you need to be your own advocate when it comes to your care. If you don't like something, say no. You can always say no. Another thing I don't like is the shaming of bottle feeding. I really hate that. Fed is best. Feed your baby. Your breastfed baby is no smarter, healthier, better than my bottlefed baby. It's not a freaking competition. Other than those issues, there are some serious problems with the healthcare system in the U.S. Especially when it comes to caring for our pregnant and postpartum population. They are so neglected compared to other countries. That is a big issue the author touches on. Do seek help with postpartum depression. Do seek support groups if you need them.
