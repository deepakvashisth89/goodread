☆☆☆☆☆The Red Badge of Courage (AmazonClassics Edition) by Stephen CraneThe horrors of war were written in old English of the period, almost as if it was written by a poet. The fear is evident through the thoughts and feelings of young Henry Fleming (the youth) fighting on the side of the North in the Civil War. This young man has his doubts on how he will stand up to the chaos, destruction, and death that is to come once the battle begins. He worries how he will react to the violence and how he will be measured by his actions when the fighting begins.It's really easy to sit back and think of yourself as being one to stand in the face of death and believe you'll be courageous, stand your ground, and be the hero who you have no doubt in your mind you'll be until you face the horror. Facing the horror head-on is the only means of truly knowing oneself's courage. You can lie to yourself and boast to those around you, but until you are truly facing a life and death situation, no one truly knows who he/she is and what you are capable of.Even in a moment of weakness, you can pull yourself up and become the hero you truly meant to be both to yourself and those around you.War is hell, and it's a real shame that we haven't evolved more than we have. The loss of both lives and resources could and should be utilized to better mankind instead of wasting those resources on war.I read this book years ago (okay decades ago in High School) and through the years I had forgotten most of what I had read and as reading it through more experienced eyes was enlighten and affected more than I would have thought.
