Many years ago I read the autobiography of Franklin, and although it was quite fascinating in a way, I did not find I could like the man.

So why now read a biography? I really do not know. But when I found the book in a store I felt the urge to buy it. Probably because of its author: Carl van Doren. I knew he was the uncle of Charles, the one who cheated in a Quiz show. Why this is a reason to read a biography about a man I do not care for beats me.

The book was written in the 1930s and it shows. There is a reason why new biographies are written all the time. There is nothing wrong with the book, but it feels very old-fashioned. It is certainly very detailed and you find information in it you do not need. So, in 1779 he had 1040 bottles of wine in his cellar and 48 bottles of rum. In 1782 there were 1203 bottles. Maybe that is a bad example because it is actually quite interesting. But you know what I mean.

What I expect from a biography is something to learn not only about the man (or woman) but also about the times. In here it seems that van Doren expects everyone to know the historical circumstances. Which may be the case for an American audience of a hundred years ago (but I doubt it.)
