I loved every moment of this. I must admit that I was initially skeptical. The premise felt fascinating and refreshingly different, yet when I realised that the author is a white man, I worried that certain nuances might be lost or misinterpreted. However, you cannot fabricate a world like this with such texture and affection. It is evident that Smith spent meaningful time in Botswana, engaging with its people and culture. I do not know what his precise vision was in writing this book, but the simple fact that he believed a story like this deserved to exist makes me appreciate it all the more.

The novel itself is beautifully written. It follows , or , a woman who opens the , Botswana. For clarity, the agency is run by a woman, though she does not handle cases exclusively for women.

My first impression of Mma Ramotswe was of a woman who takes genuine pride in her work and her achievement. Personally, I think the pacing is impeccable. The narrative briefly explores her family history and her life before the agency, and the more I read, the more I felt she had every reason to be proud.

Mma Ramotswe is no Sherlock Holmes. She is humorous, wise, and deeply kind, qualities that shape her approach to solving cases.

Several cases unfold throughout the book. Some are deceptively simple and might have been resolved had the law shown greater care for the people it serves. Others are more delicate, requiring exactly the compassion and insight Mma Ramotswe brings. The investigations range from missing persons and marital conflicts to kidnapping, medical malpractice, couple and family disputes. Each case feels purposeful, offering glimpses into everyday life, as well as the social realities and injustices faced by people in Botswana, particularly women.

I honestly do not know how this book stayed off my radar for so long, but I am grateful it finally found its way to me. I am equally grateful to the bookshop attendant who recommended it after my boss did. I look forward to reading the rest of the series throughout the year.
