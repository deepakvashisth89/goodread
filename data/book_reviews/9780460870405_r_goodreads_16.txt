Poe is the pioneer of American literature in several genres. Detective fiction, Gothic horror, perhaps even science fiction? Included in this collection are some of Poe’s best stories, including “The Cask of Amontillado,” “The Fall of the House of Usher,” The Gold Bug,” “The Mystery of Marie Rogêt,” “The Purloined Letter,” and “The Murders in the Rue Morgue.” I enjoyed them all. As a freshman at Penn State, Poe’s stories were required reading the second semester and I enjoyed them just as much then as now.The Mystery of Marie Rogêt is a fictionalized version of an actual murder that occurred in the New York City area, but Poe sets the scene in the Paris of Inspector Dupin. It is a critique of contemporary newspaper accounts of Mary Cecelia Rogers’ disappearance and the possible discovery of her body floating in the river. As such it is also a discourse on early 19th century forensic analysis of the materials appearing in the newspapers. It is thought to be “the first murder mystery based on the details of a real crime” according to the Wikipedia article. I will guess it is also of little interest to any but those fascinated by murder or the details of forensic analysis.“The Murders in the Rue Morgue” is the first “detective” story by Poe and the one most often read and mentioned. C. Auguste Dupin is introduced in this story, the first of three appearances; given his deductive methods, Dupin has to be an early version of Britain’s own Sherlock Holmes. Aside the surprise ending, could this be the first, or an early, version of a “locked room mystery?”As a biological anthropologist I was also interested in several other stories for strictly professional reasons. “The Fall of the House of Usher” seems to feature a family cursed either by tuberculosis or, because of the intergenerational description, perhaps a genetic disorder such as phenylketonuria (PKU), although in the long run, it is just a curse meant to horrify a gothic-loving audience.Last, Poe’s contribution to literary humor – or sarcasm – is seen in such stories as “The Devil in the Belfry,” and an early version of “1066 and All That,” in his interpretation of Scheherazade’s very last story. The horrible “Masque of the Red Death” might have been written after reading Boccaccio’s “The Decameron.”So much has been written about Poe that I will not duplicate the already abundant literature. I will note that Poe richly describes the settings and the characters so that you feel to be a part of the scene and almost interacting with the characters. Thus, he is also a master of the gothic novel very early on. The book is a classic and a grand compendium of Poe’s best stories although some elements are certainly dated and an annotated version would occasionally help with some minutae. Be sure to have this on your MUST READ list soon!
