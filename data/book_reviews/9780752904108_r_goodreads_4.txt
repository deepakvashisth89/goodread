The book chronicles the battle at the mountain of Isandhlwana which took place on 22 January 1879 in South Africa.The British outpost, under the command of General Lord Chelmsford, is armed with rifles, but the 30,000 spear-brandishing Zulu warriors outnumber and overwhelm the garrison. There was little that the stiff-upper-lip heroism and regimentation of the British unit could do to stave off the inevitable massacre.Following the slaughter, a reserve mass of Zulu Impis attacked the fortified mission at Rorke's Drift, and were repelled by 140 British soldiers, the action made famous by the wonderful film 'Zulu'.The author, Cyril "Cy" Raker Endfield, does a reasonable job. I've awarded 4 stars more for the story than the writing, but recommend the read to those interested in this period of the British Empire.
