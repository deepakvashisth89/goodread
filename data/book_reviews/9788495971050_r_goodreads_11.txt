Two ageing friends who once partied with Jimmy Hendrix, John Lennon and Yoko Ono find themselves in an existential crisis. The link between them is Molly, with whom they both had a relationship and whose funeral marks the beginning of the novel.Significantly, it begins with death, because the novel is also a lesson in transience: nothing should be considered certain - neither one's own agility, nor one's career, nor one's friendship. The only thing that seems to endure is the focus on the self. The characters believe to be in the sunshine of themselves and do not see the shadow that is gradually approaching them: if you regard the ego as absolute, loneliness and paranoia await.Clive the composer and Vernon the newspaper editor - both are mirrored on a meta-level, as the strict composition of the novel with its two contrapuntal storylines is reminiscent of Clive's symphony and the unexpected twists and turns of Vernon's idea of a newspaper scoop. At the center, however, is the increasing soul striptease of the two until their lack of empathy becomes unmistakable and the dignity of old age dismantles itself in wild revenge fantasies: In the end, the characters merge into a gruesome morality tale of a bigoted elite.
