What the hell did I just read?



As a classic mystery/thriller this was pretty decent.
But as a romance? Nooooo.



That Max guy was just awful. How is he any sort of romantic leading man? How was he considered a leading man? How was this ever considered a ? This dude made my skin crawl from start to finish. I thought the big twist would be
.
Sadly, no.



A huge problem for me wasn't necessarily that Maxwell was a lot older, but that he had a tendency to treat whateverhernamewas like a child. <--yicky!
All the stuff he says to her about how he or doesn't want her to know certain (basically adult) things about life just made me cringe.
He's that guy who pats you and awkwardly pets your hair. <--you know the kind of creeper that I'm talking about, ladies.



I guessed the twist-twist before the big reveal, but it was probably a real er back in the day. I think that was supposed to make me like Maxwell more, but I still thought he was terrible and that our unnamed heroine should have pushed him off a cliff and lived happily ever after, sipping margaritas on a beach somewhere with her dog.



I can't talk about my biggest gripe with the book without spoiling it. Sorry.













It's hard to believe this book was written by a woman. Seriously, Daphne? It's a How-To book on grooming your much younger, much less experienced bride into a simpering idiot who can't wait to apologize for upsetting you with her silly questions, and looks forward with delight to her future of taking care of your ancient ass.



The fact that the unnamed new wife doesn't know fuckall about life and is somewhat of a plain Jane, and the evil ex-wife was a hot temptress, isn't lost on me, either. Don't be sexy and sure of yourself because that shit will only make your husband sad, little girl.



Best character in the whole book is the creepy firestarter, Mrs. Danvers.
Jesus, that bitch was like an evil Mary Poppins! That shit where she showed her all of Rebecca's old underwear and whatnot? Ahahahaha!
But hey, we could all use a Mrs. Danvers in our corner sometimes, couldn't we?



Ok. Bottom line, I liked the way you discovered the truth of the situation and who Rebecca truly was to everyone around her. That was cool and very well done.
But in case you couldn't tell, I really wasn't fond of the message that the woman Maxwell truly loved was an easily led, highly malleable woman who lacked self-confidence, so ZERO points for the relationship stuff.
