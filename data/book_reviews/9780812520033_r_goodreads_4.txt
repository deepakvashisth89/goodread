This is the first novel set in Borderlands universe. Previously, there were three volumes of story collections (two in 1986 and one in 1991), including by the author of this book, Will Shetterly, and his wife, Emma Bull. Because the previous books collected short stories, I read them at ORBIT – Otherworldly Reads, Bold Ideas, and Tales. SF & F Short Stories and Novelettes group.

I had low expectations starting this novel and was positively impressed. Shared universes are like straitjackets for authors – they cannot kill or seriously mangle recurring characters, the world stays the same, etc., and to make a short story, with only a glimpse is one thing, and a whole novel is totally another. However, this book had surprises for me.

The book starts with the protagonist, Ron, on his way to Borderlands by steam train. He isn’t after adventures or magic; he just wants to find his older brother, Tony, who ran from home a year earlier. However, adventures find him from the start: his ticket is stolen, and he is thrown from the train before they reached Bordertown. Ron is found by two locals, and befriends one of them, half-elf Mooner, who invites Ron to their residence, Castle Pup: “Home of the Strange Pupae. The Pups. We’re a mixed bunch, humans and halfies and elves. Meaning neither the Pack nor the True Bloods like us, but they’re too busy with each other to hassle us much.”

Ron is a bit unsure of his roommates, a gay, King O’Beer, and a pretty woman, Sparks. Being down-to0earth guy, Ron soon finds a job is a bookstore and starts to discover the wonders of Bordertown. Meanwhile, his search for his brother is interrupted by his own problems, including a deal, where he is asked to help one of his new friends get something from the store where he works. There are betrayals, depression and drug abuse, a bit of magic and a big surprise
. Bearing in mind the problem with shared universes I mentioned above, the story paced admirably.
