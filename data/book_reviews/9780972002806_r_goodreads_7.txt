This was certainly an eye opening book that helped me move in the direction it delineates. However, I also found it a bit self-indulgent, too casual, and full of grammatical/textual errors, especially in the second half. With all of that it can be hard to take him seriously. It’s a shame, because so many of his ideas could be so useful, especially now.

I almost stopped reading this book because it could have been about half as long. There are a lot of personal anecdotes about non-violent communication which can interesting to read, but don’t feel replicable or necessarily helpful for my own life. A lot of it seems to be a recommendation of Marshall Rosenberg’s Nonviolent Communication.

Anyway, the last two chapters do a wonderful job of describing what creates violence in our culture and what we can do to help it (jealousy is a big factor) without turning it to politics, new-ageism, or oppressor-oppressed power dynamics. He talks about all of those, but doesn’t get too abstract or scholarly. He stays real.
