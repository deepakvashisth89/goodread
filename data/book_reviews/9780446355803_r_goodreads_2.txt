This book reminded me of what a good writer Rebecca Brandewyne is. For this story, seems that she decided to put the brakes on the purple prose (and believe me, this lady can be more gloriously purple than the Purple Eminence himself, Prince, if she chooses to), injected some downright, laugh out loud, scenes, and still managed to make this a very emotional, romantic story.Heartlandtakes place in post-Civil War Kansas. The book starts out on a very lugubrious tone as a swarm of locusts, in a matter of hours, absolutely destroys every iota of the farmlands that these nineteenth century settlers so desperately toiled on for months. This crisis precipitates the death of one of the female settlers, leaving eight orphaned children vulnerable to the uncaring, drunken administrations of their fool of a father.Our heroine, an 18 year old "spinster", is the one who takes the little plot moppets on and rides down to the local town brothel with a shotgun to retrieve their deadbeat dad. The hero meanwhile, a rough and tough bounty hunter and the uncle of the orphaned children, rides into town just in time to see the heroine plowing through the glass window of the brothel atop an out-of-control mare while her target cowers behind the skirts of a bosomy prostitute.This sets up the tone for the rest of the book. Truly hilarious scenes (the heroine poisons the hero's breakfast leading to a knock down, drag out fight in the barn; two rival suitors, with the heroine in tow, beat each other almost to death with various, scattered parts of a broken wooden chair) are alternated with sober scenes of true horror (an inferno at a revival tent turns a happy July Fourth into a tragedy, a tornado rages on the flatlands with the suddenness of a striking snake, an undernourished baby dies from bronchitis).RB really brought it here and she turned this book into a time machine transporting me to 1875 Kansas rather than set up cardboard cut out characters before a vaguely painted backdrop.The romance between the protagonists was satisfying without being earth-shattering. There are no or at least a very minimized version of OW/OM drama or Great, Big, Terrible Misunderstanding. It was clear early on that these two were meant for each other and it is their internal struggle to come to terms with their feelings, rather than interfering outside forces, that stand as the obstacle to their happy denouement.Haven't liked an RB historical romance this much sinceRose of Raptureand was frankly despairing of finding another book by this author that I would enjoy.This entire review has been hidden because of spoilers.Show full review
