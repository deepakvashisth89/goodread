This book has great merits as well as great defects.Among the advantages:1) the thrilling adventures of Carlo di Lontrone made ​​me think and remember some amazing characters out from Mordecai Richler's pen: namely Solomon Gursky and Duddy Kravitz2) the story of Carlino childhood and early adolescence in Basilicata are full of poetry, love for the homeland and its strange inhabitants3) the representation of certain alternative culture of the 60's and 70's and how it has been lived in the provinceAmong the defects:1) the excessive length of the book2) the difference in narrative depth between the Italian and the "American" sections. In the second (american) part the author, probably due to the less knowledge (and perhaps less love), completely abandons the description of the external environment and the book suffers3) the deeply masculinist angle with which the story is told4) the author goes much (too much) on the erotic side of the story5) last but not least: a comparison with the characters of the Richler's novels highlights the difference in depth and writing skills between the two authors. I mentioned S. Gursky and D. Kravitz and yet in these cases we can realise the differences in personality, ideals and courage of the protagonists. But it is on Barney Panofsky that the comparison becomes merciless. Cappelli shows himseft far from being able to create a multi-faceted character, funny and smart albeit flawed.Read in Italian (original language).
