I've shelved this as sci-fi, but it wasn't written that way. Half of this book is worth reading, and the other half is pretty irrelevant to his main point. The two good chapters are about the aliens in the Old Testament, and the aliens ancient Indian mythology. The chapter about the Fatima event seemed almost totally irrelevant, and the one about Myanmar was poorly constructed. I skimmed rather than read these two chapters. If nothing else, this is useful as a list of sources of where to find the best science fiction that ancient India has to offer. They seriously did talk about aerial vehicles, space faring vehicles, floating/space faring cities, submarines, etc which shot missiles, had biological/chemical warfare capabilities of many kinds, nuclear weapons, instant audio and visual communication, mercury powered engines, invisibility cloaks, etc. But that's because ancient Indians were brilliant and prolific science fiction authors, not because it actually happened.
