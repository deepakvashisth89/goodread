“They don’t understand—old people don’t….they can’t….They don’t know what it is to live!”Yet another case with people flocking around an old relative with lots of money,Dumb Witnessoffers a different perspective on the murder fiction staple, looking at the case with no witness statements, and months after the death. The only reason to continue enquiries is the letter to Poirot just before the death, and his grey cells telling him something is off in this case.“If you show the dog the rabbit, my friend, does he return to London? No, he goes into the rabbit hole.”“What do you mean?”“The dog hunts rabbits. Hercule Poirot hunts murderers. We have here a murderer—a murderer whose crime failed, yes, perhaps, but nevertheless a murderer. And I, my friend, am going into the burrow after him—or her as the case may be.”This case was about proving the intent of the criminal, as much as answering the who and when of the case. Poirot relies on not only attention to subtle details, but also analysis of the day-to-day psychology of the suspects to arrive at an answer - because itdoesturn out to be a murder. And Hastings' internal commentary of the dog Bob's barks add a fun flavour to the book.
