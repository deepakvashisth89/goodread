There is a certain beauty, which is also tainted with sadness, when one is reading a copy of a book that belonged to their Mum. My copy of The Last Battle is the 1975 Puffin book edition, and on the inside cover is my Mum's forename and maiden name, and the date, 1976, so she would have been about twenty. She gave me the complete Narnia collection some years ago, and although they are rather old, they are still in a fairly good condition, and I know I'll treasure them always.

My love of Narnia began young, mainly thanks to Mum, and as an adult, my love still remains strong. Aslan is my love, and he has always been my favourite in the land of Narnia. He brings a great childlike comfort to me.

I cannot remember what my specific thoughts were on 'The Last Battle' as a teenager/child but as an adult, I've noticed certain aspects that didn't sit particularly well with me. This is probably the feminist in me rearing it's head, but because it was so blatant, I feel it must be mentioned. I try to remind myself that this was written in 1956, and generally, it is a known fact that C.S. Lewis included some racism and misogyny in his writings, but this time around, the fate of Susan didn't escape my attention.

All of the characters that I know and love should have returned to Narnia, that beautiful land that has captivated so many hearts, but instead, dear Susan is left behind, because she is much too busy fixing her hair and makeup. I understand things change, but the way in which she was forgotten about, and deemed unimportant, even by her siblings, was quite saddening really. The siblings have been on all of these magnificent adventures, side-by-side, and because Susan takes a interest in something trivial, she is essentially cast aside, and surprisingly, none of her siblings seem to care. It was quite different when Edmund was in trouble in the first book, and everyone dropped everything to come to his aid.

I really hope Susan did find her way back to Narnia, and when she did, I hope she made herself a fresh start.

This book had some wonderful writing, beautiful imagery, as well an impressive battle scene. It's just a pity my heart didn't agree with Susan's fate.
