5 stars for how powerful and well-written this is, but that’s by no means an outright recommendation because the story is *extremely* bleak and will definitely not be for everyone. The blurb grossly misrepresents what is happening here - “a gripping portrayal of adolescent transgression” - these 13-year-old girls, Harriet in particular, are victims of sexual abuse, not the transgressors, and the book looks at how that comes about and the ways it shapes their thinking and behaviour. Bainbridge perfectly captures the psychological contradictions of teenage girlhood; they’re hopelessly naive and insecure while at the same time being acutely aware of their own sexuality and full of confidence at their ability to manipulate others. A very skilled character study. Would definitely be interested to read more Bainbridge and find out what other kinds of stories she’s chosen to tell.
