Despite being enthralled by Schindler’s list, I wouldn’t have selected this book about the American Civil War, also by Thomas Keneally, if it hadn’t been on the 1979 Booker Shortlist. However it did also fill in a gap in my historical knowledge and supply an interesting perspective on the conflict that of the losing ‘rebel’ side.I am not a big fan of military strategy and battlefield gore but Keneally does look at the sufferings, backgrounds, motivations of the ordinary foot soldier in a sympathetic way. It’s hard, from a twenty first century perspective, to imagine why so many were willing to go to war, against a vastly superior (in numbers, kit and supplies) Yankee army, to support slavery, exploitative plantation owners and the deprivation of the liberty of other human beings. I suppose it was a defence of a way of life for many ‘ordinary’ southerners who despised particularly the recent East Coast immigrants from Ireland and Germany whom they regarded as criminal, gutter dwelling criminals in the main ( the power of propaganda). General ‘Stonewall’ Jackson is portrayed in a sympathetic light but some of the medical and surgical standards seem to have been questionable. I did appreciate the inclusion of women’s experiences of war: the wives, lovers and families of soldiers as well as the indomitable but conflicted matron Dorothy Whipple. This wider view of how war affects whole communities made the novel much more readable.Overall a decent book and an incredible feat of imagination for the author.
