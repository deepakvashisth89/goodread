I LOVE David Macaulay books. Every time I see one at a book sale I get it, and it's typically the book I'm most excited to read. His architecture and urban planning books never disappoint. If David Macaulay has a million fans, I'm one of them. If he has only one fan, it's me. If he has no fans, I'm dead.

This book uses art and description to describe the castle-building practices of the English during conquests of Wales around the 13th century. With cross-sections, vignettes, and landscapes, you watch a castle go from a few buildings and a stockade to a towering fortress repelling invasions, then to a crumbling ruin. It answered so many questions I've had since childhood - how do drawbridges actually work? Where were the bathrooms? Who lived in the castle, and who lived outside? The glossary at the back is an extremely useful tool that I wish more informational books had. The art, as always, is excellent. When I went to write this Goodreads review, I saw there was a revised and colored edition that I will be adding to my Christmas list immediately.

One of my dearest friends from Colorado may see this review and complain that it's length and artistic focus means that it doesn't qualify as a full book and I shouldn't list on Goodreads. Lame! This book is fantastic, I learned a lot, it sparked my imagination, and I'm going to use the information I learned in both my history studies and my D&D games. What more could you want from a book? If every book I read was this fun and informative, I would be the happiest guy alive. I would recommend this to pretty much all my friends: Maura, Tom, and Ripken especially. Perfect book to check out from the library, read and enjoy, then try and use what you've learned to design a castle of your own.
