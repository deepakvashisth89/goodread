Genre/Category: Required Reading/CormierWhen two guys in an attempted terrorist move hijack a school bus filled with kids, everything goes downhill from there. People are killed, including a few of the young kids, and justice is not served in the end.Cormier’s writing was powerful; too powerful. I would never want my writing to affect someone as strongly as his writing did to me, especially in such a dark way. It worries me that teenagers, who may have a harder time differentiating truth from falsehood, would fall under the same dark spell without knowing how to handle it. Cormier’s novel was just like a computer game about killing: although it was well-crafted and impressive, too much of it can only do harm in the end.Cormier was clever in switching point-of-view, not only to let the reader know what was happening as the plot thickened, but to create deeper connections between the characters and the reader. The more you related and felt for a character, the more devastating is it to watch them be tortured or killed. I found myself caring for every child, even if I didn’t know their name, and I felt sick to my stomach when Raymond was taken off the bus. Cormier also created imperfect everyday characters—people-you-might-run-into-on-the-street characters. Kate had bladder problems, Miro had a terrible past, and Ben was trying to meet his father’s approval. They became so personal to me as the reader that I couldn’t help but cry when things happened to them (although I can’t say I ever cried for Miro even if he didn’t have complete control over who he became).Overall, I really didn't like this book and will never read another Cormier ever again.
