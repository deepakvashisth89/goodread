I love William Sleator, but you have to get into a sort of Goosebumps mindset for his books. The characters have this trademark inconsistent reasoning that can feel really rash and immature at points. It's fascinating because he ties in real world scientific understanding to all his books, so the characters are objectively smart in all matters except decision makingI wrote my junior thesis in high school on Sleator's body of work & unfortunately I fear I inadvertantly picked all his best books for that. I am slowly making my way through the rest of his catalog and I get increasingly nervous to one day reread those books I used to treasure (Singularity can never be ruined though, that's a perfect book)This one falls pretty neatly in the middle of the pack. Parts of the plot are very intriguing and gripping, while the characters are very weak as usual for Sleator. I wanted to shake them and say "take 30 more seconds to think logically before touching that time travel device!" They basically always realize the flaw in their maneuvers the moment they hit activateThe ending of this did enrage me but I'm not going to dock it points, I feel like the publisher forced the change honestly.There is a moment we believe he has killed the villain by sending her into a volcano, then he reveals he only sent her a bit into the future and wants to talk to her and fix her. This is ridiculous, she killed her own father, she will find a way to harm these characters even without time travel. At minimum, he should've sent her a hundred years in the future so she's not dead but won't overlap with their timeline at all, but WHATEVER it's fine, he's a lovesick childRanking Sleator's books I've read so far:Singularity (genuinely changed my mindset as a teenager)

   The Beasties
