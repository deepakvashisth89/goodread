Late in the novel one character decries our protagonist’s propensity for “morose delectation”, an apt phrase for this book’s fascinations and frustrations. Indeed, I sometimes felt so suffocated by its point-of-view that I wanted to throw the book at the wall: the voice it creates is solipsistic, self-loathing, and myopic to an almost contemptible degree. But this is of course precisely the point. It ultimately amounts to an immanent critique of gay male mores as damning and tragic as Mishima’sForbidden Colors. Holleran’s plainspoken (if erudite and even occasionally prissy) prose belies how sculpted his sentences are; his language vividly renders post-AIDS New York and northern Florida as wastelands of eternal grief. It is impossible not to be moved by the novel’s prolonged passages of mourning, or horrified by the stunted psyche it unsparingly maps.
