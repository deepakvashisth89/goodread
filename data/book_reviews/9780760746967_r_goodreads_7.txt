Pros: A lot of clear illustrations with captions, a variety of artists, color on shiny paper, an index, and two page bibliography.Cons: Some people are mentioned in the text as having commissioned bookplates (e.g. James Cagney, Charles Dickens) but with no description or illustration. Dates are conspicuous by their absence.The captions are informative but there is excessive use of "dates unknown" in instances where dates aren't actually unknown. I noticed that John and Ethel Van Derlip were mentioned but only John had dates after his name (1860-1935). After Ethel's name it says "dates unknown" which is certainly not true. She was born in 1876 and died in 1921. I thought, of course, the woman is shortchanged, again. But then I started looking more closely at other captions and saw that dates were missing or only partially filled in for some men as well as some women. I am disappointed that the author didn't make make any effort to fill in dates more consistently and that the editor didn't think it was important. It wasn't that difficult to look them up, even back in the dark ages of 2003. Someone could have been hired to do it for a couple of hours if the author found it too tedious.
