Weird Talesis a collection of short stories written by German author E.T.A. Hoffmann, a trailblazer of the horror genre. The pioneering nature of his work and the fact that I had before finished a few of Edgar Allan Poe’s short stories, which I enjoyed for the most part, led me to pick up this book.It took me a considerable time to finish all the stories and I am glad that I can now, finally, put this book back on its shelf. The stories vary a lot, some are quite captivating and suspenseful, while others are long and confusing and, honestly, a little boring. The latter contributed heavily to my taking ages to finish.Generally, the tone is very dark, and the stories are often quite dense in as much as they portray a subliminal, deeper meaning. This is most striking for Hoffmann’s famous workThe Sandman, which is not only a horror story, but also a study in psychoanalysis. This depth makes reading the stories, at times, rather challenging. Therefore, comparing to Poe’s short stories, the stories are not as captivating and sometimes feel lengthy.My personal favorite here isMademoiselle de Scuderi, which is not really a horror, but a crime story and which, while quite long, is a suspenseful read.I think Hoffmann’s work is essential to the genre, but in retrospective I would not read a book like this cover to cover. Instead, I would opt to read only his most relevant works.My rating is 2,5 stars, which is the middle ground between the stories I really liked and would give a 4-star-rating and the exceptionally tedious ones I would rate 1-2 stars.
