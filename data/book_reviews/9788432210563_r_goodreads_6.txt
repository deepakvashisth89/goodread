This would have been more appealing if I could 1) read french, 2) knew more about modern literary criticism, 3) the classics of the church, or 4) Paris in the 1960's. One can tell there is a lot going on stylistically, but that's the work of Peter Bush, the translator. It is a complicated work: "Friar Bugeo looked at me. Or was it his author looking at me? The dubious authorship of this book confused me: after all that soul-swapping and historical upheaval the gloomy murk persisted: the mists weren't about to lift" (p. 116-7)
Once I made it past shock and outrage, though, the book had a lot to say about religion and its abuses: "They say the Inquisition was abolished but, don't you think it might survive, deep down in our heads?" (p. 94)
