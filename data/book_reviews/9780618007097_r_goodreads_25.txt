I think that I liked this book a bit more than I liked "The Street," which is also by the same author. Where the "The Street" followed the travails of a single mother living in the Harlem section of NYC during WWII, this book focuses on the varied lives of those residents and more.

The book begins with main protagonist of the short story, the 12-year-old daughter of Samuel and Martha Layen, who own a pharmacy in Wheeling, NY. It must be noted that they are the only black family in Wheeling, NY. The first story, "Miss Muriel" follow this young girl as she engages the suitors after her aunt's affections to the understanding of some family friends and relations as to how to address Miss Muriel."The New Mirror"finds our now 15-year-old running the pharmacy in absence of her parents and the reason for the absence of the adults --- vanity. It is the stories of a teenager attempting to find her own footing through helping her parents run their pharmacy to watching the various suitors of her aunt Sophronia.

The two short stories that I liked the most were "Mother Africa," a Harlem junk dealer's dilemma when given an old statute and "The Bones of Louella Brown," which I thought was hilarious about two white undertakers to wealthy and a mysterious mixup of bodies. I also enjoyed "The Migraine Workers," whose story is as timely today as it was when Ann Petry wrote this collection.
