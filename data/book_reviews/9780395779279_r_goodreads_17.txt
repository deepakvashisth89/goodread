DNF at page 573"Constantine's Sword" is a solid pop history that is incredibly flawed. There is good information here for people who are unfamiliar with the history of Judaism and the Catholic church. If you have never heard of the Dreyfuss affair or have no idea who Hannah Arendt is this book might be worth your time.This issue with this book is that it gets bogged down by Carroll's habit of going into long asides about his relationship with his faith that have little or nothing to do with the point he is trying to make. You could easily cut out the first two hundred pages of this book and miss absolutely nothing- except for the petulant whinings of a guilty Catholic man who just realized that the Catholic church was responsible for some pretty shitty things.And the authors bias is pretty clear in this book. Don't expect a lot of insightful analysis. Expect a lot of excuses and apologia for the Catholic church. Guys, is it "ambivalence" if the church defines a group of people as subhuman in their doctrine but then goes on to stipulate that you really shouldn't kill them because god wouldn't like that? After all the Catholic church did change their ways and eventually said that the Jewish people weren't responsible for Christ's death- in 1965.Two stars for this book because there is some good information here, but James Carroll was really not equipped to write this book as it needed to be written. It is a deeply flawed book.
