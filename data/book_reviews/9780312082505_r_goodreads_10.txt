It's okay, I suppose!I disliked this book's customary implicit inverted and regular snobbish gatekeeping as to what should be considered worthy of being in print. Apparently, Caroline Forbes's THE NEEDLE IS FULL: LESBIAN FEMINIST SCIENCE FICTION is 'an astonishingly specialised' topic, alongside Schizophrenia and Capitalism. This all too often just seems like a Private Eye Pseud's Corner-influenced gallery showing the authors' anti-intellectualism. Anything potentially can make a good book, and pinpointing as "bizarre" or "eccentric" often says more about the gatekeepers Ash and Lake. It typifies their mild, submerged conservative bent that they include Carol J. Adams - THE SEXUAL POLITICS OF MEAT. A FEMINIST-VEGETARIAN CRITICAL THEORY (1990), a perspective they don't want to take seriously.Nevertheless, the book enabled me to note down many such books and others as being of future interest. Among the 'fantastic fiction', what about A-B - A ROMANCE OF BUREAUCRACY (1893)?! A genuine outre oddity: J. C. 'Keridon' Thomas - MAN: THE PRODIGY AND FREAK OF NATURE; OR, AN ANIMAL RUN TO BRAIN (1907), a great cranky title! The Victorian radical George Jacob Holyoake's PUBLIC PERFORMANCES OF THE DEAD (1865) sounds a must. A key issue with this book is there is only very occasional any context provided of who the authors were or how their texts emerged from society.There's also an obvious lowbrow scatological humour of innuendo in the early sections, which might raise titters in a certain type of reader, or as a "toilet book", but which again rather shows Ash and Lake's trivial and biased opposition to the breadth of human thinking and feeling. Yes, William Paul Wanker's NOUS AND LOGOS: PHILOSOPHICAL FOUNDATIONS OF HANNAH ARENDT'S POLITICAL THEORY is used as a punchline just due to an unusually naughty sounding surname!
